# Propulsion systems, start-up and monitoring

A small commercial vessel depends on its engine. The SVOP syllabus expects you to know the main propulsion types, how to start and stop an engine, and what to do when an alarm sounds.

## The four main types

- ==Outboard motor==: a self-contained engine and propeller that hangs on the transom. It steers by turning the whole unit, and it tilts up out of the water.
- ==Stern drive (inboard-outboard)==: an engine inside the hull connected to a drive leg and propeller outside the transom. It steers by turning the leg.
- ==Inboard engine==: an engine inside the hull driving a ==propeller shaft== that passes through the hull to a propeller. A ==rudder== steers the vessel.
- ==Jet drive==: an engine drives an impeller that pumps water out through a nozzle. Steering is by moving the nozzle. There is ==no propeller and no steering at idle== because no water is being thrown out. Jets handle shallow water well.

## Before starting

1. Check the ==fuel== level and that the fuel valve is open.
2. Check the ==engine oil== and ==coolant== levels.
3. Check ==battery== connections.
4. Look for ==leaks== in the engine space and the bilge.
5. Confirm the ==gear lever is in neutral==.
6. Attach the ==kill-switch lanyard== to the operator (where fitted).
7. For an inboard gasoline engine, ==run the bilge blower for at least four minutes== and check for fuel vapour before starting.
8. Make sure the propeller area is clear of people, lines and swimmers.

## Starting and stopping

- Start the engine and check that it runs smoothly and that ==cooling water== is coming out (the telltale stream on an outboard or at the exhaust).
- Check ==oil pressure== and any warning lights straight away.
- Let the engine warm up gently before applying high power.
- To stop: reduce to idle and let a hot engine cool briefly, then switch off. Close the fuel valve if you will be away for a long time.

## Monitoring while running

Check the gauges and the alarm panel often:

- Oil pressure
- Engine temperature
- Battery charging (voltage)
- Fuel level
- Engine speed (RPM)
- Unusual noise, vibration, smoke or smell

Always know the ==normal readings== for your engine so you can spot changes.

## Responding to alarms and faults

- ==Overheating==: reduce power, check the cooling water flow, and stop if it continues.
- ==Low oil pressure==: reduce power and stop the engine promptly. Running without oil destroys an engine in seconds.
- ==No cooling water== on an outboard: stop and check the intake for weed or debris.
- ==Engine failure==: tell the crew, anchor if you are being set onto danger, and call for help if needed.
- ==Fuel smell==: stop, ventilate, and find the cause before restarting.

## A BC example

Running back from a job near Sooke, the temperature alarm sounds on your outboard. You slow down, notice the telltale stream is weak, and shut the engine down. Eelgrass is blocking the water intake. You clear it, restart, and check the telltale before resuming speed.

## Common mistakes

- Starting an inboard gasoline engine without ventilating.
- Ignoring a low oil pressure alarm.
- Forgetting the kill-switch lanyard.
- Trusting a jet drive to steer when stopped.
