# The magnetic compass: bearings, variation and deviation

The compass is the one navigation instrument that needs no power. Learn it well.

## The steering compass

A magnetic compass has a ==card== that floats in liquid and lines up with the earth's magnetic field. It has a ==lubber line== showing the boat's heading. Read your heading at the lubber line.

Mount the compass where you can see it easily. Keep ==magnets, speakers, phones, radios, tools and metal gear== away. They disturb the compass.

## Bearings

- ==Course==: the direction you steer.
- ==Heading==: the direction the bow is pointing.
- ==Bearing==: the direction from you to an object. Use a hand-bearing compass or point the boat at the object and read the heading.

Take a ==bearing== of a mark, then plot the reciprocal on the chart to find your position line.

## Variation

The compass points to ==magnetic north==, not ==true north==. The angle between them is ==variation==. It differs by place and changes slowly over the years. On BC charts it is roughly 15 to 20 degrees east. Read it from the ==compass rose== on the chart and update it for the year if needed.

## Deviation

==Deviation== is an additional error from the boat's own iron, engines and electronics. It changes with the heading. A good installation keeps it small.

## Converting directions

The sequence is: ==True – Variation – Magnetic – Deviation – Compass==. Remember it as ==TVMDC==.

Going from True to Compass:

- ==West is added.==
- ==East is subtracted.==

Going from Compass to True: do the opposite. A handy memory phrase is ==CADET==: Compass, Add East, to get True.

**Example (True to Compass):** True course 090°, variation 17° East, deviation 2° West.

```
True            090°
Variation 17°E  −17°   (East subtracted)
Magnetic        073°
Deviation 2°W   +2°    (West added)
Compass         075°
```

Steer ==075° by the compass==.

**Example (Compass to True):** Compass bearing 075°, deviation 2° West, variation 17° East.

```
Compass         075°
Deviation 2°W   −2°    (reverse of above)
Magnetic        073°
Variation 17°E  +17°   (East added to get True)
True            090°
```

## Checking the compass and making a deviation card

1. Choose a day with a calm sea and a place where you can use a ==known direction==, such as a pair of marks in line (a ==transit==) with a known bearing from the chart.
2. Steer the boat on ==each main heading== (every 45° or 30°), with equipment running as normal.
3. At each heading, compare the compass reading with the true heading from the transit (after converting for variation).
4. The difference is the ==deviation== on that heading. Record them in a table on a ==deviation card==.
5. Keep the card next to the compass and update it if you add equipment.

## A BC example

In Saanich Inlet, you line up two navigation marks that have a charted bearing of 090° true. With variation of 17° East, the magnetic bearing is 073°. You steer toward them and your compass reads 075°. The deviation is 2° West on that heading, and you record it on the card.

## Common mistakes

- Adding variation when you should subtract it.
- Placing a phone or tool next to the compass.
- Never checking the compass.
- Using an old variation.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between variation and deviation?
2. What does CADET stand for?
3. True course 180°, variation 16°E, deviation 0°. What is the compass course?
