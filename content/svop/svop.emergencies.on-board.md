# Emergencies on board: collision, grounding, flooding and fire

Most emergencies are survived by quick, calm, sensible action. Think, act, and call for help early.

## The general approach

1. ==Protect life== first. Account for everyone.
2. Put ==lifejackets on== everyone.
3. ==Stop the problem== (stop the boat, shut off fuel, plug the hole).
4. ==Call for help== early. Don't wait until you are sure.
5. ==Keep passengers calm== and give clear orders.
6. ==Abandon only if you must==, and stay with the vessel or liferaft as long as you can.

## Collision

- Stop and check for ==injuries==.
- Check the ==damage== and look for water coming in.
- ==Sound the bilges== and start pumps.
- If the other vessel has its bow in your hull, ==keep it there== if it is plugging the hole, until you can assess.
- Exchange ==names, vessel details and contacts== with the other vessel.
- Call for help if needed, and ==report== the collision.

## Grounding

- ==Stop the engine== and check people.
- Check for ==damage== and ==flooding==. Sound the bilges.
- Check the ==tide==. Is it rising or falling? If falling, you may be stuck until it comes back.
- Don't force the boat off by power until you know the damage. You may be plugging a hole.
- Consider ==anchoring== to stop being driven further on.
- Look for ==deeper water== by sounding around the boat.
- Call for help if the boat is damaged, the tide is falling, or the weather is worsening.

## Flooding

- Find and ==stop the source== if you can: plug, bung, or stuff a soft patch.
- Use ==pumps and bailers==.
- Put the damaged side up if possible.
- Head for shallow water if the flooding can't be stopped.
- ==Send a Mayday== if the vessel is in danger. Put on lifejackets.

## Fire

- ==Raise the alarm== and tell everyone.
- ==Stop the boat== and, if you can, turn so that the ==fire is downwind==.
- ==Shut off fuel== and power.
- Use an ==extinguisher at the base of the flames==. Don't use water on a fuel or electrical fire.
- Send a ==Mayday== if the fire is not under control.
- Get everyone into lifejackets and be ready to abandon.

## Protecting crew and passengers

- Give a short, clear safety briefing before you leave.
- Keep everyone ==calm and together==.
- Make sure each person has a ==lifejacket==, and the children have the right size.
- Keep people away from hazards, and ==assign tasks== to keep them busy.

## When to send a Mayday

Send a Mayday if there is ==grave and imminent danger== to people or the vessel: fire, serious flooding, sinking, or a person in the water. Use a ==Pan-Pan== if the situation is urgent but life isn't in immediate danger. If in doubt, call.

## A BC example

Running at night near Gabriola Pass, your 6 m boat strikes a log. A hull plank cracks and water comes in. You stop, put on lifejackets, plug the crack with a bung and rag, start the bilge pump, and send a Pan-Pan on VHF channel 16. The coast guard comes alongside and tows you in.

## Common mistakes

- Delaying the call for help.
- Trying to refloat a damaged boat without checking.
- Using water on a fuel fire.
- Forgetting lifejackets.

## Check yourself

1. What is the first priority in any emergency?
2. What do you check after a grounding?
3. When should you send a Mayday?
