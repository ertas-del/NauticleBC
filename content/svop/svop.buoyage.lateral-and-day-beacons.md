# The Canadian buoyage system: lateral buoys and day beacons

Buoys and beacons are the road signs of the water. The lateral system marks the sides of channels.

## Direction of buoyage

The system assumes you are ==returning from sea== or going ==upstream== (toward the head of a channel, harbour or river). Going the other way, everything flips.

## The rule: red right returning

- ==Red== buoys are kept on your ==right (starboard)== side when going upstream.
- ==Green== buoys are kept on your ==left (port)== side.

Memory phrase: ==red, right, returning==. Going out to sea, the opposite is true: green on your right.

## Lateral buoys

| | Starboard-hand (red) | Port-hand (green) |
|---|---|---|
| Colour | ==Red== | ==Green== |
| Shape | ==Conical (nun)== | ==Flat-topped (can)== |
| Numbers | ==Even== | ==Odd== |
| Light | ==Red== | ==Green== |

Numbers increase as you go upstream. The numbers help you match the buoy to the chart.

## Day beacons

A day beacon is a fixed mark on a pole or structure, often on shore or a rock. ==Daymarks== are:

- ==Red triangle== for the starboard side (going upstream).
- ==Green square== for the port side.

They often carry numbers and may have a light.

## Fairway (mid-channel) buoys

A ==red and white vertical striped== buoy marks mid-channel or safe water all around. You can pass either side. It may carry a red ball topmark and a white light.

## Navigating a marked channel

1. Know the direction of buoyage. Are you going upstream or downstream?
2. Keep ==red on your right== and ==green on your left== when going upstream.
3. Keep to the correct side. Don't cut corners. Stay between the buoys.
4. Match the buoy to the chart using its number, colour and light.
5. Watch for the current setting you onto a mark.
6. If a buoy is off station or missing, treat the area with caution and report it.

## A BC example

Returning to Victoria harbour from the Strait, you head toward the entrance with a green buoy to your left and a red buoy to your right. Both numbers are increasing toward the harbour. You steer between them, keeping red on your right.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting that the rule reverses when going out to sea.
- Passing on the wrong side of a buoy.
- Assuming a buoy is exactly where it is charted. It can drift.

## Check yourself

1. Which side do you keep red buoys on when returning?
2. What shape is a green buoy?
3. What is a day beacon?
