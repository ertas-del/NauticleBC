# The SVOP certificate, the law and the master's duties

The Small Vessel Operator Proficiency (SVOP) is the entry-level certificate for people who run small commercial boats. Before you learn how to drive one, you need to know where the certificate applies and what the law expects of you.

## What the SVOP allows

On a ==near coastal Class 2== or ==sheltered waters== voyage, in normal operating conditions (including darkness and restricted visibility), an SVOP holder may be the operator of:

- A commercial vessel of up to ==5 gross tonnage==, except tugs.
- A fishing vessel of up to ==15 gross tonnage== or not more than ==12 metres== long.

You must be ==at least 18== to use the certificate as operator. Many courses accept younger students, but the certificate is not valid for operating until you are 18.

## Vessel, passenger, pleasure craft

- A ==vessel== is any boat, ship or craft used, or able to be used, for navigation on the water.
- A ==passenger== is anyone on board other than the master, the crew, and people working on the vessel's business.
- A ==pleasure craft== is used only for pleasure, with no commercial purpose.

A boat stops being a pleasure craft when it is used for trade or hire. Examples: a water taxi, a charter boat, a whale-watching boat, a sport-fishing guide boat, a crew boat, or a boat used for commercial fishing. Then the rules for commercial vessels apply.

## Who needs an SVOP

The person in charge of a small commercial vessel of this type needs an SVOP. Other crew may need other training depending on the vessel, for example a small vessel safety course, a marine radio certificate (ROC-M) and someone trained in marine first aid. Check what your vessel requires.

## Transport Canada checks

Transport Canada runs inspection and monitoring programs for small commercial vessels. Expect that officers may check your certificates, safety equipment and records. Keep your papers on board and your equipment in good order.

## The master's responsibilities

The master is responsible for:

- ==Safety of life== of passengers and crew.
- ==Safety of the vessel== and property.
- ==Protecting the environment==.
- The ==commercial side==: records, following the law, using a competent crew, and not overloading the vessel.

If something goes wrong, the master is the one who answers for it.

## A BC example

A whale-watching boat out of Victoria carries eight passengers on a 5 GT vessel. The operator holds an SVOP, a small vessel safety certificate and a marine radio certificate, and carries a crew member with marine first aid. That is the right shape: the vessel is under 5 GT, the voyage is sheltered or near coastal Class 2, and the people have the right training.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a certificate lets you run any boat. It covers specific vessel types and waters only.
- Treating a paid job as "just pleasure boating".
- Leaving certificates ashore.
