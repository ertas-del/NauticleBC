# Search and rescue, and VHF distress, urgency and routine calls

Help is available, but it needs to be asked for in the right way. Know who to call and what to say.

## SAR resources in BC

- ==Joint Rescue Coordination Centre (JRCC) Victoria==: coordinates marine and air search and rescue for the Pacific region.
- ==Canadian Coast Guard== vessels, lifeboats and hovercraft.
- ==Canadian Coast Guard Auxiliary==: volunteers with their own boats.
- ==Royal Canadian Air Force== aircraft and helicopters.
- ==Marine Communications and Traffic Services (MCTS)==: monitor VHF channel 16 and relay calls to JRCC.
- ==Other vessels== nearby, including commercial vessels and other boaters.

## VHF channels

- ==Channel 16== (156.8 MHz): distress, safety and calling. Keep a listening watch.
- ==Channel 70==: digital selective calling (DSC) distress alerts.
- ==Weather channels== for forecasts.
- ==Working channels== for routine conversations after calling on 16.

## What happens after a distress call

1. MCTS or another station hears the Mayday and ==acknowledges== it.
2. ==JRCC== is told and tasks suitable resources.
3. Nearby vessels may be asked to ==help==.
4. A ==coordination== is set up and updates are given.
5. You may be asked to give more details, activate beacons, and stay on the radio.

## The three priority prefixes

| Prefix | Meaning | When |
|---|---|---|
| ==Mayday== (said three times) | Distress | Grave and imminent danger to vessel or person |
| ==Pan-Pan== (three times) | Urgency | Urgent situation, not immediate danger |
| ==Sécurité== (three times) | Safety | Navigation or weather warnings |

Routine calls do not use these prefixes.

## Sending a Mayday

Switch to channel 16 (or send a DSC alert) and say:

> Mayday, Mayday, Mayday.
> This is [vessel name] ×3.
> Mayday [vessel name].
> My position is [position or distance and bearing from a known point].
> [Nature of distress, such as sinking, fire].
> [Assistance needed].
> [Number of persons on board].
> [Any other useful information: vessel type, colour, flares].
> Over.

Speak clearly and slowly. Repeat if there is no reply. Use your DSC distress button if fitted. Keep the radio on for instructions.

## Hearing a Mayday

- ==Stop transmitting== and ==listen==.
- ==Write down== the details.
- If no station has acknowledged it, and you are able to, ==acknowledge== it.
- ==Relay== the message if the vessel in distress is not being heard.
- ==Help== if you can, but do not put yourself in danger.
- The station controlling the distress may impose radio silence with the call ==Seelonce Mayday==.

## A BC example

You hear a Mayday from a fishing boat sinking off the west coast of Vancouver Island. You listen, write the position and details, and hear no acknowledgment. You acknowledge the call, relay it to MCTS and head toward the position while following instructions.

## Common mistakes

- Using Mayday when it is only Pan-Pan.
- Not giving a position.
- Not keeping a watch on channel 16.
- Talking over a distress call.

## Check yourself

1. What does Pan-Pan mean?
2. What goes in a Mayday message?
3. What do you do when you hear a Mayday?
