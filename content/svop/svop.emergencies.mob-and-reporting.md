# Man overboard and reporting marine occurrences

Falling overboard is one of the most common and deadly emergencies on small boats. Speed and calm decide the outcome.

## Immediate actions

1. ==Shout== "Man overboard" so the whole crew knows.
2. ==Point== at the person and ==keep pointing and watching==. Assign someone to do nothing else but watch.
3. ==Throw a flotation device== (lifebuoy, lifejacket, cushion) immediately, near the person.
4. ==Mark the position== with the GPS man-overboard button, or note the time and heading.
5. ==Slow and turn== the boat back toward the person.
6. If you have a radio, send a ==Mayday or Pan-Pan== with the position.

## Approach and recovery

- Plan the approach so the person is on the ==windward side== of the boat. The boat then drifts toward the person, not over them.
- Approach slowly and ==shift to neutral== when close, to keep the propeller still. Switch the engine off if you can.
- Throw a ==line== or heaving line. Use a ==boat hook== or ==ladder==.
- On small boats, bring the person in over the ==stern or the lowest point== (a swim platform). A very low point is the easiest.
- Recover someone who is unconscious using a lifting sling, a ladder, or by tying a line around them and hauling.
- Never jump in after the person unless it is absolutely necessary and you are wearing a lifejacket.

## Cold water

Cold water is dangerous.

- ==Cold shock== in the first minutes can cause gasping and panic.
- ==Swim failure== in the next 10 to 30 minutes makes it hard to use arms and legs.
- ==Hypothermia== develops after 30 minutes or more.

Get the person out and dry, wrap them in warm blankets, and keep them lying flat if possible. Get medical help.

## Prevention

- Wear a ==lifejacket== on deck.
- Use ==jacklines== and ==harnesses== in rough weather.
- Keep decks clear and non-slip.
- Have a plan and practise it.

## Reporting marine occurrences

The master has legal duties to ==report== some events, such as:

- Collision or grounding
- Loss of life or serious injury
- Fire or explosion
- Pollution
- Loss of or damage to the vessel

The usual pattern is an ==initial report== as soon as possible to the proper authority (such as the Coast Guard or Transport Canada), and a ==follow-up report== later with full details. Check the current rules for your vessel type. Keep records and a log of what happened.

## A BC example

A deckhand falls overboard in Johnstone Strait. The operator shouts, throws a lifebuoy, and tells another crew member to point at the person. The operator marks the GPS, turns the boat, and approaches with the person on the windward side. She puts the engine in neutral and the crew pull the deckhand aboard with a line and a boat hook.

## Common mistakes

- Losing sight of the person.
- Approaching with the person on the lee side.
- Leaving the engine in gear near the person.
- Not wearing a lifejacket.

## Check yourself

1. What do you do in the first seconds after someone falls overboard?
2. Which side of the boat should the person be on when you approach?
3. Why must the master report incidents?
