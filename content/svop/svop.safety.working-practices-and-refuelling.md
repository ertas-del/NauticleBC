# Safe working practices: shipshape, aloft, confined spaces and refuelling

Most accidents on small commercial boats are ordinary ones: slips, falls, crushed fingers, burns and fires. Good habits prevent most of them.

## Keep the vessel shipshape

- Stow gear and lines, coil ropes and clear the deck.
- Clean up oil, fuel and water spills at once.
- Keep walkways, hatches and exits clear.
- Secure everything that can move in a seaway.
- Report and repair damage, loose fittings and worn gear.

## Workplace hazards

- ==Slips and falls== on a wet deck.
- ==Ropes and winches== that can catch hands and clothing.
- ==Moving machinery== and hot engines or exhausts.
- ==Fire== and ==fuel vapour==.
- ==Noise== that damages hearing.
- ==Cold water== and ==falling overboard==.
- ==Fatigue==.

Use ==personal protective equipment==: gloves, safety glasses, ear protection, non-slip footwear and a lifejacket or PFD when working on deck.

## Working aloft

Work aloft is any work above deck level, such as on a mast or a roof.

- Tell the master and agree signals before you go.
- Wear a ==harness== and a ==lifejacket==.
- Stop the engines and radar and any transmitters you are close to.
- Carry tools in a bag and use a lanyard. Nothing should fall on people below.
- Don't work aloft in rough weather.

## Confined spaces

A confined space (a tank, a void, a locker, a bilge compartment) may have ==too little oxygen==, toxic gas or flammable vapour. Before entry:

1. Get permission and make a plan.
2. ==Ventilate== and ==test the air==.
3. Have a ==standby person== outside, in contact.
4. Never enter alone, and ==never go in to rescue someone== without breathing apparatus and help.

## Signals for winch or crane work

The operator should see a single signaller, who uses clear hand signals:

- ==Raise== : forearm vertical, hand making small circles.
- ==Lower==: arm out, hand making small circles pointing down.
- ==Stop==: arm out, palm down, moved side to side.
- ==Emergency stop==: both arms out, palms down, moved side to side.

Everyone agrees the signals before the lift. Stay out from under the load and don't stand in the line of a rope under strain.

## Safe refuelling

1. ==Stop engines== and switch off ==flames==, heaters and ==electrical equipment== that can spark. No smoking.
2. ==Moor the boat== and keep passengers off if possible.
3. Close doors, hatches and ports to stop vapour entering.
4. Keep the nozzle in contact with the filler to avoid static sparks.
5. Fill ==slowly==. Know the tank capacity and don't ==overfill==. Allow for expansion.
6. ==Wipe up any spill==. Dispose of rags safely.
7. For an inboard gasoline engine, ==run the blower for at least four minutes== and check for fuel smell before starting.
8. For a portable tank, refuel it ashore on the dock, not in the boat.

## A BC example

You refuel a 6 m aluminium workboat with a gasoline inboard at a fuel dock in Nanaimo. You switch off the engine and everything electrical, fill slowly, wipe a small drip, close the filler, run the blower for four minutes and check the bilge by nose before starting.

## Common mistakes

- Smoking while refuelling.
- Overfilling and spilling fuel.
- Starting the engine without ventilating.
- Entering a tank "just for a minute".

## Check yourself

1. Why must a vessel be kept shipshape?
2. What must you do before entering a confined space?
3. List four steps for safe refuelling.
