# Nautical publications and using the tide tables

A chart tells you about the permanent features of the water. Publications tell you what has changed and what is happening right now.

## The main publications

- ==List of Lights, Buoys and Fog Signals==: details of every light, buoy, beacon and fog signal, including its position, colour and characteristics.
- ==Canadian Tide and Current Tables==: times and heights of high and low water, and tidal current predictions, for reference ports and many secondary ports.
- ==Notices to Mariners==: corrections to charts and publications, and warnings of hazards, closures and changes. Check them before every trip.
- ==Sailing Directions==: written descriptions of coasts, harbours, hazards and local conditions.
- ==Chart No. 1==: symbols and abbreviations.

## Using the tide table

A tide table gives, for each day:

- The ==time== of high water and low water.
- The ==height== of each tide, in metres, above chart datum.

To find the tide height at a time between high and low water you use a ==rule of twelfths== or the interpolation table in the book. In the SVOP syllabus you need the principle: depth changes with the tide, and you need enough water under the keel.

## Working out depth

> Depth of water = charted depth + height of tide

Example: the chart shows 2.0 m at a spot. The tide table says the tide will be 3.1 m above chart datum at 14:00. The depth at 14:00 is 2.0 + 3.1 = ==5.1 m==.

> Under-keel clearance = depth − draught

If your draught is 1.0 m, you have 5.1 − 1.0 = ==4.1 m== under the keel. Always allow a safety margin.

At low water the depth is just the charted depth (plus a small amount if the tide is above datum). A rock with a drying height shows above chart datum and will be exposed at low tide.

## Tidal currents

Tidal currents flow in and out of channels. In narrows and passes they can be very strong. The current tables give the times and speeds of ==maximum flow== and ==slack water==. Plan to pass narrow, fast passes near ==slack water==, and avoid wind against tide.

## Checking Notices to Mariners

Before you go, check for corrections to your charts, new hazards, buoys out of position, and temporary closures. Mark corrections on the chart.

## A BC example

You plan to take a boat through Seymour Narrows. You look up the current tables for the day, find slack water at 10:20, and plan to arrive 15 minutes before. You check Notices to Mariners and see a note about work in the area, so you plan to avoid it.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to add the tide height to charted depths.
- Passing a narrow at full ebb with wind against the current.
- Not checking Notices to Mariners.
- Not allowing a safety margin under the keel.

## Check yourself

1. What does the List of Lights contain?
2. A charted depth is 3.0 m and the tide is 2.0 m. What is the water depth?
3. Why plan to go through narrows near slack water?
