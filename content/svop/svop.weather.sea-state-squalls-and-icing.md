# Sea state, squalls, thunderstorms and icing

The wind speed in a forecast is only part of the picture. The sea you meet depends on much more.

## What builds a sea

- ==Wind speed==: stronger wind builds bigger waves.
- ==Duration==: the longer it blows, the bigger the sea.
- ==Fetch==: the distance of open water the wind blows over. A long fetch builds big seas.
- ==Swell==: waves from distant weather that arrive even when the local wind is calm.

## Wind against current or tide

When wind blows against a current or tide, waves become ==steeper, shorter and breaking==. Tide rips and narrow passages can be dangerous for small boats, even in moderate wind. Check the tide and current tables, and try to cross when the current and wind are going the same way.

## Shallow water

Waves steepen and break as they enter shallow water, over bars and across river mouths. Avoid crossing bars when waves are large, especially on an ebb tide.

## Land effects

- ==Funnelling==: wind blowing through a valley, inlet or narrow strait can reach much higher speeds than the open-water forecast.
- ==Deflection==: wind turns and speeds up around headlands and along coastlines.
- ==Sea breezes== form on sunny days when the land heats up and draws in air from the water.
- Outflow winds in winter can blast down long fjords in BC.

Learn the local patterns for your area, and expect more wind near headlands and narrow channels.

## Thunderstorms and squalls

- A ==thunderstorm== brings lightning, heavy rain, hail and sudden, strong, gusty winds.
- A ==squall== is a sudden, sharp increase in wind speed lasting a few minutes, often with rain.
- A ==squall line== is a line of dark clouds and storms. Recognise it by a dark band of cloud, a wall of rain, and a sudden drop in temperature.
- Keep clear, reduce speed early, secure gear, and put everyone in lifejackets.
- Lightning is dangerous. Keep people low and away from metal fittings.

## Icing

Sea spray freezes onto the vessel when the air temperature is well below freezing and the wind is strong. The ice adds ==top weight==, raises the centre of gravity, and reduces freeboard. It builds up fast on small boats. Icing is a serious cause of capsizing. Avoid it by staying out of areas with freezing spray, and remove ice carefully if you get it.

## Weather and loading

The same load is riskier in rough weather. If bad weather is forecast:

- Reduce passengers and cargo.
- Keep weight low.
- Fill tanks to avoid free surface.
- Secure all gear and close hatches.

## A BC example

The marine forecast for Johnstone Strait says northwest winds 15 knots. You expect more because the strait funnels the wind. At the narrow part the wind is gusting to 30 knots against the ebb tide. You time your passage to go with the tide and keep a reserve of fuel.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the forecast speed as the same everywhere.
- Crossing a bar against the ebb with big waves.
- Ignoring dark clouds approaching.
- Overloading a boat in a forecast of rising wind.
