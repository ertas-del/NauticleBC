# Quick reference checklists

A checklist is a short list of steps you work through every time. Pilots and sailors use them because people forget things, especially when tired or busy.

## Why use checklists

- They make sure nothing is missed.
- They make routines the same every time.
- They help new crew learn what to do.
- They give a record of what was checked.

## Daily maintenance

- Check engine oil, coolant and fuel levels.
- Look for leaks in the engine space and bilge.
- Check the bilge pump and high-water alarm.
- Check lights, horn and radio.
- Inspect the hull, rigging and fittings.

## Weekly maintenance

- Check batteries and connections.
- Check belts, hoses and filters.
- Test the fire extinguishers and lifejackets.
- Run the engine and test the gear shifts.
- Clean the boat and check for wear.

## Safety gear

- ==Lifejackets== for everyone, in good condition.
- ==Flares== in date.
- ==Fire extinguishers== charged.
- ==Bailer or pump==, ==anchor== and line.
- ==First aid kit==, flashlight and horn.
- ==Radio== and a way to call for help.

## Engine start-up

- Check fuel, oil and coolant.
- Run the blower if gasoline inboard.
- Gear in neutral, kill switch lanyard on.
- After starting, check oil pressure, cooling water and gauges.

## Departure preparation

- Weather, tides and fuel.
- Safety briefing for passengers.
- Charts, publications and electronics.
- Trip plan filed.

## Pre-voyage

- Walk-round inspection.
- Equipment and safety gear in place.
- Passengers briefed on lifejackets and emergency steps.
- Lines and fenders ready.

## Post-voyage

- Secure the boat.
- Check for damage, leaks and problems.
- Refuel if needed.
- Clean and stow gear.
- Log any defects and repairs.
- Tell your trip plan contact you are back.

## Fuelling

- Engine off, no smoking.
- Absorbents in place.
- Fill slowly, don't overfill.
- Wipe spills.
- Run the blower before starting.

## Heavy weather

- Check the forecast and decide whether to go.
- Reduce load and speed.
- Secure gear and close hatches.
- Lifejackets on.
- Head for shelter early.
- Tell someone your plan.

## A BC example

Each morning at the dock in Port McNeill, you work through the daily checklist: oil, coolant and bilge, then the safety gear checklist. A crack in a lifejacket buckle shows up and you replace the jacket before the passengers arrive.

## Common mistakes

- Skipping the checklist when in a hurry.
- Doing it from memory.
- Not recording faults.
- Not updating the list when equipment changes.

## Check yourself

1. Why do checklists reduce mistakes?
2. Name three items on the safety gear checklist.
3. What belongs on a heavy-weather checklist?
