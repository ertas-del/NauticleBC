# Navigation lights, signs and shapes

At night, and in reduced visibility, lights are how other vessels know what you are and which way you are heading. Learn the colours and the arcs.

## Basic lights

- ==Sidelights==: a ==green== light on the starboard side and a ==red== light on the port side. Each shows from straight ahead to 22.5 degrees abaft the beam.
- ==Stern light==: a ==white== light showing aft.
- ==Masthead light==: a ==white== light showing forward, over the centreline.
- ==All-round light==: shows its colour in every direction, over 360 degrees.

## Small power-driven vessel, underway

- Under 12 m: a ==masthead light== (or one ==all-round white light==) and ==sidelights==. Sidelights may be in one lantern on the centreline.
- 12 m and over: a ==masthead light==, ==sidelights== and a ==stern light==.

What you see tells you what the other boat is doing:

- ==Red and green== seen together ahead: head-on. Both turn to starboard.
- ==A red light on your starboard bow== (you are seeing her port side, and she is on your right): you are the ==give-way== vessel.
- ==A green light on your port bow== (you are seeing her starboard side, and she is on your left): you are the ==stand-on== vessel.
- ==White light only==: you are looking at her stern. She is moving away, or you are overtaking her.

## Sailing vessels

A sailing vessel under sail shows sidelights and a stern light (for under 20 m, a combined tricolour at the masthead is allowed). A boat sailing and using its engine at the same time is a power-driven vessel, and shows a black ==cone, point down==, by day.

## At anchor

An anchored vessel shows an ==all-round white light== where it can be seen from all directions, and by day a ==black ball== in the bows. Very small vessels at anchor, not in or near a narrow channel or anchorage, may not need to show lights. Always check the regulations for your vessel.

## Other signals to recognise

- ==Towing==: two white masthead lights in a vertical line (for tows under 200 m), plus sidelights, a stern light and a yellow towing light above the stern light.
- ==Vessel being towed==: sidelights and a stern light.
- ==Fishing== (trawling): green over white all-round lights. Other fishing: red over white.
- ==Not under command==: two red all-round lights in a vertical line. By day, two black balls.
- ==Restricted in ability to manoeuvre==: red, white, red all-round lights. By day, ball, diamond, ball.
- ==Aground==: anchor light plus two red all-round lights. By day, three black balls.
- ==Diver down==: flag Alpha (blue and white swallowtail) is displayed by a vessel with divers in the water. Keep well clear.

## Radar reflector

A small boat is a poor radar target, especially one made of fibreglass or wood. A ==radar reflector== makes a larger echo so ships can see you. Mount it as high as practical. It is strongly recommended and required on some vessels.

## A BC example

Coming into Active Pass at night you see a red sidelight on your starboard bow. Its bearing is not changing. The vessel is on your right, so you are the give-way vessel. You make a bold alteration to starboard to pass astern of her, and resume your course once clear.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up red and green, or which side they show on.
- Leaving the anchor light off.
- Leaving a bright cabin light that hides your navigation lights.
- Not looking at the pattern of lights to work out the situation.

## Check yourself

1. Which colour is the port sidelight?
2. What lights does an anchored vessel show?
3. What does flag Alpha mean?
