# Ropes, knots and mooring

Ropes and knots are used every day. A bad knot or a worn line can cost a boat.

## Rope types

- ==Nylon==: strong, stretchy and absorbs shock. Best for mooring and anchor lines.
- ==Polyester==: strong with low stretch and good wear. Good for halyards and sheets.
- ==Polypropylene==: floats, but is weaker and breaks down in sunlight. Good for a heaving line or a tow floater.
- ==Natural rope== (manila, sisal): cheaper, heavy, rots and swells when wet.

Rope construction can be ==three-strand twisted== or ==braided==. Braided is smoother and kinks less. Three-strand is easy to splice.

**Care:** keep ropes clean and dry, out of sunlight, away from chemicals, and avoid chafing at fairleads. Replace a rope that is worn, flattened, melted or cut. Never stand in line with a rope under strain. If it breaks it can snap back.

## Mooring lines

- ==Bow line==: from the bow to the dock.
- ==Stern line==: from the stern to the dock.
- ==Breast lines==: from bow and stern, at right angles to the dock, to hold the boat in close.
- ==Spring lines==: from near the middle of the boat, leading fore and aft. A ==forward spring== stops the boat moving astern. An ==aft spring== stops it moving ahead.

## Floating dock or fixed wharf

- ==Floating dock==: it rises and falls with the tide, so the lines can be short and snug.
- ==Fixed wharf==: the boat goes up and down against a fixed height. Leave ==enough slack for the tide range== or the boat will hang or be dragged under.

## Fenders

Use fenders to protect the hull between the boat and the dock. Hang them at the point of contact, at the right height, and adjust them as the tide moves. Small boats may need more than one.

## Basic knots

| Knot | Use |
|---|---|
| ==Bowline== | a fixed loop that does not slip |
| ==Clove hitch== | quick attachment to a post or rail (can slip if not under steady load) |
| ==Round turn and two half hitches== | secure a line to a ring or post under strain |
| ==Reef (square) knot== | join two lines of the same size |
| ==Sheet bend== | join lines of different size |
| ==Figure-eight== | a stopper knot to prevent a line running out |
| ==Cleat hitch== | secure a line to a cleat |

## A BC example

Tying up at a fixed wharf in Port Hardy with a 4 m tide, you put your bow and stern lines on loosely with plenty of slack and add a forward and an aft spring. When you come back at low tide the boat is hanging safely against its fenders.

## Common mistakes

- Tying up too tight at a fixed wharf and hanging the boat as the tide drops.
- Using polypropylene as an anchor line.
- Standing in the bight of a rope under load.
- Forgetting fenders.
