# The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 and the main regulations

The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 (CSA 2001) is Canada's main shipping law. It covers safety, certificates, pollution, accident reporting and more. It sets the rules, and regulations explain the details.

## Main regulations for small vessels

- ==Small Vessel Regulations==: safety rules for small vessels, including equipment, inspection and operation for small commercial vessels.
- ==Fishing Vessel Safety Regulations==: safety rules for fishing vessels.
- ==Marine Personnel Regulations==: certificates, crew requirements and training.
- ==Collision Regulations==: rules of the road, including Canadian modifications.
- ==Prevention of Pollution from Ships and for Dangerous Chemicals Regulations==: oil, garbage and sewage.
- ==Vessel Certificates Regulations==, and ==Casualty Reporting Regulations== for reporting accidents.

Which one applies depends on the vessel's size, type and use. Always check which apply to your vessel.

## Safety equipment

Small vessels must carry safety equipment suited to their size and how they operate. This normally includes:

- ==Lifejackets or PFDs== for everyone on board.
- ==Flares== or other visual signals.
- A ==fire extinguisher== of the right type.
- A ==bailer or hand pump==.
- ==Anchor and line==.
- ==Navigation lights== when operating at night.
- ==Sound signalling device== such as a horn or whistle.
- A ==watertight flashlight==.
- ==Buoyant heaving line==.
- ==Compass and charts== as required.

Check the equipment list for your vessel size and class, and keep everything in date and working.

## Pollution regulations

The rules prohibit discharging oil and plastic. They limit discharge of sewage and other garbage. They require reporting of pollution incidents.

## Parks and marine mammals

- ==Marine mammal rules== protect whales, dolphins, porpoises and seals. In BC, you must keep a minimum distance from them, and a greater distance from killer whales. Check the current distances.
- ==National and marine parks== have their own rules, such as speed limits, no-go zones and fees.
- ==Protected areas== may have seasonal closures.

Observe, don't chase, and don't separate groups. A commercial operator is expected to know and follow these rules.

## Following the rules

Operators are responsible for knowing which regulations apply, carrying the right certificates and equipment, and keeping their crew trained. Inspectors can check compliance and issue penalties for breaches.

## A BC example

You run whale-watching trips from Victoria. You check that you carry lifejackets for everyone, in date flares, an extinguisher, a bailer, a horn and navigation lights. You also keep to the minimum distances from whales set in the regulations and slow down near them.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming recreational rules apply to a commercial boat.
- Carrying out-of-date equipment.
- Approaching whales too closely.
- Not knowing which regulations apply.
