# Collision regulations: the rules of the road

The Collision Regulations (COLREGs), with Canadian modifications, are the traffic rules of the water. SVOP candidates need a basic understanding.

## Key terms

- ==Underway==: not at anchor, made fast to the shore, or aground.
- ==Making way==: moving through the water.
- ==Give-way vessel==: the vessel that must keep out of the way.
- ==Stand-on vessel==: the vessel that must keep her course and speed.
- ==Risk of collision==: a situation where vessels will collide if nothing changes.
- ==Safe speed==: a speed that lets you take effective action and stop within a suitable distance.

## Lookout

Keep a ==proper lookout== at all times. Use sight, hearing and every available means (including radar if fitted) to judge the situation. Do not let the radio, phones or paperwork distract the person at the helm. Lookout is the most basic rule and the most commonly broken.

## Safe speed

Choose a speed that lets you act in time. Factors: visibility, traffic density, your ability to stop and turn, wind, sea and current, and nearby dangers. Slow down in poor visibility, in a crowded area and at night.

## Risk of collision

Watch the ==compass bearing== of an approaching vessel. If it stays the same while the distance falls, ==there is a risk of collision==. If in doubt, assume there is.

## Action to avoid collision

Act ==early==, make a ==large enough== change for the other vessel to see, and keep it up until the vessel is clear. Slow or stop if needed. Avoid a series of small alterations.

## The three situations

**Head-on.** Two power-driven vessels meeting on reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses: ==both alter course to starboard== and pass port to port.

**Crossing.** When two power-driven vessels cross, the vessel that has the other on her ==starboard side== is the give-way vessel. The other stands on. The give-way vessel should avoid crossing ahead of the stand-on vessel.

**Overtaking.** A vessel coming up from more than 22.5 degrees abaft the beam of another is overtaking. The ==overtaking vessel keeps clear== until she is finally past. If you are not sure whether you are overtaking, assume you are.

## The stand-on vessel

Keep course and speed. If the give-way vessel is clearly not acting, you may act on your own. If the collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone, you must act. Avoid turning to port for a vessel on your port side.

## Order of responsibility

In general a power-driven vessel keeps out of the way of:

1. A vessel not under command
2. A vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre
3. A vessel engaged in fishing
4. A sailing vessel

A sailing vessel keeps clear of vessels not under command, restricted in their ability to manoeuvre and fishing vessels.

## Sailing vessels

- Wind on different sides: the vessel with the wind on the ==port== side gives way.
- Wind on the same side: the ==windward== vessel gives way.

## Narrow channels

Keep as near to the ==outer limit on your starboard side== as is safe. Vessels under 20 metres and sailing vessels must not impede a vessel that can only navigate safely inside the channel. Do not anchor in a channel.

## Restricted visibility

Go at a ==safe speed==, with engines ready for immediate manoeuvre. Use radar if fitted. Sound the proper fog signals. If you hear a signal ahead of you apparently forward of your beam, slow to the minimum steerage speed and be ready to stop.

## A BC example

Crossing Georgia Strait in daylight you see a vessel on your starboard bow. You take a bearing: it isn't changing and the distance is closing. The other vessel is on your starboard side, so you give way. You make a clear alteration to starboard, pass astern of it, and resume course.

## Common mistakes

- Not keeping a lookout.
- Alterations so small that the other vessel can't see them.
- Crossing ahead of a stand-on vessel.
- Assuming the other boat has seen you.

## Check yourself

1. In a head-on meeting, which way does each vessel turn?
2. Which vessel gives way in a crossing situation?
3. How do you know there is risk of collision?
