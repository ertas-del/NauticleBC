# Cardinal, isolated danger, safe water, special and diver marks

Beyond lateral marks, there are other kinds of marks that warn you of specific dangers and show where it is safe to pass.

## Cardinal marks

A ==cardinal mark== is named for a compass direction (north, east, south, west). It tells you the safe water lies on that side of the mark. Cardinal marks are ==black and yellow== and have distinct topmarks of two cones.

| Mark | Colours | Topmarks | Pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==North== | black above yellow | two cones both pointing up | to the ==north== of the mark |
| ==East== | black, yellow, black | cones base to base | to the ==east== |
| ==South== | yellow above black | two cones both pointing down | to the ==south== |
| ==West== | yellow, black, yellow | cones point to point (hourglass) | to the ==west== |

Their lights are white and quick flashing, with a different number of flashes for each direction.

## Isolated danger mark

An ==isolated danger mark== sits on or over a small danger, such as a rock. It is ==black with a red horizontal band== and two black balls on top. Water around it is navigable, but you must keep ==well clear== of the mark itself.

## Fairway (safe water) mark

==Red and white vertical stripes== mark safe water all around, such as the approach to a channel. You can pass on either side.

## Special purpose buoys

==Yellow== buoys mark special areas: cables, pipelines, anchorages, military zones, fishing or swimming areas, or traffic separation. Read the chart to learn what a yellow buoy means. Some carry a yellow X topmark.

## Cautionary buoys

A cautionary buoy warns of a hazard or restricted area. Treat it with care and check the chart and Notices to Mariners.

## Bifurcation (preferred channel) marks

A ==bifurcation== mark is where a channel splits into two. It has ==horizontal bands of red and green==. The ==top band colour== shows which side is the preferred (main) channel, according to the usual lateral rules. Always check the chart to confirm which way to go.

## Diver's flag and scuba buoys

A boat with divers in the water displays flag ==Alpha== (blue and white swallowtail). A float or buoy marking divers displays a ==red flag with a white diagonal stripe==. Always keep well clear, slow down and watch for bubbles and divers. Keep your propeller and wake away from them.

## A BC example

Approaching Ogden Point you see a yellow-over-black buoy with two cones pointing down. It is a south cardinal mark. You pass to the south of it, because the rocks lie to the north of it.

## Common mistakes

- Passing the wrong side of a cardinal mark.
- Cutting close to an isolated danger mark.
- Ignoring a yellow buoy because it "isn't a channel mark".
- Running over a diver's float.

## Check yourself

1. Which side do you pass a north cardinal mark?
2. What does an isolated danger mark look like?
3. What colour are special purpose buoys?
