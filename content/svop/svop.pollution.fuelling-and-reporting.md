# Pollution prevention: fuelling, reporting, sewage and garbage

A small spill can do big harm in a bay or harbour. Prevention is simple, and the rules are firm.

## Precautions when fuelling

- Moor securely and ==stop the engine==.
- Put an absorbent pad or ==drip tray== under the filler and any connection.
- Know the ==tank capacity== and fill ==slowly==. Never overfill.
- Stay with the nozzle and watch the vents.
- ==Stop immediately== if fuel starts to spill.
- Wipe any drips and dispose of oily rags safely.
- Keep a ==spill kit== on board.

## Reporting a pollution incident

If you release or see a pollutant in the water, you must:

1. ==Stop the source== if it is safe.
2. ==Contain== it with absorbents.
3. ==Report immediately== to the Coast Guard (through MCTS on VHF channel 16 or by phone) giving position, type of pollutant, amount and what you are doing.
4. Follow instructions and keep records.

Don't use detergents or dispersants unless told to.

## Garbage

- ==Never throw plastic== overboard anywhere.
- Don't dump other garbage in the water close to shore. Take it back to port and use proper disposal.
- Keep a bag for garbage, secure it from blowing away, and carry the rubbish ashore.
- Fishing gear that is lost should be reported where required.

## Sewage

- Don't discharge ==untreated sewage== close to shore, in harbours or marinas, or in other sensitive areas.
- Use ==pump-out stations== for holding tanks.
- If you have a marine toilet, know whether it discharges overboard and where that is allowed.
- Check the current rules for your area.

## A BC example

At a fuel dock in Nanaimo, you place an absorbent pad under the filler, fill slowly and stop when the pump clicks. A few drops spill and you wipe them up at once. Your garbage goes into a bag and then into the marina bin.

## Common mistakes

- Overfilling and spilling at the dock.
- Throwing plastic overboard.
- Reporting late.
- Using detergent on a spill.

## Check yourself

1. What should you put under the fuel filler?
2. Who do you report a spill to?
3. What must never be thrown overboard?
