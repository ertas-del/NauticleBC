# Manoeuvring and anchoring

Most damage to small boats happens at low speed around docks. Good boat handling is calm, slow and planned.

## What affects handling

- ==Propeller walk==: a spinning propeller pushes the stern sideways, most strongly when you go astern. On a right-hand propeller going astern, the stern usually walks to port. Learn how your boat behaves.
- ==Rudder==: only works when water flows past it. More effective with propeller thrust over it.
- ==Outboard and stern drive==: steer by turning the whole thrust direction. Excellent control, but easy to over-steer.
- ==Jet drive==: steers by redirecting thrust. ==No thrust, no steering== at idle.
- ==Pivot point==: a vessel turns around a point about one third from the bow. The stern swings out as the bow turns in.
- ==Turning circle==: the path of a full-rudder turn. Know it for your boat at different speeds.

## Wind and current

- Wind pushes on freeboard and cabin sides. A high-sided boat is pushed sideways at low speed.
- Current carries the whole boat. At a dock, you can use it to help slow the boat.
- ==Approach into the wind or current== so you have steerage and can stop easily.
- Allow for ==leeway== (sideways drift) and ==set== (the current's push).

## Berthing and departing

1. Plan the approach and check the wind and current.
2. Ready lines and fenders before you arrive.
3. Come in slowly at a shallow angle, then use a short burst of astern to stop.
4. Get a ==bow line== and a ==spring== ashore first, and use the spring and engine to ease the stern in.
5. To depart, release lines in an order that lets the wind or current take you clear, and check traffic.

## Anchoring

**Choosing a spot.** A good anchorage has:

- ==Good holding ground== (mud, sand or clay, not rock or weed).
- ==Shelter== from the wind and swell.
- Enough ==depth and room== to swing as the wind or tide changes.
- No ==cables, pipelines or moorings==, and not in a channel.

**Types of anchor.** ==Fluke (Danforth)== types hold well in sand and mud. ==Plough (CQR)== and ==Bruce== types suit mixed bottoms. A ==grapnel== suits rocky bottoms.

**Setting.** Approach slowly into the wind or current. Stop, lower the anchor to the bottom (don't throw it), then back down slowly while paying out the ==scope==, which is usually ==5 to 7 times the water depth== plus the height of the bow. Check it is holding by taking ==bearings== or marks ashore.

**Riding.** Keep an anchor watch if the weather changes. If the boat drags, lengthen the scope, re-set the anchor or move.

**Weighing and stowing.** Motor slowly up to the anchor to take the weight off, haul in the rode, clean the anchor, and stow it securely so it cannot fall.

## A BC example

You anchor in a bay in the Gulf Islands in 6 m of water with 3 m of tide still to rise. You work out the depth at high tide (9 m), add bow height, and pay out about 55 m of rode. You back down gently and check two shore marks. They don't move, so the anchor is holding.

## Common mistakes

- Coming into a dock too fast.
- Anchoring with too little scope.
- Anchoring where the swinging circle crosses other boats.
- Forgetting the rise of the tide when working out scope.

## Check yourself

1. Why should you approach a dock into the wind or current?
2. What is a good scope for anchoring?
3. How do you check that an anchor is holding?
