# Departure preparation: blower, fuel, trip plan and local hazards

Most problems at sea start with something missed at the dock. Good preparation makes the trip easy.

## Run the bilge blower

Gasoline vapour is heavier than air and collects in the bilge. Before starting an inboard gasoline engine, ==run the bilge blower for at least four minutes== and check by smell that no vapour is present. Diesel engines are safer but ventilation is still good practice.

## Plan the fuel

- Work out your ==route and distance==.
- Work out your ==fuel use== at your cruising speed (litres per hour or per nautical mile).
- Use the ==rule of thirds==: one third to go out, one third to come back, one third as ==reserve==.
- Allow extra for ==wind, tide and weather==.
- Check the ==fuel level== by sounding or gauge, not just by guess.

## Use a departure checklist

A ==departure checklist== makes sure you don't forget anything. It might include:

- Weather forecast and tides checked
- Fuel, oil and coolant checked
- Lifejackets, flares and safety gear on board and in date
- Radio, GPS, radar and lights tested
- Charts and publications on board
- Passengers briefed
- Trip plan filed

## File a trip plan

A ==trip plan== (sail plan) tells someone ashore where you are going, who is on board, what the boat looks like, and when you expect to return. Include:

- Vessel name, description and registration
- Number of people and contact details
- Route and destination
- Expected return time
- A contact who will raise the alarm if you are overdue

==Tell the contact when you return==, so they don't call out a search.

## Know the local hazards

Before you go, check the area for:

- ==Rocks, reefs and shoals==.
- ==Tide rips and strong currents==.
- ==Traffic== such as ferries, tugs and tows, and shipping lanes.
- ==Weather patterns== and funnelling winds.
- ==Log booms, nets, deadheads and floating debris==.
- ==Restricted areas==, such as military zones, marine parks and whale sanctuaries.

Check Notices to Mariners and talk to local people when you can.

## A BC example

Before leaving Campbell River for a day of crew transfers, you run the blower, check the forecast and the tide table for Seymour Narrows, sound the fuel tank and plan on a third in reserve. You call the dock office and give a trip plan: three people on board, route, and a return by 5 p.m.

## Common mistakes

- Starting the engine without ventilating.
- Running too close to empty.
- Leaving without telling anyone.
- Not knowing local hazards.
