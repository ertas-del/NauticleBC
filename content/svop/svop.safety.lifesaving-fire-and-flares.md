# Lifesaving gear, fire equipment, flares and towing

Safety gear is only useful if it works when you need it. Checking it is part of the job.

## Lifejackets, PFDs and anti-exposure suits

- A ==lifejacket== is designed to turn an unconscious person face up. A ==PFD== (personal flotation device) helps keep you afloat but does not turn you over.
- Everyone on board should have a correctly fitting lifejacket or PFD that is ==easy to reach==. On small open boats it is best to wear them.
- Check them regularly: fabric not torn or faded, buckles and straps work, stitching intact, no mould, and any inflatable cylinder is full and in date.
- ==Anti-exposure suits== (immersion suits) protect against cold water. Check zips, seals and the suit for holes. Store them dry and unfolded as the maker says. Practise putting one on.
- Replace anything damaged. Don't store gear in a place that gets wet.

## Fire detection and extinguishing equipment

- Check ==fire extinguishers== monthly: in place, pin and seal intact, pressure in the green, nozzle clear, not corroded. Have them serviced as required.
- Know the ==class== of each extinguisher and where each is.
- Test ==smoke or heat detectors== and alarms.
- Know where the ==engine room shut-offs== and ventilation controls are.
- Keep the area around extinguishers clear.

## Flares

Types:

- ==Hand-held red flares==: a night and day signal showing your position.
- ==Red parachute rockets==: reach high and are visible from a long way.
- ==Orange smoke==: for daytime use, hand-held or floating.
- ==Floating orange smoke== and ==white flares== are used in specific ways.

Rules for use:

- Only use flares when you are in ==distress==, or for approved practice.
- Hold them away from your body, downwind, and never point them at a person or the boat.
- ==Store flares dry==, in a waterproof container, where you can reach them.
- ==Check the date== regularly. Out-of-date flares should not be relied on.
- ==Dispose of expired flares properly==, through a marine supplier or a recognised collection point. Never fire them to get rid of them, and never put them in the rubbish.

## Safety in towing

Towing is a way to help a vessel in distress, but it adds risk.

- Agree the plan and signals with the other vessel.
- Use a ==strong, long line== with some stretch and attach it to a ==strong point== (not a light cleat).
- Keep people ==clear of the line== and out of the bight.
- Start slowly and take up the strain gradually.
- Tow at a ==low speed== that the towed vessel can handle.
- Keep the line away from the propellers.
- Watch the towed vessel and be ready to cast off quickly.

## A BC example

At the start of the season you check the boat's safety gear. One of the lifejackets has a cracked buckle and the flares are past their dates. You replace the jacket, take the old flares to a marine store for disposal, and buy new ones.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving lifejackets stuffed in a damp locker.
- Using out-of-date flares.
- Tying a tow to a small cleat.
- Standing near a loaded tow line.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between a lifejacket and a PFD?
2. How should you store flares?
3. Why should the tow line be kept clear of people?
