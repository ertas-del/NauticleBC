# Sound signals and distress signals

Sound signals tell others what you are about to do and warn them of danger. In fog, they are how vessels find each other.

## Blast lengths

- A ==short blast== lasts about ==one second==.
- A ==prolonged blast== lasts ==four to six seconds==.

A vessel of 12 m or more must carry a whistle (and a bell if 20 m or more). Under 12 m you must still be able to make some ==efficient sound signal==. An air horn or a hand-held horn is fine.

## Manoeuvring signals (vessels in sight of each other)

| Signal | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==One short== | I am altering course to starboard |
| ==Two short== | I am altering course to port |
| ==Three short== | I am operating astern propulsion |
| ==Five or more short, rapid== | Danger or doubt: I do not understand your intentions |
| ==One prolonged== | Warning: sounded by a vessel approaching a bend where others may be hidden |

## Overtaking in a narrow channel

- Two prolonged then one short: I intend to overtake on your starboard side.
- Two prolonged then two short: I intend to overtake on your port side.
- The vessel being overtaken replies with prolonged, short, prolonged, short to agree.

## Fog signals

In or near restricted visibility, day or night:

| Vessel | Signal |
|---|---|
| ==Power-driven, making way== | one prolonged blast, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes |
| ==Power-driven, underway but stopped== | two prolonged blasts, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes |
| ==Sailing, fishing, not under command, restricted== | one prolonged and two short, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes |
| ==At anchor== | rapid ringing of a bell for about 5 seconds, at intervals of not more than 1 minute |

A vessel under 12 m that does not carry these must make some other efficient sound signal at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

## Distress signals

You may use these only when in grave and imminent danger and in need of help:

- ==Mayday== by radio, or a DSC distress alert.
- ==SOS== in Morse code by any means.
- ==Red hand flares== or ==red parachute rockets==.
- ==Orange smoke== (a day signal).
- ==Flames== on the vessel (such as a burning tar barrel).
- ==Slowly and repeatedly raising and lowering outstretched arms==.
- A square flag with a ball above or below it.
- An ==EPIRB== or personal locator beacon.
- A gun or other explosive signal at intervals of about a minute.

Using a distress signal when you are not in distress is an offence. Flares must be in date and stored where you can reach them.

## A BC example

Out in fog near Race Rocks you are moving at slow speed and you hear a single long blast ahead and to your starboard. You slow right down, keep your lookout, sound your own signal, and prepare to stop if the sound gets closer.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to sound fog signals.
- Mixing up the number of blasts for port and starboard.
- Using expired flares.
- Setting off a distress signal for a non-emergency.
