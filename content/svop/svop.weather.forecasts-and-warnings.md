# Marine weather: forecasts and warnings

The weather decides whether a trip is safe. Learn to read the forecast, and don't ignore what the sky and the barometer tell you.

## Where forecasts come from

In Canada, marine forecasts are produced by ==Environment and Climate Change Canada== from observations, satellites, radar and computer models. You can get them from:

- ==VHF weather broadcasts== (continuous marine weather channels) and Coast Guard radio broadcasts.
- The ==Environment Canada marine weather website or app==.
- ==Radio and phone services==.
- Local observation: sky, wind, cloud, and barometer.

Forecasts are ==predictions==, not guarantees. Reliability falls with time: the next 12 to 24 hours are more reliable than days ahead. Local effects can be stronger than forecast. Update your forecast before leaving, and keep listening while underway.

## Wind speed categories

| Description | Wind speed (knots) |
|---|---|
| ==Light== | 1 to 12 |
| ==Moderate== | 13 to 19 |
| ==Strong== | 20 to 33 |
| ==Gale== | 34 to 47 |
| ==Storm== | 48 to 63 |
| ==Hurricane force== | 64 and over |

## Warnings

- ==Small craft or strong wind warning==: winds of about 20 to 33 knots. Dangerous to small boats.
- ==Gale warning==: winds 34 to 47 knots.
- ==Storm warning==: winds 48 to 63 knots.
- ==Hurricane force wind warning==: 64 knots and over.

The wording of the lowest warning can vary by region, so check what it means in your area. If a warning is out for your area and you are in a small boat, ==delay or cancel the trip==.

## The barometer

A ==barometer== measures air pressure.

- ==Falling==: weather deteriorating. A ==rapid fall== means strong winds are on the way.
- ==Steady==: weather unchanged.
- ==Rising==: weather improving. A rapid rise can mean strong, gusty winds after a front.

## Wind shifts

In the Northern Hemisphere, a rule of thumb is ==Buys Ballot's law==: stand with your back to the wind and the low pressure is on your left.

- Wind that ==backs== (shifts counter-clockwise) while the barometer falls usually means a low approaching and worsening weather.
- Wind that ==veers== (shifts clockwise), often with the barometer rising, usually means the front has passed and conditions are clearing.

## Small vessel operating procedure in bad weather

1. Check the forecast before you go. Delay if conditions are marginal.
2. Reduce load and speed. Secure gear and close hatches.
3. Make sure everyone has a lifejacket.
4. Head for shelter early, not late.
5. Keep the bow into the waves where possible and reduce speed enough to avoid slamming.
6. Tell someone shore-side what you are doing.

## A BC example

You are planning a run up Georgia Strait. The marine forecast says winds northwest 15 knots increasing to 25 in the afternoon, with a strong wind warning for tomorrow. You leave early, get the job done before the wind picks up, and are in the harbour by noon.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on one forecast at the start of the day.
- Ignoring the barometer.
- Leaving it too late to head for shelter.
- Assuming the forecast wind is the same everywhere.

## Check yourself

1. What wind speed range is a gale?
2. What does a rapidly falling barometer mean?
3. Where can you get a marine forecast?
