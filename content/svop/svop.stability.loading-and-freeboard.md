# Stability: loading, free surface and freeboard

Stability is a boat's ability to come back upright when something tips it. On small boats the biggest danger is overloading and poor loading. This lesson is about what you can see and do, not the maths.

## Weight and where it goes

- A boat's weight acts through a point called the ==centre of gravity==.
- ==Heavy weight placed high== raises that point, which makes the boat tender and slower to recover.
- ==Heavy weight placed low== and near the centreline makes the boat more stable.
- Passengers moving to one side, gear stacked on deck or on a flying bridge, and loaded rigging all raise or shift weight.

Stow ==heavy things low==, ==central==, and ==secured==. Don't carry more than the vessel is designed for.

## Load distribution and trim

- Weight spread evenly fore and aft keeps the boat on an even keel.
- Too much weight forward or aft changes ==trim==, makes steering harder, and can cause water to come in over the bow or transom.
- Uneven side-to-side loading causes ==list==.

Always load and unload in an order that keeps the boat level.

## Free surface effect

A part-full tank, a wet fish hold, or ==water on deck== is ==free surface==. The liquid rushes to the low side when the boat leans, making things worse. Keep tanks ==full or empty==, not half full. Clear water off the deck and make sure scuppers and drains work.

## Loose water on deck

Water or fish that is allowed to stay on deck adds weight high up, shifts to one side, and can ==capsize== a small boat. Clear it quickly.

## Icing

In freezing weather, sea spray freezes on the boat. Ice adds ==top weight==, raises the centre of gravity and reduces freeboard. Icing is dangerous. Avoid conditions that cause it and remove ice carefully.

## Freeboard and reserve buoyancy

- ==Freeboard== is the height of the deck edge above the water.
- ==Reserve buoyancy== is the watertight volume above the waterline that can keep the boat afloat if she is pushed down by a wave or a heel.
- ==Overloading reduces freeboard and reserve buoyancy==, so the boat is at risk when a sea comes aboard.

## Fresh water and salt water

A boat floats ==lower in fresh water== than in salt water, because fresh water is less dense. The ==Fresh Water Allowance (FWA)== is the amount a vessel may sink further when loaded in fresh water. Remember this when you go from salt water into a river or lake.

## Watertight integrity

Hatches, doors, ports and vents must be ==closed and secure== at sea. An open hatch or door is a way for water to flood in. Check that seals are good and that drains are clear.

## Proper stowage

Secure gear, cargo and equipment so it cannot move. Moving cargo can shift the weight and cause a list or capsize.

## A BC example

A crew boat on a rough day near Cape Caution is carrying extra gear stacked on deck and has partly full fuel tanks. As the swell lifts the boat, it leans and hangs there. The operator removes the high gear, lashes everything down, tops up the slack tank, and goes to a sheltered route. The boat now recovers quickly.

## Common mistakes

- Loading heavy gear on top.
- Leaving tanks part full.
- Leaving hatches open.
- Overloading in poor weather.
