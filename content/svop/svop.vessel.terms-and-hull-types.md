# Boat terms and hull types

Seafarers use fixed words so that instructions mean the same thing to everyone. Learn them first. They appear in every later lesson.

## Directions and locations

- ==Bow==: the front of the vessel.
- ==Stern==: the back.
- ==Port==: the left side, looking forward. Its colour is red.
- ==Starboard==: the right side, looking forward. Its colour is green.
- ==Ahead== is forward of you. ==Astern== is behind.
- ==Abeam== is directly out from the side.
- ==Fore and aft== means along the length of the vessel.
- ==Athwartships== means across the vessel.
- ==Windward== is the side the wind comes from. ==Leeward== (or lee) is the sheltered side.

## Parts of the vessel

- ==Hull==: the body of the vessel.
- ==Keel==: the backbone along the bottom.
- ==Deck==: the working surface across the top.
- ==Gunwale (gunnel)==: the upper edge of the side.
- ==Transom==: the flat back of many small boats.
- ==Bulkhead==: an upright wall inside the hull.
- ==Bilge==: the lowest part inside, where water collects.
- ==Freeboard==: the height from the waterline to the deck edge.
- ==Draught==: the depth of the vessel below the waterline.
- ==Cleat, bollard, fairlead==: fittings for lines.
- ==Wheelhouse==: the enclosed steering position.

## Displacement and planing hulls

A ==displacement hull== sits in the water and pushes it aside. It is steady and efficient at low speed, but its top speed is limited by its length.

A ==planing hull== lifts up and skims across the surface once it has enough power and speed. It is fast but burns more fuel, and it behaves differently at low speed. A planing boat slowed to idle behaves like a displacement boat.

## Other types

- ==Open vessel==: no deck cover, such as many aluminium workboats. Water can enter easily.
- ==Enclosed hull vessel==: decked over with a cabin or wheelhouse. Better for weather.
- ==Inflatable rescue craft==: a hull of air tubes. Light and stable, but easily damaged by sharp objects and sensitive to wind.
- ==Catamaran==: two hulls joined by a bridge deck. Wide, stable and with a shallow draught.
- Other types include rigid-hull inflatable boats (RHIBs), jet boats and landing craft.

## A BC example

A 7 m aluminium planing workboat runs a crew from Campbell River to a fish farm. At 25 knots it planes on top of the water. At the dock it goes to idle and behaves like a displacement boat. The operator uses port and starboard for every instruction ("keep the dock on your port side") so nobody mixes up directions.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up port and starboard when giving instructions.
- Forgetting that a planing hull handles differently at idle.
- Overloading an open boat.

## Check yourself

1. Which side is starboard?
2. What is freeboard?
3. How is a planing hull different from a displacement hull?
