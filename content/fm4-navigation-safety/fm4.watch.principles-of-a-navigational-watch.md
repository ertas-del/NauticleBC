# Keeping a safe navigational watch

NS 1 includes the principles of watchkeeping drawn from the STCW Code. On a fishing vessel, the watch is often shared among a small crew, so knowing the duties is essential.

## The officer in charge of the watch (OOW)

The OOW is the ==master's representative== and is ==primarily responsible for the safe navigation of the vessel and for complying with the collision regulations==. The OOW must keep the watch on the bridge, never leave it until relieved, and not undertake duties that interfere with a safe navigational watch.

## Look-out

A proper look-out must be kept at all times by sight and hearing and all available means. In busy areas, at night or in restricted visibility, a separate look-out may be needed. Use radar and other aids but not in place of a visual look-out. Check that the person on look-out is able to concentrate.

## Duties during the watch

- ==Position monitoring==: check the position at suitable intervals and compare the course steered with the intended track.
- ==Checks==: the compass and steering, navigational equipment, lights and signals.
- ==Weather==: track weather and sea conditions and the effect on the vessel.
- ==Traffic==: detect other vessels early and apply the collision regulations.
- ==Log==: keep records of courses, positions, speeds, events and actions.
- ==Standing orders==: follow the master's orders and any special instructions.

## When to call the master

Call the master without hesitation if:

- Restricted visibility is encountered or expected.
- Traffic conditions or the movements of other vessels cause concern.
- Difficulty is experienced in maintaining course.
- You fail to sight land or a navigation mark at the expected time, or unexpectedly sight land or a mark, or the depth changes unexpectedly.
- The engine, steering or any essential equipment fails.
- Radio communication is lost.
- The weather worsens or ice or a hazard is seen.
- You are in any doubt.

## At anchor

Determine and plot the anchor position. Check regularly that the vessel is not dragging (bearings, GPS anchor alarm, depth). Keep a look-out and check weather. Be ready to start engines. Show the correct lights and shapes, and sound fog signals if needed.

## In port

Maintain a watch appropriate to the situation: mooring lines, tide changes, other vessels, security, fire and pollution. Be ready to respond to emergencies.

## Fitness for duty and fatigue

Watch arrangements must allow ==enough rest==. Fatigue is a major cause of groundings and collisions. Don't take the watch if impaired by fatigue, alcohol or drugs. On fishing vessels, plan watches during long fishing operations so the person on the bridge is alert.

## A BC example

Crossing Queen Charlotte Sound at night, the deckhand on watch sees a reduction in visibility and several fishing boats on radar. He wakes the skipper at once, as the standing orders require, and the skipper takes over the conn.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving the bridge for a task without a relief.
- Relying on radar alone.
- Not calling the master when in doubt.
- Working too long without rest.
