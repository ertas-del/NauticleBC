# Lights and shapes: power-driven, fishing, towing, anchored and others

Lights and shapes tell other vessels what you are and what you are doing. Under NS 1 you need to recognise and apply them.

## Definitions

- ==Masthead light==: white, over an arc of ==225°== (from dead ahead to 22.5° abaft the beam on each side), showing forward.
- ==Sidelights==: ==green on the starboard side, red on the port==, each over ==112.5°==.
- ==Sternlight==: white, over ==135°==, showing aft.
- ==Towing light==: yellow, same arc as the sternlight.
- ==All-round light==: over 360°.
- ==Flashing light==: a light flashing at regular intervals at a frequency of 120 flashes or more per minute.

## Visibility of lights

| | Masthead | Side | Stern | Towing | All-round |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50 m or more | 6 NM | 3 NM | 3 NM | 3 NM | 3 NM |
| 12 m to under 50 m | 5 NM (3 NM if under 20 m) | 2 NM | 2 NM | 2 NM | 2 NM |
| under 12 m | 2 NM | 1 NM | 2 NM | 2 NM | 2 NM |

## Power-driven vessels underway

- ==Masthead light== forward, a second behind and higher if 50 m or more.
- ==Sidelights== and a ==sternlight==.
- Under 12 m: an ==all-round white light== and sidelights are permitted instead.
- Under 20 m: sidelights may be combined in one lantern on the centreline.

## Towing and pushing

- ==Two masthead lights== in a vertical line (three if the tow exceeds 200 m).
- Sidelights and sternlight.
- A ==yellow towing light== above the sternlight.
- Shape by day: a ==diamond== where the tow is over 200 m.

## Sailing vessels

Sidelights and sternlight. Under 20 m: a combined ==tricolour== at the masthead is permitted. A vessel sailing and also using her engine is a power-driven vessel and shows a ==cone, point down==, by day.

## Fishing vessels (Rule 26)

- ==Trawling== (dragging a trawl): two all-round lights in a vertical line, ==green over white==, or a shape of ==two cones apex together== (a basket may be used if under 20 m). A ==masthead light abaft and higher== than the green light is shown if 50 m or more, and optionally if less.
- ==Fishing other than trawling==: two all-round lights, ==red over white==, or the two-cone shape.
- ==Outlying gear extending more than 150 m horizontally==: an all-round white light, or a cone apex up, in the direction of the gear.
- ==Making way==: add sidelights and a sternlight. Not making way: only the fishing lights.
- ==Purse seiners== hampered by their gear may show two yellow lights in a vertical line flashing alternately.
- A vessel not engaged in fishing shows the lights of a power-driven vessel, not these signals.

## Not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre

- ==Not under command==: two all-round ==red== lights in a vertical line. By day, two ==black balls==. Add sidelights and sternlight if making way.
- ==Restricted in ability to manoeuvre==: ==red, white, red== all-round lights vertically. By day, ==ball, diamond, ball==. Add masthead, side and sternlights if making way.

## Anchored and aground

- ==At anchor==: an all-round white light in the fore part (and a second lower aft if 50 m or more). By day, one ==black ball==. A vessel under 7 m not in or near a narrow channel need not show these.
- ==Aground==: anchor lights plus two all-round ==red== lights in a vertical line. By day, ==three black balls==.

## Other

- ==Pilot vessel==: white over red all-round lights.
- ==Constrained by her draught==: three all-round red lights, or a cylinder by day.
- ==Diver==: flag Alpha is displayed from a vessel with divers in the water, in addition to the rule signals.

## A BC example

Approaching Triangle Island at night, a skipper sees a green over white light ahead, and sidelights below. It is a trawler making way. He keeps clear, remembering that the trawler is restricted by her gear.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing the red-over-white and green-over-white fishing lights.
- Showing fishing lights when not fishing.
- Forgetting the sidelights when making way.
- Missing the 150 m outlying gear signal.

## Check yourself

1. What lights does a trawler show?
2. What arc does a masthead light cover?
3. What does red, white, red mean?
