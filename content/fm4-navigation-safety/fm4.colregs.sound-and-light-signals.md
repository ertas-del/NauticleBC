# Sound and light signals: manoeuvring, warning and fog

Signals are how vessels share intentions, especially in fog. Know the signals and when each applies.

## Definitions

A ==short blast== lasts about one second. A ==prolonged blast== lasts four to six seconds. Vessels of 12 m or more need a whistle (and 20 m or more a bell). Smaller vessels must be able to make an efficient sound signal.

## Manoeuvring signals (vessels in sight of each other)

Whistle signals, which may be supplemented by a light signal:

- ==One short==: I am altering my course to starboard.
- ==Two short==: I am altering my course to port.
- ==Three short==: I am operating astern propulsion.
- ==Five or more short and rapid==: danger or doubt (I don't understand your intentions or I doubt you are taking enough action).

## Narrow channels

- ==Two prolonged then one short==: I intend to overtake you on your starboard side.
- ==Two prolonged then two short==: I intend to overtake on your port side.
- The vessel to be overtaken, if she agrees, sounds ==prolonged, short, prolonged, short==.
- ==One prolonged==: approaching a bend where other vessels may be hidden. The reply is one prolonged.

## Fog signals (Rule 35)

In or near an area of restricted visibility, day or night, at intervals of not more than ==2 minutes== unless noted:

| Vessel | Signal |
|---|---|
| ==Power-driven making way== | one prolonged |
| ==Power-driven underway, stopped== | two prolonged |
| ==Not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught, sailing, fishing, towing or pushing== | one prolonged and two short |
| ==Towed vessel== (if manned) | one prolonged and three short, after the towing vessel's signal |
| ==At anchor== | rapid bell for about 5 seconds, at intervals of not more than 1 minute |
| ==Aground== | anchor signal plus three separate strokes of the bell before and after |

A vessel at anchor may add one short, one prolonged and one short to warn an approaching vessel of her position. A vessel at anchor ==engaged in fishing== sounds the ==fishing signal== (one prolonged and two short). A vessel under 12 m not obliged to make these signals must make some other efficient signal at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

## Signals to attract attention

Any vessel may attract attention by light or sound signals that cannot be mistaken for any signal authorised elsewhere in the rules. A searchlight directed in the direction of danger, not dazzling others, is acceptable.

## A BC example

Fishing in fog near Nootka, a skipper whose boat is at anchor with gear out sounds one prolonged and two short blasts every two minutes. A passing vessel hears the unusual signal, knows she is fishing, and gives her room.

## Common mistakes

- Sounding the anchor bell instead of the fishing signal.
- Mixing up one short and two short blasts.
- Forgetting the fog signal while drifting in fog.
- Forgetting that a stopped vessel makes two prolonged blasts.

## Check yourself

1. What does two short blasts mean?
2. What is the fog signal for a vessel making way?
3. What signal does a fishing vessel at anchor sound in fog?
