# Fishing vessels and the collision regulations

Fishing vessels get special treatment in the rules because their gear restricts manoeuvring. But the rules also limit them. As a fishing master you must know both sides.

## Definition

The rules define a ==vessel engaged in fishing== as any vessel fishing with ==nets, lines, trawls or other fishing apparatus which restrict manoeuvrability==. It does ==not include a vessel fishing with trolling lines or other fishing apparatus which do not restrict manoeuvrability==. A troller with lines out but free to manoeuvre is a power-driven vessel.

## Priority and duties

- ==Power-driven vessels== and ==sailing vessels== must keep out of the way of a vessel engaged in fishing.
- A ==vessel engaged in fishing==, when underway, must as far as possible keep out of the way of a ==vessel not under command== or a ==vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre==.
- Other vessels must not take this priority for granted. Give-way and stand-on rules still apply when both vessels are fishing. Keep a look-out.

## In narrow channels and traffic schemes

A vessel engaged in fishing ==must not impede the passage of any other vessel navigating within a narrow channel or fairway== (Rule 9). The same applies in a ==traffic separation scheme== (Rule 10): a vessel engaged in fishing shall not impede the passage of any vessel following a traffic lane.

## Restricted visibility

Rule 19 applies to fishing vessels too. Reduce to a safe speed and use the proper fog signal (one prolonged and two short blasts, while engaged in fishing, whether at anchor or underway).

## Fishing vessel and fishing vessel

When two vessels are engaged in fishing, neither has priority under Rule 18. The ordinary steering rules apply (head-on, crossing, overtaking), and a vessel with the other on her starboard side gives way.

## Master's obligations

The master of a fishing vessel must ensure an adequate look-out is kept while fishing, even when busy on deck. Gear on the sea bed or outlying gear can restrict manoeuvrability. Show the correct lights and shapes (see the lights lesson).

## A BC example

A 15 m seiner is setting a net across a narrow channel on the BC coast when a tug with a tow approaches. The seiner's net restricts her manoeuvring, but the channel rule says she must not impede the tug. She recovers or opens the net in time to let the tow past.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming every fishing boat has right of way, including a troller under way.
- Impeding vessels in a narrow channel.
- Failing to keep a look-out while working gear.
- Not showing the correct signals.
