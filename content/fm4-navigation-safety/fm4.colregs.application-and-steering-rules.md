# Collision regulations: general and steering and sailing rules

The International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea apply in Canadian waters with Canadian modifications. NS 1 asks for a thorough knowledge of their content, application and intent. This lesson covers Parts A and B, the rules of conduct.

## Application and responsibility

The rules apply to ==all vessels== on the sea and connected navigable waters. Nothing excuses a vessel or its master from the consequences of neglecting the rules, or the ordinary practice of seamen. You may depart from the rules when necessary to avoid immediate danger.

## Look-out, safe speed, risk of collision

- ==Rule 5 Lookout==: maintain a proper look-out by sight and hearing and by all available means.
- ==Rule 6 Safe speed==: a speed at which you can take proper and effective action and be stopped within a distance appropriate to the conditions. Factors include visibility, traffic density, manoeuvrability, wind, sea and current, nearby dangers, draught and radar limitations.
- ==Rule 7 Risk of collision==: use all available means. If the compass bearing of an approaching vessel does not appreciably change, risk exists. Do not rely on scanty information. If in doubt, assume it exists.

## Rule 8 Action to avoid collision

Action must be ==positive==, made in ==ample time== and with due regard to good seamanship. Alter course or speed enough to be ==readily apparent== to another vessel observing visually or by radar. Avoid a succession of small changes. If there is room, an alteration of course alone may be the most effective. Keep checking until the other vessel is ==finally past and clear==. You may slow down, stop or reverse to allow more time.

## Rule 9 Narrow channels

Keep ==as near to the outer limit of the channel on your starboard side== as is safe. ==Vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and fishing vessels must not impede== a vessel that can only navigate safely within the channel. Don't cross a channel if it impedes a vessel that can only navigate inside it. Overtaking in a channel needs the signals in Rule 34. Sound ==one prolonged blast== approaching a bend. Avoid anchoring in a narrow channel.

## Rule 10 Traffic separation schemes

Proceed in the ==appropriate lane== in the ==general direction of traffic flow==, keep clear of the separation line, and join or leave at the end of a lane or at a small angle. Cross at ==right angles== as far as practicable. ==Vessels under 20 m, sailing vessels and fishing vessels must not impede== the passage of vessels following a lane. Use the ==inshore traffic zone== only for local needs, with vessels under 20 m, sailing and fishing allowed to use it.

## Steering rules in sight of one another

- ==Rule 13 Overtaking==: a vessel more than 22.5° abaft the beam of the other is overtaking. The ==overtaking vessel keeps clear== until finally past.
- ==Rule 14 Head-on==: two power-driven vessels meeting on reciprocal or nearly reciprocal courses, each ==alters to starboard== and passes port to port.
- ==Rule 15 Crossing==: the vessel ==with the other on her starboard side gives way== and avoids crossing ahead.
- ==Rule 16 Give-way vessel==: take early and substantial action.
- ==Rule 17 Stand-on vessel==: keep course and speed. If the give-way vessel is not acting, you may act. If collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone, you must act. Avoid altering to port for a vessel on your own port side.
- ==Rule 12 Sailing vessels==: wind on different sides, the vessel with the wind on the port side gives way. Same side, the windward vessel gives way.

## Rule 18 Responsibilities between vessels

In general a power-driven vessel keeps out of the way of, in order: vessel not under command; vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre; vessel engaged in fishing; sailing vessel. A sailing vessel keeps out of the way of: vessel not under command; restricted in ability to manoeuvre; engaged in fishing. A vessel engaged in fishing keeps out of the way of a vessel not under command or restricted in ability to manoeuvre. Any vessel other than these should avoid impeding a vessel constrained by her draught.

## Rule 19 Restricted visibility

Proceed at a ==safe speed== with engines ready for immediate manoeuvre. When you detect another vessel by radar alone, decide if a close-quarters situation is developing. If so, take avoiding action in ample time. ==Avoid altering course to port== for a vessel forward of the beam (except when overtaking) and ==avoid altering toward== a vessel abeam or abaft the beam. If you hear a fog signal apparently forward of the beam and can't avoid a close-quarters situation, reduce speed to the minimum at which you can keep on course and, if necessary, take all way off.

## A BC example

A 12 m troller is crossing Hecate Strait in thick fog with radar on. She picks up a target that has a steady bearing and closing range. She slows to a safe speed, sounds her fog signal, makes a large alteration to starboard in plenty of time, and plots the target until it has passed clear.

## Common mistakes

- Alterations too small to be seen.
- Altering to port for a vessel forward of the beam in fog.
- Assuming a fishing vessel always has priority.
- Crossing the bow of a stand-on vessel.
