# Distress signals and attracting attention

Distress signals are listed in Annex IV of the collision regulations. A fishing master must recognise them and use them correctly.

## When to use them

Use a distress signal only when you are in ==grave and imminent danger and need immediate assistance==. If help is needed but life is not in immediate danger, use an urgency call (Pan-Pan).

## The signals

- A gun or other explosive signal fired at intervals of about a minute.
- Continuous sounding with any fog-signalling apparatus.
- Rockets or shells throwing red stars at short intervals.
- A signal by radiotelegraphy or any other method sending ==SOS== in Morse.
- A spoken signal ==Mayday== by radiotelephone.
- The International Code of Signals distress signal ==NC==.
- A square flag with a ==ball== (or anything resembling a ball) above or below it.
- ==Flames== on the vessel (such as a burning tar barrel or oil barrel).
- A ==rocket parachute flare== or a ==hand flare== showing a red light.
- A smoke signal giving ==orange-coloured smoke==.
- ==Slowly and repeatedly raising and lowering outstretched arms==.
- Radiotelegraph and radiotelephone alarm signals.
- Signals from an ==EPIRB== or personal locator beacon, and other approved signals.
- A dye marker on the water.

## Prohibition

==Use or exhibition of any of these signals except to indicate distress, and use of signals that may be confused with them, is prohibited.==

## Other lifesaving signals

The International Code of Signals also provides signals for lifesaving and distress, such as the single-letter signals and the shore-to-ship and ship-to-aircraft signals. An officer should recognise common meanings: for example flag ==Alpha== (I have a diver down), ==Oscar== (man overboard) and the ==NC== distress signal. Learn those that apply to your area of operation.

## In practice

If in distress: switch to VHF channel 16 and send a Mayday (or use DSC), show a visual signal if help is near, activate your EPIRB, and put on lifejackets. Keep your signals dry and in date.

## A BC example

A fishing boat sinks off the west coast. The crew fire a red parachute rocket and a hand flare after sending a Mayday by VHF. A nearby vessel sees the red light and responds.

## Common mistakes

- Setting off flares in training when not allowed.
- Keeping expired flares.
- Not knowing the day and night signals.
- Using signals that could be mistaken for distress.
