# The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 and the engineer's duties

The oral examines basic knowledge of the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 as it affects an engineer. You aren't expected to quote sections, but you must know what the law requires and who is responsible.

## The Act and its regulations

The ==Canada Shipping Act, 2001== sets the rules for vessels in Canada: safety, certification, crewing, pollution, and enforcement. ==Regulations== under it give the details. Transport Canada administers and enforces them.

## Regulations that matter to an engineer

- ==Marine Personnel Regulations==: certificates, crewing and training.
- ==Small Vessel Regulations== (and others for larger vessels): equipment and operation.
- ==Vessel pollution and dangerous chemicals regulations==: oil, garbage and sewage discharge and reporting.
- ==Fire and boat drills regulations==: drills and station bills.
- ==Life-saving equipment== and ==fire safety== regulations.
- ==Marine occupational health and safety== rules: safe work, hazard reporting and the right to refuse unsafe work.
- ==Vessel certificates and inspection== regulations.

Which ones apply depends on the vessel's size, type and operation. Check the ones for your vessel.

## Steering, lifesaving and fire equipment

- ==Steering gear==: must be kept in working order, with emergency steering available and tested before departure.
- ==Lifesaving equipment==: carried, maintained and in date.
- ==Fire equipment==: extinguishers, fixed systems, quick-closing valves and remote stops kept ready and tested.
- Report ==defects== to the master at once. A vessel may not be operated if essential equipment is defective in a way that endangers safety.

## Drills

Regular ==fire and boat drills== must be held, and recorded in the log. The engineer takes part, knows the station bill, and knows how to operate the fire pump, quick-closing valves and remote stops.

## Dangerous goods and pollution

- ==Dangerous goods== must be carried and handled as the regulations require. Know the basic labels and where the information is kept.
- ==Oil==: do not discharge oil or oily mixtures except as the rules allow.
- ==Garbage==: never discharge plastic.
- ==Sewage==: discharge only where and how the rules allow.
- ==Report== any discharge or threat of one to the Coast Guard.

## Casualty reporting and records

The master reports ==casualties== (collision, grounding, fire, loss of life or serious injury, pollution). The engineer helps with information on machinery. Keep an accurate ==engine room log== and ==maintenance records==. Records support compliance and are evidence if an incident is investigated.

## Duties of the engineer

- Keep the ==machinery and equipment== in safe working order.
- Keep the ==engine room== safe, clean and tidy.
- ==Report defects== and breakdowns to the master.
- Follow ==safe working practices== and the vessel's procedures.
- Take part in ==drills== and emergencies.
- ==Keep records== and take proper watch handovers.
- Carry out ==pollution prevention== duties.

## Official language

You must be able to communicate clearly in ==English or French== in the engine room and with the bridge. Orders and reports must be understood and repeated back. The oral is conducted in the official language you choose.

## Model answers

**Examiner: What is the Canada Shipping Act, 2001?** "It is the main federal law for shipping in Canada. It sets rules for safety, certification, crewing, pollution and enforcement, and regulations under it give the detail."

**Examiner: What are your duties as the machinery operator?** "I keep the machinery in safe working order, report defects to the master, keep logs, follow safe working practices, take part in drills and prevent pollution."

## A BC example

A pump seal starts leaking oil into the bilge on a tug in Prince Rupert. The engineer reports it to the master, fixes the leak, cleans the bilge without discharging it, and logs it. He knows an unreported leak could lead to a pollution incident and a breach of the regulations.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming the regulations are the same for every vessel.
- Not recording drills.
- Pumping oily bilge overboard.
- Not reporting defects.
