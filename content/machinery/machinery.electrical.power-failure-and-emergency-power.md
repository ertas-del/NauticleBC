# Power failure, restarting and emergency power

Power failure is one of the most likely oral scenarios. Describe your actions in order and give the reasons.

## What happens in a blackout

The ==main electrical power== is lost: lights, pumps, steering and some alarms may stop. The engines may stop if their pumps or controls lose power. The ==emergency generator== or ==emergency batteries== take over the emergency loads.

## Emergency power supplies

- ==Emergency lighting== at stations and escape routes.
- ==Navigation lights== and signals.
- ==Communications== (radio, internal).
- ==Steering gear== (or alternative steering).
- ==Fire pump and alarms==.
- Charged ==starting batteries== or ==starting air== for restart.

The emergency generator should start ==automatically== and be tested regularly on load.

## Actions after a power failure

1. ==Tell the bridge== at once.
2. Check that the ==emergency generator== or batteries are carrying the emergency loads.
3. Make the machinery space safe: ==lights==, a safe way out.
4. Check for the ==cause==: overload trip, fuel starvation, low oil pressure trip, cooling, governor or breaker fault.
5. ==Reset== or fix what tripped, only once you know why.
6. Start the ==standby generator== or the main one if available.
7. Restore ==essential services== first: steering, lube oil and fuel pumps, cooling water, then non-essential loads.
8. Restart the ==main engine== when the bridge is ready and the services are up.
9. ==Log== the event and cause.

## Common causes and prevention

- ==Overload== (too many loads on one generator): watch the load and share it.
- ==Fuel problems==: water or empty tank, blocked filter.
- ==Low oil pressure or high temperature trips==.
- ==Loose connections and faulty breakers==.
- ==Human error==: starting a large motor on a generator already at its limit.

Prevent with checks, maintenance, load management and testing standby equipment.

## Loss of propulsion only

If the main engine fails but electricity remains: tell the bridge, check for the cause (fuel, oil, cooling, air), and fix if safe. If near danger the master may ==anchor== or call for help. Keep communication open.

## Loss of steering power

Tell the bridge and shift to ==emergency steering== (emergency tiller or hand pump). The master reduces speed. Keep the emergency steering ready and know how to rig it.

## Starting after a dead ship

If all power and air are lost, you need ==charged starting batteries== or ==starting air== to restart. Keep batteries charged and air receivers topped up. Never rely on only one start source.

## Model answers

**Examiner: You have a blackout. What do you do?** "I tell the bridge, check the emergency generator has started and is carrying the emergency lights, steering and navigation lights, find the cause, reset the tripped breaker only after finding why, start the standby generator, restore essential services first, then the main engine, and log it."

**Examiner: Why restore essential services first?** "Because steering, lube oil and cooling are needed to run the engine and keep the vessel safe. Non-essential loads can come on later and might overload the generator again."

## A BC example

In the middle of Johnstone Strait the lights go out on a crew boat. The engineer tells the master, sees the emergency lights come on, finds the generator tripped on low fuel pressure from a blocked filter, changes the filter, restarts, restores steering and lube oil pumps, then the main engine, and logs the event.

## Common mistakes

- Resetting a breaker without finding the cause.
- Starting big loads first.
- Not telling the bridge.
- Forgetting to log it.

## Check yourself

1. What does the emergency generator supply?
2. In what order do you restore power?
3. What if the emergency generator does not start?
