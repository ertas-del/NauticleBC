# Lifesaving appliances and abandoning the vessel

The SVMO oral asks about lifesaving appliances, abandon-ship drills, survival craft and rescue boats. Engineers have a role in every drill. Know the equipment and what you do when the alarm sounds.

## Lifesaving appliances

- ==Lifejackets== and ==PFDs== for everyone, within reach.
- ==Immersion (anti-exposure) suits== for cold water.
- ==Lifebuoys== with a light and a buoyant line.
- ==Liferafts== (inflatable) with a canopy, painter and survival equipment. They are launched by hand or by a ==hydrostatic release== if the vessel sinks.
- ==Lifeboats or rescue boats== on larger vessels.
- ==Flares==: red hand flares, parachute rockets, orange smoke.
- ==EPIRB== (emergency beacon) and sometimes a ==radar transponder (SART)==.
- A ==line-throwing appliance== on some vessels.

## Checking and maintaining them

- Look over lifejackets, suits and lifebuoys regularly for damage, mould and faulty buckles or lights.
- ==Liferafts== are serviced at approved stations at set intervals. Check the date and that the ==painter== is secured to the vessel, not to the raft cradle.
- ==Hydrostatic releases== have expiry dates. Replace them on time.
- ==Flares== have expiry dates. Replace them and dispose of old ones properly.
- Test the ==EPIRB== as the maker advises and check the battery date.
- Keep appliances where they can be reached quickly.

## Drills and the station bill

Regulations require ==fire and boat drills== at set intervals. A ==station bill== (muster list) tells each person where to go and what to do. On hearing the alarm, go to your station. The engineer is usually told to ==stop machinery==, ==shut fuel and ventilation== if it is safe, and then ==report to the muster station==. Check the details for your vessel.

## Engineer's actions if the vessel is to be abandoned

1. Follow the master's orders. Stop engines when told to.
2. ==Shut off fuel== with the quick-closing valves and ==stop ventilation== if it can be done safely.
3. Make sure fires and flooding are controlled as far as possible.
4. Put on a ==lifejacket== and, if cold, an ==immersion suit==.
5. Take warm clothing, and any grab bag you are assigned.
6. Go to the muster station. ==Do not go back for belongings.==
7. Be among the last to leave if ordered, but never at the cost of your life.

## Launching and boarding a liferaft

1. Check the ==painter== is made fast to the vessel.
2. Throw the raft over the side, clear of obstructions, and pull the painter to ==inflate== it.
3. Board ==dry== if possible by stepping from the vessel, or use the boarding ramp or ladder if you have to enter the water.
4. Cut the painter when ordered and get clear of the vessel.
5. ==Close the canopy==, take roll, ==stream the sea anchor== and look for survivors.
6. Keep the ==EPIRB== activated and ready flares.

## Surviving in the water and in a raft

- In the water, keep your clothes on and ==keep still== in the ==HELP position== (arms in, knees up) to save heat. Huddle in a group.
- ==Cold shock== in the first minutes, then ==swim failure==, then ==hypothermia==.
- In a raft, ==stay together, stay warm, stay dry== as far as possible and bale out water.
- ==Stay with the raft or vessel== unless you can see rescue or the vessel is on fire or about to sink.
- Keep a look-out and ==save flares== until you can see a ship or aircraft.
- Ration water, and do not drink sea water.

## Model answers

**Examiner: The abandon-ship alarm sounds. What do you do?** "I stop the engine if ordered, shut the fuel at the quick-closing valve and stop the ventilation if I can do it safely, put on my lifejacket and immersion suit, and go to my station."

**Examiner: How would you launch a liferaft?** "I check the painter is secured to the vessel, throw the raft over the side clear of the hull, pull the painter to inflate, and board dry. Then I cut the painter and get clear."

## A BC example

During a drill on a 15 m passenger boat in Victoria, the alarm sounds. The engineer stops the main engine on the master's order, closes the fuel valve and fan switches in the engine room, puts on a lifejacket and reports to the aft muster station within a minute. The master records the time.

## Common mistakes

- Securing the liferaft painter to the cradle instead of the vessel.
- Going back for belongings.
- Forgetting to shut off fuel.
- Letting servicing dates lapse.
