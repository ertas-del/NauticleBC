# Batteries: construction, rating, charging and safety

Batteries start the engines and supply emergency power, so they have to work when everything else has failed. They
are also one of the most hazardous items in the engine room, because they can give a huge current and a dangerous
gas. The SVMO syllabus covers battery circuits and the precautions.

## Construction

A lead-acid battery is made of cells. Each cell has:

- A ==positive plate== of lead dioxide and a ==negative plate== of spongy lead.
- ==Separators== that stop the plates touching.
- ==Electrolyte==, dilute sulphuric acid.
- A case and terminals.

Each cell gives about ==2 volts==. A 12 V battery has ==six cells== in series, and a 24 V system uses two 12 V
batteries in series. Sealed (maintenance-free) batteries work the same way but cannot be topped up.

## Rating

Capacity is stated in ==ampere-hours (Ah)== at a given discharge rate. A 100 Ah battery can supply about 5 A for 20
hours. Starting batteries are also rated by their ==cold cranking amps==. Deep-cycle batteries are built for repeated
heavy discharge, and starting batteries for short bursts. Use the right type.

## Looking after batteries

- Keep them ==secured== in a box so they cannot shift when the vessel rolls.
- Keep terminals clean, tight and coated with grease.
- Check the electrolyte level in flooded batteries. If the plates are exposed, top up with ==distilled water== to
  just cover the plates. Never add acid, and never use tap water.
- Check state of charge by a ==hydrometer== (specific gravity of the electrolyte) or voltage.
- Do not let batteries stay discharged for long, which damages the plates.

## Hydrogen and other hazards

During charging, batteries give off ==hydrogen==, which is explosive when mixed with air. So:

- The battery space must be ==ventilated==.
- Keep ==sparks, flames and smoking== away.
- Do not lay tools across the terminals. They can short and cause an explosion or burn.
- Ventilate before entering a battery room, and switch off the charger if you need to work on connections.
- The electrolyte is ==acid==. Wear eye protection and gloves. If acid splashes on skin or eyes, flush with plenty
  of clean water straight away and get medical help.

## Connecting and disconnecting

- ==Disconnect the negative lead first== and ==reconnect it last==. That way a dropped spanner cannot short
  between positive and the hull.
- Check polarity before connecting. Reversed polarity can destroy electronics.
- Never jump start from a damaged or frozen battery.

## A BC engine-room example

You open the battery box on a 10 m water taxi in the spring and find white crust on the terminals and low electrolyte
in two cells. You switch off the charger, disconnect the negative first, clean the terminals, top up with distilled
water and reconnect, negative last, then grease the terminals. You check the charging voltage on the engine and
note it in the log.

## Common mistakes

- Topping up with tap water.
- Smoking or using a naked flame near a charging battery.
- Disconnecting the positive terminal first.
- Mixing old and new batteries in one bank.
