# Abandon ship and cold-water survival

**Study aid only.** Abandoning ship is the last resort. The survival craft, rescue boat and drill rules for larger vessels
are in `bridge-watch.survival-craft-and-rescue-boats`. This lesson is the master's decision, the order on a small
vessel, and the cold-water facts behind it.

## What you need to know

You leave a vessel when staying aboard is more dangerous than leaving. The Transport Canada fire template says the master
decides whether ==the risk of spread or explosion requires abandoning ship==; the taking-on-water template says to
==prepare to abandon if stability is threatened==. In both, the preparation starts before the order. People who were
mustered, dressed and counted in the first minutes leave quickly and calmly.

## The sequence

Transport Canada's abandon-ship template for small vessels, in order:

1. Tell the crew to ==get ready to abandon ship==.
2. ==Notify the authorities== that you are preparing to abandon.
3. Passengers ==gather on the main deck==, dressed warmly and ==wearing lifejackets==. Keep them sheltered, and ==do not put
   lifejackets on indoors==.
4. Confirm ==everyone on board is accounted for==.
5. Prepare the ==life raft== and gather the ==medical kit, blankets, food and EPIRB== to load into it.
6. Review the evacuation plan; assign capable people to help the ==elderly and children==.
7. Review the symptoms of ==cold shock== and how to counter them.
8. If conditions worsen, ==call the abandon-ship order==.
9. ==Throw the life raft over the side== and inflate it, ==keeping its painter attached to the vessel==.
10. ==Help passengers into the raft==. Consider whether a crew member should be inside to help them down.
11. ==Keep the raft tied to the vessel== for as long as it is safe.
12. ==Cast off== if the vessel appears likely to sink or capsize, or if staying alongside causes other hazards.

Why the painter stays on: it holds the raft beside you while people board, so it cannot blow away, and the pull on the
painter is what inflates it (general). Why you cast off: a sinking or capsizing vessel drags the raft down with her.

## Life rafts and the rules

For a passenger-carrying vessel of up to 15 GT with 12 passengers or fewer (SVR Part 4):

- ==Section 411==: she must carry life rafts with total capacity for all on board, ==unless== she is not more than ==8.5 m==,
  is on a ==sheltered waters voyage==, or is within ==two nautical miles== of the shore of a river or lake.
- ==Section 403==: if the water is ==under 15 degrees Celsius== and she carries ==no life raft==, equipment must be carried
  or ==procedures established== to protect all persons from ==hypothermia or cold shock== after swamping, capsizing or
  falling overboard.

For tugs (workboats, Part 5) ==section 509(1)== requires a tug of ==more than 8.5 m== with ==two or more persons on board== to carry
life rafts with capacity for all of them. A workboat of more than 12 m must also carry life rafts for all (s. 508), and may
carry a buoyant apparatus instead if the water is above 15 degrees (s. 508(2)).

Transport Canada's guide, TP 14070, adds that a life raft is ==preferred where the water is under 15 degrees Celsius== if the
vessel can carry one safely; that a ==hydrostatic release== operates at about ==4 m== depth, with a weak link that
breaks as the vessel sinks further; and that crews must be ready to launch a raft on a moment's notice, with regular drills.

## Beacons

==NSR 2020 s. 209(4)== says a ==manually activated EPIRB, a PLB or a DSC handheld VHF== (in the vessels where those are
the choice) ==must be worn by the person in charge of the navigational watch or, if not practicable, stowed so it is readily
accessible for immediate use in the event of abandonment==. An EPIRB or PLB must be ==registered with the Canadian Beacon
Registry== and the registration updated within ==30 days== of a change (==s. 228==). Cancel an inadvertent alert immediately
(s. 244). Details are in `radio.epirb-sart-plb`.

## Cold water

The Canadian Safe Boating Council's ==1-10-1 principle== describes cold-water immersion:

| Time | Stage | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| About ==1 minute== | ==Cold shock==: a deep gasp then very fast breathing | Control your breathing, keep the airway clear, wear a lifejacket |
| About ==10 minutes== | ==Cold incapacitation and swim failure==: fingers, arms and legs stop working effectively | Self-rescue first; otherwise keep the airway clear |
| About ==1 hour== | ==Hypothermia== to unconsciousness, even in ice water | Learn the signs and how to delay them; call for help |

BC water is cold all year, so even a short swim is dangerous. The sources read
give no water temperature thresholds for the three stages, and the "under 15 degrees" figure in the regulations is a
separate threshold. The immersion suit or anti-exposure suit rule in the Fire and Boat Drills Regulations (==s. 26==) that
crew must be able to don them applies to the vessels covered by those Regulations; vessels of 15 GT or less with 12
passengers or fewer are outside them (FBDR s. 2).

## A BC example

An invented example. A 13 m whale-watching vessel with 10 passengers has fire in the engine space off the west coast of Vancouver Island. The skipper
decides the fire may spread to the fuel tank. Her crew bring the passengers to the open aft deck, warmly dressed and
wearing lifejackets, count them, and prepare the life raft with the blankets, medical kit and EPIRB. The skipper has already
called MAYDAY. When the order is given the raft is thrown over with the painter fast, inflated, and the elderly and children
are helped aboard first. The raft stays tied alongside while the fire is downwind; when the fuel tank vent begins to roar, they cast off and paddle clear.

## Common mistakes

- Putting lifejackets on **indoors** (the template says not to).
- Throwing the raft over **without the painter attached**, or never **casting off** when she is sinking.
- Skipping the **headcount** and the **notification** of authorities.
- Forgetting **cold shock**: the first minute can kill a person with no lifejacket.
- Leaving the EPIRB in a locker rather than **worn or ready for abandonment**.
- Mixing up **8.5 m** (life raft exemption, and the tug threshold) with other length limits.
