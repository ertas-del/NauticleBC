# Calling for help: the master's alerting plan

**Study aid only.** The call words (MAYDAY, PAN PAN, SECURITE), DSC and the signals themselves are taught in the radio
lessons (`radio.distress-urgency-safety`, `radio.dsc-and-gmdss`, `radio.epirb-sart-plb`) and the Annex IV and bridge-watch
lessons. This lesson is about the master's decisions: when to call, what must be on board, and the law around it.

## What you need to know

The commonest mistake is to call late. Every Transport Canada emergency procedure puts the call ==early==: in the fire
procedure the master informs the shore authorities of the vessel's position and situation ==once the fire is
assessed==; in the flooding procedure the master notifies authorities and nearby vessels ==if the vessel is at risk==; in
the abandon-ship procedure the authorities are notified ==before leaving==, at the stage the crew are told to get
ready. A call that is cancelled costs a few minutes. A call that is late may cost a life. Boating Magazine's sinking
advice has the same order: lifejackets first, then a MAYDAY on VHF channel 16 giving position, persons on board and the nature of the
distress.

## What the vessel must carry

### Beacons: NSR 2020 s. 209

- ==Section 209(1)==: a ==float-free EPIRB== is required on a vessel on a ==near coastal voyage, Class 1== or an ==unlimited
  voyage==, and on a vessel ==more than 12 m== in length on an ==inland voyage== or a ==near coastal voyage, Class 2==.
- ==Section 209(3)==: a vessel of ==12 m or less== on the Great Lakes, their connecting and tributary waters or the St Lawrence on an inland
  voyage, or on a ==near coastal voyage, Class 2==, must have one of: a ==float-free EPIRB==, a ==manually activated EPIRB==,
  a ==PLB==, or, for a vessel ==under 8 m== operating only in ==sea area A1==, a ==waterproof portable VHF handheld capable
  of DSC==.
- A manual EPIRB, PLB or DSC handheld is worn by the person in charge of the navigational watch or stowed ready for
  abandonment (==s. 209(4)==).
- ==Section 228==: an EPIRB or PLB must be ==registered with the Canadian Beacon Registry== (run by the Department of
  National Defence), and the registration ==updated within 30 days== after a change.

The voyage classes are defined in the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations and explained in
`canadian-regulations.voyage-classes`; check which one your vessel is on.

### Flares: SVR s. 410

For a passenger-carrying vessel of up to 15 GT with 12 passengers or fewer, SVR ==s. 410== requires:

| Length | Visual signals |
|---|---|
| Not more than 6 m | a ==watertight flashlight== and ==three pyrotechnic distress signals other than smoke== |
| Over 6 m to 9 m | a watertight flashlight and ==six== pyrotechnic distress signals other than smoke |
| Over 9 m | a watertight flashlight and ==twelve== pyrotechnic distress signals, ==not more than six of which are smoke signals== |

A workboat has the same table in s. 507, with a log booming ground exception for a tug of 6 m or less.

## The report ashore

Help can only come if someone knows you are missing. SVR ==s. 402== requires the operator of a passenger-carrying vessel to
report the ==number of persons on board== to a person ashore, designated by the operator and responsible for talking to search
and rescue, ==before departure==. If the vessel is in a remote area and no one ashore can be told, the information is left
in a ==location on shore known and readily available to search and rescue authorities==. TP 14070 recommends a sail plan
with a responsible person ashore as well (see `canadian-regulations.svr-passenger-vessels`).

## Making the call and cancelling it

- Distress, urgency and safety calls are made according to ==articles 32 to 34== of the ITU Radio Regulations
  (NSR 2020 ==s. 243==). In practice: DSC distress alert, then voice on channel 16 (see the radio lessons).
- ==Section 244==: a person who ==inadvertently sends a distress alert==, or finds after sending it that help is no longer
  needed, must ==cancel it immediately== using the procedure in IMO Resolution A.814(19). Cancel; do not just switch off.
- TP 14070 notes it is ==against the law to use distress signals when you do not need help==. Do not fire a flare to
  signal a friend.

## When you hear a call

The Canada Shipping Act, 2001, ==s. 131(1)==: on receiving a signal from any source that a person, vessel or aircraft
is in distress, the master of a vessel in Canadian waters must ==proceed with all speed to render assistance== and, if
possible, ==inform== the persons in distress or the sender. A master who cannot, or who thinks it unreasonable or
unnecessary, enters the reason in the official log book (s. 131(2)). A ==search and rescue mission coordinator== may direct
vessels to report their positions, take part in a search or otherwise help (==s. 130==), and the master of a vessel in
distress may ==requisition== one or more of the vessels that answer (==s. 131(3)==). Failing to render assistance carries
a fine of up to $1,000,000 or imprisonment for 18 months (s. 137), with a defence if compliance would have imperilled life or the
vessel. See `canadian-regulations.distress-assistance-collision`.

## A BC example

An invented example. A 6 m charter boat loses her engine 5 nautical miles off Tofino as the wind rises. Before
departure the skipper told the person ashore who is designated to speak to search and rescue that three people were on
board. The skipper calls the Coast Guard early on VHF channel 16 with position, three persons on board and the problem,
before the vessel is in grave danger. A passing fishing boat takes her in tow, and the skipper tells the Coast Guard that
help is no longer needed. No flare is fired: the vessel is not in grave and imminent danger.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling late**, after the situation has become a disaster.
- Mixing up the flare counts: **3, 6 and 12** by length, with **six smoke at most** over 9 m.
- Forgetting **registration** of the beacon and **30 days** to update it.
- Turning off a radio after a false alert instead of **cancelling** it.
- Not leaving the **persons-on-board** report ashore.
- Thinking the **float-free EPIRB** is required on every vessel; the rule depends on voyage class and size.
