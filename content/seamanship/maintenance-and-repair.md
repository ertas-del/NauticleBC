# Maintenance and repair on deck

**Study aid only.** The routine jobs below are learned on a ship from the bosun. The safety rules are the ones in the
Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (MOHS); paint, solvent and equipment instructions always come from the
maker's data sheet and your ship's planned maintenance system.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 lists painting, ==lubrication==, ==cleaning==, ==surface preparation==, use of tools, following the
==manufacturer's guidelines== and ==waste disposal==. A ship that is not maintained rusts, wears out and, eventually, fails
when it matters. The exam-sized facts are mostly safety rules:

- ==Hot work== = flame or any source of ignition. It needs a ==permit==, a ==fire watch== (30 minutes after, if needed) and a
  ==gas-free test== where flammable gas, vapour or dust could be present.
- A ==defective== tool or protective item is ==reported== and ==tagged and taken out of service==.
- ==Lock out== equipment before working on it.
- ==Plastics== (including ==synthetic rope==) are ==never== thrown overboard.

## Cleaning, preparation and painting

The aim is to protect steel and show up problems early. A usual order (general seamanship; check with your instructor and
the paint maker's data sheet):

1. **Clean**: wash off salt, oil and grease. Paint will not stick to a greasy surface.
2. **Remove loose rust and paint**: chip, scrape, wire-brush or grind. Wear eye protection and the PPE the ship requires.
3. **Spot-prime bare metal** immediately so it does not rust again before the coat goes on.
4. **Undercoat**, then **topcoat**, allowing each coat to dry as the maker says. Do not paint wet or in rain, fog or when dew is forming.
5. **Clean up and dispose of waste** correctly (see below). Keep rags, brushes and used paint cans away from heat.

**Lubrication.** Moving parts (blocks, hinges, winch gear, wire rope) are oiled or greased on the planned
maintenance schedule, using the right grease and not too much. Wipe up spills at once; an oily deck is a slipping hazard.

**Manufacturer's guidelines.** Each machine, tool and paint has instructions. Follow them; never improvise with a
substitute product without the officer's agreement.

### Worked example (invented numbers)

You must give a deck house wall 2 coats of paint. The wall is 12 m long and 3 m high, and the paint covers about 8 square metres
per litre per coat (invented figure).

1. Area = 12 x 3 = 36 square metres.
2. Paint for 2 coats = 36 x 2 / 8 = 9 litres.
3. Allow about 10 per cent for waste and touching up: 9 x 1.10 = 9.9 litres, so draw 10 litres.

Always use the coverage figure printed for your actual paint. It varies with the surface and the paint.

## Safe maintenance rules

### Hot work

==Hot work== means any work where a flame is used or a source of ignition may be produced (welding, cutting, grinding,
torch work).

- A ==qualified person== must be assigned to patrol the work area and adjoining areas and keep a ==fire protection watch== for
  the whole job and, if the qualified person decides it is necessary, for ==30 minutes== afterwards. ==Enough fire
  extinguishers== must be provided in the working area and the adjoining areas.
- Hot work ==must not be done== where ==flammable gas, vapour or dust== may be present unless the area has been ==freed of gas==,
  ==tested by a marine chemist or other qualified person== and found safe. The same applies where an explosive or flammable
  substance may be present, unless a marine chemist or other qualified person has ensured that adequate protection exists.
- A ==work permit== is needed for hot work assessed as hazardous, and for ==entry to a confined space==.

### Lock out and defective equipment

- ==Lock out== means placing a lock on equipment, machines or energy-isolating devices, following an established
  procedure, to show they must not be operated until the lock is removed by that procedure. Lock out and tag out any
  machine before working on it, and keep the key yourself.
- A person must not use a structure, machine or equipment that has been ==reassembled after being dismantled== until a qualified
  person has examined it and found it safe.
- If you find a ==defect== in protection equipment (a harness, life jacket, helmet) ==report it as soon as feasible==. The
  employer must ==mark or tag it as unsafe and remove it from service==. The same good practice applies to any
  defective tool: tag it and take it out of use (general; check with your instructor).

### Tools and working aloft

- On a temporary or raised structure, ==tools, equipment and materials must be arranged or secured== so they cannot be
  knocked off. If a toe board is not possible, tools are tied on or caught by a safety net so they cannot fall on anyone below.
- A ==non-metallic portable ladder must not be painted== (the usual reason is that paint can hide cracks and damage; general).
- Working over the side or aloft: fall protection, life jacket where there is a drowning hazard, and a person told; see the
  rigging lesson and the safe working practices lesson.

### Preventive maintenance

The employer must develop and implement a ==preventive maintenance programme== to avoid failures that could create a hazard to
employees. This is why the ship has a planned maintenance schedule: it is a legal safety measure as well as good
housekeeping. Record the work you do as the system requires.

## Waste and disposal

- ==Plastics== may ==never be thrown overboard==. Plastics include ==synthetic ropes==, ==synthetic fishing nets== and ==plastic garbage
  bags==, so a worn-out nylon line goes ashore with the garbage, not over the side.
- Other garbage limits depend on the distance from the nearest land and the type of waste (see the pollution prevention lesson).
- Paint cans, rags, solvent and oily waste are stored in the proper containers and landed ashore (general).
- Never wash paint, oil or solvent over the side or into a drain that leads overboard.

## Exam traps

- Calling **grinding** or **chipping** "not hot work". Grinding produces sparks, so it is hot work if it can produce an ignition source.
- Thinking the **fire watch** ends when the work stops. It continues for **30 minutes** after if the qualified person says it is needed.
- Painting a **ladder** that is **non-metallic**.
- Throwing **old synthetic rope** overboard because "it is just rope".
- Using a tool that has a defect "just to finish the job". Tag it and take it out of service.
- Taking the **lock-out** key and leaving it in the lock.
