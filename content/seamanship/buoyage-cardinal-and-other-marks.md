# Cardinal and isolated danger marks

**Study aid only. Not for navigation.** Check the current Coast Guard publication and your chart.

## What you need to know

- A cardinal mark is named for the ==compass direction of the safest water== from the mark.
- Colours, topmarks and light groups all give the same message. Learn all three, because a night mark may have only a
  light and a day mark only a shape.
- An ==isolated danger== mark is ==black with a red band==, ==two black spheres==, white ==Fl(2)==.

## Cardinal marks: the safe-water side

Where lateral marks mark the *sides of a channel*, a cardinal mark marks a *position relative to a danger*. It sits on
one of the four sides of a hazard (a shoal, wreck or reef) and tells you which side the **safe water** is.

> A **North** cardinal means: the safe water is to the ==north== of the mark. Pass to its north.

The same holds for the East, South and West marks. Picture the danger in the middle of a compass and each mark
standing on its own point.

All cardinal marks have ==white lights== and white retroreflective material.

| Mark | Colours | Topmark (two black cones) | Light (quick) | Light (very quick) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| North | ==Black over yellow== (black on top) | ==Both point up== | ==Q== continuous | ==VQ== continuous |
| East | ==Black with a broad yellow band== | ==Base to base== | ==Q(3) 10s== | ==VQ(3) 5s== |
| South | ==Yellow over black== (yellow on top) | ==Both point down== | ==Q(6)+LFl 15s== | ==VQ(6)+LFl 10s== |
| West | ==Yellow with a broad black band== | ==Point to point== | ==Q(9) 15s== | ==VQ(9) 10s== |

### Memory aids (ours, not official)

- **Topmarks.** North points *up* (north is "up" on a map); South points *down*. East looks like an egg (two cones
  base to base, a fat middle). West looks like a wine glass or an hourglass (point to point).
- **Colours.** On North and South the black sits where the cones point: North has black on top with cones up;
  South has black on the bottom with cones down. If you doubt a colour, use the topmark to check.
- **Light groups: the clock face.** The Coast Guard gives this aid: the number of flashes for East, South and West is
  the number on the clock face in that direction: East is ==3 o'clock==, South is ==6 o'clock==, West is ==9 o'clock==.
  North would be 12, shown as a continuous series of flashes. South adds a ==long flash== at the end to tell it
  apart from a plain six-flash group.

### Reading a cardinal light

"Q(9) 15s" means a group of nine quick flashes, then darkness, repeating every 15 seconds. The long flash on the
South mark (LFl) lasts two seconds or more, so you can tell it from a flash of the quick group even if you lose count.

### Worked example: which way past?

At night you see a white light making a group of three quick flashes, repeating about every ten seconds: Q(3) 10s.

1. Three flashes on the clock face means East.
2. An East cardinal says: the safe water is to the east of the mark.
3. So you pass to the ==east== of it, keeping the mark on your port side if you are heading north, and on your
   starboard side if heading south.
4. Check the chart for the danger itself and keep a good margin.

## Isolated danger marks

An **isolated danger** mark is placed on, or moored over, a ==small danger with navigable water all round it==. It is
usually a buoy in Canada.

- Colour: ==black with one broad red horizontal band==.
- Topmark: ==two black spheres, one above the other==.
- Light: white ==Fl(2)== (a group of two flashes) with a period of ==5s or 10s==.

Because there is safe water all round, you may pass it on any side, but give it a **wide berth**: the mark is on the
danger or tethered to it, and its mooring may stretch out.

## Exam traps

- Reading the topmark backwards. North = both cones **up**; South = both **down**.
- Confusing East and West colours. East is **black with a yellow band**; West is **yellow with a black band**.
- Remembering the group numbers but not the matching directions: **3 East, 6 South, 9 West**.
- Forgetting the **long flash** on the South mark.
- Treating an isolated danger as a mark you must keep on a given hand. It is passed on **any side**, well clear.
- Mixing the cardinal's quick light with the isolated danger's group of **two** flashes.
