# Emergency procedures, drills and records

**Study aid only.** The alarm signals, muster lists and survival craft drills for larger vessels are in
`bridge-watch.emergency-alarms-and-muster` and `bridge-watch.survival-craft-and-rescue-boats`. This lesson is the
master's programme: who must have procedures, how often to practise, and what to write down, especially on a small vessel
that falls outside the big-ship drill rules.

## What you need to know

Three layers of rules apply, and an examiner may ask which layer your vessel is under:

1. The ==Canada Shipping Act, 2001== makes someone responsible for having procedures and training (s. 106).
2. The ==Small Vessel Regulations== (SVR) say the crew must practise the procedures (s. 420 and s. 520).
3. The ==Fire and Boat Drills Regulations== (FBDR) set the frequency, content and records for larger vessels, and ==do
   not apply== to the smallest passenger vessels.

## Layer 1: the authorized representative (CSA s. 106)

The authorized representative (usually the owner) of a Canadian vessel must (==s. 106(1)==):

- ==develop procedures for the safe operation of the vessel and for dealing with emergencies==; and
- ensure the ==crew and passengers receive safety training==.

Failing to do so is an offence under s. 121(1). The master, for the master's part, must take all reasonable steps to
ensure the safety of the vessel and persons on board (s. 109).

Transport Canada publishes written-procedure templates for small vessels: pre-departure checks, refuelling, person
overboard, fire fighting, taking on water, pollution response, abandon ship, plus record forms for personnel, crew
certification, number of persons on board, emergency drills and maintenance. The page says Transport Canada does ==not
approve== the procedures, but an ==inspector may ask to see that they exist and are used==.

## Layer 2: the Small Vessel Regulations (SVR s. 420 and s. 520)

For a passenger-carrying vessel (==s. 420==) and a workboat (==s. 520==) of 15 GT or less, the owner and the operator must
ensure that:

- ==procedures are established== for the use of the vessel's ==life-saving appliances and fire extinguishing equipment== in
  an emergency; and
- the ==crew practises== the procedures so as to be ==proficient at all times==.

The SVR do not say how often. Your own procedures must; a sensible programme (a suggestion, not a legal list) rotates
fire, flooding, person overboard and abandon ship through the year and records each.

## Layer 3: the Fire and Boat Drills Regulations (SOR/2010-83)

The FBDR apply to self-propelled Canadian vessels that are Safety Convention vessels or are required to hold a safety
certificate. ==Section 2(2)== excludes ==fishing vessels of 150 GT or less==, ==cable ferries== and ==vessels of 15 GT or less
carrying 12 or fewer passengers==. So a small Part 4 passenger vessel does ==not== have to meet the FBDR drill intervals, but
it still has SVR s. 420, and the FBDR are a good model.

### Intervals (Schedule)

| Vessel | Voyages | Fire and survival craft drills at least once each |
|---|---|---|
| Safety Convention passenger vessel | All | ==One week== |
| Certified for ==50 or more passengers==, carrying passengers | All except sheltered waters | ==One week== |
| Certified for ==fewer than 50 passengers==, carrying passengers | All except sheltered waters | ==Two weeks== |
| Any vessel carrying passengers | Sheltered waters voyages | ==Two weeks== |
| Cargo vessel | All | ==One month== |
| Fishing vessel over 150 GT | All | ==One month== |

### The 25 per cent rule, and every month

- ==Section 20(2) and (3)==: hold a fire drill and a survival craft drill ==within 24 hours after the vessel embarks== if
  ==more than 25 per cent of the crew== did not take part in such a drill on board ==in the month before==.
- ==Section 21==: every crew member takes part in ==at least one fire drill and one survival craft drill every month==.
- ==Section 22==: hold enough extra drills so that the whole crew is competent and ready at all times.

### How drills are run

- ==Section 16==: before sounding the signal, ==notify all passengers== in either or both official languages that a ==drill
  will be held and there is no actual emergency==.
- ==Section 17==: carry out drills ==as if there were an actual emergency==, as far as is feasible.
- ==Section 18==: equipment used is ==returned to full operational condition immediately==, and faults found are
  ==remedied as soon as practicable==.
- Where passengers are scheduled to be on board more than 24 hours, a practice muster is held within 24 hours of embarkation
  (s. 13), otherwise a safety briefing is given.

### Records (s. 37)

The master must ==record and keep==:

- the ==date of each muster==;
- a ==detailed report of each fire drill== and of each ==survival craft drill==, including inspection and testing of equipment;
- a report of any drill ==not held or held only in part==, with the ==reason==;
- an ==evaluation of the competency and operational readiness of the entire crew== for each drill.

These go in the ==official log book== where one is required, or otherwise in a ==deck log or other document== that is kept on board for
==five years== after the last entry and made available to the Minister on request.

## Training records (MPR ss. 205 and 206)

The Marine Personnel Regulations require the master and authorized representative to ensure every person assigned a
function receives ==on-board familiarization and safety training (TP 4957)== ==before starting any duty== (s. 205(1)). The
master must train the crew at the start of employment and keep their knowledge up to date, and keep a ==record== of ==each
person trained, the equipment, the subject-matter and the dates== (s. 206(2)). The record is kept on board or, if the vessel
does not travel more than ==five nautical miles from its home port==, at the home port.

## A BC example

An invented example. A 10 m whale-watching vessel with 12 passenger capacity out of Tofino is outside the FBDR (15 GT or
less, 12 passengers or fewer). The owner is the authorized representative and has written procedures from the Transport
Canada templates. The skipper runs a short fire drill and a person-overboard drill with each new deckhand before their
first trip, then a rotation through the month, and the deckhand signs the record. Each drill ends with the skipper's note on how ready
the crew looked, and any fault, such as a stiff extinguisher pin, goes into the maintenance list.

## Common mistakes

- Assuming the **FBDR frequencies** apply to a 12-passenger vessel; they **do not** (s. 2(2)).
- Writing procedures but **never practising** them (SVR s. 420 requires practice).
- Missing the **25 per cent trigger**: if more than a quarter of the crew skipped drills last month, a drill is due within **24 hours** of sailing.
- Forgetting to tell the passengers that it is **only a drill**.
- Failing to **restore equipment** after the drill.
- Keeping no **evaluation** of the crew and no record of a drill that was cut short.
