# Managing passengers

**Study aid only.** The equipment a passenger vessel must carry is in `canadian-regulations.svr-passenger-vessels` and the
stability maths in `stability.passengers-standards-and-assessment`. This lesson is about the master's control of
people: telling them, counting them, controlling their movements and their access.

## What you need to know

A passenger is the part of the load that can think, wander, panic and stand on the rail. The master's job is to
make them safe by telling them, counting them, placing them and being obeyed. The law gives the master the tools: the
briefing duty (SVR), authority over passengers and boarding (CSA), a limit on numbers, and records.

## The safety briefing (SVR s. 401)

Before the vessel leaves the place where passengers embark, the operator must brief all passengers, in ==either or both
official languages according to their needs==, on the safety and emergency procedures relevant to the type of vessel and
her length. The briefing must cover (==s. 401(1)==):

- (a) the ==location of lifejackets==, specifically ==children's lifejackets==;
- (b) the ==location of survival craft==;
- (c) for passengers in each area, the lifejackets and survival craft ==closest to them==;
- (d) the ==location and use of personal life-saving appliances, visual signals and vessel safety equipment==;
- (e) ==safety measures==: ==protection of limbs==, ==avoiding ropes and docking lines==, and ==the effect of the movement and
  grouping of passengers on stability==;
- (f) the ==prevention of fire and explosions==.

During the briefing the operator must ==demonstrate how to don each type of lifejacket== (==s. 401(2)==).

Brief before you leave the dock, not after, because that is the moment they are standing still and listening. On larger
vessels (FBDR s. 13), passengers on board more than 24 hours get a practice muster within 24 hours, and others a safety
briefing that covers lifejacket instruction, the location of survival craft and muster stations, and uses the public
address system if there is one.

## Counting and numbers

- ==SVR s. 402==: report the ==number of persons on board== to a designated person ashore before departure (or leave the
  information at a known place in a remote area).
- ==CSA s. 110(1)==: the master must ensure that the ==number of persons carried is not more than the number authorised== by
  any certificate. ==Section 110(3)== excepts persons carried to meet the obligation to carry ==shipwrecked or distressed
  persons==.
- On vessels the Fire and Boat Drills Regulations cover (not Part 4 small passenger vessels), the master must have the number
  of persons on board communicated and recorded, along with details of anyone who has ==declared a need for special care
  or assistance== (==FBDR s. 10==). On certain voyages (near coastal Class 1 or unlimited, over 12 hours, or with a
  berth) the ==name and gender== of each person is recorded in a way that distinguishes ==adults (12 or over), children (5 to
  under 12) and infants (under 5)==, kept ashore for search and rescue, and used only for that (==s. 11==).

## Authority over passengers

The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 gives the master the power:

- ==Section 115(1)==: every passenger must ==comply with any direction given by the master or a crew member== to carry out the
  Act or regulations. ==Section 115(2)==: every passenger must comply with a direction from the master to ==leave the vessel
  before the voyage begins==. A passenger who fails to comply commits an offence under s. 123.
- ==Section 116==: no one may go on board or leave a vessel ==without the permission of the master== (or person in charge of
  embarkation), or against the master's orders, or ==if there is no safe means of embarkation or every such means is
  blocked==.
- ==Section 118==: no one may do anything that might jeopardize the safety of the vessel or persons on board.

So the master may refuse boarding to someone who is intoxicated or who will not follow instructions, and may order them
off before sailing.

## Children and lifejackets

SVR ==s. 409(1)(a)== requires ==a lifejacket of an appropriate size for each person on board== and ==s. 408== requires a
lifejacket for a person ==under 16 years== to be ==inherently buoyant==. The briefing must show where children's lifejackets
are. In the Transport Canada abandon-ship sequence, capable people are assigned to help the ==elderly and children==.

## Movement, stability and towing

Passengers moving change the vessel's balance. Moving to one side shifts G sideways and causes a list; moving to an upper
deck raises G and reduces GM (see the stability lesson). That is why the briefing includes "the effect of movement and
grouping". The master can control it by seating, by limiting deck areas and by asking passengers to move one at a time.

Two more passenger rules for operators: ==SVR s. 404(2)== forbids operating a passenger-carrying vessel in circumstances that
exceed her design limitations, and ==s. 404(3)== says that, ==except in an emergency, a vessel that is towing must have no
passengers on the vessel or the tow==.

## A BC example

An invented example. A 10 m whale-watching vessel prepares to leave from Tofino with 11 passengers including three
children. The skipper briefs them at the dock in English and French, where needed, shows the lifejackets, including the
small ones, points out the life raft, and has everyone put on a lifejacket and shows how to fasten it. She tells
them to keep away from lines and the gunwale edge. Last, she reports the number on board to the person ashore designated to talk to search and rescue. When a passenger refuses to sit
on the bench and leans on the rail, she stops the vessel, directs him calmly and, as the master, may direct him to leave before departing if he
will not comply.

## Common mistakes

- Giving the **briefing underway**, when the engine noise starts.
- Forgetting the **lifejacket demonstration** (s. 401(2)) or **children's lifejackets**.
- Counting passengers but not **reporting the number ashore**.
- Carrying more people than the **certificate allows**, forgetting that rescued persons are the only exception.
- Treating the FBDR passenger records as **applying to small Part 4 vessels**.
- Allowing **passengers on a tug or tow**.
