# Light characteristics and reading a light entry

**Study aid only. Not for navigation.** Always identify a light from the current chart and the *List of Lights, Buoys
and Fog Signals*.

## What you need to know

- A light is identified by ==colour, rhythm and period==. At night this is how you tell one light from another.
- Words: ==F== steady; ==Fl== flashing (dark longer); ==Oc== occulting (light longer); ==Iso== equal; ==LFl== flash
  of at least ==2 seconds==; ==Q== ==50 to 79== a minute; ==VQ== ==80 to 159== a minute; ==Mo== Morse.
- The ==period== is the time for ==one full cycle==, in seconds.

## The basic rhythms

| Abbreviation | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| ==F== | Fixed | Continuous, steady light with no darkness |
| ==Fl== | Flashing | The light is on for ==less time than it is dark== (short flashes) |
| ==Oc== | Occulting | The light is on for ==more time than it is dark== (short eclipses) |
| ==Iso== | Isophase | ==Light and dark are equal== |
| ==LFl== | Long flash | A flash of ==2 seconds or longer== |
| ==Q== | Quick flashing | ==50 to 79== flashes a minute (usually 50 or 60) |
| ==VQ== | Very quick flashing | ==80 to 159== flashes a minute (usually 100 or 120) |
| UQ | Ultra quick | 160 or more a minute |
| ==Mo(A)== | Morse code | Short flash then long flash (the letter A) |

**Fl versus Oc: a quick way to remember.** Flashing is "mostly dark with flashes of light". Occulting is "mostly lit
with brief eclipses". The words *flash* and *eclipse* describe the *short* part.

### Groups, composites and periods

A number in brackets is the number of flashes in a ==group==.

- ==Fl(2)== means a group of two flashes, then a dark gap, repeating.
- ==Q(3)== means a group of three quick flashes.
- A **composite group** shows two groups with a plus: ==Fl(2+1)== is two flashes, a short gap, one flash, then a long
  dark, repeating.
- ==Q(6)+LFl== is six quick flashes followed by one long flash.

The number followed by "s" is the ==period==: the time for the **complete cycle** from the start of one group to the
start of the next, in seconds. It includes the dark time. So ==Fl(2) 10s== is two flashes, then dark, all repeating
every 10 seconds. It is not "two flashes for ten seconds".

## Light colours and sectors

Lit aids use white, red, green and yellow (marks have their own colours: lateral marks red and green; special marks
yellow; cardinals, isolated danger and fairway marks white).

A **sector light** has its beam divided into sectors of ==different colours==. The Coast Guard says white indicates
the ==safe passage== and red marks the ==obstruction== side. When you see a sector light change colour you have moved
from one sector to another, which is a way to tell where you are (see a chart for the arcs).

## Reading a light entry

A chart or list describes a light in a short string. Chart 1's own example is:

> ==Fl(2) W 10s 25m 15M==

Read it left to right:

1. ==Fl(2)==: flashing, a group of two flashes.
2. ==W==: white.
3. ==10s==: the whole cycle takes 10 seconds.
4. ==25m==: the height of the light, 25 metres.
5. ==15M==: its range, 15 nautical miles (M for nautical miles). The chart or list explains exactly how that range is defined.

Not every entry has all five parts; buoys often leave out height and range. For the full layout of the *List of Lights,
Buoys and Fog Signals*, use the front pages of the volume for your area (the Pacific coast volume for BC).

### Worked example: counting flashes per minute

Arithmetic verified in a script.

- **Fl 4s**: one flash per 4 seconds. 60 divided by 4 = **15 flashes a minute**. That is slow, so it is plain Fl,
  not Q.
- **Fl(2) 10s**: two flashes per 10 seconds. In a minute there are 60 divided by 10 = 6 cycles, so 6 x 2 =
  **12 flashes a minute**.
- **Q 1s**: one flash per second, so **60 a minute**, inside the Q band of 50 to 79.
- **Iso 4s**: equal light and dark, so **2 seconds of light and 2 seconds of dark**.

### Worked example: identifying a light

At night you see a green light. You time it with your watch and count: flash, 4 seconds, flash, 4 seconds, flash.
Each flash is short, the dark is long.

1. Short light and long dark means **flashing (Fl)**, not occulting or isophase.
2. The period is **4 seconds**, so this is Fl 4s.
3. Green with Fl 4s fits a port hand buoy light (Lesson: lateral buoyage), but the chart must confirm which buoy.

**Tip for timing:** start your count on the *first flash of a group* and stop on the *first flash of the next
group*. A period is measured from the same point in the pattern to the same point again.

## Don't forget the limits of lights

- A light you cannot see is not proof it is not there. Aids can be unlit, off position or damaged. Do not rely on one
  aid alone: use the chart and other aids.
- You cannot judge distance from how bright a light looks.
- If you find an aid that is damaged, missing, off position or not working, the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 expects you
  to report it to ==MCTS without delay==. (The Act sets this duty on the master.)

## Exam traps

- Reversing Fl and Oc. **Flash = short light.** **Occulting = short dark.**
- Giving the period as the time of one flash. The period is the **whole cycle**.
- Mixing up Q and VQ. **Q is about 60 a minute; VQ about twice that (80 to 159).**
- Forgetting that a long flash is **2 seconds or more**.
- Reading Fl(2+1) as three flashes in a group. It is **a group of two plus a group of one**.
- Treating an unlit buoy as proof the danger is not there.
