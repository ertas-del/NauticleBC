# Draft marks, load lines, soundings and ullage

**Study aid only.** Draft, load line and sounding figures for a real ship come from that ship's own stability book,
tank tables and certificates, not from this lesson. The numbers here are invented to show the method.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 lists ==draft marks==, ==load line== marks, and ==sounding== of tanks, bilges and holds by pipe or rod. The exam-sized facts:

- Draft marks are on ==both sides==, at the ==bow==, ==stern== and often ==amidships==. Read the figure at its ==bottom==.
- ==Metric== figures: ==10 cm== high, ==20 cm== apart. ==Imperial==: ==6 in== high, ==12 in== apart.
- The master must ==not submerge the applicable load lines==; the fine is up to ==$500,000 per centimetre==.
- Letters: ==TF==, ==F==, ==T==, ==S==, ==W==, ==WNA==.
- ==Sounding== is liquid depth from the bottom; ==ullage== is empty space from the top.

## Draft marks

The ==draft== is the depth of the hull below the waterline, measured vertically from the keel. Figures painted on both
sides of the bow and stern (and often amidships) let you read it from alongside or from a boat.

- Metric marks: each figure is ==10 cm high== and the figures are ==20 cm apart== (the gap between figures is 10 cm).
- Imperial marks: each figure is ==6 inches high== and the figures are ==12 inches apart==.
- ==Read the water level against the bottom of the figure.== If the water touches the bottom of the figure for 4.20 m, the
  draft is 4.20 m. If it reaches the top of that figure, it is 10 cm more (4.30 m), halfway to the next figure.
- Take readings on ==both sides== and average them if the ship is listed. Waves make the water level rise and fall: read the
  middle of the movement.

### Worked example (invented numbers)

On a calm day a small coaster reads, forward 4.20 m and aft 5.00 m.

1. ==Mean draft== = (forward + aft) / 2 = (4.20 + 5.00) / 2 = 4.60 m.
2. ==Trim== = aft draft minus forward draft = 5.00 - 4.20 = 0.80 m, ==by the stern== (deeper aft).
3. If the ship loads cargo forward and the new readings are 4.50 m forward and 5.00 m aft, trim is 0.50 m by the stern and
   mean draft is (4.50 + 5.00) / 2 = 4.75 m.

(Definitions of mean draft and trim are general seamanship; check with your instructor.) Mean draft at the midship
mark is not always the same as the average of the end marks, because the hull can bend (hog or sag).

## Load lines

The ==load line== marks show the deepest a ship may be loaded in different waters and seasons. They are painted on both
sides amidships. In Canada the law is in the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 and its regulations.

- The ==master must ensure that the applicable load lines are not submerged== (s. 110(2)).
- It is an offence to submerge them: a fine of up to ==$500,000 for each centimetre or part of a centimetre== submerged,
  or up to ==18 months== in prison, or both (s. 122).

| Letters | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==TF== | ==Tropical fresh water== |
| ==F== | ==Fresh water== |
| ==T== | ==Tropical seawater== |
| ==S== | ==Summer== (temperate seawater) |
| ==W== | ==Winter== (temperate seawater) |
| ==WNA== | ==Winter North Atlantic== |

- The ==Plimsoll mark== is a ==disc== (the original was 12 in across) with a ==horizontal line through it (18 in long)== that sits
  at the ==summer== load line. The other marks are measured from it.
- ==Letters of the classification society== (such as AB, BV, LR, NK) are painted beside the disc to show who assigned the marks.
- Ships carrying timber on deck have separate marks with an ==L== in front (LS, LW and so on).
- A ship floats ==deeper in fresh water== than in salt water. Fresh water is less dense, so the fresh water marks (F and TF)
  sit ==above== the summer and tropical marks: a ship in fresh water may load to F and be at the same safe freeboard when she
  reaches the sea. This is the ==fresh water allowance==.
- The ==deck line== is a horizontal line marked at the deck edge from which the load line marks are measured (general).

Every ship must also keep its other certificates in order. Load lines are part of a ship's stability and strength
margin: loading below them reduces freeboard and reserve buoyancy.

## Sounding and ullage

Tanks, holds and bilges are ==sounded== to find out how much liquid is in them.

- ==Sounding== = the measured height of the liquid from the ==bottom of the tank to the surface==.
- ==Ullage== = the empty space from the ==top of the tank (reference point) down to the surface== of the liquid. It is used
  for viscous contents or when a tank is nearly full.
- A ==sounding pipe== runs from the tank top, is ==capped== at the top and is ==open at the bottom==, so the level in the pipe
  matches the level in the tank and you can measure without opening the tank. A vent pipe lets air escape so the tank is never pressurised.
- You lower a ==sounding tape with a bob== (or a rod) down the pipe, note where the wet mark is, and look up the reading in the
  ==sounding table== (sounding book) to find the volume. The table is specific to each tank.
- Record the time, tank and reading in the log. Sound ==carefully and regularly==; how often depends on the tank and
  operation (one ship's example is fuel and waste twice a day, ballast and fresh water daily, and every 15 minutes during
  bunkering).
- General practice (check with your instructor): close self-closing sounding cocks after use, sound only with a
  tape that cannot spark near fuel tanks, and use water-finding paste to detect water at the bottom.

### Worked example (invented numbers)

A tank is 12.40 m from its sounding reference point at the top to the bottom. The sounding is 1.85 m.

- Ullage = total depth - sounding = 12.40 - 1.85 = 10.55 m.
- If the next reading is ullage 10.20 m, the sounding is 12.40 - 10.20 = 2.20 m, so the tank has risen by 2.20 - 1.85 = 0.35 m.

Both measurements must be taken from the same reference point, or the sum is wrong.

## Depth soundings: the hand lead line

A ship also sounds the ==water depth== with a ==hand lead line==. Under the Navigation Safety Regulations a vessel that is
==20 m or more== long and on a voyage other than a sheltered waters voyage must carry one that is:

- at least ==46 m== long,
- ==clearly and accurately marked== to indicate depth, and
- fitted with a ==lead weighing at least 3.2 kg== that can be ==armed== (a hollow in the base takes tallow to bring up a sample of
  the bottom).

## Exam traps

- Reading a draft figure at the **top** instead of the **bottom**.
- Swapping **sounding** and **ullage**. Sounding is from the **bottom**; ullage is from the **top**.
- Mixing up **TF/F** (fresh water, above the summer mark) with **WNA** (winter North Atlantic, below it).
- Saying the master **may** submerge the load line for a short voyage. The law says the applicable load lines **must not** be submerged.
- Taking **mean draft** from only **one side** of a listing ship.
- Forgetting the **same reference point** when converting between sounding and ullage.
