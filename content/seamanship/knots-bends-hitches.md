# Knots, bends and hitches

**Study aid only.** Knots are learned with your hands. Use this lesson to learn the names and jobs, then practise with a
rope under an instructor. The tying descriptions here are written in our own words and are not a substitute for
practical instruction.

## What you need to know

- A ==knot== (in the wide sense) is any tie. Strictly: a ==bend== joins ==two ropes==, a ==hitch== fastens a rope to an
  ==object== (post, ring, spar or another rope), and a ==knot== makes a loop, a stopper or a tie in one rope.
- Know the job of each: ==bowline== (fixed loop), ==clove hitch== (quick hold), ==rolling hitch== (grips along a
  spar or rope), ==sheet bend== (joins different sizes), ==reef knot== (same sizes), ==figure of eight== (stopper),
  ==sheepshank== (shortens a rope).
- Good knots are ==easy to tie, hold under load and untie== when the load is off.

## What makes a good knot

1. It holds under the load you expect.
2. It does not slip when the load comes on or off.
3. It can still be untied after it has been loaded (some knots jam; know which).
4. It is quick enough to tie in the situation, even wet, cold or in the dark.

A knot reduces the strength of a rope at the bend, so ropes never carry as much with a knot in them as when
straight. (General seamanship; check with your instructor.)

| ==Bowline on a bight== | Loop | A ==fixed loop made with both parts== of a doubled rope; tied in the middle of a rope, no end needed |
| ==Clove hitch== | Hitch | ==Quick, temporary hold== on a post, spar, rail or stake; it can jam. Two half hitches in a row |

## How to tie the key knots (words only)

- **Bowline.** Make a small loop in the standing part (the "rabbit hole"). Pass the end up through it (the rabbit
  comes out of its hole), round behind the standing part (round the tree) and back down through the loop (back into the
  hole). Pull the standing part and the loop together to tighten.
- **Clove hitch.** Take a turn round the post, cross over the standing part, and make a second turn round the post;
  tuck the end under the last turn. Two half hitches that go the same way.
- **Round turn and two half hitches.** Pass the rope twice round the bollard or spar (a round turn), then tie two
  half hitches round the standing part.
- **Sheet bend.** Make a bight in the thicker rope. Pass the end of the thinner rope up through it, round behind both
  parts of the bight, and tuck it under its own standing part. For the double, take a second turn round the bight.
- **Reef knot.** Right over left and under, then left over right and under. Both ends must come out on the same side of
  the knot, or it is a granny knot, which slips.
- **Rolling hitch.** Take turns round the larger rope or spar, two of them lying together with the load pulling
  against them, then a further turn on the other side, and finish with a half hitch. The turns bite on the larger
  rope, so the hitch resists sliding along it in the direction of pull. Learn the exact layout (which side the
  two turns lie) from an instructor.

## Choosing the right knot: worked examples

1. **Joining two mooring lines of different diameters for a temporary lengthening.** The ropes differ in size, so use a
   ==sheet bend== (or a double sheet bend). A reef knot is for lines of the same size and slips with unequal sizes.
2. **Making a non-slip loop to put over a bollard on the dock from the boat.** A ==bowline==: the loop will not
   tighten or slip, and it can be untied afterward.
3. **Taking the load off a loaded mooring line while you move it to a bitts.** Put a ==stopper== on it (a rope
   attached with a ==rolling hitch==), then take the stopper's tail to a bitt or winch so the main line is free.
4. **Stopping the end of a halyard from running out through a block.** A ==figure of eight== at the end.
5. **A line is too long and you do not want to cut it.** A ==sheepshank== shortens it.
6. **Tying a small boat painter to a post for a few minutes.** A ==clove hitch==, plus a ==half hitch== if it may
   jerk loose. For a long stay, a round turn and two half hitches.

## Exam traps

- Using a **reef knot** to join two lines of different size, or for a heavy pull. Use a **sheet bend**.
- Calling a **clove hitch** a permanent tie. It is **quick and temporary** and it can jam.
- Mixing up **bend** (joins ropes), **hitch** (rope to an object) and **bowline** (a loop).
- Tying a **granny knot** by accident (the ends come out on opposite sides).
- Using a **timber hitch** alone for a continuous pull. It needs a **half hitch** added.
- Believing a **sheepshank** works under a slack load. It needs the pull on it to hold.
