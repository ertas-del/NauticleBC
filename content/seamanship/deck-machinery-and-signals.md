# Deck machinery and hand signals

**Study aid only.** Do not operate any deck machinery until you have been trained on that machine and authorised by
your ship. Follow the manufacturer's instructions and your ship's code of signals.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 gives deck equipment and machinery the biggest block of hours in the course, and says you must be able to use and
understand ==basic signals for winches, windlass, cranes and hoists==. In Canada the rules for operating this equipment
are in the Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (MOHS). The exam-sized facts:

- ==Windlass== (horizontal) and ==capstan== (vertical axis) raise anchors; ==winches== haul wires and ropes; ==cranes== and
  ==derricks== lift.
- No operation without a ==clear view== or a ==signaller==.
- The employer sets a ==code of signals==; the ==signaller does nothing else== while the load moves.
- Never exceed the ==SWL==; never leave equipment ==unattended== unless secured.

## The machines

| Machine | What it is | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| ==Windlass== | A ==horizontal winch==; a drum or wildcat on a horizontal shaft | Raising and lowering the anchor and cable; often with warping drums |
| ==Capstan== | A ==vertical-axis== drum | An alternative to a windlass for anchors; mooring lines on some ships |
| ==Winch== | A drum (on a horizontal shaft) driven by a motor, with a brake | Hauling mooring lines or wires; cargo gear |
| ==Warping drum== (warping head) | A drum on the end of a winch or windlass shaft | Hauling a mooring line by turns round the drum (general) |
| ==Crane== | A slewing boom on a pedestal, with hoist motors | Lifting cargo, stores and boats |
| ==Derrick== | A boom (often fixed in the slewing plane) with a topping lift and guys | Lifting cargo on older or simpler ships (general) |
| ==Hoist== | A lifting machine with a rope and hook | Light loads, stores |
| ==Brake== | A band or disc that holds the drum | Holding the anchor or line when the motor stops |
| ==Wildcat== (cable lifter) | A drum with pockets that grip chain links | Hauling and holding the anchor cable (general) |

(Where a machine is marked "general", the description is ordinary seamanship; check with your instructor and with the
machine's manual.)

A windlass is a horizontal winch that ships use to raise the anchor; it replaces the vertical capstan that older ships used for
the same job. The words overlap in practice, so always use the name your ship uses.

## Operating deck equipment safely

The Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations set these rules for materials handling equipment (winches,
cranes, hoists and the like):

1. ==Only an operator who can work it safely== may operate it. The employer must not require anyone else to do so.
2. The operator must have a ==clear and unobstructed view== of the area where the equipment is working, ==or be
   directed by a signaller==. A signaller is a person assigned to direct, by visual or sound signals, the safe movement
   and operation of the equipment.
3. The employer must ==establish a code of signals==, ==instruct every signaller and operator== in it, and ==keep a copy==
   where signallers and operators can examine it.
4. The ==signaller must not perform any other duty== while equipment under their direction is moving.
5. If visual signals are not feasible (distance, light, weather), the employer must provide a ==telephone, radio or other
   audible signalling device== for the signaller.
6. Equipment must ==not be left unattended== unless it has been ==secured== so that it cannot move by accident and cannot
   be used by someone who is not authorised.
7. Equipment must ==never be operated with a load above its safe working load (SWL)==, and the SWL is ==marked== on the
   equipment or on a label fixed to it. Cargo gear is covered by the same rule in the Ships' Tackle Regulations.
8. Equipment must not be operated on a gangway with a slope greater than the manufacturer's maximum.

Good practice around machinery (general seamanship; check with your instructor): keep clear of turns and bights on any
drum, wear the PPE your ship requires, never reach across a running wire, tag out or lock out equipment that is
being repaired, and report any unusual noise, leak or brake slip.

## Hand signals

In Canada the ==employer's code== is the one that applies on your ship. Many ships also follow a published standard.
The signals below come from the United States federal crane standard (OSHA Appendix A). They are U.S. construction
practice, not Canadian marine law, and they are given here as a worked example of what a code looks like. The first
rule is the same everywhere: ==learn your own ship's code and agree it before the lift==.

| Signal | What the signaller does |
|---|---|
| ==Hoist== | Upper arm out to the side, ==forearm and index finger straight up==, hand and finger make ==small circles== |
| ==Lower== | Arm and ==index finger pointing down==, hand and finger make ==small circles== |
| ==Use main hoist== | A hand taps the top of the head, then the regular signal is given |
| ==Use whip (auxiliary) line== | The elbow of a bent arm (forearm vertical) is tapped with the other hand, then the regular signal |
| ==Raise boom== | Arm extended to the side, ==thumb up==, other fingers closed |
| ==Lower boom== | Arm extended to the side, ==thumb down==, other fingers closed |
| ==Move slowly== | A hand is held in front of the hand giving the action signal |
| ==Swing== | Arm extended horizontally, ==index finger points the way== the boom is to swing |
| ==Stop== | Arm extended to the side, ==palm down==, swung ==back and forth== |
| ==Emergency stop== | ==Both arms== extended to the side, palms down, swung back and forth |
| ==Dog everything== | ==Hands held together at waist level== (stop all motion and make everything secure) |

Rules that go with all signal codes (general):

- Use one signaller only, and make sure the operator can see them at all times; use radio or telephone if you cannot.
- The signaller keeps clear of the load, in a safe place with a clear line of sight.
- ==Anyone may give a stop signal== if they see danger, and the operator obeys it at once (a common rule; check your ship's code).
- If you lose sight of the signaller, ==stop==.

### Worked example (invented situation)

A crane is lifting stores from the wharf to the deck. The operator's view of the hatch is blocked by a stack of pallets.
The rule is that the operator must have a clear view or be directed by a signaller, so a signaller is posted by the hatch.
The signaller points the index finger of an extended arm toward the hatch (swing), then holds the arm out with the palm
down and swings it back and forth (stop) when the load is over the pallets. The signaller does not help with the
slings during this time, because the signaller must not perform other duties while the equipment is moving.

## Exam traps

- Saying a **windlass** is **vertical**. It is **horizontal**; the **capstan** is the vertical one.
- Letting the **signaller** also **tend slings** or **handle lines** while the load moves.
- Using hand signals when the operator **cannot see** the signaller; a radio or telephone is required.
- Thinking the SWL may be exceeded **briefly**.
- Assuming the **U.S. crane signals** are the Canadian marine rule. The employer's code is what applies.
- **Stop** (one arm swung) and **emergency stop** (both arms swung) are different.
