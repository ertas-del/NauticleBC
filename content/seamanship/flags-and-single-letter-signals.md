# Flags, single-letter signals and the phonetic alphabet

**Study aid only.** The International Code of Signals (ICS) is published by the IMO and the full code has many more
signals than are in this lesson. This lesson uses the U.S. printing of the code as its source; check meanings against
the current edition carried on your ship.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 expects you to know the ==flag etiquette== (hoisting and dipping) and the ==single-letter signals==. A
single flag, hoisted by itself, is a complete message. Some letters also have a meaning in the Collision Regulations or in
fishing practice. Learn them in groups, not alphabetically.

- ==A== diver down; ==B== dangerous goods; ==G== pilot; ==H== pilot on board; ==O== man overboard; ==P== in port, all
  aboard; ==Q== free pratique; ==T== pair trawling; ==Z== tug.
- ==Y== dragging anchor; ==D== manoeuvring with difficulty; ==F== disabled; ==U== running into danger.
- The Canadian flag is flown ==entering, leaving, moored or anchored in port==, and when ==signalled== by a government vessel.
- The ==phonetic alphabet== uses ==Alfa== and ==Juliett== (note the spellings).

## Flag etiquette

- A Canadian vessel has the right to fly the ==Canadian flag==. The master of a Canadian vessel (other than one on the
  small vessel register) must make sure it flies the flag ==when signalled to do so by a government vessel or a vessel of the
  Canadian Forces==, and ==when entering or leaving, or while moored at or anchored in, a port== (Canada Shipping Act, 2001,
  s. 64).
- The national flag (the ensign) is normally flown from the ==stern== (ensign staff) or the gaff when under way; in port
  it is customarily hoisted in the morning and lowered at sunset (general; ship's custom varies).
- ==Dipping== the ensign is a courtesy: the flag is lowered to salute another vessel and the other ship returns it,
  then both hoist again (general custom; check with your instructor).
- A ==courtesy flag== of the country being visited is flown from the starboard yardarm or mast (general custom).
- A flag that is torn or dirty is replaced (general).
- Vessels over ==150 GT== on a ==near coastal Class 1== or ==unlimited== voyage must carry the ==set of signal flags== shown in
  Appendix 2 of the International Code of Signals, of a size suitable for signalling (Navigation Safety Regulations, s. 122).

## Single-letter signals: the meanings

All 26 letters except R are given in the source for this lesson. (The source summary had no row for R; ask your
instructor, and do not rely on this lesson for it.)

### Dangerous situations (urgent)

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==O== | ==Man overboard== |
| ==J== | ==I am on fire and have dangerous cargo on board: keep well clear of me==, or I am leaking dangerous cargo |
| ==V== | ==I require assistance== |
| ==W== | ==I require medical assistance== |
| ==Y== | ==I am dragging my anchor== |
| ==U== | ==You are running into danger== |
| ==L== | ==You should stop your vessel instantly== |
| ==F== | ==I am disabled; communicate with me== |
| ==D== | ==Keep clear of me; I am manoeuvring with difficulty== |

### What a ship is doing

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==A== | ==I have a diver down; keep well clear at slow speed== |
| ==B== | ==I am taking in, or discharging, or carrying dangerous goods== |
| ==E== | ==I am altering my course to starboard== |
| ==I== | ==I am altering my course to port== |
| ==S== | ==I am operating astern propulsion== |
| ==M== | ==My vessel is stopped and making no way through the water== |
| ==T== | ==Keep clear of me; I am engaged in pair trawling== |
| ==X== | ==Stop carrying out your intentions and watch for my signals== |

### Pilots, tugs, port and health

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==G== | ==I require a pilot.== Fishing vessels close together on the grounds: ==I am hauling nets== |
| ==H== | ==I have a pilot on board== |
| ==Z== | ==I require a tug.== Fishing vessels close together on the grounds: ==I am shooting nets== |
| ==P== | In harbour: ==all persons should report on board as the vessel is about to proceed to sea== |
| ==Q== | ==My vessel is "healthy" and I request free pratique== (permission to enter and have contact with the shore) |

### Talking

| Flag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==K== | ==I wish to communicate with you== |
| ==C== | ==Yes== (affirmative) |
| ==N== | ==No== (negative) |

### Memory aid (our own, to help learn the list)

- **Danger pairs:** D (difficulty) and F (disabled) are both about *our* ship; U (running into danger) and L (stop) are
  about *your* ship; Y is dragging, J is fire with dangerous cargo. Learn the meanings, not a trick.

### Worked example (invented situation)

A fishing boat among a fleet on the grounds hoists flag Z alone. Close together on the fishing grounds that means
**I am shooting nets**, so other vessels keep clear. The same flag hoisted by a ship in a harbour approach means
**I require a tug**. Likewise G means **I require a pilot**, except among fishing vessels close together where it
means **I am hauling nets**. The letter alone is the message; the setting tells you which meaning applies.

## The phonetic alphabet

The spelling alphabet is used on the radio and at the wheelhouse to spell names and codes clearly. Note the spellings
==Alfa== (not Alpha) and ==Juliett== (with two t's).

| A | B | C | D | E | F | G | H | I |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ==Alfa== | Bravo | Charlie | Delta | Echo | Foxtrot | Golf | Hotel | India |

| J | K | L | M | N | O | P | Q | R |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ==Juliett== | Kilo | Lima | Mike | November | Oscar | Papa | ==Quebec== | Romeo |

| S | T | U | V | W | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sierra | Tango | Uniform | Victor | ==Whiskey== | ==X-ray== | Yankee | ==Zulu== |

Say the code word for each letter; say numbers digit by digit (general).

## Exam traps

- **P** in harbour means **all persons report on board** because the vessel is about to sail; do not confuse with **Q** (free pratique).
- **G** and **Z** have **fishing meanings** (hauling nets, shooting nets) in addition to pilot and tug.
- **E** is **starboard**, **I** is **port**.
- **D** (I am manoeuvring with difficulty) is not **F** (I am disabled).
- **J** is **fire with dangerous cargo (or a leak)**, not just fire, and **B** is **dangerous goods** being handled or carried.
- The spellings **Alfa** and **Juliett**.
