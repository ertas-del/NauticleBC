# Cargo, stores and dangerous goods labels

**Study aid only.** Cargo and dangerous goods work is governed by the ship's cargo securing manual, the shipper's declaration,
the IMDG Code and your officers' orders. This lesson gives general awareness only. The IMDG Code itself was not read
for this lesson.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 gives cargo and stores a big block of hours and asks for safe handling, stowage and securing, including
dangerous goods. The exam-sized facts:

- ==Nine UN classes==: ==1 explosives==, ==2 gases==, ==3 flammable liquids==, ==4 flammable solids==, ==5 oxidizers and
  organic peroxides==, ==6 toxic and infectious==, ==7 radioactive==, ==8 corrosives==, ==9 miscellaneous==.
- The label is a ==diamond== with the ==class number at the bottom==.
- Flag ==B== = taking in, discharging or carrying dangerous goods.
- ==Never exceed the SWL== of cargo gear; ==no entry to a hold or tank without a permit==.

## Cargo and stores: stowage and securing

Cargo is anything the ship carries for hire; stores are the items the ship itself uses (provisions, deck and engine
stores, paint, chemicals, spare parts). Both can hurt people and damage the ship if stowed or secured badly.

The reasons for careful stowage and securing (general seamanship; check with your instructor and with your ship's
cargo securing manual):

1. **Safety of the ship.** Heavy items low, weight spread, the ship kept upright and with the right trim. Cargo that shifts
   can make a ship list dangerously or capsize.
2. **Safety of people.** Loose cargo can crush; unsecured stores can trip or injure.
3. **Safety of the cargo.** Chafe, crushing, wet, heat, fumes and contamination damage goods, so dunnage, ventilation and
   segregation are used.
4. **Safety of the next job.** Cargo stowed so it can be discharged without moving other cargo and without opening it.

| Principle | What it means (general) |
|---|---|
| ==Stow low and spread weight== | Heavy cargo at the bottom and distributed to keep the ship stable and avoid overloading the deck |
| ==Fill gaps and use dunnage== | Wood, mats or air bags stop cargo shifting and keep it off wet floors |
| ==Secure== | Lashings, chains, turnbuckles and stops hold cargo against the ship's rolling and pitching |
| ==Segregate== | Keep goods that can react with each other apart; keep food away from chemicals and fumes |
| ==Protect== | Chafe gear, tarpaulins and ventilation as the cargo needs |
| ==Label and record== | Marks and a cargo plan show what is where; the officer of the watch is told |

**Lifting.** Cargo gear (cranes, derricks, slings, shackles, spreaders) has a ==safe working load== that must ==never be
exceeded== (Ships' Tackle Regulations; Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations). Slings and shackles are
checked before use. Nobody stands under a load.

**Holds and tanks.** A hold, tank or other confined space can have too little oxygen or poisonous or explosive gas, even
if it looks empty. The Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations say:

- ==No entry to a confined space without a work permit.==
- A marine chemist or other qualified person assesses the space; the oxygen level must be ==19.5 to 23 per cent by volume==
  before someone enters.
- Never enter to rescue a person without breathing apparatus; raise the alarm (general; check with your instructor).

## Dangerous goods: the nine classes

Dangerous goods are goods that can hurt people, ships or the environment. The ==UN system== sorts them into ==nine classes==,
some with divisions. The Canadian Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations use the same class names, and the
==IMDG Code== (the International Maritime Dangerous Goods Code) applies them to sea transport.

| Class | Name | Divisions and examples |
|---|---|---|
| ==1== | ==Explosives== | Six divisions by explosion hazard; 1.1 mass explosion hazard (such as TNT); 1.4 includes consumer fireworks and ammunition |
| ==2== | ==Gases== | 2.1 ==flammable== (acetylene, hydrogen), 2.2 ==non-flammable== (nitrogen, carbon dioxide), 2.3 ==toxic== (chlorine) |
| ==3== | ==Flammable liquids== | Petrol, diesel, kerosene |
| ==4== | ==Flammable solids==, spontaneous combustion, water-reactive | Three divisions |
| ==5== | ==Oxidizing substances and organic peroxides== | 5.1 oxidizers, 5.2 organic peroxides |
| ==6== | ==Toxic and infectious substances== | 6.1 ==poisonous==, 6.2 ==infectious== |
| ==7== | ==Radioactive materials== | Uranium and similar |
| ==8== | ==Corrosives== | Acids and alkalis |
| ==9== | ==Miscellaneous== | ==Dry ice==, asbestos and other goods that do not fit above |

**Memory aid (our own):** "**E**very **G**ood **F**isherman **S**ails **O**ver **T**all **R**ough **C**hop **M**ostly" is a made-up sentence
whose initials stand for Explosives, Gases, Flammable liquids, flammable Solids, Oxidizers, Toxic, Radioactive, Corrosives,
Miscellaneous. Make up one that sticks for you.

### The label

A ==dangerous goods label== (hazard diamond) is a ==diamond== (a square turned on its point) with a symbol at the top and the
==class number at the bottom==. Colour tells the hazard: ==red== for flammable, ==orange== for explosive and ==green== for
non-flammable gas. (The IMDG Code has the full set of labels, placards and marks; these details are the ones the source
reported.) The class number lets you look up the right handling and segregation rules.

### Flag B

Flag ==B== of the International Code of Signals means "I am taking in, or discharging, or carrying dangerous goods".
Flag ==J== is the different signal for a ship that is on fire with dangerous cargo (or leaking it): keep well clear.

### Worked example (invented situation)

A drum on the wharf is marked with a diamond label with the number **3** at the bottom and a flame symbol. The class is
3, flammable liquids, so the label is red. If you see a diamond with **2.2** at the bottom and a green background, the contents
are a non-flammable gas under pressure (the green colour for non-flammable gas, the class 2, division 2.2). The right
action in both cases is to tell the officer and not move or open the package until you are told how to handle it.

## Exam traps

- Putting **class 5** (oxidizers) among the **flammable** goods. Class **3** is flammable liquids and **4** is flammable solids.
- Saying class **6** is just "poisons". It is **toxic and infectious**.
- Saying **class 7** is **corrosives**. Radioactive is **7**, corrosives is **8**.
- Confusing **flag B** (dangerous goods) with **flag J** (fire with dangerous cargo).
- Entering a **hold** because it has been **open to the air** for a while. A **permit** and a **test** are still needed.
- Treating the **SWL** of a sling as a **target** rather than a **limit**.
