# Bosun's chair, staging, ladders, gangways and pilot ladders

**Study aid only.** Work aloft and over the side is where deck crew fall. Rig and use this equipment only as your ship's
procedures require, with a permit and a lookout where the ship's safety management system says so.

## What you need to know

TP 10936 asks you to be able to rig and unrig a ==bosun's chair==, ==staging==, ==pilot ladder==, hoist, ==gangway== and
==rat guards==. Canada's rules are in the Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations (MOHS), the older Safe
Working Practices Regulations, and, for pilot ladders, the Notices to Mariners item that the Navigation Safety
Regulations point to. The exam-sized facts:

- A ==stage== is hung from ==above== (safety factor ==six==); a ==scaffold== stands on ==below== (==four times== the load).
- ==Fall protection== above ==2.4 m==; ==safety belt and line== on a stage above ==3 m==.
- ==Pilot ladder==: climb ==1.5 m to 9 m==; ==two man-ropes 28 to 32 mm==.

## Bosun's chair

A ==boatswain's chair== (bosun's chair) is a ==single-point adjustable suspension scaffold== with a seat or sling for
==one person sitting==. It is used to work aloft or over the side (painting, inspecting, rigging).

- It must give ==stable and adequate support== to the user.
- It must hang from a ==solid anchorage== (such as a thrust-out beam, cornice hook or parapet clamp) with a
  ==working load limit at least equal to== that of the chair's own suspension system.
- It is used ==only when visibility and weather permit== safe use.
- ==Inspect before each use==, and replace any rope, wire or vital part with significant wear.
- A ==qualified person== tests the apparatus and its safety devices ==before it is first operated==, ==after any
  alteration== and ==every 12 months==, and a signed record is kept.
- A persons transfer apparatus must not be used when the ==roll of the vessel exceeds the maker's maximum==, with a load
  above its design load, or while a safety device is inoperative.
- Good practice (general; check with your instructor): a second person tends the fall, the user wears a harness
  tied to a separate line, tools are tied on, the ship is told, and any engine or radar that could injure the user is
  stopped or locked out.

## Stages and scaffolds

| | Stage | Scaffold |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | A working platform ==supported from above== | A working platform ==supported from below== |
| Safety factor | Supporting structure and ropes or tackle ==not less than six== | Must support ==at least four times== the likely load |
| Other points | ==Flat, horizontal surface==; fitted with a means of ==holding it away from the working area== | Platform ==at least 500 mm wide==, fixed in place, with flat horizontal surface; base plates on uneven ground; built by or under a qualified person |

Other staging rules:

- A person on a stage more than ==3 m== high wears a ==safety belt== with a safety line rigged to a ==life line== so that
  the free fall is not more than ==1.2 m== (Safe Working Practices Regulations).
- ==Fall protection== is required for unguarded work more than ==2.4 m== above the nearest permanent safe level, above an
  open hold or above moving machinery (MOHS). See the safe working practices lesson.
- Where a raised structure or deck opening with a coaming lower than 900 mm has a drop of more than 1.2 m, a
  ==guardrail== is required: a top rail ==900 to 1100 mm== high, an intermediate rail midway, posts at most 3 m apart.
- If tools could fall, a toe board (at least 125 mm high) or panel is fitted, or tools are tied on or a net is rigged.
- Inspect by eye before each work period (qualified person), and after the tide has changed if it can affect the
  structure.

### Worked example (invented numbers)

A stage is hung from two ropes, each with a breaking load of 18 kN. With a safety factor of 6 the working load of
each rope is 18 / 6 = 3 kN. Two people (about 1 kN each with tools) and a 0.5 kN plank give about 2.5 kN in all, so each
rope carries about 1.25 kN. That is well below 3 kN, so the ropes pass on strength. A single rope carrying the whole
2.5 kN would still be under 3 kN, which is why the factor of six leaves room for a mistake. (The numbers are made up to
show the method.)

## Ladders, gangways and accommodation ladders

- An ==accommodation ladder== is a means of boarding that may have platforms and is ==hung by chains or wire ropes==, ==hinged at the top==
  and movable so the lowest platform reaches the shore. A ==gangway== is a ramp or walkway between ship and shore.
- Every access ladder or gangway must give a ==safe means of access==, be ==secured== against slipping, be ==rigged to
  allow for ship movement==, be ==adequately lighted==, and have a ==lifebuoy with line== placed ready for immediate use.
  A ship alongside a wharf must have at least one.
- Where the access is more than ==1 m== above the deck, steps or a bulwark ladder must give safe access to the deck.
  A bulwark ladder is firmly secured and has ==two handhold stanchions== at least 40 mm across, rising at least 1.2 m
  above the bulwark top and 0.7 to 0.8 m apart.
- A ==safety net== goes under every part of the ladder, accommodation ladder or gangway unless it is unnecessary or
  impossible: it extends ==1.8 m== on each side and is kept ==taut==.
- A ==rope ladder== must reach its landing point, has flat wooden treads wider than the ladder at regular intervals and
  must be securely rigged. It may not be used where the distance from the water to the access point is ==more than
  9 m==, and it may not be used for access ashore except in an emergency.
- ==Rat guards== are discs or funnels fitted on mooring lines to stop rats using them to board; they are checked and
  put on at each berth (general; check with your instructor).
- Persons on a ladder or gangway may need someone stationed to assist them.

## Pilot ladders

Vessels that will probably need a licensed pilot must meet the pilot transfer equipment and arrangements in Section B
of the annual Notices to Mariners (the Navigation Safety Regulations require it). The main requirements:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Climb | ==Not less than 1.5 m and not more than 9 m== above the water |
| Great Lakes and St. Lawrence | If the distance is over 5 m, an accommodation ladder or similar limits the pilot ladder climb to ==5 m== |
| Position | ==Within the parallel body length==, as far as possible in the ==mid-ship half length==, ==clear of any discharges== |
| Man-ropes | ==Two man-ropes of 28 mm to 32 mm== diameter, ready for immediate use |
| Other gear | ==Lifebuoy with self-igniting light== and a ==heaving line== |
| Supervision | A ==responsible officer== who can ==communicate with the bridge== supervises rigging and boarding and arranges a safe route to the bridge |
| Lighting | Adequate light on the ==transfer arrangements overside== and on the ==deck position== |
| Over 9 m | An ==accommodation ladder== may be used ==together with the pilot ladder== |

How the ladder is built (IMO standard, as reported):

- ==Steps== not more than ==400 mm x 115 mm x 25 mm==, spaced ==310 mm== (plus or minus 5 mm).
- A ==spreader step== at least ==1.8 m== long replaces a standard step ==at least every eighth step== so the ladder does not twist.
- Two pairs of ==side ropes== of at least ==18 mm== manila.
- The ==lowest four steps== are normally synthetic or composite.
- The ladder is ==tied by knots to a fixed securing point on deck==, ==never by shackles==.

## Exam traps

- Swapping the **safety factors**: **stage six**, **scaffold four**.
- Saying a **stage** rests on the ground. It hangs from **above**.
- Forgetting the **12-month** test of a bosun's chair, or thinking that **inspection before each use** is enough.
- Using a **rope ladder** where the climb is over **9 m**.
- Confusing the **pilot ladder climb range** (1.5 to 9 m) with the **man-rope size** (28 to 32 mm).
- Fixing a pilot ladder with **shackles**. It is **knotted** to the deck.
