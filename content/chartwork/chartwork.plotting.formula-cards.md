# Chart plotting and distance, speed, time: the cards

This lesson is built to be easy to read. One card at a time. Use a pencil and a chart if you can.

---

## Card 0: Which scale?

```
Latitude    side scale (left or right)
Longitude   top or bottom scale
Distance    LATITUDE scale (side), at the same level as your track
```

Memory hook:

> **LAT** is **flat**: lines go sideways, and you read them up the side.
> **LONG** is **long**: lines go up and down, and you read them along the bottom.

- 60 minutes = 1 degree.
- ==1 minute of latitude = 1 nautical mile.==

---

## Card 1: Plot a position

**Position:** 48° 24.5′ N, 123° 22.0′ W

1. Find 48° 24.5′ on the **side** scale. Draw a faint line across.
2. Find 123° 22.0′ on the **bottom** scale. Draw a faint line up.
3. Where the lines cross is the position.
4. Mark it with a small circle. Write the time.

---

## Card 2: Read a position

1. Draw a faint line from the point across to the **side** scale. Read the **latitude**.
2. Draw a faint line from the point down to the **bottom** scale. Read the **longitude**.
3. Write it as: latitude first, longitude second.

---

## Card 3: Course and distance

1. Draw a line from A to B.
2. Put the plotter or parallel rule on the line.
3. Move it to the nearest **compass rose**. Read the **true course**.
4. Open the dividers across A to B.
5. Move them to the **side scale**. Read the **distance** in nautical miles.

For long distances, step the dividers along in smaller bites.

---

## Card 4: Distance, speed, time

Three forms. One triangle.

```
D = S × T
T = D ÷ S
S = D ÷ T
```

- **D** is distance in nautical miles.
- **S** is speed in knots (nautical miles per hour).
- **T** is time in hours.

**Example 1: find time**

- Distance: 18 NM. Speed: 7.2 knots.

```
T = D ÷ S
T = 18 ÷ 7.2
T = 2.5 hours = 2 hours 30 minutes
```

**Example 2: find distance**

- Speed: 6 knots. Time: 2 hours 30 minutes = 2.5 hours.

```
D = S × T
D = 6 × 2.5
D = 15 NM
```

**Example 3: find speed**

- Distance: 12 NM. Time: 90 minutes = 1.5 hours.

```
S = D ÷ T
S = 12 ÷ 1.5
S = 8 knots
```

---

## Card 5: Minutes to hours

Divide the minutes by 60.

- 30 min = 0.5 h
- 45 min = 0.75 h
- 90 min = 1.5 h

Quick rule at 10 knots: ==1 NM every 6 minutes.==

---

## Card 6: ETA (estimated time of arrival)

> ETA = departure time + time taken

**Example:** depart 08:15. Time taken 2 h 30 min.

```
08:15 + 2:30 = 10:45
```

---

## Card 7: Dead reckoning (DR) position

**DR** means you work out where you are from your last known position.

1. Plot your last **fix**.
2. Draw the **true course** you steered.
3. Work out the distance: ==speed × time==.
4. Measure that distance along the course line.
5. Mark the DR position (a small dot with a half circle). Write the time.

**Example**

- Last fix 10:00. Course 045° true. Speed 6 knots.
- DR at 12:30. Time is 2 h 30 min.

```
D = 6 × 2.5 = 15 NM
```

Plot 15 NM along the 045° line from the fix.

Answer: ==The DR position is 15 NM along 045° from the fix.==

---

## Card 8: Bearing fix

1. Take **two compass bearings** of two marks at the same time.
2. Convert each to **true** (see the compass cards).
3. Plot the **reciprocal** (add or subtract 180°) from each mark.
4. Where the lines cross is your position.

Best angle between the two bearings: close to 90°.

---

## Common mistakes

- Measuring distance on the bottom scale.
- Forgetting to change minutes into hours.
- Plotting a bearing from the boat instead of from the mark.
- Mixing up latitude and longitude.

## Check yourself

1. Which scale do you measure distance on?
2. How long does 15 NM take at 5 knots?
3. What is the speed for 8 NM in 40 minutes?
4. How far do you go in 2 hours 30 minutes at 6 knots?
