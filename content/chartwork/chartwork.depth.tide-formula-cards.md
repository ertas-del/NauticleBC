# Depth and tide sums: the cards

This lesson is built to be easy to read. One card at a time. Say each sum out loud.

---

## Card 0: The words

- **Chart datum**: the low-water level the chart uses. Charted depths are measured from it.
- **Sounding**: a charted depth.
- **Height of tide**: how far the water is above chart datum.
- **Draught**: how deep your boat sits in the water.
- **Under-keel clearance**: water between your keel and the bottom.
- **Drying height**: the height of a rock above chart datum. It shows on the chart as an underlined number.

Picture:

```
         water surface (tide above datum)
  --------------------------------------  height of tide
  chart datum (line of charted depth) -----
                 |
         charted depth (sounding)
                 |
              seabed
```

## Card 1: Depth of water

> **Depth = charted depth + height of tide**

**Example**

- Charted depth: 2.0 m
- Height of tide: 3.1 m

```
Depth = 2.0 + 3.1 = 5.1 m
```

Answer: ==5.1 m of water.==

---

## Card 2: Under-keel clearance

> **Clearance = depth − draught**

**Example**

- Depth: 5.1 m
- Draught: 1.0 m

```
Clearance = 5.1 − 1.0 = 4.1 m
```

Answer: ==4.1 m under the keel.== Always keep a margin.

---

## Card 3: Height of tide by the rule of twelfths

Use this when the tide is between high and low water.

**Step 1.** Find the **range**.

> Range = high water height − low water height

**Step 2.** Count the hours from the nearest tide. For a six-hour tide, the water moves like this each hour:

```
Hour 1:  1/12 of the range
Hour 2:  2/12
Hour 3:  3/12
Hour 4:  3/12
Hour 5:  2/12
Hour 6:  1/12
```

**Step 3.** Add the twelfths up to your time. Multiply by the range. Add to the low water height.

**Example**

- Low water 10:00, height 0.5 m.
- High water 16:00, height 4.7 m.
- You want the height at 12:00.

```
Range = 4.7 − 0.5 = 4.2 m
Hours after low water: 2
Twelfths: 1 + 2 = 3
Rise = 4.2 × 3/12 = 1.05 m
Height = 0.5 + 1.05 = 1.55 m
```

Answer: ==Height of tide at 12:00 is 1.55 m.==

**Another time: 14:00**

```
Hours after low water: 4
Twelfths: 1 + 2 + 3 + 3 = 9
Rise = 4.2 × 9/12 = 3.15 m
Height = 0.5 + 3.15 = 3.65 m
```

Answer: ==3.65 m at 14:00.==

## Card 4: Water over a drying rock

A drying height is above chart datum.

> **Water over the rock = height of tide − drying height**

**Example**

- Drying height: 1.2 m
- Height of tide: 2.0 m

```
Water over rock = 2.0 − 1.2 = 0.8 m
```

Answer: ==0.8 m of water over the rock.==

If the answer is **zero or less**, the rock is **showing**.

---

## Card 5: Safe time to cross a shallow spot

> **Tide needed = draught + margin − charted depth**

**Example**

- Charted depth: 1.0 m
- Draught: 1.5 m
- Margin you want: 0.5 m

```
Tide needed = 1.5 + 0.5 − 1.0 = 1.0 m
```

Answer: ==Cross when the tide is at least 1.0 m.==

Check the tide table for the time.

---

## Card 6: Currents and slack water

- A strong tidal current runs in narrow passes.
- **Slack water** is when the current stops and turns.
- Plan to pass narrow, fast channels near ==slack water==.
- Wind against current makes steep, short seas.

---

## Common mistakes

- Taking the tide away from the charted depth. It is added.
- Forgetting the draught.
- Using the wrong hour count in the rule of twelfths.
- Treating a drying height like a depth.

2. Depth 5.0 m, draught 1.2 m. What is the clearance?
