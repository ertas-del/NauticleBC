# Magnetic and gyro compasses

**Study aid only.** For the parts of a compass and how a gyro works, read the Bridge Watch lesson on magnetic and gyro compasses first. This lesson is the chartwork view: which compass, which errors, which corrections.

## What you need to know

A boat can carry up to five direction instruments. Know what each reads and what must be applied to get **true**, because charts are plotted in true.

| Instrument | Reads | Errors present |
|---|---|---|
| ==Standard magnetic compass== | Heading (ship's head) by magnetic north | ==Variation and deviation== |
| ==Steering magnetic compass== | Heading at the wheel, used to steer | ==Variation and deviation==; may differ from the standard compass |
| ==Gyro-compass== (and repeaters) | Heading by true north | ==No variation, no deviation==; may have ==gyro error== |
| ==Hand-bearing compass== | Bearing to an object | ==Variation== only if held clear of iron |
| ==Pelorus / compass bearing device== | Bearing relative to the ship's head or to the compass | Same errors as the compass it is set from |

## The rules (Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020)

- ==s. 106(1)==: every vessel must be fitted with a ==standard magnetic compass==, independent of any power supply, able to show the heading at the main steering position. ==Exceptions==: vessels of ==8 m or less== that stay within sight of navigation marks, and cable ferries.
- ==s. 106(2)==: a vessel under ==150 GT== may carry a ==steering magnetic compass== instead (with a gyro-compass too if on an international voyage).
- ==s. 106(4)==: the compass must be properly ==compensated==, and its ==table or curve of residual deviations== must be on board, near the compass.
- ==s. 106(5)==: a ==means of correcting headings and bearings to true== at all times (not required of pleasure craft under 150 GT).
- ==s. 109(1)(c)==: ==150 GT or more== must also carry a spare magnetic compass.
- ==s. 132==: at an inspection show the ==latest deviation card== for each compass, ==signed and dated by the master and a deck officer==, or a signed statement that the compasses are satisfactory.
- ==s. 145(b)==: a vessel that must carry charts must also carry the ==navigation accessories needed to determine the accuracy of compass readings==.

The Master Limited competence also requires you to keep a ==log book and a record of compass errors==.

## Why the gyro is still checked

A gyro has no iron error, but it can drift, can be set up wrongly, and wanders after rapid manoeuvres. Its error is checked in the same ways as the magnetic compass: against a charted transit or a known bearing. The gyro error is named east or west and applied like a compass error: gyro heading plus easterly error, minus westerly error.

## Which corrections for which instrument

1. **Ship's magnetic compass heading to true heading**: apply deviation (for the heading) and variation (for the place). Lesson 8.
2. **Gyro heading to true**: apply gyro error only.
3. **Hand-bearing compass bearing to true**: apply variation only, if held where the ship's iron cannot affect it. A hand compass held near the engine casing or a steel stanchion is not trustworthy, so move to a spot clear of iron or compare with a known bearing first.
4. **Compass bearing taken with a pelorus set on the ship's compass**: use the ship's compass error for the heading.

Mistake to avoid: applying the ship's deviation to a hand-bearing compass bearing. The deviation card is for the ship's compass on that heading, not for the hand compass.

## Worked example (invented)

Variation 17° E. The ship's deviation on the present heading is 3° W. A hand-bearing compass reading, taken from the open wing, to a light is 052°. A bearing taken with the ship's compass, using the steering position pelorus on the same light, reads 054°.

1. Hand compass: 052° + 17° E = ==069° true== (variation only).
2. Ship's compass: compass error = 17° E + 3° W = 14° E. 054° + 14° = ==068° true==.
3. The two agree to 1°, so both are reasonable; the plot would use the average or the better instrument.

## Checking a compass

Checks you can do on passage: compare the steering compass with the standard compass (they should differ by a stable amount); compare with the gyro; take a bearing of a charted transit (lesson 9); take a bearing of an object and compare with the chart. Record every check in the log.

## BC example

A 12 m vessel working inshore follows the coast within sight of marks. If she is 8 m or less she can legally go without a standard compass. A 12 m vessel is not exempt. She needs a magnetic compass independent of power, a deviation table near it, and a way to correct to true (a plotting tool and the variation from the chart).

## Exam traps

- Applying deviation to a hand compass, or variation alone to a ship's compass.
- Confusing the exception for "8 m or less" with the 150 GT limit for steering compasses.
- Thinking the gyro never needs checking.
- Forgetting that the deviation table belongs near the compass, not locked in the office.
