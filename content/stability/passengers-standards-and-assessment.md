# Passengers, stability standards and assessments

**Study aid only.** The IS Code numbers below come from a secondary source and have not been checked in the code. Do not use them to judge a vessel.

## What you need to know

You have the physics: G, GM, GZ, loading, free surface. This lesson puts that physics into the rules. It covers how people aboard affect a vessel, what the regulations say a vessel and its owner must do about stability, how an assessment is done, and what a master should expect to find in the wheelhouse.

## Passengers and crew move weight

People are weight that moves by itself. Two movements matter most.

1. **Sideways**: a crowd going to one rail to look at a whale shifts G sideways, so the vessel lists.
2. **Upwards**: people going up to the flying bridge or an upper deck raise G, which cuts GM.

### Worked example 1: a crowd at the rail

A passenger vessel (invented) displaces 25 t with GM 0.50 m. Twelve passengers weighing 80 kg each (0.96 t) all move 1.5 m to starboard.

1. GG1 = 0.96 x 1.5 / 25 = **0.0576 m** sideways.
2. tan(list) = 0.0576 / 0.50 = 0.1152; list = **6.6 degrees**.

### Worked example 2: and they climb first

Before that, the same twelve passengers move up from the main deck to an upper deck 2.0 m higher.

1. Rise of G = 0.96 x 2.0 / 25 = **0.0768 m**. New GM = 0.50 - 0.0768 = **0.423 m**.
2. Now they move to the rail: tan(list) = 0.0576 / 0.423 = 0.136; list = **7.8 degrees**.

The two together have made the list bigger: raising G cuts GM, and a smaller GM means a larger list for the same sideways shift. This is why the law requires passengers to be told about it.

## What the Small Vessel Regulations say

The Small Vessel Regulations (SOR/2010-91) apply to, among others, passenger-carrying vessels of not more than 15 gross tonnage carrying not more than 12 passengers, and workboats of not more than 15 GT. On stability they say, in plain words:

- The ==passenger safety briefing== must cover the effect of the movement and grouping of passengers on the vessel's stability (s. 401(1)(e)).
- The owner and operator of a vessel other than a pleasure craft must make sure it has ==adequate stability and structural strength== for its intended operations (s. 601(2)), and the owner must be able to demonstrate this when the Minister asks (s. 716).
- The owner must ==inform the Minister of a major modification==, which includes a change that affects watertight integrity or stability (s. 710).
- Vessels built, rebuilt, imported or changed in use on or after 1 April 2005 (or with a changed area or operation after that date) must meet a stated standard: those of 6 m or less the construction standards, longer ones the construction standards or the recommended practices and standards (s. 717).

The regulation does not say a vessel has a particular GM. It asks that the stability be adequate and provable, and lets the construction standard or an assessment say how.

## Larger certificated vessels and the IS Code

For larger certificated vessels, the Vessel Certificates and Equipment Regulations (SOR/2023-257) require compliance with Part B of the ==2008 IS Code== (the International Code on Intact Stability, annexed to IMO Resolution MSC.267(85)) as modified by the Canadian modifications in ==TP 7301== (December 2023) (s. 101). Which regulations apply to a particular vessel depends on its size, type and operation, so confirm for your vessel.

One secondary source lists the general criteria in that code for passenger and cargo ships built on or after 1 January 2010 as:

| Criterion | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Area under GZ, 0 to 30 degrees | 0.055 m.rad |
| Area under GZ, 0 to 40 degrees (or to the downflooding angle if less) | 0.09 m.rad |
| Area under GZ, 30 to 40 degrees | 0.03 m.rad |
| GZ at 30 degrees or more | 0.20 m |
| Angle of maximum GZ | at least 25 degrees, preferably above 30 |
| Initial GM | 0.15 m |

There is also a weather criterion. ==These numbers are unverified== and apply to vessels that fall under that code; a small vessel is judged by its own standard.

### Worked example 3: check the Tern's curve

The curve from the GZ lesson had areas of 0.0646 m.rad (0 to 30), 0.1082 (0 to 40) and 0.0436 (30 to 40), GZ 0.24 m at 30 degrees, maximum GZ at 40 degrees, and GM 0.45 m. Against the table: 0.0646 is at least 0.055, yes; 0.1082 is at least 0.09, yes; 0.0436 is at least 0.03, yes; 0.24 is at least 0.20, yes; 40 degrees is above 30, yes; 0.45 is at least 0.15, yes. All pass.

### Worked example 4: the downflooding angle cuts it short

Suppose the Tern's downflooding angle were only 25 degrees (an open door). The 0-to-40 area is then taken only to 25 degrees. GZ at 25 degrees (halfway between 0.17 and 0.24) is 0.205 m, so area 0 to 20 = 0.0288 and area 20 to 25 = (0.17 + 0.205) / 2 x 0.0873 (5 degrees in radians) = 0.0164. The total is **0.0452 m.rad**. That is less than 0.09, so it **fails**. The same hull passes with the door shut and fails with it open. This is why watertight integrity is a stability item.

## The inclining experiment

KG is hard to know for a real vessel. The ==inclining experiment== finds it. Move a known weight a known distance across the deck, measure the angle of heel (with a long pendulum, or a heel sensor), and solve the list formula for GM:

==GM = w x d / (displacement x tan(heel))==

### Worked example 5

A 0.5 t weight is moved 2.0 m across a 25 t vessel, and the list is 4.57 degrees (tan 0.08). GM = 0.5 x 2.0 / (25 x 0.08) = **0.50 m**. This is the list lesson run backwards. Then KG = KM - GM. A real experiment uses careful procedure, several moves and calm conditions, and is done by a competent person.

## Booklets, notices and limits

A stability assessment ends in documents. The Fishing Vessel Safety Regulations, which govern fishing vessels rather than your certificate, show the pattern that applies in practice. A ==competent person== supplies a ==stability booklet== (after a full assessment) or a record of stability (simplified assessment) and a ==stability notice== for the wheelhouse (ss. 3.57 to 3.6). A copy is kept on board, the master operates within the safe limits, the crew know the notice, and the vessel's permanent draft marks show the limit where a full assessment has been done. Assessment is needed for new vessels over 9 m, after a major modification, with an anti-roll tank, and where large tanks or fish in bulk give free surface (s. 3.48). A passenger vessel or a workboat will have its own documents: the master should know what they say, where they are kept, and how they limit loads, passengers, deck cargo and sea state.

## Exam traps

Passengers cause a list by moving sideways and a loss of GM by moving up; the briefing covers both. The Small Vessel Regulations ask for adequate stability, not a number. The owner informs the Minister about a major modification, not the master. The IS Code numbers here are unverified, and a downflooding angle shorter than 40 degrees shortens the 0-to-40 area. And a stability booklet is a limit to obey, not a suggestion.
