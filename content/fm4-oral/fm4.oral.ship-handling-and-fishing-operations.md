# Oral: ship handling in various conditions and during fishing operations

The oral exam asks you to demonstrate handling in words. Describe your plan step by step.

## Factors affecting handling

Know how your vessel behaves: ==propeller walk==, effect of ==rudder== at slow speed, ==pivot point==, turning circle, windage (a high wheelhouse catches wind), and the effect of ==current== on the whole vessel.

## Berthing

1. Assess ==wind, tide and current== and where the others are.
2. Prepare ==fenders== and ==lines== (bow, stern and springs).
3. Approach ==slowly==, heading into the stronger of wind or current, at a shallow angle.
4. Stop the vessel with astern thrust. Allow for propeller walk.
5. Get a ==bow line and spring== ashore first. Use the spring and rudder to ease the stern in.
6. Make fast and adjust for tide.

## Unberthing

Plan how to leave clear of other vessels and gear. Use a ==spring== to swing the stern or bow out. Let go lines in the right order. Check the wind and current take you clear, and look for traffic.

## During fishing operations

- ==Set== and haul gear with the vessel positioned so wind and current don't set it onto the gear or onto danger.
- Keep the ==propeller clear of lines and net==. Stop or declutch when gear is near.
- Reduce speed and make small, planned turns when towing.
- Keep a ==look-out== for other vessels and their gear.
- Use ==slow speed== when working near other fishing vessels.
- Allow for ==tide and wind== when the vessel is stopped.

## Model answers

**Examiner: How would you berth in a strong cross-wind?** "I would approach slowly, heading up into the wind, with fenders and lines ready. I would put a bow line and a spring ashore first, hold the vessel against the dock using the spring and a touch of engine, then make fast the stern."

**Examiner: What effect does current have on berthing?** "I would approach against the current for control. A following current pushes me past the berth, so I would plan more room and use astern engine carefully."

## A BC example

At the dock in Port Hardy a skipper explains: "The tide is ebbing, so the current sets from the bow. I'll come in against it, stop the vessel alongside, and use an aft spring to hold her while we take the other lines."

## Common mistakes

- Coming in fast.
- Forgetting the propeller walk.
- Letting the propeller catch gear.
- Ignoring the current at the berth.

## Check yourself

1. How do you berth in a cross-wind?
2. What do you do with the propeller when gear is near?
3. Why head into current?
