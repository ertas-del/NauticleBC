# Oral: safe working practices and the Small Fishing Vessel Safety Manual

Fishing is dangerous work. The examiner wants to see that you take safety seriously and know how to keep your crew safe.

## Common hazards

==Falls overboard==, ==entanglement== in lines and winches, ==crushing== by gear, ==slips and falls==, ==fire==, ==cold water==, ==fatigue==, and ==vessel loss== from poor stability or flooding.

## Safe working practices

- ==Lifejackets or PFDs== worn on deck, and immersion suits within reach.
- ==Non-slip decks==, good lighting, and clear walkways.
- ==Guards== on rotating machinery. ==Emergency stops== for winches and hauler, known to all crew.
- ==Never stand in the bight== or in the line of a running warp or gear.
- ==Safe lifting==: clear signals, no one under loads.
- ==Hot work and fuel handling== with fire precautions.
- ==Briefings==: tell new crew about hazards and emergency drills.
- ==Rest==: manage fatigue; a tired crew makes mistakes.
- ==Working alone==: tell someone your plan and wear a lifejacket.

## The Code of Safe Working Practices

A code of practice sets out accepted safe methods for work on vessels. The examiner expects you to know the principles and to apply them to fishing vessels: risk assessment, correct equipment and training, and safe systems of work.

## Small Fishing Vessel Safety Manual (TP 10038)

The manual is a Transport Canada guide for fish harvesters. It covers matters such as ==stability and loading==, ==safe operation==, ==maintenance==, ==emergency equipment and drills== and ==survival==. Know that it exists, what it covers, and use it to prepare your vessel.

## Model answers

**Examiner: How would you prevent a deckhand falling overboard?** "Everyone wears a lifejacket on deck, the deck is clear and non-slip, rails and bulwarks are in good order, and no one works alone near the rail without telling someone."

**Examiner: What are the dangers around a winch?** "Entanglement in the warp or drum, being caught in the bight, and crushing by the load. I would keep guards on, know the emergency stop and stay clear of the line."

## A BC example

On a small longliner a new crew member's glove is caught by the winch. The skipper hits the emergency stop. Afterwards he runs a briefing on glove use, guards and the stop button for everyone.

## Common mistakes

- Not wearing a lifejacket on deck.
- Standing in the bight of a line.
- Skipping briefings for new crew.
- Disabling guards.

## Check yourself
