# Oral: weather forecasts, warnings and voyage decisions

Examiners want to hear that you plan around the weather and that you act early.

## Why forecasts matter

Weather decides whether a trip is safe, how heavily the vessel can be loaded, and whether to fish or go home. Ignoring a forecast is a common cause of loss.

## Where to get them

==Environment and Climate Change Canada== marine forecasts, ==VHF weather broadcasts==, Coast Guard broadcasts through MCTS, the website and app, and local observation: sky, barometer, wind and sea.

## Warnings

Know the categories: ==light, moderate, strong (20 to 33 knots), gale (34 to 47), storm (48 to 63), hurricane force (64+)==. Warnings are broadcast on VHF weather channels and by the Coast Guard. Listen regularly.

## Voyage planning

- Check the forecast for the ==whole trip== and the return.
- Plan ==shelter== along the route.
- Check ==tides and currents== for wind-against-tide.
- Check that the vessel's ==loading and stability== suit the expected sea.
- Tell someone ashore your plan.

## Deciding during the trip

Ask: Is it getting worse? Is my vessel loaded safely? How far to shelter? Is the crew rested? ==Interrupt fishing== and ==return or seek shelter== while it is still safe, not when the sea is already rough. Take account of the actual weather and the forecast, not just one.

## Model answers

**Examiner: The forecast changes to a gale warning while you are fishing. What do you do?** "I would stop fishing, recover and secure the gear, close hatches and doors, check my stability and loading, head for the nearest shelter and tell the Coast Guard my plan."

**Examiner: How do you receive weather warnings?** "On VHF weather channels, Coast Guard broadcasts through MCTS, and on the Environment Canada website."

## A BC example

In Hecate Strait a skipper hears a strong wind warning for tomorrow. He changes his plan: he fishes in the sheltered side of the inlet and is back in port by noon.

## Common mistakes

- Relying on one forecast.
- Waiting too long to head for shelter.
- Not checking the forecast for the return.
- Not listening to warnings.

## Check yourself

1. What is a gale in knots?
2. How do you receive warnings?
3. What would you do after a gale warning?
