# Oral: emergencies, rescue and the master's duties

Emergency questions are scenario-based. State your first actions in order and give reasons.

## General approach

1. ==Protect life== (count heads, lifejackets).
2. ==Stop the problem== (fire, flooding, grounding).
3. ==Call for help== early (Mayday or Pan-Pan on VHF 16 or DSC, EPIRB).
4. ==Control the situation== with clear orders.
5. ==Abandon only if you must==, in good order.

## Fire

Raise the alarm. Stop the vessel, turn to keep the fire downwind, ==shut off fuel== and ventilation, ==close doors and hatches==, fight with extinguishers at the base, send a Mayday if not controlled, put on lifejackets, and prepare to abandon.

## Collision

Stop. Check ==injuries==, check ==damage== and ==flooding==, sound bilges, start pumps, report, exchange details, and call for help if needed. If the other bow is plugging a hole, keep it there until you can assess.

## Flooding

Find and ==stop the source==. Use ==pumps==, plug or patch, reduce speed, head for shallow water if needed, and send a Mayday if the flooding cannot be controlled.

## Grounding

Stop engines. Check people and damage, sound the bilges, check the tide, avoid going astern until sure of damage, consider anchoring, call for help.

## Man overboard

==Shout, point, throw a lifebuoy, mark the position, turn the vessel, approach with the person on the windward side, stop the propeller, and recover.== Treat for cold water and hypothermia.

## Abandoning

Only abandon if you must. ==Lifejackets and immersion suits== on, ==EPIRB== and ==flares== taken, ==liferaft== launched and boarded dry if possible, ==distress call== sent, crew together, and ==stay with the raft== until rescued.

## Rescue and assistance

The master must ==render assistance== to persons in danger at sea, so far as it can be done safely. If you hear a Mayday, acknowledge it, go to help if you can, and relay it. When assisting a vessel or aircraft, agree the plan with the rescue coordination centre and the vessel in distress.

## The master's duties

The master is responsible for the ==safety of life==, ==the vessel and its equipment==, ==compliance with the law==, ==the crew's training and drills==, ==pollution prevention==, ==accurate records==, and ==reporting casualties==. The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 sets these duties.

## Model answers

**Examiner: A fire starts in the engine room. What do you do?** "I raise the alarm, stop the vessel, shut off the fuel using the quick-closing valve, close the ventilation and door, fight it from outside with an extinguisher if safe, send a Mayday if it is not controlled, put on lifejackets, and be ready to abandon."

**Examiner: You hear a Mayday. What do you do?** "I listen, write down the details, acknowledge if no one else does, relay it if needed, and go to help if I can do so safely."

## A BC example

While fishing near Cape Scott, a deckhand falls overboard. The skipper shouts, throws a lifebuoy, marks the GPS, turns the boat, and recovers the man over the stern after approaching from the windward side. He sends a Pan-Pan for medical advice.

## Common mistakes

- Delaying the call for help.
- Abandoning too early.
- Forgetting lifejackets.
- Not knowing where the EPIRB is.

## Check yourself

1. What are the first steps in a fire?
2. What do you do if you hear a Mayday?
3. What are the master's duties?
