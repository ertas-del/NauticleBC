# Oral: practical stability and using stability information

The oral tests whether you can use stability in daily decisions, not only in calculations. Explain with simple examples.

## Effect of weights

- ==Adding== a weight above G raises G and reduces GM. Below G lowers G.
- ==Removing== a high weight lowers G. Removing a low weight raises G.
- ==Transferring== a weight sideways causes a list. Moving it up raises G.
- ==Raising or suspending== a weight acts as if the weight is at the point of suspension, so G rises.
- Allow for the effect on ==draft, list and trim== and on ==free surface== in tanks.

## Free surface

A part-full tank or water on deck acts as if G rose. It reduces GM. ==Keep tanks full or empty== and clear water off the deck. Wide tanks are worst. A centreline division reduces the effect.

## Using the stability booklet

Before sailing, work out your loading condition. Compare it with the booklet's pre-calculated conditions. If your condition is covered, you can rely on it. If not, recalculate or change the loading: reduce catch, lower weights, press up tanks. Check the ==GZ curve== and the fish loading limits.

## Stiff and tender

A ==stiff== vessel has a large GM and rolls quickly and hard. A ==tender== vessel has a small GM and rolls slowly and leans a long way. Too tender is dangerous. The ==roll period== is a guide.

## Freeboard and overloading

Less freeboard means less reserve buoyancy and a shorter range of stability. Water gets on deck more easily and freeing ports may not clear it. Don't overload.

## Changes during the voyage

Stability changes as fuel is burned, catch is added, ice forms, or gear gets wet. Check again at each stage, especially the return trip.

## Model answers

**Examiner: What is the danger of a slack tank?** "It creates free surface, which acts like raising G and reduces GM, making the vessel less stable. I keep tanks full or empty."

**Examiner: What do you do before loading fish?** "I check the booklet for the maximum fish load with my fuel, water and crew, check the loading condition against the pre-calculated conditions, and keep fish below deck in divided pens."

## A BC example

A skipper in Bella Bella reaches his planned fish load. He compares his fuel, ice and catch against the booklet's loaded departure condition, finds he is inside it, presses up the last slack tank, and heads home.

## Common mistakes

- Ignoring slack tanks.
- Piling fish on deck.
- Assuming stability stays the same all trip.
- Not checking the booklet.

## Check yourself

1. What is the effect of a weight raised by a boom?
2. What do you do with a slack tank?
3. How do you use the booklet before sailing?
