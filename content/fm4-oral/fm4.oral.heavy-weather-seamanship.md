# Oral: heavy weather, flooding and ice

Heavy weather questions test your seamanship. Describe what you would do in order.

## Before heavy weather

- ==Close and secure== watertight hatches, ==fish scuttles==, doors and ==portholes==.
- ==Lower and secure weights== on board: gear, blocks, booms.
- ==Check freeing ports== and drains are clear and working.
- Secure deck gear and anything that can shift.
- Check ==bilge pumps== and alarms.
- Keep tanks ==full or empty== to avoid free surface.
- Check ==stability== and loading: reduce catch on deck.
- Make sure everyone has a ==lifejacket== and the crew is rested.
- Head for ==shelter== early.

## Preventing and monitoring water ingress

- Keep hull openings closed and valves shut.
- Check ==bilge alarms== and look in compartments regularly (engine room, fish hold, steering flat).
- Sound or inspect tanks and compartments.
- Keep the ==pumps== ready and know the ==damage control== steps.

## If water ingress or disabled

- Find the source and stop it. Start the pumps.
- Send a ==Pan-Pan or Mayday== early.
- If the engine fails, use the ==anchor== or a ==sea anchor== to hold the bow to the sea.
- Put on ==lifejackets==, prepare the ==liferaft== and ==EPIRB==, and keep the crew together.
- Tell the Coast Guard your position and what you are doing.

## Handling in heavy weather

- Reduce speed to avoid ==slamming== and heavy loads.
- ==Head to the sea== or take it on the bow at an angle you can hold.
- In ==following seas== keep enough speed to steer, but avoid being overtaken and ==broaching==.
- Avoid ==beam seas== and synchronous rolling by changing course or speed.
- ==Heave to== if the vessel can't make way.
- Keep a good ==look-out== and consider shelter.

## Ice accretion

Prevent it: avoid freezing-spray areas, reduce speed to reduce spray, and keep the vessel in warmer or sheltered water. If ice starts to form, ==remove it early== with mallets and by other safe means, starting with the highest and heaviest ice, and clear freeing ports. Report if it is getting out of control and head for shelter or warmer water.

## Model answers

**Examiner: A gale is forecast. What do you do?** "I would stop fishing and stow gear, close hatches, doors and ports, lower and secure weights, check freeing ports and pumps, check stability and tanks, put on lifejackets and head for shelter."

**Examiner: Your engine fails in heavy seas. What do you do?** "I'd put the bow to the sea with the anchor or sea anchor, call the Coast Guard, put on lifejackets, prepare the liferaft and EPIRB, and try to restart the engine."

## A BC example

Off the north tip of Vancouver Island a troller's skipper hears a gale warning. He has the crew bring in the poles, lash the gear low, close the hatches and check the freeing ports. They are tied up in Winter Harbour before the wind reaches 40 knots.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving gear high on deck.
- Forgetting freeing ports.
- Waiting too long to call for help.
- Letting ice build up.

## Check yourself

2. What do you do if the engine fails in heavy seas?
3. How do you deal with ice?
