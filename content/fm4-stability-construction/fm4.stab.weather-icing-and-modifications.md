# Weather, icing, modifications and estimating GM

The sea and the weather test stability in ways a calm-water calculation misses. Learn the main hazards.

## Wind and rolling

Strong wind on the side of a vessel creates a heeling force. Combined with rolling, it may exceed the righting lever. ==Synchronous rolling== (when the wave period matches the roll period) can build up large rolls. Reduce risk by changing course or speed to alter the encounter period.

## Following seas

In following or quartering seas, a vessel can lose stability when a ==wave crest is amidships== and the ends are in troughs, because the waterplane shape changes. Danger signs: poor steering, ==broaching==, ==pooping== (a wave breaking over the stern). Slow down or alter course to avoid matching speed with the waves.

## Water on deck

Water on deck adds weight high up, and as ==free surface== it flows to the low side. Water trapped behind bulwarks must drain through ==freeing ports== quickly. Keep them clear and working. Don't lash gear that blocks them.

## Ice accretion

Spray freezes on the vessel when the air is well below freezing and the wind is strong. The ice adds ==top weight==, raises G, reduces freeboard and increases windage. A small vessel can capsize quickly. Avoid freezing-spray areas, reduce speed to reduce spray, and ==remove ice== early with mallets or by other safe means. Never let ice build up beyond what the vessel was designed for.

## Gear and vessel modifications

Stability is affected by:

- ==Gear modifications==: heavier or higher gear, new winches, gantries, nets and blocks raise G.
- ==Hull or superstructure changes==: added deckhouses, extended shelters, bulwarks, or taking out freeing ports.
- ==Holds converted from dry to wet stowage== (such as refrigerated sea water): adds free surface.

A modification can reduce stability and may need a new stability assessment and approval.

## Inclining test

A known weight is moved across the deck and the resulting heel is measured. This gives GM, and then KG from KM:

> GM = (w × d) ÷ (Δ × tan θ)

It establishes the vessel's actual KG and is carried out by a qualified person.

## Rolling test

The vessel's natural ==roll period== is timed with the vessel afloat and not moored tight. A rough estimate of GM is:

> GM ≈ (C × B ÷ T)²

where B is the breadth, T is the roll period in seconds and C is a constant (roughly 0.7 to 0.8 for fishing vessels). It is only approximate. Understand the principle: a shorter period means a larger GM.

## A BC example

A skipper heading home down the west coast in a quartering sea notices the stern lifting and the boat yawing. He slows down and alters course 20 degrees to take the sea on the quarter at a different angle. The boat settles.

## Common mistakes

- Not knowing the freeing ports are blocked.
- Letting ice build up.
- Fitting a heavier gantry without checking stability.
- Racing ahead in a following sea.
