# The stability booklet, TPC and curves of statical stability

The booklet is your most important stability tool. It is open-book in the exam.

## What the booklet contains

- Vessel particulars and ==lightship== weight and KG.
- ==Hydrostatic data==: displacement, TPC, KM, trim moments for each draft.
- ==Tank data== with free surface moments.
- ==Pre-calculated loading conditions== (such as departure full, arrival with 10% fuel).
- ==Curves of statical stability== for each condition.
- ==Stability criteria== and instructions.

## Displacement from draft

Read the ==displacement scale== (or table) at the mean draft. The reverse also works: find the draft for a known displacement.

## TPC

> Sinkage (cm) = weight added (t) ÷ TPC

**Example.** TPC 0.95 t/cm. Load 10 t of fish.

```
Sinkage = 10 ÷ 0.95 = 10.5 cm
```

The imperial equivalent is TPI (tons per inch immersion).

## Fish loading limits

Work out how much fish you can carry while staying within the draft or freeboard limit and the stability criteria.

```
Load displacement          120 t
− Lightship                 70 t
− Fuel                      15 t
− Fresh water                3 t
− Crew, stores, gear         6 t
Maximum fish                26 t
```

Then check the stability for that loading condition.

## Pre-calculated conditions

Use the booklet's conditions to check adequate stability. If your loading ==matches or is safer== than a listed condition, you may rely on it. If not, you must ==recalculate== or ==change the loading==: reduce catch, lower weights, move fuel, or fill slack tanks. ==Recognise when your vessel does not meet the pre-calculated conditions== and fix it before sailing.

## Reading the curve

- ==Initial slope== = GM.
- ==Maximum GZ== and the ==angle== at which it occurs.
- ==Range of stability==: from upright to the angle where GZ becomes zero (angle of vanishing stability).
- ==Angle of deck edge immersion== and ==downflooding angle==: stability may fall quickly beyond them.
- ==Area under the curve== represents the energy needed to heel the vessel. Booklets set minimum areas.

To find the righting moment at an angle, read GZ and multiply by Δ. Example: GZ at 30° = 0.25 m, Δ = 80 t gives RM = 20 t·m.

## Criteria

The booklet states the criteria to meet (for example a minimum GM and minimum areas under the curve). Check your actual condition against them. Different vessels have different limits.

## When the vessel does not comply

Take action before sailing: reduce load, lower weights, fill or empty slack tanks, ballast low. Don't sail to find out.

## A BC example

At Port Hardy a seiner loads 24 t of fish. The booklet's maximum fish figure is 26 t for the planned fuel and ice, and the full-load GZ curve meets all criteria. The skipper presses up the fuel tank, closes the hatches, and sails.

## Common mistakes

- Using the lightship KM instead of the right displacement.
- Forgetting to include ice or gear in the loading.
- Assuming a loading close to the booklet is fine.
- Ignoring free surface moments.

## Check yourself

1. TPC is 0.8. Add 8 t. What is the sinkage?
2. What does the initial slope of the GZ curve show?
3. What do you do if your loading is not in the booklet?
