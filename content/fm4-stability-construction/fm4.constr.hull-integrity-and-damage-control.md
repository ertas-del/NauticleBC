# Hull integrity, bulkheads and damage control

A fishing vessel survives by keeping the sea out and, if the sea gets in, by controlling it.

## Weathertight and watertight integrity

Keep all openings in the hull and deck closed at sea, and keep seals and fittings in good condition. Check hatches, doors, windows, vents and valves regularly.

## Freeing ports and bulwarks

==Freeing ports== are openings in the bulwarks that let water on deck run out quickly. The ==minimum size and number== are set by regulations based on the length of the bulwark and how high it is. They must be kept ==clear==, and any flaps should open outward and not be locked shut. Bulwarks, rails and guards also protect crew from falling overboard.

## Doors, windows, ventilators and hatches

- ==Doors==: with sills of the required height, strong, weathertight and able to be closed from both sides.
- ==Windows and portholes==: strong glass and effective closure (deadlights) where needed.
- ==Ventilators and air pipes==: fitted with coamings and closing appliances to keep water out.
- ==Fish hold hatchways and scuttles==: strong covers with seals and securing devices.
- ==Sounding devices==: pipes or electronic gauges to measure tanks and bilges.

## Hull openings

Openings below the waterline need ==seacocks or valves== that can be shut, and ==stern gland or shaft seals== that are kept in good condition. Check hoses, clamps and fittings. Know where each seacock is.

## Bulkheads

==Watertight bulkheads== divide the hull into compartments so that damage to one does not flood the whole vessel. The ==collision bulkhead== is the forward one and should be intact and without openings below the freeboard deck. Bulkheads also give strength.

## Bilge and pumping

A ==bilge system== of pumps, pipes and suction strainers removes water. ==Water level detectors== and alarms in compartments (engine room, fish hold, steering flat) warn early. Test them regularly and keep suction lines clear.

## Damage control

- ==Small hole==: plug with wooden bungs, rags or a patch.
- ==Large breach==: use a collision mat or shore up a patch, slow the vessel, and heel the vessel if you can to lift the hole clear.
- ==Damaged through-hull fitting==: close the seacock, or plug it.
- ==Split pipe==: clamp or bind it, or isolate it.
- ==Chafed hose==: replace it or clamp it; close the seacock.
- ==Leaking gland==: tighten or repack the gland.
- Always ==start the pumps==, ==put on lifejackets==, and ==send a call for help== early if you can't control the flooding.

## A BC example

A skipper off Cape Scott hears the bilge alarm in the engine room. A hose to the raw water pump has chafed through and is spraying. He closes the seacock, starts the bilge pump, replaces the hose with a spare and clamps, and logs the repair. Total flooding is a few centimetres.

## Common mistakes

- Blocking freeing ports with gear.
- Not testing bilge alarms.
- Not knowing where the seacocks are.
- Leaving hatches open at sea.

## Check yourself

1. Why are freeing ports needed?
2. What is the collision bulkhead?
3. What do you do with a chafed hose?
