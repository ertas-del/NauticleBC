# Stability terms and the three kinds of equilibrium

Stability is the ability of a vessel to return upright after being heeled. SCS 1 begins with the words and the pictures. Get these clear and the rest is easy.

## Weights

- ==Displacement (Δ)==: the weight of the vessel with everything on board, equal to the weight of the water displaced.
- ==Lightship weight==: the empty vessel with no fuel, water, crew, stores, gear or catch.
- ==Deadweight==: the weight of everything added to the lightship: fuel, water, crew, stores, gear and catch.
- ==Load displacement==: the displacement when loaded to the maximum permitted draft.

> Displacement = lightship + deadweight.

## Points

- ==K==: the keel, the base line for heights.
- ==G (centre of gravity)==: the point where the whole weight acts. ==KG== is its height above the keel.
- ==B (centre of buoyancy)==: the centre of the underwater volume, where the upward force acts.
- ==M (metacentre)==: the point above G around which B swings for small angles of heel. ==KM== is its height above the keel.
- ==GM (metacentric height)==: M above G. ==GM = KM − KG==.
- ==F (centre of flotation)==: the centre of the waterplane. The vessel trims about F.

## The righting lever

When a vessel heels, B moves toward the low side. The weight (acting down through G) and the buoyancy (acting up through B) form a couple. The horizontal distance between them is the ==righting lever GZ==. If GZ is positive, it pushes the vessel back upright. For small angles ==GZ = GM × sin θ==.

## Equilibrium

- ==Stable==: M is above G, GM is positive. The vessel returns upright.
- ==Neutral==: M and G are at the same point, GM is zero. The vessel stays at whatever angle she is put.
- ==Unstable==: G is above M, GM is negative. The vessel heels further. She may settle at an ==angle of loll==.

## List, heel and loll

- ==Heel==: a temporary lean caused by an outside force such as wind or a turn.
- ==List==: a steady lean to one side caused by an uneven distribution of weight, with positive GM.
- ==Loll==: a lean caused by negative GM. It can flop suddenly to the other side.

## Stiff and tender

- ==Stiff==: large GM, quick return, short sharp roll, hard on gear and crew.
- ==Tender==: small GM, slow return, long lazy roll, dangerous.

The ==roll period== is an indication of initial stability: the shorter the period, the larger the GM.

## Freeboard and related terms

- ==Draft==: depth from the waterline to the keel.
- ==Freeboard==: height from the waterline to the deck edge.
- ==Reserve buoyancy==: watertight volume above the waterline.
- ==Deck edge immersion==: when the deck edge goes under water as the vessel heels.
- ==Downflooding==: water entering the vessel through an opening that can't be made weathertight.
- ==Trim==: the difference between forward and aft drafts.

## A BC example

A 15 m longliner is loaded with bait and ice and rolls slowly and lazily in a swell. The skipper recognises a tender vessel: GM is too small. He moves heavy gear lower and burns fuel from the high tank first, and the roll becomes quicker.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing list and loll.
- Treating displacement as volume.
- Believing a long slow roll is comfortable and therefore safe.
- Forgetting that G moves when weights move.

## Check yourself

1. What is GM?
2. What is the difference between list and loll?
3. What does a short roll period indicate?
