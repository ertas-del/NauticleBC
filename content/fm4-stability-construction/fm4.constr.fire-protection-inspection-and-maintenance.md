# Fire protection, inspection and maintenance

A well-maintained vessel is safer and easier to inspect. This lesson covers fire protection and the inspection regime.

## Fire protection

- ==Quick-closing valves== on fuel tanks can be shut quickly, often from outside the machinery space, to stop fuel feeding a fire.
- ==Fire dampers== close ventilation openings to cut off air to a space.
- ==Fire extinguishing systems==: fixed systems (such as CO₂ or clean agent) flood the space. Portable extinguishers handle small fires. People must leave a space before a fixed gas system is released.
- Know the location and operation of each, and test them as required.

## Normal maintenance

Keep the vessel in good order and in ==compliance== with standards:

- ==Hull== and ==superstructure==: check for corrosion, cracks, leaks and damage.
- ==Machinery==: follow servicing schedules.
- ==Safety equipment==: lifejackets, flares, extinguishers, liferaft, EPIRB: check dates and condition.
- ==Pumps, valves and alarms==: test regularly.
- ==Records==: keep a maintenance log.

## Corrosion and cathodic protection

Metal in sea water corrodes. ==Coatings== protect the surface, and ==sacrificial anodes== (zinc, aluminium or magnesium) corrode instead of the hull. Check them regularly and replace them when they are mostly gone. Don't paint over anodes. Keep dissimilar metals electrically isolated.

## The Transport Canada inspection regime

Transport Canada Marine Safety and Security uses several kinds of inspection:

- ==Construction and installation== inspection while the vessel is built or modified.
- ==Initial== inspection before the vessel enters service.
- ==Periodic== inspections at set intervals.
- ==Random== inspections.
- ==Annual self-inspection== by the owner or master.
- ==Targeted== inspections for particular concerns.

Be ready at all times: keep certificates, records and equipment in order.

## A BC example

Hauling out for the winter in Port Alberni, a skipper finds his zinc anodes are nearly gone. He replaces them and notes the date in the maintenance log, ready for the annual self-inspection.

## Common mistakes

- Painting over anodes.
- Not testing quick-closing valves.
- Skipping the annual self-inspection.
- Letting equipment dates lapse.
