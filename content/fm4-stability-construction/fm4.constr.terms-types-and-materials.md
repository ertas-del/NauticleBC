# Construction terms, hull materials and fishing vessel types

You need the language to describe a vessel and its parts. Examiners expect correct names.

## Dimensions

- ==Length overall (LOA)==: the extreme length from the forward end to the aft end.
- ==Length between perpendiculars (LBP)==: from the forward perpendicular (where the design waterline meets the stem) to the after perpendicular (usually the rudder stock).
- ==Breadth==: the width (extreme or moulded).
- ==Depth==: the vertical distance from the baseline to the deck at the side.
- ==Moulded dimensions==: measured to the inside of the shell plating or planking.
- ==Baseline==: the horizontal line from which heights are measured (usually the keel).

## Tonnage

- ==Gross tonnage (GT)==: a measure of the ==total internal volume== of the enclosed spaces. It is not weight.
- ==Net tonnage (NT)==: a measure of the volume of the ==cargo spaces== (such as fish holds).

These figures set which regulations apply.

## Open and closed construction

- ==Open==: has no continuous weathertight deck, so water can enter the hull spaces.
- ==Closed==: has a continuous weathertight deck with openings fitted with weathertight closures.

A vessel may be open, partly decked or fully decked.

## Weathertight and watertight

- ==Weathertight==: water will not penetrate into the vessel in any sea conditions.
- ==Watertight==: capable of preventing water passing through in any direction under the head of water likely to occur, as with bulkheads below the waterline.

## Principal structural members

==Keel==, ==stem==, ==stern post==, ==frames==, ==floors==, ==beams==, ==deck==, ==bulkheads==, ==shell plating or planking==, ==stringers==, ==knees==, ==bulwarks== and ==gunwale==. Know their names and what each does.

## Hull materials

- ==Wood==: traditional planking over frames (carvel or clinker). Easy to repair, but needs maintenance against rot and worm.
- ==Fibre-reinforced plastic (FRP)==: glass fibre in resin, single skin or sandwich. Low maintenance, but damage can delaminate and fire is a concern.
- ==Steel==: welded plate. Strong and durable but corrodes and needs coatings and ==cathodic protection== (anodes).
- ==Aluminium==: light and corrosion-resistant but sensitive to galvanic corrosion with other metals and needs careful welding.

## Fishing vessel types

==Trap vessels== (pots), ==stern and side trawlers==, ==seine vessels== (purse seiners), ==longliners==, ==gillnetters==, ==trollers==, ==dredgers== and others. Each has its own layout, deck gear and stability considerations.

## A BC example

A skipper buys an aluminium gillnetter built in 1998. He checks that zinc anodes are fitted and electrically isolated from steel fittings, because he knows aluminium and steel in sea water cause galvanic corrosion.

## Common mistakes

- Treating gross tonnage as weight.
- Mixing up weathertight and watertight.
- Using the wrong part names.
- Forgetting anodes on metal hulls.
