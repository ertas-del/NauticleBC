# Free surface effect and angle of loll

Free surface is one of the biggest causes of fishing vessel capsizes. A part-full tank or a pound of fish and water can take away your stability.

## What it is

When a tank is part full, the liquid moves to the low side as the vessel heels. The centre of gravity of the liquid shifts toward the low side too, which makes the vessel's overall G act as if it had ==risen==. The result is a ==virtual rise of G== and a ==loss of GM==. The effect depends on the tank's ==breadth, length and the density of the liquid==, not on how much liquid is in it, as long as it is slack.

## Calculating it

> Step 1: i = l × b³ ÷ 12  (m⁴), l is the length, b is the breadth across the vessel
> Step 2: Loss of GM = (i × ρ) ÷ Δ

**Example.** A diesel tank, 4 m long and 2.5 m wide. ρ = 0.85. Δ = 80 t. GM with the tank full = 0.60 m.

```
i = 4 × 2.5³ ÷ 12 = 4 × 15.625 ÷ 12 = 5.21 m⁴
Loss = 5.21 × 0.85 ÷ 80 = 0.055 m
Corrected GM = 0.60 − 0.055 = 0.545 m
```

**A flooded fish hold** of sea water (ρ = 1.025), 3 m long and 2 m wide: i = 3 × 8 ÷ 12 = 2.0, loss = 2.0 × 1.025 ÷ 80 = 0.026 m.

## Why width matters

The breadth is ==cubed==. A tank twice as wide has ==eight times== the free surface. A ==centreline division== halves the breadth, so each half has one-eighth, and the two halves together give ==one quarter== of the original.

## Reducing free surface

- Keep tanks ==full or empty== (press them up).
- Use as few ==slack tanks== as possible, and use them one at a time.
- Fit ==longitudinal divisions== or baffles.
- Keep deck clear of water and make sure ==freeing ports== work.
- Don't carry fish in a hold that has loose water, and make sure fish pounds are divided.
- Plan fuel and water use to keep free surface to a minimum.

## Angle of loll

If GM becomes negative, the vessel can't stay upright. She heels to an angle where the righting lever becomes zero again and sits there. This is the ==angle of loll==. Dangers:

- The vessel may ==flop== to the other side at about the same angle, especially in waves.
- Her stability at that angle is poor.
- Moving weight across the deck can make things worse.

## Correcting loll

- Lower G: fill ==low centreline tanks== if available.
- If wing tanks must be used, start with the ==low side== so the vessel doesn't flop over.
- Don't pump out slack tanks without thinking. It can increase free surface in other tanks.
- Remove top weight, such as ice or gear, where safe.
- Get expert advice and be ready to abandon. Seek help early.

## A BC example

A skipper notices his boat is lolling to port. Two fuel tanks are slack. He presses up a low centreline tank, closes the cross-connection, and clears water from the deck. The loll reduces, and he heads for shelter.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking free surface depends on how much liquid is in the tank.
- Ignoring slack tanks because they look small.
- Pumping the high side to correct loll.
- Confusing loll and list.
