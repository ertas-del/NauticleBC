# Stability in fishing operations and catch handling

Fishing changes the vessel's stability all the time. The master must think of it at every stage.

## Active and passive gear

- ==Active gear== is towed or moved through the water (trawls, seines, dredges, trolling). It applies forces to the vessel while towed.
- ==Passive gear== is set and left (traps, gillnets, longlines). Handling heavy traps and lines can move large weights.

## External forces from gear

When gear is ==hung up== on the sea bed or an obstruction, the pull on the warps can heel the vessel dangerously, especially if the lead is through a ==high point== such as a gantry or block high on the vessel. The heeling arm increases with height. Plan to:

- Know the leads and ==release arrangements== for warps.
- Reduce speed and release the gear immediately if the vessel heels dangerously.
- Keep towing points low and near the centreline.

## Moving weights

Adding, removing, ==transferring==, ==raising==, ==lowering== and ==suspending== weights changes draft, list and trim, and the free surface in tanks. Think before lifting a heavy catch, and allow for tank movements.

## Catch handling and stowage

- ==Division bulkheads== (pen boards) in fish holds are strong and keep fish from shifting. Use them properly and keep them in place.
- ==Fish carried in bulk== can shift, and loose water or slush adds free surface. Dividing the hold or using ==individual containers== is better.
- ==Fish on deck== is dangerous: it raises G, can shift, traps water on deck and blocks freeing ports.
- ==Overloading==: reduces freeboard and can cause excessive ==trim by the stern==, which stops freeing ports working and makes steering hard.

## Anti-rolling devices

- ==Paravane stabilisers== are towed fins that reduce rolling. They can foul gear or be lost, and can create heeling forces if one fails.
- ==Anti-rolling tanks== use moving water to counter roll. They add free surface effect if used wrongly, and need correct operation.

Understand their use, effect and risks.

## Change of stability during the voyage

- ==Fuel and water== are used, usually from low tanks, so G rises.
- ==Catch== is added, changing displacement and G.
- ==Ice== on deck adds top weight.
- ==Gear== wet and heavy adds top weight.
- ==Free surface== changes as tanks are used.

Check stability at departure, with the catch aboard, and on return.

## A BC example

A trawler out of Prince Rupert hangs her net on a rock. The warp leads through a block high on the gantry and she heels sharply. The skipper has rehearsed the release: the crew slip the winch brake, the warp runs, and the vessel returns upright.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving division boards out.
- Piling fish on deck.
- Forgetting the high lead of a warp.
- Not recalculating stability after loading.

## Check yourself

1. Why is a high lead for a warp dangerous?
2. Why use division bulkheads in a hold?
3. How does stability change as fuel is burned?
