# Fixing a position: bearings, distances and lat/long

Chart work for C/P 1 is practical: you plot positions, lay off courses and measure distances. These skills come up every day on deck.

## By two bearings

1. Take compass bearings of two marks at the same time.
2. Convert each to ==true== (apply deviation and variation).
3. From each mark, draw the ==reciprocal== of its true bearing (add or subtract 180°).
4. Where the lines cross is your position.

Choose marks with ==about 90 degrees== between the bearings. A small crossing angle gives a poor fix.

## By three bearings

Three lines rarely meet at a point. They form a small triangle called a ==cocked hat==. A small one means a good fix. A large one means error. Assume you are in the part of the triangle nearest danger.

## By bearing and distance

A radar or rangefinder gives a ==distance== to an object, plus a bearing. Plot the bearing from the object and measure the distance along it with dividers. Radar ==distances are more accurate than radar bearings==.

## By two or more distances

Draw an arc of the measured radius around each object (radius measured on the latitude scale). Your position is where the arcs cross. Three arcs give a check.

## Latitude and longitude

- To ==find== the position of a point, read its latitude from the side scale and its longitude from the top or bottom scale.
- To ==plot== a position, draw lines from the scales and mark the crossing.
- To locate a position by ==bearing and distance== from a known point, plot the bearing line and measure the distance.

## Courses and distances

- ==Lay off== a course by drawing a line between two positions.
- ==Measure the true direction== with a plotter or parallel rule against the compass rose, or against the nearest meridian.
- ==Measure the distance== with dividers on the latitude scale.
- Care of equipment: keep dividers sharp but not damaging, and don't press parallel rules too hard on the chart.

## A BC example

Off Cape Beale, a fisherman takes compass bearings of two marks: Cape Beale light 064° and Pachena Point 118°. Compass error is 5°E. He converts to true, plots two lines from the marks, and gets a fix with a crossing angle of about 54°. He adds a radar range as a check and is satisfied.

## Common mistakes

- Plotting the bearing from the boat instead of the mark.
- Forgetting to convert compass to true.
- Choosing marks nearly in line.
- Measuring distance on the wrong scale.

## Check yourself

1. What is the best crossing angle for two bearings?
2. What is a cocked hat?
3. Why are radar ranges better than radar bearings?
