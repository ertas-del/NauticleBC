# The log book, compass error and deviation

The compass is a master's main steering aid. You need to know how accurate it is and keep a record.

## The log book

Keep an accurate ==record== of the vessel's progress: times, positions, courses, speeds, weather, engine hours, fuel, events, and any compass checks. It supports navigation, is evidence after an incident, and shows safe practice.

## Care of instruments

Keep ==dividers== and ==parallel rules== clean and undamaged. A bent divider or a sloppy rule gives wrong positions. Store them where they can't roll or fall.

## Compass error by comparison

Compare the compass with a ==true bearing==:

- Take a compass bearing of an object, such as a transit or a distant mark.
- Read its ==true bearing== from the chart.
- ==Compass error = True − Compass==. Positive is East. Negative is West.

Example: compass 062°, true 071° → error +9° = 9° East.

## Deviation

Error is the sum of variation and deviation, with East positive and West negative.

> Error = Variation + Deviation

If the variation is 16°E and the error is 9°E, deviation = 9 − 16 = −7° = ==7° West==.

Record the deviation for the heading in a ==table of deviations==. Check on several headings and at least periodically.

## Correcting courses and bearings

- ==True to Compass==: apply variation then deviation. East −, West +.
- ==Compass to True==: the reverse. East +, West −. A memory phrase is ==Compass Add East== (CADET).

Use the table of deviations to find deviation for the heading you are steering.

## Gyro compass

If a gyro compass is fitted, compare it with the magnetic compass at intervals. The gyro has its own ==error== (True − Gyro), found against a true bearing.

## A BC example

Leaving Bamfield, a skipper lines up two marks with a charted true bearing of 125° and reads 118° on the compass. Error is +7° (7°E). Variation is 16°E, so deviation is −9° (9° W). He records it, and next time uses the table when he plots courses.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the direction of correction.
- Using an old variation.
- Never checking the compass.
- Forgetting to record it.

## Check yourself

1. True 120°, compass 113°. What is the error?
2. Variation 16°E, error 5°E. What is deviation?
