# Dead reckoning, set and drift, and course made good

Between fixes, you keep track by dead reckoning, then correct it for wind and current.

## Dead reckoning (DR)

1. Plot the last ==fix==.
2. Draw the ==true course== steered.
3. Distance = ==speed through the water × time==.
4. Mark the DR position and the time.

Keep the plot going. Mark a DR at least every hour and after any change of course or speed.

## Set, drift and leeway

- ==Set==: the direction the current flows toward.
- ==Drift==: the speed of the current in knots.
- ==Leeway==: the sideways slip caused by wind.

They push the vessel off its DR track. The vessel moves through the water on one track and the water moves over the ground.

## Estimated position (EP)

From the DR position, draw the current's push: direction = set, distance = drift × time. The new point is the ==estimated position==.

**Example.** Course 090°T, speed 8 knots, 2 hours. DR distance 16 NM along 090°. Current sets 180°T at 2 knots for 2 hours = 4 NM. Draw 4 NM toward 180° from the DR point. That is the EP.

## Course made good and speed over ground

Join the fix to the EP (or between two fixes).

In the example: 16 NM east and 4 NM south.

```
Course made good: tan θ = 4/16 → 14° south of east → 104°T
Distance made good: √(16² + 4²) = 16.5 NM
Speed over ground: 16.5 ÷ 2 = 8.25 knots
```

## Course to steer allowing for current

To make good a track of 090°T against a current setting 180°T at 2 knots with a speed through the water of 8 knots, steer a little into the current (north of the track).

```
sin(angle) = 2 ÷ 8 = 0.25 → angle 14.5°
Course to steer = 090° − 14.5° ≈ 076°T
Speed over ground ≈ 8 × cos 14.5° ≈ 7.7 knots
```

Check the chart plot with a current triangle: your course and speed, plus the current, gives the resultant.

## Judging speed over ground between fixes

Distance between two fixes ÷ time between them = speed over ground. Direction of the line joining them is the ==course made good==.

## A BC example

Running home to Port Alberni with the ebb setting you southwest at 2 knots, a skipper holds a compass course that is a little north of the track and checks every half hour that the bearing to the entrance isn't changing.

## Common mistakes

- Treating DR as certain. It drifts.
- Forgetting to allow for current over the full time.
- Using speed over ground when plotting a DR from the log speed.
- Not marking times on the plot.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between DR and EP?
2. A 2-knot current for 3 hours moves a boat how far?
3. How do you find speed over ground?
