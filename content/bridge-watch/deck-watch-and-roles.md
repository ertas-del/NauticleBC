# The deck watch: who is on it and who does what

**Study aid only.** The numbers here come from the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR) text. Confirm against the
current regulation and any exemption that applies to your vessel.

## What you need to know

The ==deck watch== is the team on the bridge that keeps the vessel safe while it is underway, at anchor or alongside.
The Canadian rules say who must be on it, what certificates they hold, and who is in charge.

The master of the vessel must make sure a deck watch is kept in line with ==STCW Code section A-VIII/2, Parts 2, 3
and 3-1== (MPR s. 213). The MPR then sets the minimum team by vessel size.

## The minimum deck watch (MPR s. 216)

| Vessel | Who must be on the deck watch |
|---|---|
| Under 5 GT | The person in charge of the deck watch |
| ==5 GT or more== | The person in charge, plus ==one additional person== |
| ==500 GT or more== | As above, and the additional person holds at least ==Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating== |
| ==More than 1,000 GT==, not securely anchored in port or moored to shore | As above, plus a ==second additional person== who holds at least Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating |

A **person in charge of the deck watch** (the officer of the watch, OOW) holds a ==certificate of competency that
authorizes watchkeeping and radio watch duties==. If the vessel has a VHF radio the OOW also needs the matching
radio operator certificate.

Two points trip people up:

- The **500 GT** line is about the certificate the additional person holds. From **5 GT** an additional person is
  needed at all; s. 216(2)(b) names Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating as the minimum only from 500 GT up. For
  smaller vessels, s. 214 still says every deck watch member needs a certificate suited to the vessel class, area and
  duties, so check which one applies.
- The ==rating under training== rule: the additional person and second additional person are **not both** required
  to hold the certificate if **one, but not both,** is a rating under training to obtain it (s. 216(4)).

## Where an additional person is not needed

The regulation lists exceptions (s. 216(3), (5)). Examples: a tug that is assisting another vessel while attached by a
tow line; a vessel up to 500 GT with an unobstructed all-round view from the steering position on a short harbour
voyage (not more than five nautical miles) in good visibility between sunrise and sunset; a vessel up to 1,000 GT that
is securely anchored in port or moored. Each exception has conditions, so do not apply it from memory in real life.

## Is the master part of the deck watch?

The master is on duty as the ordinary practice of seamen requires, but is ==not counted as a member of the deck
watch== unless one of these applies (MPR s. 215(2)):

1. the vessel is ==securely anchored in port or securely moored to shore==;
2. the vessel is ==1,000 GT or less==; or
3. the vessel is over 1,000 GT but under 3,000 GT and ==at least three deck watches== are established.

## Who does what on the bridge

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| **Master** | Overall command. Writes the standing orders and decides when to take over. Must be called when the watch officer has doubts (see the calling-the-master lesson). |
| **Officer of the watch** | In charge of the deck watch. Keeps the watch on the bridge, stays responsible for safe navigation until the master explicitly takes over, and does not leave the bridge until properly relieved. |
| **Rating (additional person)** | Keeps look-out, steers when ordered, reports what is seen and heard, understands and repeats orders. Carries out the orders given; does not take over the OOW's decisions. |

A short chain to remember: **master, then officer of the watch, then rating.** Information goes up the chain, and
orders come down it. A rating who sees a danger reports it at once; the OOW decides what to do.

## The sole look-out question

Normally a look-out is a person whose only job is looking. Transport Canada's ship safety bulletin, written after
tug and barge incidents, and the STCW principles allow the officer to be the ==sole look-out== only in ==daylight==,
and only after the officer has carefully assessed the situation, taking weather, visibility, traffic density,
closeness of dangers and any traffic separation scheme into account. The officer must also have
==help immediately available== on the bridge if things change. Because the allowance is for daylight only, it does not
cover night watches. The next lessons cover look-out duties in detail.

## On the bridge: a worked example

A 650 GT coastal freighter leaves Vancouver harbour on a clear morning and is not anchored.

1. 650 GT is over 5 GT, so an additional person is needed.
2. It is 500 GT or more, so that person must hold at least an ==Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating== certificate.
3. It is not over 1,000 GT, so a second additional person is not required by this section.
4. The master is not counted in the watch unless the vessel is 1,000 GT or less. It is 650 GT, so the master **can**
   be counted under s. 215(2)(b); the master may still decide to add more people to the watch if the practice of
   seamen requires it (s. 216(1)).

Now the same vessel, 1,400 GT, leaves in fog: a second additional person is required (over 1,000 GT, not anchored),
and the master is **not** counted in the watch unless three deck watches are kept and the vessel is under 3,000 GT.

## Exam traps

- **5, 500 and 1,000 GT** each mean something different: additional person, certified additional person, second
  additional person.
- Thinking the master always counts toward the watch. The conditions are the three above.
- Thinking the rating shares responsibility for navigation. The OOW remains responsible until the master takes over.
- Daylight only for sole look-out. "Fair weather at night" is not a reason.
