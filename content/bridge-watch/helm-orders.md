# Helm orders and replies

**Study aid only.** The wording here follows the IMO Standard Marine Communication Phrases (SMCP) as shown in a
secondary copy. Your ship, pilot or instructor may add phrases. Learn the standard ones first, then learn the habits
of the bridge you work on.

## What you need to know

A **helm order** is an instruction from the officer of the watch (OOW), the master or a pilot to the helmsman about
the rudder or the course. Three habits matter more than any single phrase:

1. The helmsman ==repeats every order word for word==.
2. The helmsman ==carries out the order and reports when it is done== (for example "Steady on one-two-five").
3. The officer listens to the reply and corrects it at once if it is wrong.

This closed loop exists because a wheel order is heard over engine noise, in a hurry, sometimes from a pilot you have
never met. A misheard "port" for "starboard" can put a ship aground. A repeat-back lets the giver catch the error in
seconds.

## The standard wheel orders

The SMCP list is short. Learn each phrase and its meaning.

| Order | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==Midships== | Rudder held in the fore-and-aft position (zero angle). |
| ==Port five== / ==Starboard five== | Hold 5 degrees of rudder to that side. |
| ==Port ten== ... ==Starboard twenty-five== | Hold 10, 15, 20 or 25 degrees to that side (the SMCP steps are 5, 10, 15, 20, 25). |
| ==Hard-a-port== / ==Hard-a-starboard== | Rudder held fully over to that side (the ship's limit, often 35 degrees). |
| ==Ease to== five, ten, fifteen, twenty | Reduce the rudder to the stated angle and hold it. |
| ==Meet her== | Check the swing of the ship's head in a turn (apply some opposite rudder). |
| ==Steady== | Reduce the swing as quickly as possible. |
| ==Steady as she goes== | Steer a steady course on the compass heading shown when the order is given. |
| ==Nothing to port== / ==Nothing to starboard== | Do not let the ship's head go to that side of the course. |
| ==Keep the buoy (mark, beacon) on port side== | Keep that mark on the stated side. |
| ==Report if she does not answer the wheel== | Tell the officer immediately if the ship is not responding. |
| ==Finished with wheel== | No more steering is needed. |

**Port and starboard, never left and right.** United States training material often says "right rudder" and "left
rudder". Canadian and IMO practice uses ==port and starboard==. Do not mix the two systems on one bridge: "right" can
be misheard, and a rudder order has no room for doubt. The hard-over angle is a property of the ship (many ships have 35 degrees); the
order only says "hard".

**"Steady" versus "Steady as she goes".** "Steady" is about stopping the swing now. "Steady as she goes" means hold the
heading you are on. The helmsman then reports the heading.

## Courses

Courses are said ==digit by digit==, for example "steer one-two-five" for 125 degrees, "steer zero-zero-five" for
005. This is standard bridge practice and is the SMCP style, but the secondary sources read for this lesson show it
only in an example, so confirm the wording with your instructor. When the ship is on the new course the helmsman
reports it: "Steady on one-two-five".

## Working language

The master decides and records the ==working language for navigation safety==, and the crew must understand it,
give orders in it and report back in it (Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020, s. 137(1)). **English** must be used
for bridge-to-bridge and bridge-to-shore safety communications, and between a pilot and the bridge watchkeepers,
unless the people involved share another language (s. 137(3)). Section 137 sits in the same part of the Regulations
as the steering sections, so the scope note in the lesson on steering systems applies here too: check which vessels
the section covers.

## A worked exchange

Invented ship, steering by compass, course 090. Read each line as OOW then helmsman.

| Step | OOW says | Helmsman replies | What the rudder and heading do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Port ten." | "Port ten, sir." | Rudder to 10 degrees port; the head starts to swing to port. |
| 2 | "Steer zero-six-zero." | "Steer zero-six-zero, sir." | Helmsman aims for 060. |
| 3 | (heading passes 075) | "Passing zero-seven-five, sir." | The helmsman reports progress. |
| 4 | "Meet her." | "Meet her, sir." | Helmsman puts on a little starboard rudder to check the swing. |
| 5 | "Steady." | "Steady, sir. Steady on zero-six-two." | Head is steadied; helmsman reports the heading he steadied on. |
| 6 | "Nothing to port." | "Nothing to port, sir." | Helmsman must not let the head fall off to port of 062. |
| 7 | "Midships." | "Midships, sir. Wheel is amidships." | Rudder to zero. |

Notice step 5: "Steady" stopped the swing at 062, not 060. The officer then decides whether that is close enough or
orders a small correction. The helmsman reports exactly what he did, not what he meant to do.

## A BC example

A small BC passenger vessel enters a narrow channel in a tidal stream. The OOW says "Starboard fifteen." The
helmsman repeats "Starboard fifteen, sir" and turns the wheel until the rudder angle indicator shows 15 degrees
starboard. The OOW, watching the shore marks, says "Ease to ten", then "Midships", then "Steady as she goes".
Every order has its own repeat-back, so a mishearing is caught before the ship has moved far.

## Exam traps

- **Repeating is not carrying out.** The helmsman must repeat the order **and** report when it is done. A
  silent helmsman is a hazard.
- **"Ease to ten" is not "Port ten".** "Ease to" reduces rudder you already have on; it does not choose a side.
- **Hard over is the ship's maximum**, not always 35 degrees.
- **"Steady" is not "Midships".** "Steady" stops the swing; "midships" centres the rudder. They may be given
  together, but they are different orders.
- **Port and starboard, not left and right.**
- **A helmsman who cannot get the ship to answer** says so at once ("Report if she does not answer the wheel").
