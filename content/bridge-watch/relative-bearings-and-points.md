# Relative bearings and points

**Study aid only.** The reporting methods below follow a public-domain U.S. Navy seamanship manual and general
seamanship. Canadian instructors may add phrases of their own, so follow their wording where it differs.

## What you need to know

A look-out must report **where** something is. Pointing and saying "over there" is no use to the officer. Two ways of
saying where are in everyday use on the bridge. Both are measured **from the ship's head**, which is the direction
the bow points, and not from north:

1. **Degrees.** A ==relative bearing== is measured ==clockwise from the ship's head== through 360 degrees.
2. **Points.** Older but still common: the compass circle is split into ==32 points==, and each point is
   ==11.25 degrees== (written 11¼°).

TP 10936, the Bridge Watch Rating course standard, asks for the look-out to report bearings "in degrees or points",
so you need both.

## Relative bearing in degrees

Picture the ship from above with the bow at the top. Count clockwise.

| Position | Relative bearing |
|---|---|
| ==Dead ahead== | ==000== |
| ==Starboard beam== (abeam to starboard) | ==090== |
| ==Dead astern== | ==180== |
| ==Port beam== (abeam to port) | ==270== |

The look-out normally reports relative bearings in degrees, ==usually to the nearest 10 degrees==. An object a little
to the right of the bow might be "bearing 020" or "020 relative". Saying the side in words ("starboard bow") as well
guards against mixing up 020 and 340.

## Points: from ahead, abeam and astern

One point is 360 ÷ 32 = **11.25 degrees**. These numbers are worth knowing:

| Points | Degrees |
|---|---|
| 1 | 11.25 |
| 2 | 22.5 |
| 3 | 33.75 |
| 4 | ==45== |
| 6 | 67.5 |
| 8 | ==90== |

So ==8 points = 90°== and ==4 points = 45°==. Points are counted from the nearest of **ahead**, **abeam** or **astern**,
and the **side** (port or starboard) is always stated, as in "two points on the starboard bow".

## Names for relative positions

| Name | Meaning | Starboard side | Port side |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==Dead ahead== | straight ahead | 000 | 000 |
| ==Fine on the bow== | a small angle off dead ahead | just over 000 | just under 360 |
| ==Broad on the bow== | 4 points (45°) off ahead | 045 | 315 |
| ==Abeam== | 8 points (90°) off ahead, at right angles to the ship | 090 | 270 |
| ==Broad on the quarter== | 4 points (45°) off dead astern | 135 | 225 |
| ==Fine on the quarter== | a small angle off dead astern | just under 180 | just over 180 |
| ==Dead astern== | straight astern | 180 | 180 |

"On the bow" covers everything forward of abeam. "On the quarter" covers the sector abaft the beam. "Abaft the beam"
means towards the stern from abeam, and "forward of the beam" means towards the bow.

## Reports in points and how to convert

A report may read "two points on the starboard bow" or "three points abaft the port beam".

**Worked example 1.** "Two points on the starboard bow."

1. Counted from ahead, 2 points = 2 × 11.25 = 22.5°.
2. Starboard is clockwise, so the relative bearing is **022½**, which you report as about 020 or 022.

**Worked example 2.** "Three points on the port bow."

1. 3 points = 3 × 11.25 = 33.75°.
2. Port is the other way round, so the relative bearing is 360 − 33.75 = **326.25**, about 326.

**Worked example 3.** "Two points abaft the starboard beam."

1. Starboard beam is 090. Abaft means towards the stern, so add: 90 + (2 × 11.25) = 90 + 22.5.
2. The relative bearing is **112½**, about 110.

**Worked example 4.** "Three points abaft the port beam."

1. Port beam is 270. Abaft means towards the stern (180), so subtract: 270 − 33.75.
2. The relative bearing is **236.25**, about 236.

Now in the other direction: a boat bears 045 relative. 045 ÷ 11.25 = 4 points from ahead, on the starboard side, so
it is **broad on the starboard bow**.

## Relative, true and compass bearings

- A ==relative bearing== is measured from the ship's head.
- A ==true bearing== is measured from true (geographic) north.
- A ==compass bearing== is measured from compass north on the ship's compass, and differs from true by the compass error
  (variation and deviation for a magnetic compass).

You do not need to do this arithmetic as a look-out, but you should know the difference. If you ever need to convert
by hand, the ship's heading links them: true bearing = true heading + relative bearing (subtract 360 if over 360).
For example, heading 080 true and an object at 045 relative gives 125 true. With heading 350 true and the object at 100
relative, 350 + 100 = 450, minus 360 = 090 true. Remember that the **relative** bearing changes whenever the ship turns,
even though the object has not moved.

## How to word a bearing report

- Say **what** first, then **where**: "Fishing boat, two points on the starboard bow."
- Say the side in words: "starboard" or "port", then the points or degrees.
- Keep to the same system the officer uses. If they ask for degrees, give degrees.
- Repeat the report if the officer does not acknowledge it.

## Exam traps

- **Point = 11.25°, not 10°.** Rounding a point to 10 loses accuracy as you add more points.
- **Port side:** a port bearing counts up towards 360, so "two points on the port bow" is **337½**, not 022½.
- **Abeam = 8 points = 90°**, not 4. Four points is **broad on the bow or quarter**.
- A **relative** bearing is not a **true** bearing. When the ship turns, the relative bearing changes.
- "Abaft the beam" is **behind** the beam, "forward of the beam" is **ahead** of it.

## Check yourself

1. What is the relative bearing of an object dead astern? On the port beam?
2. How many degrees are 5 points?
3. "Three points on the port bow": give the relative bearing.
4. An object is at 135 relative. What is the common name for that position?
5. Heading 200 true, a vessel is at 060 relative. What is its true bearing?
