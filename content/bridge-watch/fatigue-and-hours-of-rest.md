# Fatigue and hours of rest

**Study aid only.** The hours of rest rules below are paraphrased from the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR), ss. 319 to
324. Collective agreements may set equal or better rules, and the regulation has exceptions. Check the live text.

## What you need to know

A tired watchkeeper sees less, reacts more slowly and is more likely to make a bad decision. Transport Canada defines
==fatigue== as reduced mental or physical ability from sleep-related factors, and the Transportation Safety Board has had
fatigue management in the marine sector on its ==Watchlist since 2018==.

Typical causes at sea (Transport Canada): poor-quality sleep, bad sleeping conditions (noise, light, vibration, poor
ventilation, vessel motion), rotating shifts, disrupted body clock and stress or heavy workload.

The law answers with minimum rest. ==Hours of rest== means ==rest time outside hours of work and does not include short
breaks==. A coffee break does not count.

## Which rule applies to which voyage?

| Voyage | Rule | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Canadian vessel on a ==sheltered waters== voyage, or a ==near coastal voyage (Class 1 or 2)== in other waters than those of a foreign state that has ratified the Maritime Labour Convention | ==6 consecutive hours in 24 and 16 in 48== | s. 320 |
| Canadian vessel on ==unlimited voyages==, or on near coastal voyages in the waters of a ratifying foreign state; ==foreign vessels in Canadian waters== | ==14/72 or 10/77== | s. 321 |

The rules cover the master and every crew member.

## Domestic voyages: s. 320

The master must make sure the master and every crew member has:

- at least ==6 consecutive hours of rest== in every 24-hour period; and
- at least ==16 hours of rest== in every 48-hour period; and
- ==not more than 18 hours but not less than 6 hours== between the end of one rest period and the start of the next.

## Unlimited voyages and foreign waters: s. 321

Either option satisfies the rule:

- **Work limit:** not more than ==14 hours of work in any 24-hour period== and not more than ==72 hours in any 7-day period==; or
- **Rest minimum:** at least ==10 hours of rest in any 24-hour period== and at least ==77 hours in any 7-day period==.

In addition, the rest hours must be ==divided into no more than two periods==, one of them ==at least 6 hours== long, and
the ==interval between two rest periods must not exceed 14 hours==.

## Other rules that matter

- **Fatigue must be considered (s. 322(1)).** When setting hours, the master must account for the danger of fatigue, especially for people whose
  duties involve ==navigational safety== and safe operation.
- **Collective agreements.** The master may follow an agreement that is no less favourable to the crew.
- **Drills.** Rest rules do not apply during practice musters, fire-fighting drills or survival craft drills, if they are
  done so as to minimise disturbance of rest and not cause fatigue.
- **On call.** Rules do not apply to a crew member on call if compensatory rest is given when the rest is disturbed.
- **Emergency.** The master may ==suspend the schedule== for the immediate safety of the vessel, people or cargo, or to help
  vessels or persons in distress. As soon as feasible, anyone who worked in a scheduled rest period gets
  ==compensatory rest==.
- **Records (s. 323).** The master must ==keep a record of every crew member's daily hours of work or rest== until the person is
  discharged.
- **Posted table (s. 324, for the s. 321 voyages).** A table of shipboard working arrangements is posted in a conspicuous place, in the
  working language of the vessel and in English.

## Fatigue training for seafarers

Transport Canada Ship Safety Bulletin 01/2026 says all Canadian seafarers enrolling in Marine Emergency Duties courses must complete the free
==Fatigue Management at Sea== e-course from the Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety. The completion certificate
must be ==produced before the MED training certificate is issued==. Transport Canada says the course certificate does not
expire.

## What fatigue looks like and what helps

Transport Canada's page does not list warning signs, so ask your instructor which signs your fatigue course teaches. What
helps is simple: protected, quality sleep, schedules that allow rest, and telling the officer when you are too tired to keep a
safe look-out or steer. Do not just push on.

## Worked example 1: a 6-on, 6-off schedule on a coastal vessel (s. 320)

A crew member on a near coastal voyage in Canadian waters works 0600-1200 and 1800-2400 every day.

1. Rest periods: 1200-1800 (6 hours) and 0000-0600 (6 hours).
2. Rest per 24 hours = 6 + 6 = **12 hours**. Is there at least **6 consecutive**? Yes, each rest period is 6 hours.
3. Rest per 48 hours = 2 × 12 = **24 hours**, which is at least 16. Yes.
4. Time between the end of one rest and the start of the next: 0600 to 1200 = 6 hours and 1800 to 2400 = 6 hours, both
   between 6 and 18. Yes.

The schedule meets s. 320.

## Worked example 2: a schedule that fails

Another crew member works 0600-1800 and 2000-0200 (the next day).

1. Rest periods: 1800-2000 (2 hours) and 0200-0600 (4 hours). Total rest in 24 hours = 6 hours.
2. s. 320 needs **6 consecutive hours**. The longest rest here is only **4 hours**, so it fails.
3. Under s. 321, 24 − 6 = 18 hours of work exceeds 14 in 24, and 6 rest hours is below 10, so it fails there too.

## Worked example 3: compare the 7-day options (s. 321)

- Work limit: 72 hours of work in 7 days. A week has 168 hours, so that leaves 168 − 72 = **96** hours not worked.
- Rest minimum: 77 hours of rest in 7 days leaves up to 168 − 77 = **91** hours that could be worked.

The two are different tests. A schedule needs to meet only one of them (and the daily test and the rest-division rules).

## Exam traps

- **6 consecutive** is in the domestic rule (s. 320). The **two periods, one of at least 6 hours** wording is in the
  unlimited/foreign rule (s. 321).
- **16 in 48** (domestic) vs **77 in 7 days** (s. 321): do not mix them.
- **14 and 72** are limits on **work**. **10 and 77** are minimums of **rest**.
- Short breaks **do not count** as hours of rest.
- Drills and emergencies are exceptions, and **compensatory rest** follows an emergency.
- Records are of **work or rest**, and are kept until the crew member is discharged.
