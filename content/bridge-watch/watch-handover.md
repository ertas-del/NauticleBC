# Handing over and taking over the watch

**Study aid only.** The handover rules are in the STCW Code, section A-VIII/2, which Canada applies through the Marine
Personnel Regulations (s. 213). The wording below is paraphrased from a government reproduction of that text.

## What you need to know

A watch change is a risky moment. The person who knew the situation is leaving and the person who must know it has
just arrived. TP 10936 lists "relief, maintenance and handover of the watch" and "information required to maintain a
safe watch" as things a rating must understand, ==at sea, at anchor and in port==.

## When a watch must not be handed over

1. **The relief is not fit.** The officer in charge ==must not hand over== the watch if there is reason to believe the
   relieving officer is ==not capable of carrying out the watchkeeping duties effectively==. In that case the
   ==master must be notified==. Illness or heavy tiredness could be reasons.
2. **A manoeuvre is under way.** If a ==manoeuvre or other action to avoid any hazard== is taking place, the relief is
   ==deferred until the action has been completed==. The person who knows what is happening should finish the job.

The same idea applies to a rating at the wheel or on look-out: you do not hand over to someone who is not fit, and
you do not leave a critical moment, such as a close crossing, for someone who has just arrived.

## The relieving officer's duty before taking over

The officer coming on watch must:

- make sure the relieving watch members are ==fully capable== of their duties, ==particularly as regards adjustment to
  night vision==, and ==not take over until their vision is fully adjusted== to the light conditions;
- check the ship's ==estimated or true position== and confirm the intended ==track, course and speed==;
- note any ==dangers to navigation== expected during the watch.

On top of that, the relieving officer checks the ==master's standing orders==; the ship's position, course, speed and
==draught==; the ==tides, currents, weather and visibility== and their effect on the course and speed; the ==engine
manoeuvring procedures==; the ==operational condition of the navigational and safety equipment==; the ==compass errors==;
the movements of ==other ships==; and conditions and hazards likely to be met during the watch.

The watch officer who is leaving ==stays on the bridge until properly relieved==.

## The rating's part: look-out and helm

For a look-out the handover is shorter, but it follows the same idea. You tell the relief what is around: vessels
already seen, aids to navigation passed, expected hazards, fog banks, and what the officer has said to watch for.
Allow time for the relief's eyes to adjust to the dark at night before you leave your post (see the look-out lesson).

At the wheel, the outgoing helmsman passes to the relief:

- the ==course being steered==;
- the ==compass or repeater in use== (for example, the gyro repeater or the magnetic compass);
- any ==steering peculiarities== such as the ship pulling to one side or a lag in the response;
- special instructions from the officer, and any mark being steered on.

The relief ==repeats the course back==. Then the officer is told that the helm has changed hands and the relief
reports "Steering 245, gyro." It is not complete until the relief has the wheel and the course in mind.

**Worked example.** You are on the afternoon watch steering by gyro.

1. At 1555 the relief arrives. The officer is in the middle of altering course to clear a fishing boat, so the
   relief is **deferred until the alteration is complete** at 1558.
2. At 1558 you say: "Course 245 gyro, magnetic standby compass reads 252 on this heading, ship pulls slightly to port in the
   swell." The relief repeats: "245 gyro, 252 magnetic, pulls to port." The officer confirms.
3. You report the relief to the officer, and the officer acknowledges and notes the time.

(The 252 magnetic reading is an invented figure; real compass differences come from the compass errors on your ship.)

## What a handover covers

| Area | Examples of what is passed on |
|---|---|
| Position and track | Where the ship is, planned route, next alteration point |
| Course and speed | Current values, compass in use |
| Traffic | Vessels in sight, and any on a steady bearing |
| Navigation aids | Buoys and lights passed or ahead, reported or expected |
| Weather and visibility | Present conditions, forecast, any fog |
| Equipment | Anything not working, such as a radar range or alarm |
| Orders | Master's standing orders, special orders and anything the master asked to be called about |

At **anchor** and **in port**, add: how the vessel is lying, which lights and shapes are shown, moorings or anchor
cable condition, any work or visitors, and tide and weather expected.

## Exam traps

- **Do not hand over when the relief is unfit**; tell the master.
- **Do not hand over during a manoeuvre.** Finish it first.
- Eyes first: the **relief's night vision** needs to be adjusted before taking over a night watch.
- At the wheel, the relief **repeats** the course back.
- Handover applies **at sea, at anchor and in port**, not only underway.
