# The Bridge Watch Rating certificate pathway

**Study aid only.** The Marine Personnel Regulations change, and Transport Canada examiners decide what they accept.
Check the current regulation and your training provider before you apply.

## What you need to know

A ==Bridge Watch Rating== is a certificate of competency for a **rating**: a crew member who is not an officer. On
the bridge you are the extra pair of eyes and the person at the wheel. You are not in charge of the watch (see
the next lesson). It sits beside the Able Seafarer and Engine-room Rating certificates in the Marine Personnel
Regulations (MPR).

The regulation names the requirements in one table (MPR s. 171). Keep it in three parts: **experience**,
**documents**, **exam**.

## The requirements, one by one

**Age.** You must be ==at least 18 on the day you receive the certificate==. You may start earning the sea service
earlier, but it only counts if you ==started it at age 16 or older==.

**Sea service.** You need sea service ==performing bridge watchkeeping duties==:

- ==at least 6 months==, or
- ==at least 2 months== if you have completed an ==approved training program in bridge watchkeeping duties==.

**Documents.** The examiner must be given four things:

1. ==MED, STCW basic safety== (Marine Emergency Duties training).
2. ==MED, proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats other than fast rescue boats==.
3. A ==steering testimonial==.
4. ==Marine basic first aid==.

**Exam.** A ==written examination on general seamanship==, written after the other requirements are met.

## The steering testimonial

The testimonial is a letter about your steering. It records the vessel (name, port of registry, official number,
type, cargo, gross tonnage, voyage classification), when you signed on and off, and your rank. The ==master== signs
a declaration in the regulation's wording: you stood regular watches at the wheel for a total of some hours, and the
master is satisfied you are a ==competent wheelsman==.

The regulation leaves the number of hours **blank**. Some providers ask for about 10 hours, but that is provider
practice, not law. Ask your provider and examiner what they expect.

## Medical, application and exam rules

- A ==medical certificate== is needed before the certificate is issued. The Bridge Watch Rating is not on the list of
  certificates exempted from the ==colour vision== standard, so the standard applies (MPR ss. 111 and 270).
- Give your application and papers to the examiner ==at least two weeks before the first exam==. The application
  ==stays valid for one year== (MPR s. 110).
- A pass in the exam ==stays valid for 5 years==. Once you have the certificate, the pass is valid ==for life==
  (MPR s. 113).
- Transport Canada requires every Canadian seafarer taking MED courses to complete the free ==Fatigue Management at
  Sea== e-course. The completion certificate must be produced before the MED certificate is issued.

## The approved course

The training standard is ==TP 10936== (Edition 2). It is ==300 hours==, built from ten competences, and includes the
STCW rating-forming-part-of-a-navigational-watch training. Keeping a look-out by sight and hearing gets ==40 hours==
of the 300. The biggest single block is deck equipment and machinery at 65 hours.

An approved program like this is what lets you use the shorter 2-month sea service route instead of 6 months.

## Worked example: counting your path

Sam is 17 and starts a 17-week approved Bridge Watch Rating course in October. A training course is not yet sea
service, so nothing has started under MPR s. 110 yet.

1. Sam finishes the course at 17 and takes the MED and first aid courses (plus the Fatigue Management e-course).
2. At 17 Sam signs on a tug for bridge watchkeeping duties. That is after age 16, so the sea service counts.
3. With an approved program completed, Sam needs ==2 months== of that service, not 6.
4. The master signs the steering testimonial.
5. Sam still cannot **receive** the certificate until the day Sam turns 18, even with everything else done.

## Open questions: check before you rely on them

These are unresolved in our sources. Do not memorise a single answer for the exam until your instructor confirms.

- **Pass mark.** Provider pages mention 60% for the written exam in TP 10936, and 70% for course marks.
  Transport Canada's own exam pass mark was not confirmed.
- **Is the course exam the exam?** The regulation says "written examination on general seamanship". Whether graduates
  write a separate Transport Canada exam after the course varies in what providers say.
- **Steering hours** (see above) and how long a **medical** stays valid.
- **60 days or 6 months.** Some providers describe different certificates for each. The regulation text we read does not.

## Exam traps

- **16 or 18?** 16 is when sea service may **start**. 18 is the age on the day you **receive** the certificate.
- Confusing the first aid and MED lists: the **fast rescue boat** course is not required; the one required is
  survival craft and rescue boats **other than** fast rescue boats.
- Thinking the exam pass is lost after 5 years once you hold the certificate. After the certificate is obtained, the
  pass is valid for life.
- The master signs the steering testimonial, not an instructor or the mate.

## Check yourself

1. At what age may your sea service start, and at what age may you receive the certificate?
2. How much sea service is needed with and without an approved training program?
3. Name the four documents the examiner must receive.
4. Who signs the steering testimonial, and what does the regulation say about the number of hours?
5. How long is a passing grade valid before and after you hold the certificate?
