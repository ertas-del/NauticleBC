# Highs, lows and fronts

**Study aid only.** It is not a weather forecast and not a substitute for the Environment Canada marine forecast.

## What you need to know

Weather on the BC coast is driven by big pressure patterns that move west to east off the Pacific. Learn three shapes and what each does to the wind and sky and you can read the forecast: **lows** (storms), **highs** (fair, light winds) and **fronts** (the edges between air masses). The technical marine synopsis in each forecast tells you where they are.

## Highs and lows

Air moves from high pressure toward low pressure but the earth's spin turns it. In the ==Northern Hemisphere== the wind goes:

- ==counterclockwise and inward around a low==, where the air rises, cools, forms cloud and rain;
- ==clockwise and outward around a high==, where the air sinks, dries and clears.

So a ==low== means ==cloud, precipitation, fronts and strong wind==, and a ==high== means ==light wind, clear skies and fair weather==. Near the centre of a high the wind can be light and unreliable. Near the centre of a low it is strong and the waves build. Between a low and a high the weather is often moderate.

### Isobars and wind

On a weather map, ==isobars== join points of equal pressure. Wind blows nearly parallel to them, crossing slightly toward the low because of friction at the surface, and in the Northern Hemisphere the low is on the ==left of the wind==. ==Isobars close together== mean ==strong wind==; far apart means light wind. Map labels are shortened: add a decimal before the last digit and put 10 or 9 in front so the value is near 100 kPa (a label of 24 is 102.4 kPa, 92 is 99.2 kPa).

Average pressure is about ==101.3 kPa==. Highs are near ==103 kPa== and lows near ==100 kPa==. Older units are still in use: ==10 mb = 10 hPa = 1 kPa==.

### Buys Ballot's law

Stand with your ==back to the wind==. In the Northern Hemisphere the ==low is on your left== and the high on your right. It was stated by C. H. D. Buys Ballot in 1857. Surface friction bends the wind toward the low, so it is a rough guide to where the low lies, not an exact bearing, and it does not work near the equator.

### The barometer

A ==falling barometer== means a ==low or a front is coming==, with worse weather. A ==rising barometer== means a high is approaching, with fair weather and lighter wind. Read it together with the forecast and the sky.

## Fronts

A front is the edge of an air mass. Weather changes when one passes. On a map, a cold front is drawn with triangles, a warm front with semicircles, and an occluded front with both on the same side.

| Front | Typical weather |
|---|---|
| ==Cold== | Showers, thunderstorms and squall lines along or ahead of it; colder air behind; wind ==veers== (southwest to northwest in the Northern Hemisphere) as it passes; pressure falls, then rises sharply |
| ==Warm== | Broad cloud, drizzle and light rain ==ahead== of it on the cold side; warmer air behind |
| ==Occluded== | Forms near a low where a cold front overtakes a warm front; weather like both |
| Stationary | Moves little; weather like a warm front |

A cold front moves faster than a warm front and tends to bring the sharper change. A squall line, a long narrow chain of thunderstorms, can sit along or ahead of a cold front and move across the water faster than the front itself. Such lines can be thousands of kilometres long but only about 10 to 15 km wide, so the worst of it passes quickly.

## The BC coast

In winter, a new low tends to arrive ==every 1 to 4 days==. Many form far out in the Pacific, over the warm Kuroshio current, and are ==occluded and weakening== by the time they reach the Gulf of Alaska and BC. They bring widespread overcast, rain and, ahead of an occluding low, a stream of fast, humid air called an ==atmospheric river== that can bring heavy rain and flooding. Local effects on top of these patterns are covered in the lessons that follow.

As a rule, forecasts lose skill with time. A UBC course author warns against trusting a winter Pacific forecast beyond a day or two, and the Environment Canada forecast reflects this: the detailed part covers two days.

## A BC example

At 1000 your barometer reads 100.8 kPa and has fallen 0.6 kPa since breakfast. The 0430 synopsis put a low west of Vancouver Island moving northeast, with a warm front ahead. Winds on West Coast Vancouver Island are already southeast and rising. Back to the wind, the low is on your left: to the west and southwest. Plan for the wind to increase and veer southwest or west after the cold front passes, with squalls and showers behind it. Seek shelter before the front, or choose a time to cross when it has passed.

## Common mistakes

- Using the clockwise rule for lows. Lows are counterclockwise in the north.
- Thinking Buys Ballot gives an exact bearing to the low.
- Forgetting that the front's squalls can be sharp but short.
- Treating a rising barometer as always calm; the wind can be strong right behind a cold front.
- Reading isobar numbers as hPa without the leading 10 or 9.
