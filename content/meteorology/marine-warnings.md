# Marine warnings

**Study aid only.** Warning rules and dates change. Take the current ones from Environment Canada and the RAMN.

## What you need to know

A warning is the forecaster's way of saying "this is serious enough to change your plan." Learn the four wind levels with their numbers, then the few non-wind warnings that matter on the BC coast: freezing spray, squalls and waterspouts.

## Wind warnings

Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) issues four categories, based on the ==mean wind speed, excluding gusts==:

| Warning | Knots |
|---|---|
| ==Strong wind== | ==20 to 33== |
| ==Gale== | ==34 to 47== |
| ==Storm== | ==48 to 63== |
| ==Hurricane force wind== | ==64 or greater== |

A hurricane force wind warning ==does not mean a hurricane== is coming. It means wind of comparable speed, often from a deep winter low.

Warnings often cover several marine areas at once and are issued with as much notice as possible.

### Strong wind warnings are seasonal

Strong wind warnings are issued ==mainly for coastal and inland waters in the boating season==. On the Pacific they are issued from ==March 20 to Remembrance Day== (November 11) for six inner waters: ==Juan de Fuca Strait, Haro Strait, Howe Sound, Strait of Georgia, Johnstone Strait and Queen Charlotte Strait==. Gale, storm and hurricane force warnings have no such limit and can cover any marine area with a forecast, including the offshore areas. So a winter forecast for the Strait of Georgia with 25 knots and no warning is not a calm forecast; it is simply outside the strong-wind season.

## Freezing spray

Freezing spray happens when breaking waves throw spray into very cold air and it freezes on the vessel. ECCC warns when ice is expected to build up at ==0.7 cm per hour or more==. In the forecast guide, ==moderate is 0.7 to 2 cm/h== and ==severe is above 2 cm/h==. Salt water spray needs air colder than about ==minus 2 C== to freeze. Ice adds weight high up and raises the centre of gravity, which can make a vessel ride lower and capsize. The Canada Shipping Act, 2001 s. 112 lists subfreezing air with gale-force winds and severe icing as something the master must report. See the icing lesson in stability.

## Localized warnings and watches

These cover hazards that need immediate attention and are not part of the wind scale:

- ==Squall warning==: gusts above 34 knots tied to a squall line, a line of storm clouds.
- ==Waterspout warning== (and tornado over water), when radar or observers see one.
- ==Special marine warning== for other hazards.

A **watch** is advance notice that the hazard is possible later in the day. A **warning** says it is occurring or expected. There are squall, waterspout, tornado and special marine watches. A ==marine weather statement== describes hazardous conditions in plain language between scheduled forecasts. A ==marine weather advisory== covers conditions below alert level that may still be a hazard or an inconvenience.

Map colours: ==red== for a warning, ==yellow== for a watch, ==grey== for an advisory.

## Where warnings appear

In the regular forecast the warning is a headline ==immediately after the area name, before the forecast text==, using the keyword ==in effect==. In ECCC's online marine forecast, regions with warnings in effect show in red and you click through to the detail. Wind warnings on the BC coast state whether they are tied to an ==outflow== or an ==inflow==, a clue to where the wind will be strongest (next lessons). Ice warnings are issued by the ==Canadian Ice Service==.

## What you do with a warning

The warning does not decide for you. It tells you the category. You compare it with your vessel's limits and the route (see the go/no-go lesson). A gale warning with a short run to shelter is a different problem from the same warning on an open crossing. If you meet force 10 or more without a storm warning, you must report it.

## A BC example

It is 22 October. The 0430 forecast headline reads "Gale warning in effect" for Johnstone Strait with "wind southeast 30 to 40 knots" (invented). The strong wind season is on until 11 November, but this is a gale, which has no season. Thirty to 40 knots straddles force 7 and 8. The master looks at where the nearest shelter is, how long the transit takes, and when the forecast says the wind falls.

## Common mistakes

- Treating gusts as the warning threshold. Warnings use the mean wind.
- Assuming no warning means light wind outside the strong-wind season.
- Thinking hurricane force means a hurricane.
- Mixing up watch and warning.
- Remembering the 0.7 cm/h threshold but forgetting that salt water needs about minus 2 C.
- Looking for a strong wind warning in the offshore areas, where they are not issued.
