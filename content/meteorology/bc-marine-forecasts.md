# Reading a BC marine forecast

**Study aid only. The practice forecast below is invented.** Real decisions use the current Environment Canada product.

## What you need to know

The forecast is the first thing a master reads and the thing an examiner will ask you to explain. You need to know the products and times, the area names, the layout, and the small rules of wording that change the meaning of a line.

## Products and times

The Pacific Storm Prediction Centre in Vancouver issues:

| Product | What it covers | Issued (local) |
|---|---|---|
| ==Regular marine forecast== | Days 1 and 2: warnings, wind, weather and visibility | ==04:00, 10:30, 16:00, 21:30== |
| ==Technical marine synopsis== | Position and movement of main lows, highs and fronts | ==04:00, 10:30, 16:00, 21:30== |
| ==Extended forecast== | Days 3 to 5, ==wind only== | ==04:00, 16:00== |
| ==Wave height forecast== | Significant wave height in metres | ==04:00, 16:00== |
| ==Marine weather statement== | Hazardous conditions in plain language | As needed |
| NAVTEX forecast | Short forecast on NAVTEX | Same times as regular |

Updates come out when the weather departs from the forecast by set amounts. The forecast text is valid to midnight of the following day.

## The areas

The Pacific coast is split into named areas. The ones to know: ==Explorer== and ==Bowie== (offshore); ==Dixon Entrance==, ==West Coast Haida Gwaii==, ==Queen Charlotte Sound==, ==Hecate Strait==, ==Douglas Channel== and the ==Central Coast== (north); and ==West Coast Vancouver Island North and South==, ==Juan de Fuca Strait==, ==Haro Strait==, ==Howe Sound==, ==Strait of Georgia==, ==Johnstone Strait== and ==Queen Charlotte Strait== (south). Some areas are split further, such as the Strait of Georgia north and south of Nanaimo, so check the area your route crosses.

## Layout and wording

A regular forecast runs in this order: title block (issue time and next issue), a ==fog statement==, the ==area name==, ==warnings or watches==, then ==wind==, ==weather and visibility==, ==freezing spray== and ==air temperature==, each only when it applies.

Rules for reading the words:

- **Wind**: knots, mean at 10 m, one of eight points, where the wind blows ==from==, true north. Gusts are added when the mean will rise 10 knots or more briefly.
- **Fog**: the fixed line says ==fog implies visibility less than 1 mile==. Weather is stated only when visibility is ==1 nautical mile or less==.
- **Air temperature** appears only when ==zero or below==.
- **Seas** are in ==metres== and combine wind waves and swell. Near shore they can differ.
- **Time terms**: today, to 5:59 p.m.; tonight, 6:00 p.m. to 5:59 a.m.; morning, 6:00 to 11:59 a.m.; afternoon, noon to 5:59 p.m.; evening, 6:00 to 11:59 p.m.; overnight, midnight to 5:59 a.m.
- **No repeat**: if wind is given once and nothing later changes, it ==persists== for the rest of the period.
- **Significant change** means 10 knots or more, more than 45 degrees in direction when the speed is over 10 knots, or a change in warning category.

==Significant wave height== is the average of the highest third of the waves; the maximum wave may be up to twice that.

## Getting it on the water

- ==Continuous Marine Broadcast== on VHF (Channel 21B on the BC coast, also WX channels): the Coast Guard reads the forecast on a loop for all areas, updating at the four forecast times. Wait for your area in the loop.
- ==NAVTEX== (518 kHz): Prince Rupert MCTS sends from Amphitrite Point and Digby Island.
- ==Web and app==: weather.gc.ca marine pages and the WeatherCAN app.
- ==Phone==: the forecast consultation service for marine users.

The RAMN lists Weatheradio transmitters and a phone service, while an older ECCC page says they are out of service. Do not depend on either; the RAMN also warns that the internet is not part of the maritime safety information system, so have a radio source.

## Worked reading (invented forecast)

"Strait of Georgia - north of Nanaimo. Gale warning in effect. Wind southeast 25 to 35 knots becoming northwest 15 to 25 late this evening. Showers. Seas 2 to 3 metres. Fog implies visibility less than 1 mile."

1. A ==gale warning== is in effect, so expect mean wind of 34 knots or more at some point. The range 25 to 35 reaches that.
2. The wind is ==from the southeast== now and turns to ==northwest by late evening== (after 9:00 p.m.). The direction change is far more than 45 degrees, so it is a significant change.
3. 25 to 35 knots is force 6 to 8. Later 15 to 25 is force 4 to 6.
4. The weather line says "showers". Precipitation amounts are never stated.
5. Seas of 2 to 3 metres are the combined sea and swell.


## A BC example

You are in Comox at 0600 and plan to cross to Vancouver. The 0400 forecast is on the CMB loop. You listen for Strait of Georgia north of Nanaimo, write the warning, the wind and the time of the shift, and note the next issue at 1030. At 1030 you listen again before you commit.

## Common mistakes

- Reading the wind direction as where it is going.
- Ignoring the shift in a "becoming" line.
- Forgetting the extended forecast gives wind only.
- Taking the technical synopsis as a forecast of wind in your area. It places the systems.
- Assuming "fog" means zero visibility. It means under 1 mile.
- Relying on a single source such as a phone app.
