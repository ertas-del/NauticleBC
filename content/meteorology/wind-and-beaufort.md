# Wind and the Beaufort scale

**Study aid only. Invented examples.** Use the official Environment Canada Beaufort table and current forecasts for real decisions.

## What you need to know

Every weather question on a small vessel comes back to wind. The Beaufort scale is the short code that turns knots into force numbers and describes what the sea looks like. Forecasts, warnings, vessel limits and your own decisions all use it. Learn the key forces cold: **4, 6, 8 and 10**, and the rest follow.

## The scale

| Force | Name | Knots | Sea |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Calm | under 1 | Mirror-like |
| 1 | Light air | 1 to 3 | Scale-like ripples |
| 2 | Light breeze | 4 to 6 | Small wavelets, crests do not break |
| 3 | Gentle breeze | 7 to 10 | Large wavelets, scattered whitecaps |
| 4 | ==Moderate breeze== | ==11 to 16== | Longer waves, fairly frequent whitecaps |
| 5 | ==Fresh breeze== | ==17 to 21== | Moderate waves, many whitecaps, some spray |
| 6 | ==Strong breeze== | ==22 to 27== | Large waves, more foam and spray |
| 7 | ==Near gale== | ==28 to 33== | Sea heaps up, foam in streaks |
| 8 | ==Gale== | ==34 to 40== | Higher, longer waves, crests break into spindrift |
| 9 | ==Strong gale== | ==41 to 47== | High waves, dense foam, spray reduces visibility |
| 10 | ==Storm== | ==48 to 55== | Very high waves, long overhanging crests, white sea |
| 11 | ==Violent storm== | ==56 to 63== | Exceptionally high waves |
| 12 | ==Hurricane== | ==64 and over== | Air full of foam, sea white |

To convert, ==1 knot = 1.85 km/h==.

### Linking Beaufort to warnings

Environment Canada's wind warnings are written in knots but follow the Beaufort breaks:

| Warning | Knots | Force |
|---|---|---|
| Strong wind | 20 to 33 | upper 5, 6 and 7 |
| ==Gale== | ==34 to 47== | ==8 and 9== |
| ==Storm== | ==48 to 63== | ==10 and 11== |
| ==Hurricane force== | ==64 or more== | ==12== |

(The hurricane force warning is about wind speed only. It does not mean a hurricane is expected.)

## Reading forecast wind

A forecast gives wind as:

- ==knots==, the ==mean speed expected over open water== at 10 m above the sea;
- a ==direction one of eight compass points==, being the direction the wind ==blows from==, measured from ==true north== (not magnetic);
- ==gusts== stated when the mean is expected to ==rise by 10 knots or more for short periods==, as in "south 25 knots with gusts to 35 along the coast."

The wind warning categories use the mean wind only. Gusts do not trigger them. Winds in rugged coastal terrain can differ a lot from the forecast, so treat it as a regional figure.

## Wave size: wind, fetch, duration

Waves grow when three things act together:

1. ==wind speed==;
2. ==fetch==, the distance of open water the wind blows across;
3. ==duration==, how long it blows.

Slow wind gives small waves however long the fetch or duration. A moving storm adds to the effective fetch. Forecasts give seas in metres as ==significant wave height==, the average of the highest third of the waves; the largest waves can be about ==twice== that. Wave heights from a Beaufort table are approximate, in feet in the UBC table: force 6 about 8 to 13 ft, force 8 about 18 to 25 ft, force 10 about 29 to 41 ft.

### Worked example 1: forecast to force

The forecast says "southeast 25 knots." In the table, 25 lies in 22 to 27, so it is ==force 6, strong breeze==. In km/h: 25 x 1.85 = 46.25 km/h. The table range for force 6 is 39 to 49 km/h, so the conversion fits. A strong wind warning would be in effect only if the area and season allow one.

### Worked example 2: gust

"Wind northwest 20 knots, gusts to 35." The mean 20 knots is force 5 (17 to 21). Gusts of 35 knots are in force 8. The mean is not at gale level, so no gale warning is triggered by this wind alone. The gust is 15 knots above the mean, and a gust of that size is stated because it is 10 knots or more above it.

### Worked example 3: feet to metres

A table says force 8 waves of 18 to 25 ft. In metres: 18 / 3.281 = 5.5 m and 25 / 3.281 = 7.6 m. A forecast of "seas 6 metres" is inside the gale range in that table. The Environment Canada forecast shows seas in metres.

## The force 10 reporting duty

Canada Shipping Act, 2001 s. 112: a master who meets ==winds of force 10 or more== for which ==no storm warning has been received==, or a tropical storm, dangerous ice, a derelict, or subfreezing air with gale-force winds and severe icing, must ==give notice to all vessels in the vicinity and the prescribed authorities ashore==. It is a legal duty, not a courtesy. Report by radio on the proper channel.

## A BC example

An invented forecast for the Strait of Georgia says northwest 15 to 25 knots. That is force 4 (15 is in 11 to 16) up to force 6 (25 is in 22 to 27). Wind, fetch and duration all build the sea, so a day of wind along a long strait raises more sea than the same wind for an hour. The master reads the range as "moderate to strong" and checks the vessel's limit before leaving.

## Common mistakes

- Reading wind direction as the direction the wind is going to. It is where it comes from.
- Mixing up magnetic and true. Forecast directions are true.
- Using the gust to decide on a gale warning. Warnings use the mean wind.
- Forgetting that force 12 starts at 64 knots, the same as the hurricane force warning.
- Treating Beaufort wave heights as exact.
