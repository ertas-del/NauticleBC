# Crewing, deck watch and certificates on board

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from Part 2 (Crewing) of the Marine Personnel Regulations, SOR/2007-115 (MPR). The tables of which certificate may serve as master or officer on which vessel are long and have transitional dates; this lesson gives the structure and the numbers the exam asks for. Check the live text for the table that applies to your certificate.

## What you need to know

The MPR answer three questions about any Canadian vessel under way. How many people do I need? What must each hold? And who keeps watch? The answers depend on the vessel's gross tonnage (GT), whether it carries passengers, and the voyage class from the previous lesson.

The rules in Part 2 Division 2 apply to ==self-propelled Canadian vessels engaged on a voyage== (s. 200(1)). The prohibition is blunt: ==no Canadian vessel may navigate== anywhere unless the Part's requirements are met (s. 201). That puts the burden on the authorized representative and the master together.

## Who must be on board

Every vessel on a voyage must have on board, and the authorized representative must employ (s. 212(4)):

1. a ==master==;
2. a ==chief mate== if the vessel is ==500 GT or more or carries more than 50 passengers==; and
3. enough ==deck officers== to meet the deck watch rules in ss. 213 to 216.

A person holding a certificate listed in the section's tables may fill the position the table names on the class of voyage the table names (s. 212(5)). The Master, Limited under 60 GT certificate you are studying for is issued under a different section (s. 131), but the idea is the same: the certificate on the wall must match the vessel and the voyage.

Larger vessels also need a person in charge of the machinery, and various persons for first aid, medical care and survival craft. The minimum complement must be enough to run the watches and to deal with emergencies (s. 207). Those matters sit mostly outside a small-vessel ticket, but you should know the complement is not just "who is on the bridge".

## The deck watch

The master must ensure that the intended voyage is planned and that a deck watch is kept in accordance with the watchkeeping parts of the STCW Code (Part A, section A-VIII/2, Parts 2, 3 and 3-1) (s. 213).

==No person may act as a member of the deck watch, and no master may permit it, unless that person holds a certificate appropriate to the class of vessel, the area in which it operates and the duties to be performed== (s. 214). The rule is subject to s. 216(4): where two additional persons are assigned to the watch, one of them may be a rating under training for the required certificate.

**The master and the watch.** The master is on duty as the ordinary practice of seamen requires (s. 215(1)), but is counted as a deck watch member only when the vessel is (a) securely anchored in port or moored to shore, (b) ==not more than 1 000 GT==, or (c) more than 1 000 GT but less than 3 000 GT and at least three deck watches are established (s. 215(2)). On a small BC vessel the master therefore counts as the watch.

**What a minimum deck watch consists of (s. 216).** The minimum is supplemented whenever the master decides the ordinary practice of seamen requires more. At least:

| Person | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Person in charge of the deck watch | A certificate of competency authorizing ==watchkeeping and radio watch duties==; if the vessel has a ==VHF radiotelephone, a radio operator certificate== suitable for the class of vessel and area; and an ECDIS training certificate plus the master's written instructions if ECDIS is used to meet the chart requirement |
| Additional person | On a vessel of ==5 GT or more==; if the vessel is ==500 GT or more==, at minimum an ==Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating certificate== |
| Second additional person | Over 1 000 GT and not anchored or moored (not relevant to small vessels) |
| Person in charge of the radio watch | Qualified under s. 266, unless the person in charge of the deck watch is so qualified and also in charge of the radio watch |

For a vessel required to have a VHF installation, s. 266(1) lists the certificates accepted for the person in charge of the radio watch: Restricted Operator Certificate with Maritime Qualification, General Operator Certificate, or Restricted Operator's Certificate (ROC-MC). On a voyage other than a sheltered waters voyage, where the VHF must be capable of digital selective calling, s. 266(2) is narrower: a Class 2 vessel whose only radiotelephone is that VHF needs a Restricted Operator Certificate with Maritime Qualifications issued after January 1, 2005, and in all other cases the ROC-MC is needed.

### When no additional person is needed

An additional person is ==not required== (s. 216(3)) if:

- the vessel is in a log sorting or yarding operation at a booming ground that does not use lines or chains;
- the vessel is ==up to 500 GT==, has an ==unobstructed all-round view from the steering position==, and is on a voyage of ==not more than 5 nautical miles within harbour limits==, in good visibility between sunrise and sunset;
- the vessel is a ==tug assisting another vessel while attached to it by a tow line==; or
- the vessel is up to 1 000 GT and securely anchored or moored.

All the conditions in the second bullet must be met together. A harbour hop in the dark, or in fog, takes the exception away.

## Documents: the Safe Manning Document

A Canadian vessel that is not a Safety Convention vessel but must carry an inspection certificate needs a ==Safe Manning Document== issued by the Minister (s. 202(3)(b)). It is valid for ==no more than 5 years== and states the minimum number of complement, the certificates they must hold, any conditions on those certificates, the voyages the vessel may make and, if applicable, the number of passengers. The authorized representative must see that it is carried on board (s. 202(5)). The master must keep required certificates and endorsements readily available for a marine safety inspector (s. 203). If no person holding a required certificate is available, the Minister may grant a dispensation for a specified person on a specified vessel, lasting until a certificate holder is available or six months after the dispensation is granted, whichever comes first (s. 204).

## A BC example

A 9 GT passenger boat runs sightseeing trips in Vancouver Harbour with a VHF radio on board. It is 5 GT or more, so a deck watch must include an additional person. The skipper is the master and the person in charge of the deck watch, and must hold the appropriate radio operator certificate because the boat has a VHF. The vessel is under 500 GT, so the additional person need not hold an AB or Bridge Watch Rating certificate. But on a clear August afternoon, if the boat has an unobstructed all-round view from the helm and the trip is a 4 nautical mile loop within harbour limits between sunrise and sunset, the s. 216(3) exception applies and the additional person is not required. If the same trip runs after dark, it is not.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the 500 GT chief mate rule as the only trigger. More than 50 passengers also requires a chief mate.
- Assuming the additional person must hold a Bridge Watch Rating certificate at 5 GT. That certificate is the minimum only at 500 GT and above.
- Reading the s. 216(3) harbour exception as just "up to 500 GT". It has four conditions together: view, 5 nm within harbour limits, good visibility, daylight.
- Forgetting that a VHF on board means a radio operator certificate for the person in charge of the deck watch.
- Counting the master as part of the watch on a 1 500 GT vessel with only two watches.
