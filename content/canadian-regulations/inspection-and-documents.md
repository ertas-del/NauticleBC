# Certificates and documents the vessel needs

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 (CSA), the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations (VSCR), SOR/2021-135, and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR). The crew certificate side (safe manning document, master's certificate) is in the crewing lesson; radio operator certificates are in the radio module.

## What you need to know

Papers on a small commercial vessel come from three places: the **Minister** (certificates), the **regulations** (documents you must carry or keep at a particular place) and the **vessel's own records** (log book entries). The exam asks who is responsible, whether a particular vessel needs the certificate, and where the paper lives.

### Who must see to it (CSA ss. 106, 107, 17)

- The ==master== must ensure, ==before the vessel embarks on a voyage from a port in Canada==, that ==all Canadian maritime documents required under Part 4 have been obtained== (s. 107).
- The ==authorized representative== must ensure the vessel and its equipment are ==inspected for the purpose of obtaining those documents== and that ==every term or condition== attached to a document is met (s. 106(2)).
- A document is ==valid for the period specified by the Minister==. The Minister may ==extend== it on an application made ==before it expires== if it is not feasible to issue a new document in time (s. 17).

## Does my vessel need a safety certificate?

| Vessel | Regime |
|---|---|
| Pleasure craft | outside Division 2 (VSCR s. 11(2)(a)); Part 4 of the CSA does not apply to them (CSA s. 105) |
| ==15 GT or less, 12 passengers or fewer==, or any workboat of 15 GT or less | Small Vessel Regulations: notice to the Minister, equipment, compliance notice; ==no inspection certificate== |
| ==15 GT or less with more than 12 passengers== | ==safety certificate== (VSCR Division 2) |
| ==More than 15 GT== (not a Safety Convention vessel) | ==safety certificate== (VSCR Division 2) |
| Safety Convention (SOLAS) vessel | Division 1: the SOLAS certificates, with a continuous synopsis record (not likely on this ticket) |

Division 2 does not apply to ==pleasure craft==, vessels under the ==Special-purpose Vessel Regulations==, ==human-powered vessels==, or a vessel with ==no mechanical propulsion that carries no persons, dangerous chemicals in bulk or oil in bulk== (s. 11(2)).

## The safety certificate (VSCR Division 2)

- A vessel in the regime ==must hold the applicable safety certificate to engage on a voyage== (s. 12).
- The Minister ==issues the certificate on application== by the authorized representative if the vessel meets the regulations on: structure and machinery; watertight subdivision; load lines; fire protection; life-saving appliances and equipment; line-throwing appliances and radio installations used in life-saving; ==shipborne navigational equipment, pilot embarkation and nautical charts and publications==; and lights, shapes and means of making distress and sound signals (s. 13(1)).
- If the certificate is ==valid for more than one year==, the authorized representative must ensure the vessel is ==inspected by the date shown on the certificate== and that the Minister ==endorses== it after the inspection (s. 13(2)).
- A certificate for a higher class ==also covers== the lower ones: Class 2 covers sheltered and inland, Class 1 adds Class 2, unlimited adds Class 1 (s. 14; see the voyage classes lesson).
- The Minister may add ==terms and conditions== that restrict the vessel's service and limit the voyage after weighing the intended area: wave height, wind, current, visibility, nearness to assistance, a place of refuge or shore, navigation hazards, communications coverage, depth, water and air temperature, maximum tide range and ice (s. 16). The master must read the conditions, not just the voyage class.
- The authorized representative must ==keep all safety certificates and any attached documents on board== (s. 15). If an inspection report finds a requirement unmet, the authorized representative reports on the measures taken within the period set. And if a ==modification, alteration or damage== means the vessel no longer meets the requirements, the report to the Minister is made ==without delay==, and the authorized representative must obtain ==written confirmation== that the requirements continue to be met (s. 17).

## The radio papers

- A ==radio inspection certificate== is required to engage on a voyage by a vessel of ==20 m or more==, a ==towboat==, and a ==passenger vessel== (more than 12 passengers) on a voyage in sea area A1 or more than five nautical miles from shore on Canada's sea coasts. The Minister issues it if the radio installation meets Part 2 of the NSR (s. 240).
- The master and authorized representative keep ==each radio operator's certificate== on board in a readily accessible location, and at the ==main operating position of the radio== they keep the ==Radio Aids to Marine Navigation== and the ==radio inspection certificate== if one is required (s. 212).

The radio module has the carriage rules and the 20 m and towboat triggers in detail.

## Other papers on the bridge

- ==Log book==: the master or authorized representative records the ==working language== for navigational safety in the official log book (or any log book) (s. 137(1)); the person in charge of the deck watch records the dates of the ==steering gear checks== and the dates and details of ==emergency steering drills== (s. 136(4)).
- ==Compass deviation card==: latest card for each compass, ==signed and dated by the master and a deck officer== (s. 132).
- ==Manuals==: the ==manufacturer's operating and maintenance manuals== for the navigation equipment the vessel must carry (s. 102(2)); and, where the type approval of that equipment is evidenced by a document, the document is kept in a readily accessible location on board (s. 101(4)).
- ==Charts and publications== for the area (NSR s. 142), covered in the equipment lesson.
- ==SVR papers==: the compliance notice fixed in view of the operating position on a non-pleasure vessel covered by Part 8 (SVR s. 805), covered in the workboats lesson.

## A BC example

A 22 m, 40 GT sightseeing vessel runs summer whale-watching trips from Victoria with 30 passengers on a Near Coastal Class 2 voyage. Walk the paper trail. She is over 15 GT and carries more than 12 passengers, so she needs a ==safety certificate== for Class 2 (which also covers sheltered). She is 20 m or more, so she needs a ==radio inspection certificate== and the RAMN and that certificate at the radio. Each radio operator's certificate is on board. The compass has a current deviation card signed by the master and a deck officer. The master has read the ==terms and conditions== on her certificate (say, an invented one that limits wind and wave height) and checks the forecast on every departure. In January the owner adds a heavy upper deck canopy. If that modification means she no longer meets all the requirements behind her certificate, the authorized representative must ==report it to the Minister without delay== and obtain ==written confirmation== that the requirements continue to be met.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the master alone is responsible for all documents. The master obtains them before sailing; the authorized representative arranges the inspection and meets the conditions.
- Assuming a certificate for a smaller voyage class covers a bigger one. The coverage runs the other way.
- Forgetting the terms and conditions. A certificate can say yes to a class and still restrict wave height, wind and service.
- Applying the safety certificate regime to a 12-passenger, 14 GT boat. That one is under the SVR with no inspection certificate.
- Confusing the radio inspection certificate with the safety certificate. They have different triggers: 20 m, towboat or offshore passenger vessel versus more than 15 GT or more than 12 passengers.
