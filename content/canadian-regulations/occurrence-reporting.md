# Reporting accidents and hazards

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from the Transportation Safety Board Regulations (SOR/2014-37), ss. 1, 3 and 6 to 8, the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 (CSA), ss. 112 and 129, and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR), s. 6.

## What you need to know

Three different reports sit under this heading, and a master has to keep them apart.

| Report | Trigger | To whom | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| ==Marine occurrence report== | a listed accident or incident | the Transportation Safety Board (TSB) | TSB Regulations s. 3 |
| ==Danger message== | ice, derelict, direct danger, severe weather | vessels nearby and the shore station | CSA s. 112, NSR s. 6 |
| ==Aid to navigation or hazard report== | aid damaged, missing or malfunctioning; uncharted hazard | MCTS, else a Coast Guard officer | CSA s. 129 |

## The TSB report (TSB Regulations s. 3)

==Who reports==: the ==operator== of the ship (owner or not), the ==master==, the ship's ==pilot==, any ==crew member== and the ==harbour master==, if they have ==direct knowledge== of the occurrence. The rule is for any ship ==other than a pleasure craft== (a ship used for pleasure and not for a commercial purpose). For a ship under tow, the operator and master of the towing ship count too (s. 3(7)).

==What is reportable== (s. 3(1)). Learn it in three groups.

- ==People==: a person killed or ==seriously injured== boarding, on board, falling overboard or touched by the ship or its contents; ==any person falls overboard==, injured or not; a crew member with safety-related duties who is ==incapacitated== in a way that threatens safety.
- ==The ship==: ==sinks, founders or capsizes==; ==collision or risk of collision==; ==fire or explosion==; ==aground==; ==unforeseen contact with the bottom== without going aground; damage that affects ==seaworthiness== or makes her unfit for her purpose; ==anchored, grounded or beached to avoid an occurrence==; ==missing or abandoned==; fouls a utility cable, pipe or pipeline; and ==total failure== of the navigation equipment (if it threatens safety), of the main or auxiliary machinery, or of propulsion, steering or deck machinery (if it threatens safety).
- ==Cargo and dangerous goods==: all or part of the cargo ==shifts or falls overboard==; an accidental release of dangerous goods of the kind listed in the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations.

==Serious injury== has a definition (s. 1): a fracture (except a simple fracture of a finger, toe or nose); a laceration causing severe hemorrhage or nerve, muscle or tendon damage; an injury to an internal organ; second or third degree burns, or any burns over more than ==5 percent== of the body surface; a verified exposure to infectious substances or injurious radiation; or an injury likely to require hospitalization.

==Time limits== (s. 3(3)): send ==all the information available at the time, as soon as possible and by the quickest means available==, and the ==remainder as soon as it becomes available, within 30 days== after the occurrence. The "30 days" is for the follow-up, not the first report. The report has a long content list (s. 3(2)): the ship's name or number, nationality and type, date and time, operator, owner and master, tonnage and length, departure and destination, people on board and casualties, weather and sea state, position, description of the occurrence and damage, and what is being done to protect people, property and the environment.

==How==: a report to a ==radio ship reporting station== counts as a report to the Board (s. 3(6)). That term covers a Canadian Coast Guard radio station, a ==Marine Communications and Traffic Services centre==, a Canadian harbour radio station and a St. Lawrence Seaway radio station (s. 3(8)). On the BC coast this means a call to Victoria or Prince Rupert MCTS satisfies the first report. The Board and the person reporting may also agree a different format and timetable for occurrences unlikely to need an immediate response (s. 3(4)).

### Evidence and voluntary reports (ss. 6 to 8)

Anyone who has possession or control of evidence about a transportation occurrence must ==keep and preserve it== unless the Board provides otherwise (s. 8). Safety comes first: you may take the measures needed to protect people, property or the environment, but you should first ==record the evidence by the best means available== and then ==advise the Board== of what you did. Separately, anyone not required to report may ==voluntarily report== information they believe relevant (s. 6), and if they ask, the Board must ==protect their identity== (s. 7).

## The danger message (CSA s. 112)

The master of a Canadian vessel who meets ==dangerous ice, a dangerous derelict or other direct danger to navigation==, a ==tropical storm==, ==winds of force 10 or more== on the Beaufort scale for which no storm warning has been received, or ==subfreezing air temperatures with gale-force winds causing severe ice accretion== must tell all vessels in the vicinity and the prescribed authority on shore. The NSR names the authority: ==the shore station for the area== where the vessel is navigating. The message follows the danger-message procedure in ==Section A5 (Navigation Safety)== of the annual Notices to Mariners (s. 6).

## Aids to navigation and uncharted hazards (CSA s. 129)

If a vessel, or anything it tows, ==runs down, moves, damages or destroys an aid to navigation== in Canadian waters, the person in charge reports ==without delay== to an MCTS officer or, if that is not feasible, to a Canadian Coast Guard officer. The same duty applies to a person in charge who ==discovers an uncharted hazard== or finds an aid that is ==missing, out of position or malfunctioning==. The duty falls on the person in charge of the towing vessel even if it was the tow that struck the aid. If the vessel is also in a VTS zone, the VTS regulations add their own report, covered in the next lesson.

## A BC example

A 14 m tug is pushing a barge up Howe Sound. A deckhand slips off the barge corner and is recovered unhurt within three minutes. Later, going through a pass at low water, the tug touches the bottom briefly without damage, and on the way home you notice a lit buoy that is dark. What do you report?

1. The man overboard is reportable under s. 3(1)(b): ==any person falling overboard== counts, injured or not.
2. The brief bottom contact is reportable under s. 3(1)(d)(v), ==unforeseen contact with the bottom== without going aground.
3. Call MCTS promptly. That satisfies the first TSB report; the remaining details follow within 30 days.
4. The dark buoy is a CSA s. 129(2) report to MCTS without delay, separate from the TSB report.
5. Keep what you can (the log, the track, the damage if any) until the Board says otherwise.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking only injuries are reportable. A person overboard, a near miss ("risk of collision"), a touch on the bottom and a total steering failure that threatens safety all count.
- Treating "within 30 days" as the deadline for the first report.
- Reporting an aid to navigation to the TSB, or a TSB occurrence only to MCTS, and assuming the other duty is met. The section 129 report is a separate duty, although the same MCTS call may carry both.
- Forgetting that a pleasure craft is outside s. 3 of the TSB Regulations, while a commercial vessel of any size is inside it.
- Throwing away evidence (a damaged part, a recording) while "tidying up" after an incident.
