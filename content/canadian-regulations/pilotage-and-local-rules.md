# Pilotage and local rules in BC

**Study aid only, not a navigation aid.** Paraphrased from the Pilotage Act (ss. 2, 38.01, 38.02 and 44), the General Pilotage Regulations (SOR/2000-132) Division 4 (Pacific Pilotage Authority region) and Schedule 5, and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR) s. 119. The old Pacific Pilotage Regulations are repealed; the Pacific rules now sit in the General Pilotage Regulations.

## What you need to know

A pilot is a local expert who takes the **conduct** of the ship while the master stays in command. Pilotage matters on this ticket for one reason above all: the tonnage test counts a **tug and tow together**, so a small tug pushing a big barge can be a compulsory-pilotage vessel while the same tug on her own is not.

### The basic rule (Pilotage Act)

- ==Compulsory pilotage== means a ship must be ==under the conduct of a licensed pilot or a pilotage certificate holder==. In a compulsory pilotage area no one may have the conduct of a ship unless they are a ==licensed pilot for that area== or a ==regular member of the ship's complement who holds a pilotage certificate for it== (ss. 2, 38.01(1)). A regular member of the complement is someone in a position that counts toward the safe manning rules for a proper deck watch.
- It does not apply when the ==master takes the conduct== (s. 38.02), when the ==Authority waives== compulsory pilotage, or in some Canadian waters next to US waters where the regulation's conditions are met (s. 38.01(2)).
- The pilot is ==responsible to the master== for the safe navigation of the ship (s. 38.01(3)). The master is not displaced. If the master believes on reasonable grounds that the pilot's actions endanger the ship, the master ==may take the conduct== and must file a ==written report with the Minister within three days== (s. 38.02).
- A ship subject to compulsory pilotage that goes through the area ==without a pilot== (and without a waiver) is liable for ==all the pilotage charges as if a pilot had been used== (s. 44).

### Which ships (General Pilotage Regulations s. 25.9)

In the Pacific region a ship is subject to compulsory pilotage if it is ==more than 350 GT and not a pleasure craft==, or a ==pleasure craft of more than 500 GT==. A "ship" in the Act includes ==any vessel or boat used or designed for use in navigation, whether or not it has propulsion==, so a barge counts. Where a ship is part of an ==arrangement of ships== (ships travelling together joined by lines or other means), the ==combined tonnage== is what is tested. Three ships are excepted: a ==government vessel==, a ==ferry== (on a fixed schedule between terminals) and a ==US government ship of less than 10,000 GT==.

### The five areas (Schedule 5)

| Area | Where, in short |
|---|---|
| ==1== | the Fraser River and its tributaries including the North Arm, with the seaward waters off Point Grey and Boundary Bay north of latitude 49°00′07″ N |
| ==2== | the south coast inside waters from the international boundary through the Strait of Georgia to the line from Cape Caution to Cape Sutil, and the inner part of Juan de Fuca Strait (apart from the Area 1 waters excluded by the schedule) |
| ==3== | the outer west coast of Vancouver Island from Christopher Point to Cape Sutil |
| ==4== | the central and north coast from Cape Caution to the Alaska boundary |
| ==5== | the waters off Haida Gwaii |

These are descriptions in coordinates. Read Schedule 5 for the exact limits, including how the Area 1 and Area 2 descriptions meet off Point Grey.

### Boarding, notice and pilots (ss. 25.11 to 25.15)

- ==Boarding stations==: ==Fairway Buoy== off Brotchie Ledge near Victoria; ==Cape Beale== at the Trevor Channel entrance in Barkley Sound; ==Triple Island== near Prince Rupert; ==Pine Island== near Port Hardy; ==Sand Heads== at the Fraser mouth for Area 1; and any other place the Authority needs.
- ==Notice==: ETA in UTC at least ==12 hours== before the Victoria station (confirmed or corrected ==4 hours== before), or at least ==48 hours== before the other stations (confirmed or corrected at least ==12 hours== before). The notice gives the service wanted, the ship's name, nationality, length, breadth, deepest draft and GT, and anything that affects speed, manoeuvrability or safety. If a ==pilotage certificate holder== is in charge of the deck watch, or a ==waiver== has been granted, the Authority is told ==48 hours== before entering the area (s. 25.14).
- ==Number of pilots==: one is the minimum. ==Two== if the pilot's bridge watch would exceed ==8 consecutive hours== or ==105 consecutive nautical miles==, if one pilot cannot navigate the ship safely, or if the ship asks for two. A pilot of a ==towed ship may ride the tug== (s. 25.15).

### Waivers (s. 25.10)

The Authority may, on application, waive pilotage for a ship in ==distress==, needing ==medical evacuation==, in ==rescue or salvage==, ==seeking refuge==, or where ==no pilot is available== and the notice and route knowledge conditions are met. It may waive for a ship ==warping== without engines or a tug, or proceeding ==directly to or from a pilot boarding station==. For a ship of ==less than 10,000 GT== the Authority may waive where ==every person in charge of the deck watch==:

- holds the certificates required by Part 2 of the Marine Personnel Regulations (or equivalents);
- has ==150 days of service in the previous 18 months==, or ==365 days in the previous 60 months including 60 days in the previous 24==, as a person in charge of the deck watch in the region; and
- has served in that area ==at least once in the previous 24 months==.

Further tests apply by place: in Area 1 ==west of the New Westminster railway bridge==, ==five== return voyages with a licensed pilot (or with a watchkeeper who has done them) in the previous 24 months, with the Authority given prior notice; ==east== of it, ==ten==; for ships carrying dangerous goods in the ==Second Narrows Traffic Control Zone==, ==six==, at least one in the previous 24 months. A waiver is ==not available if there is a risk to navigational safety== from ship safety orders resulting from an environmental risk, exceptional circumstances on board, or extreme weather, tide, current or freshet conditions (s. 25.10(9)). The combined tonnage of an arrangement of ships is used for waivers too.

## Local rules for a BC master: where they come from

Pilotage is one local layer. The others, in the order you meet them:

- ==Traffic zones and VTS== (previous lesson): clearance and reports in the Victoria and Prince Rupert zones, with the zone's VHF channel replacing Channel 16 for listening watch and navigation safety calls inside a traffic zone.
- ==Collision Regulations Rule 10 and Canadian provisions==: traffic separation schemes in Juan de Fuca Strait and its approaches, Haro Strait and the Strait of Georgia (colregs lessons).
- ==Navigation Safety Regulations Part 3==: mostly regional rules for other places (the Burlington Canal, the St. Clair and Detroit rivers). One BC item is in Division 1: ==it is prohibited to anchor in the Schedule 5 waters== (s. 301), which include ==Parry Bay== (the bay at Albert Head near Victoria; the schedule defines it by coordinates, with reference to Chart 3440), and vessels in those waters must follow any ==instruction or direction in Notices to Mariners or a navigational warning== about navigation limits or other shipping measures (s. 302). The other Schedule 5 items are in Newfoundland, between New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island, and at the Welland Canal entrances.
- ==Sailing Directions, Notices to Mariners and harbour authority rules==: for restricted areas, speed limits, anchorages and local procedures that are not in these regulations. This lesson did not read PAC 200 or any port authority bylaw; check them for the harbour you work in.

## A BC example

A 24 GT, 14 m tug is hired to push a 600 GT barge from a Gulf Island dock to the Fraser River. The tug alone is far below 350 GT. The pair is "an arrangement of ships", with combined tonnage ==624 GT==, which is more than 350, so the tow is subject to compulsory pilotage in Area 2 and then Area 1. The company has two options: take a licensed pilot (who may ride the tug, because the barge is the ship being towed), or apply for a waiver. For a waiver every person in charge of the tug's deck watch needs the right certificates, the service-day record, a trip into the area within 24 months, and, for the Area 1 leg west of the New Westminster railway bridge, five return voyages in the last 24 months with a licensed pilot. The Authority has to be told 48 hours ahead of the waiver voyage, and a risk to navigational safety (a freshet, for example) can cancel the waiver. If the tow goes through without either, s. 44 makes the ship liable for the pilotage charges as if a pilot had been used.

## Common mistakes

- Testing the tug's own tonnage and forgetting the combined tonnage of an arrangement of ships.
- Thinking the pilot takes command. The pilot has the conduct and is responsible to the master, who can take the conduct back and must report within three days.
- Remembering 12 hours at every station. Victoria is 12 hours with a 4 hour confirmation, and the others are 48 hours with a 12 hour confirmation.
- Treating the waiver tests as a licence. A waiver is granted by the Authority on application, and a safety risk overrides it.
- Overlooking the Parry Bay anchoring prohibition because most of NSR Part 3 is about other places.
