# Who is responsible: the Canada Shipping Act, 2001

**Study aid only.** This lesson paraphrases Part 4 of the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 (CSA 2001). Section numbers are given so you can check the live text on Justice Laws.

## What you need to know

CSA 2001 is the Act that sits behind almost every other lesson in this module. The regulations (crewing, small vessels, pollution, navigation) are made under it. Part 4, Safety, spreads the responsibility for a vessel across four groups of people: the authorized representative, the master, the crew and the passengers. The exam keeps asking the same question in different clothes: "whose job is this?"

Part 4 ==applies to Canadian vessels everywhere, other than pleasure craft, and to foreign vessels in Canadian waters== (s. 105). In the Act a ==pleasure craft== is a vessel used for pleasure that does not carry passengers. A water taxi, a whale-watching boat or a fishing charter is therefore not a pleasure craft, however small it is.

## The authorized representative

Every Canadian vessel must have one person who is responsible under the Act for everything that is not specifically given to someone else (s. 14). Usually that is the ==owner==, but it is the ==bare-boat charterer== if the vessel is chartered that way, and it is a ==qualified person== (a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or a Canadian corporation) where the owner has made an arrangement for that person, such as the operating company, to take on the role. The owner is bound by what the representative does or fails to do.

The three general duties in s. 106(1) are worth knowing word for word in outline:

1. ==Ensure the vessel and its machinery and equipment meet the regulations.==
2. ==Develop procedures for the safe operation of the vessel and for emergencies.==
3. ==Ensure crew and passengers receive safety training.==

Section 106(2) adds that the representative must have the vessel inspected to obtain the Canadian maritime documents the Act requires and must see that every term or condition on those documents is met.

## The master

The master is the person in command and charge of the vessel. A licensed pilot doing pilotage is not the master. The master's main duties in Part 4 are:

- ==Documents (s. 107).== Before the vessel embarks on a voyage from a port in Canada, the master ensures all the required Canadian maritime documents, such as the inspection certificate where one is required, have been obtained.
- ==Safety (s. 109).== Take all reasonable steps to ensure the safety of the vessel and of persons on board, or loading and unloading it while using equipment. If the master is informed of a safety hazard, the master must take reasonable measures to protect the vessel and the people on board, including eliminating the hazard if feasible. If it cannot be eliminated, the master of a Canadian vessel notifies the authorized representative.
- ==Persons and load lines (s. 110).== The number of persons on board must not exceed the number authorized by the vessel's certificate, and the applicable load lines must not be submerged. The person limit does not apply to people carried because the master is rescuing shipwrecked or distressed persons.

Elsewhere in Part 4 the master of a Canadian vessel who meets dangerous ice, a dangerous derelict or another direct danger to navigation must give notice of it to nearby vessels and the prescribed authorities ashore (s. 112).
## Crew and passengers

Every crew member on board must carry out their duties without jeopardizing the vessel or anyone on board, report safety hazards to the master, report to the master any change in their circumstances that could affect their ability to do the job safely (for example an injury or illness), and comply with the master's lawful directions (s. 113).

Every passenger must comply with directions from the master or a crew member given to carry out the Act or the regulations, and with a direction to leave the vessel if the master gives it before the voyage starts (s. 115). Nobody may board or leave a vessel without the master's permission or against the master's orders, or when there is no safe means of embarkation (s. 116).

Two prohibitions apply to everyone: no person may tamper with or vandalize the vessel, its machinery, equipment, or its notices and plans on emergency procedures, safety or navigation (s. 117), and ==no person may take any action that might jeopardize the safety of a vessel or of persons on board== (s. 118).

## Why it matters: enforcement

The Minister of Transport can direct a master or crew member to stop an operation that poses an undue risk because of unsafe conditions (ss. 111, 114). Contraventions are offences. The Act sets heavy penalties; for example, submerging a load line is punishable by a fine of up to $500,000 for each centimetre or part of a centimetre submerged (s. 122). You do not need the figures for the exam, but you should know that load lines and passenger numbers are taken seriously.

## A BC example

A company runs a 12 m passenger boat out of Tofino under an inspection certificate for 24 persons. The owner has contracted the operating company to run the boat, so the operating company is the authorized representative. The skipper is the master. On a busy morning a deckhand notices a loose guard on the bilge pump drive and tells the skipper (s. 113(b)). The skipper cannot fix it at once, so before sailing the skipper tells the operations manager (s. 109(2)). Later 26 people show up at the dock. The skipper may not take more than 24 (s. 110(1)); the rule would not apply if they were rescuing 26 people from a swamped boat.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the owner is always the authorized representative. It can be the bare-boat charterer or a qualified person under an arrangement.
- Treating "master" as a rank. The Act defines it by the role: the person in command and charge.
- Forgetting that a deckhand has duties. Reporting a hazard to the master is the crew's job, not an optional courtesy.
- Applying the persons-on-board limit to a rescue. The Act carves out shipwrecked or distressed persons.
- Calling a passenger boat a pleasure craft. In this Act, pleasure craft do not carry passengers.
