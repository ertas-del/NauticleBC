# Vessel Traffic Services on the BC coast

**Study aid only, not a navigation aid.** Paraphrased from the Vessel Traffic Services Zones Regulations (SOR/2025-275), the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 (CSA) ss. 125 to 127 and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR). Calling-in points, sector channels and report formats are in the Radio Aids to Marine Navigation (RAMN) Part 3, which is the document to use at sea.

## What you need to know

The rules for BC changed in the spring of 2026. The new Vessel Traffic Services Zones Regulations ==repealed the old zone regulations (SOR/89-98 and the Eastern and Northern ones)== and the consolidation shows the provisions in force from ==31 March 2026== (the coming-into-force clause sets the date as three months after Gazette publication). If you are using a study note written before then, check it.

Two layers stack. The CSA says who may be required to do what; the Regulations say which vessels, which zones and when.

### The legal hook (CSA s. 126)

A vessel of a prescribed class must not ==enter, leave or proceed within a VTS Zone without a clearance==, and must not ==proceed within a zone unless it can keep direct communication with a marine communications and traffic services (MCTS) officer== (s. 126(1)). An MCTS officer may grant the clearance, ==direct the master to give information==, ==direct which radio frequencies to use== and ==direct the vessel to leave, stay out of or stay at a place in the zone== (s. 126(3)). The Regulations name the prescribed classes (ss. 4 and 9(2)), require communication to be kept up according to RAMN Part 3 (s. 5) and require the content and manner of reports to follow RAMN Part 3 (s. 6).

## The two zones that matter in BC

Section 7 creates four groups of zones (Western, Eastern, Local Eastern and Northern). BC is entirely in the ==Western VTS Zones== of Schedule 1, which has two items.

- ==Victoria==: the inside waters of the south coast, inside the line from Cape Sutil to Cape Caution and down the east side of Vancouver Island, including the ==Strait of Georgia, Howe Sound, Burrard Inlet and the main (south) arm of the Fraser River==, and south through ==Haro Strait and Juan de Fuca Strait== to the international boundary, ending at a line along longitude 124°40′ W.
- ==Prince Rupert==: the ==central and north coast== from Cape Caution and Cape Sutil north to ==Dixon Entrance and the Alaska boundary==, and the ==outer west coast of Vancouver Island== from the international boundary to Cape Sutil, out to the territorial sea.

The schedule describes each zone by coordinates, so for exact limits read the schedule or the chart notes. The point for the exam is that Victoria is the inside zone, Prince Rupert is the north-and-outside zone, and they meet at ==Cape Caution and Cape Sutil==.

## Who is covered

==General class (s. 3)==, in every zone, for vessels about to enter, leave or proceed within a zone:

- a vessel of ==300 GT or more==;
- a vessel towing or pushing another with a ==combined 500 GT or more==;
- a vessel ==carrying a pollutant or dangerous goods as cargo==, or towing or pushing one.

==Western add-on class (s. 9)==, which brings in many smaller BC vessels:

- a vessel of ==20 m or more in length==, ==except== a ==pleasure craft under 30 m== and a ==fishing vessel under 24 m that is also not more than 150 GT==; and
- a vessel towing or pushing another vessel or an object (other than fishing gear), unless the whole tow is inside a log booming ground, if the ==combined length is 45 m or more== or the ==tow itself is 20 m or more==.

Notice the "and" in the fishing exception: a 22 m fishing vessel of 160 GT is covered, because it is over 150 GT. The add-on class gets ss. 11 to 15 only, so the 24-hour plan does not apply to it. The Regulations do not define "log booming ground"; check the RAMN or the zone's local practice.

## What to send, and when

| Report | When (Western zones) | Section |
|---|---|---|
| ==Sailing plan, 24 hours== | at least 24 hours before entering from outside Canadian waters, or as soon as feasible if the voyage is shorter; general class only | s. 10 |
| ==Sailing plan, other== | at least ==15 minutes== before entering the zone (unless a clearance was already granted), before a manoeuvre that may be detrimental to safe navigation, and before leaving a berth; ==immediately== before getting underway after stranding, a main propulsion or steering breakdown or a collision | s. 11 |
| ==Position report== | at each ==calling-in point==; as soon as feasible after the manoeuvre ends; ==immediately before leaving a berth== | s. 12 |
| ==Final report== | on arrival at a berth in the zone; ==immediately before leaving the zone==, unless the vessel enters another zone | s. 13 |
| ==Other reports== | ==as soon as feasible== after the master becomes aware of a listed circumstance | s. 14 |
| ==Deviation report== | as soon as feasible after a ==significant change== in the information in an earlier report | s. 15 |

The ==other reports== list (s. 14) covers: fire or explosion; collision, grounding or striking; unforeseen contact with the bottom; a defect in the hull, main propulsion, steering, anchor or any equipment required under the NSR; seaworthiness affected; cargo shifting or falling overboard; another vessel in apparent difficulty; an obstruction to navigation; an aid to navigation damaged (in addition to the CSA s. 129(2) report); hazardous ice or weather; a pollutant in the water; another vessel that may impede movement; and risks to health or to safety or the environment identified in RAMN Part 3. It overlaps with the occurrence-reporting lesson, but it is a VTS duty owed to MCTS, separate from the TSB report.

## If you cannot reach MCTS

Under CSA s. 126(4) to (6), a vessel that cannot get a clearance, or loses communication after getting one, ==may still proceed on its route==, but the master must ==take all reasonable measures to communicate as soon as possible== and obtain a clearance as soon as communication is established. If the cause is ==equipment failure== on the vessel, the vessel must ==stay== in a port or anchorage where the equipment can be repaired until communication works, or, if not in such a place, ==proceed to the nearest reasonably safe port or anchorage on its route== when it is safe to do so and stay there until communication works.

## Radio

Inside a traffic zone the listening watch and navigation safety call are on the ==appropriate VHF channel== for the zone rather than Channel 16 (NSR Schedule 4, item 2). A vessel ==reporting to a traffic centre== need not make the departure or dredge-approach navigation safety call (s. 253(4)(b)). The sector channels are in RAMN Part 3: for example ==Victoria Traffic is on Channel 11== for Haro Strait, Boundary Pass and the Strait of Georgia, and ==Prince Rupert Traffic is on Channel 74== for the west coast of Vancouver Island. These come from the 2026 RAMN page as seen in summary only, so confirm them in the current edition.

## A BC example

A 17 m, 48 GT tug, towing a 40 m barge on a wire, leaves a berth in Vancouver Harbour for Nanaimo. Is she covered? She is under 300 GT, and in this example the barge is small enough that the combined tonnage stays under 500 GT, so the general class does not catch her. But she is a tow, and the barge alone is 40 m, which is 20 m or more, so s. 9(1)(b)(ii) catches her whatever the combined length. She gives a sailing plan at least 15 minutes before leaving the berth and a position report immediately before departure. She reports at each calling-in point on the route and gives a final report on arrival at her berth in Nanaimo, which is still inside the Victoria zone. If the engine quits en route, she tells MCTS as soon as feasible (s. 14, a defect in main propulsion) and, once repaired, gives a sailing plan immediately before getting underway again.

A 14 m recreational boat on the same water is not covered. A 31 m private yacht is covered: it is over 20 m, and the pleasure-craft exception stops below 30 m.

## Common mistakes

- Using notes written before 31 March 2026 as if the old zone regulations were still in force.
- Thinking the 24-hour sailing plan applies to every covered vessel. It applies to the general class under s. 3, not the add-on class.
- Forgetting the tow routes into coverage: combined length 45 m or more, or a tow of 20 m or more, even for a small tug.
- Misreading the fishing exception. It needs both under 24 m and not over 150 GT.
- Assuming loss of communication means you must stop. You may carry on and keep trying, unless the cause is equipment failure on your vessel, in which case the repair-or-nearest-safe-place rule applies.
