# Hours of work and rest

**Study aid only.** This lesson is the regulation-side view of hours of rest. The full explanation of fatigue, worked schedules and the e-course requirement is in the Bridge Watch lesson "Fatigue and hours of rest", which you should read first. Paraphrased from the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR), Part 3, ss. 300, 301 and 319 to 324.

## What you need to know

The exam does not ask whether sleep is good. It asks which numbers apply to which boat, who is covered, what the master may suspend, and what the master must write down. This lesson answers those four questions.

Start with a definition: ==hours of rest are rest time outside hours of work and do not include short breaks== (s. 300). A coffee break is not rest.

## Is my boat covered at all? (ss. 301 and 319)

Part 3 of the MPR applies to Canadian vessels everywhere and foreign vessels in Canadian waters, but with exclusions:

- It does ==not apply to pleasure craft or to vessels of traditional build such as canoes and kayaks== (s. 301(3)).
- It does ==not apply to fishing vessels==, except where a provision says so (s. 301(2)). The hours of rest rules do say so for ==fishing vessels of 100 gross tonnage or more== on the voyages in s. 319.

So a 12 m passenger boat is covered. A 14 m recreational yacht with no passengers is not. A 60 GT fishing boat is not; a 120 GT one on a sheltered or near coastal voyage is.

## Which rule applies? (s. 319)

The MPR give two rules and sort vessels between them by voyage class, which is why the voyage classes lesson comes first:

| Vessel and voyage | Rule |
|---|---|
| Canadian vessel on a ==sheltered waters== voyage, or a ==near coastal voyage, Class 1 or Class 2==, in any waters other than those of a foreign state that has ratified the Maritime Labour Convention | ==s. 320== (with ss. 322 and 323) |
| Canadian vessel on an ==unlimited== voyage, or on a near coastal voyage in the waters of a foreign state that has ratified the Convention; ==foreign vessels in Canadian waters== | ==s. 321== (with ss. 322, 323 and 324) |

For a BC small vessel working in BC waters, s. 320 is the rule. A common error is to quote the 14 and 72 or 10 and 77 figures for a harbour boat. Those belong to s. 321. Whether a particular foreign state has ratified the Convention is a matter of fact outside the MPR; check before a cross-border run.

## The two rules

**Section 320** (domestic). The master ensures that the master and every crew member have:

- at least ==6 consecutive hours of rest in every 24-hour period==;
- at least ==16 hours of rest in every 48-hour period==; and
- ==not more than 18 hours, but not less than 6 hours==, between the end of one rest period and the start of the next.

**Section 321** (unlimited, ratifying foreign waters, foreign vessels). Either:

- ==not more than 14 hours of work in any 24-hour period and not more than 72 hours in any 7-day period==; or
- ==at least 10 hours of rest in every 24-hour period and at least 77 hours in every 7-day period==.

And in both cases the hours of rest are divided into ==no more than two periods, one of at least 6 hours==, with ==no more than 14 hours== between two consecutive rest periods.

## Exceptions the master may use (s. 322)

The master must take the danger of fatigue into account when setting the schedule, especially for those whose duties involve navigational safety and the safe and secure operation of the vessel. Then:

- A collective agreement that is no less favourable to crew may govern instead of ss. 320 and 321.
- Sections 320 and 321 do not apply during practice musters, fire-fighting drills or survival craft drills done in a way that minimizes disturbance of rest periods and does not induce fatigue.
- They do not apply to a crew member who is on call if the member has compensatory rest periods when rest is disturbed.
- The master ==may suspend the schedule== for the ==immediate safety of the vessel, persons on board or the cargo==, or to give assistance to ==other vessels or persons in distress at sea==. As soon as feasible, every crew member who worked during a scheduled rest period must get ==compensatory rest==.

## Records and posting (ss. 323 and 324)

The master must ==keep a record of every crew member's daily hours of work or hours of rest until the crew member is discharged== (s. 323). On vessels covered by s. 321, a ==table of shipboard working arrangements== must be posted in a conspicuous place showing, for every position, the schedule of service at sea and in port and the maximum hours of work or minimum hours of rest. It is written in the working language of the vessel and in English (s. 324). Section 319 applies s. 324 only to the s. 321 vessels, so a domestic BC boat on a s. 320 voyage must keep records but is not required to post the table under s. 324.

## A BC example

A 16 m passenger vessel runs sightseeing trips from Victoria on a near coastal Class 2 voyage in Canadian waters, so s. 320 applies. A deckhand's roster is work 0700 to 1300, rest 1300 to 1900, work 1900 to 2300, rest 2300 to 0700.

1. Rest in 24 hours: 6 + 8 = 14 hours. Over 48 hours it is 28, which is at least 16. Both pass.
2. Longest unbroken rest: 8 hours, which is at least 6. Passes.
3. Interval from the end of one rest period to the start of the next: rest ends 0700 and the next starts 1300, so 6 hours (passes); rest ends 1900 and the next starts 2300, so only 4 hours (fails, because the interval must be between 6 and 18 hours).

The roster fails on the interval rule even though total rest is generous. One fix is to drop the evening work block: work 0700 to 1900 with a single rest from 1900 to 0700. Then rest is 12 hours in 24, 24 hours in 48, the unbroken rest is 12 hours and the interval is 12 hours, all within the limits. Notice that s. 320 does not cap daily work at all; it protects the rest blocks. That is why the master must still weigh fatigue under s. 322(1) and not simply meet the minimums.

## Common mistakes

- Using the 14/72 or 10/77 numbers on a domestic voyage. Those are s. 321.
- Forgetting that the 6-to-18 hour interval is a separate test from total rest.
- Counting short breaks as rest.
- Assuming a posted working-arrangements table is required on every vessel. Section 324 attaches to the s. 321 vessels.
- Overlooking that pleasure craft and most fishing vessels fall outside Part 3.
