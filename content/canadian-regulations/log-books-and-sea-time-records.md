# Log books, discharge book and sea time

**Study aid only, not legal advice.** Transport Canada sets the current forms. The examiner decides what service counts. The in-app Sea time log is your private notebook. It is not an official record.

## What you need to know

A ticket needs sea time. Sea time only counts if it is ==proved on paper==. Four records do that job. Know which is which.

| Record | Whose | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| ==Official log book== | the vessel's (master keeps it) | legal record of the voyage, crew, injuries, accidents |
| ==Deck log== | the vessel's | day-to-day navigation and work record |
| ==Radio record== | the vessel's | radio checks and distress, urgency and safety traffic |
| ==Record of sea service== | ==yours and the authorized representative's== | proof of your time for a certificate |

## Your record: sea service and discharge

- The ==authorized representative and every crew member each keep a record of the member's sea service==, in the form the Minister specifies (CSA s. 93). Two copies exist on purpose. Yours is your job.
- When you are ==discharged== from a Canadian vessel, the authorized representative must give you a ==certificate of discharge== (CSA s. 92).
- A ==discharge book== holds those sign-on and sign-off entries in one place. The examiner asks for ==all discharge books and any certificates of discharge== for the service you claim.

## The testimonial

The ==testimonial of sea service== is the signed statement that says what you did. For deck service it must hold at least the items in ==Schedule 4== of the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR):

1. Vessel owner's name and address.
2. Your name and candidate number (==CDN==).
3. Number of ==emergency drills== you took part in.
4. The vessel: ==name, port of registry, official number, type==, cargo carried, ==gross tonnage, voyage classification==, extreme ports of call.
5. Date you ==signed on== and date you ==signed off==.
6. Your ==rank==.
7. ==Days underway and type of watch (hours)==.
8. ==Days worked==.
9. Description of service.
10. Fitting-out, lay-up or refit dates, if any.
11. ==Signature of the master or authorized representative==.

Two cousins:

- ==Engine testimonial (Schedule 5)==: propulsive power and engine details instead of tonnage, signed by the ==chief engineer and the master or authorized representative==.
- ==Steering testimonial (Schedule 3)==: the master declares the ==total hours at the wheel== and that you are a competent wheelsman. Some certificates ask for it, for example Watchkeeping Mate.

## What goes to the examiner

==At least two weeks before your first exam== (MPR s. 110(2)):

- signed application;
- birth certificate or equivalent;
- ==all discharge books and certificates of discharge==;
- ==testimonials== with the required signatures;
- any certificates you already hold.

No signature, no credit. The hours rule (==8 hours = 1 day, 12-hour cap, divide by 30==) is in "Counting sea service".

## The vessel's log books

**Official log book (MPR ss. 339 and 340).**

- Required on a Canadian vessel of ==100 GT or more== on an ==unlimited voyage, near coastal voyage Class 1 or international voyage== (other than an inland voyage). Most small BC boats on Class 2 or sheltered waters voyages fall outside this.
- It ==may be united with the deck log==.
- Entries are made ==as soon as possible==, with the ==date of the event and the date of the entry==.
- ==The master signs every entry==, with the ==chief mate or another crew member==.
- It records, among other things: vessel particulars, master's name and certificate number, start and end of the voyage, the crew list, ==injuries, deaths, accidents and incidents==.
- The authorized representative keeps a finished book until ==five years after completion or a change of registration==, whichever is first.

**Smaller vessels.** Where no official log book is required, some things must still be written down in ==any log book==: the ==steering gear checks and tests, emergency steering drills== (NSR s. 136(4)) and the working language (NSR s. 137). So a plain deck log still matters on a small boat.

**Radio record (NSR s. 247).** Each entry is ==initialled by the radio operator== and timed in the ==24-hour system==. The record is kept ==at least 12 months after the last entry==.

**Honesty.** A ==false entry made to mislead, or an entry wilfully left out==, in a required log book is an offence (CSA s. 23). Never "tidy up" a log later. Add a new dated entry instead.

## The two-minute daily habit

This is a working method, not a rule. It makes the testimonial a copying job instead of a memory test.

1. **Once per vessel:** write the name, ==official number==, port of registry, type, ==gross tonnage== and owner. Take them from the vessel's certificate, not from memory.
2. **Every day you work:** date, ==hours on duty==, underway or alongside, your rank, the waters. Tick a drill if you did one.
3. **Every sign-off:** get the ==certificate of discharge== or the discharge book entry, and ask for the ==testimonial while the master is still there==. Masters move on. Companies close.
4. **Every traffic call:** time, channel and calling-in point go in the deck log.
5. **Photograph** each signed page the day it is signed.

The app's Sea time log follows this order and adds up credited days for you.

## A BC example (invented)

Sam decks on a 40 GT crew boat out of Port McNeill, from 2 May to 30 September. Sam logs 100 days of 12 hours. Credited days: 100 x 1.5 = 150. Months: 150 / 30 = 5. At sign-off Sam gets the certificate of discharge and a testimonial signed by the master, showing the boat's official number, 40 GT, the voyage classification, 100 days worked and 12-hour watches. Two years later the master has retired. Sam still has the paper, so the time still counts.

## Exam traps

- Thinking only the company keeps your sea service record. ==You must keep one too.==
- Thinking every vessel needs an official log book. It starts at ==100 GT== on the listed voyages.
- Leaving the official number or gross tonnage off a testimonial.
- Sending documents the day before the exam. It is ==two weeks==.
- Mixing the retention times: official log ==five years==, radio record ==12 months==.
