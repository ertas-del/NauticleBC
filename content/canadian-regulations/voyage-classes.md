# Voyage classes and BC sheltered waters

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from section 1 and Schedules 1 and 2 of the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations (SOR/2021-135). The Marine Personnel Regulations repeat the same definitions. Schedule lines and boundaries change; check the live text before you rely on a line for a real voyage.

## What you need to know

Canadian regulations do not talk about "inshore" or "offshore". They talk about **voyage classes**. A voyage class is a legal box defined by two things: where you are going and how far from shore you will ever be. Nearly every rule in this module (what certificate the master needs, how many crew, what training they hold, what equipment you carry, how many hours of rest) is written by voyage class. So the first skill is to read a voyage and put it in the right box.

The five classes, from smallest to widest, are:

| Class | Short rule |
|---|---|
| ==Sheltered waters== | Rivers above the tide or lakes where you cannot be more than ==2.5 nautical miles== from shore, plus listed waters and listed ferry routes |
| Inland | Fresh water voyages within the inland waters of Canada that are not sheltered |
| ==Near coastal, Class 2== | Always within ==25 nautical miles of shore== and within ==100 nautical miles of a place of refuge== |
| ==Near coastal, Class 1== | Between listed North American and Caribbean places, always ==north of 6 degrees N== and within ==200 nautical miles of shore== or over the continental shelf |
| ==Unlimited== | Everything else |

Much BC small-vessel work falls in the first and third rows, so learn those two best. The inland class covers the fresh waters of Canada, including the St. Lawrence River as far seaward as the lines named in the definition.

## Sheltered waters in detail

A sheltered waters voyage is any of three things:

1. On a ==river above tidal waters== or a ==lake==, where it is impossible to be more than ==2.5 nautical miles== from the closest shore. Okanagan Lake is also listed for BC in Schedule 1 (Item 6), all year.
2. On the ==waters listed in Schedule 1== during the ==period== shown there.
3. A ==ferry run== between two or more of the points listed in Schedule 2, during the period shown.

The exam trap is that the Schedule 1 BC entries are **narrow**. For example, Item 7 is not "the Strait of Georgia". It is the waters of the Strait of Georgia, Burrard Inlet and the Fraser River that are north of the Canada-US border at the 49th parallel and east of longitude 123 degrees 22.666 minutes W, excluding Boundary Bay, and not already covered by items 1, 2 and 8. A boat in the middle of the Strait is out of sheltered waters.

Other BC entries you should recognise, each with its boundaries in the Schedule:

- Item 1: Vancouver Harbour east of the line from Point Atkinson to the "QA" buoy and on to the North Arm Jetty and Point Grey, including False Creek and Indian Arm.
- Item 3: Juan de Fuca Strait inside a line south from the mainland along 123 degrees 18.3 minutes W to the Trial Islands, then to Albert Head, including Esquimalt Harbour and the streams flowing into Victoria Harbour.
- Item 5: Nanaimo Harbour (with the stated inner lines).
- Item 8: Howe Sound north of the Gower Point to Cape Roger Curtis line, but ==only June 1 to September 30==. In winter that water is not sheltered.
- Item 12: the Gulf Islands bays, channels, inlets and passages west of the described line from Dodd Narrows through Gabriola, Valdes, Galiano, Mayne, Saturna and Sidney Islands.
- Item 13: Clayoquot Sound east of the Sharp Point to Cox Point line; Item 14: Sooke Harbour, Basin, Inlet and Bay.

The Schedule 2 BC ferry list includes routes such as Quathiaski Cove to Campbell River, Swartz Bay and the southern Gulf Island terminals, Horseshoe Bay with Snug Cove and with Langdale, Nanaimo Harbour to Descanso Bay, and Saltery Bay to Earls Cove, all for the whole year.

## Near coastal voyages

A ==near coastal voyage, Class 2== is one that is not sheltered or inland and during which the vessel is ==always== 25 nautical miles or less from shore in waters contiguous to Canada, the United States (except Hawaii) or Saint Pierre and Miquelon, and 100 nautical miles or less from a place of refuge. The word "always" is the test. A single leg that goes to 26 miles offshore turns the whole voyage into something wider. The regulation does not define "place of refuge" in the section I read; ask your instructor how that is applied.

A ==near coastal voyage, Class 1== is wider: not sheltered, inland or Class 2, between listed places in North America and the Caribbean area, ==always north of 6 degrees N==, and within ==200 nautical miles of shore== or above the continental shelf. An ==unlimited voyage== is anything that is none of the other four.

## Which certificate covers which?

A certificate issued for a wider class also covers the narrower ones (Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations s. 14). A Class 2 certificate covers sheltered and inland voyages; Class 1 covers sheltered, inland and Class 2; unlimited covers all four. A certificate for an inland voyage also covers a sheltered voyage in the same inland waters. The Minister may also add terms and conditions to a certificate limiting the voyage, considering factors such as wave height, wind, visibility, proximity to a place of refuge and water temperature (s. 16).

## Why it matters for you

The class decides things you will read in the next lessons. Which first-aid certificate is needed (the marine basic or advanced first aid rule in the Marine Personnel Regulations differs by class), whether the hours-of-rest rule is s. 320 or s. 321, and the extra equipment tables all key off it. Your own certificate is tied to it too: for a Master, Limited certificate the sea service must be earned on vessels engaged on voyages that correspond to those the certificate vessel is permitted to engage on (MPR s. 131). Sea service in a Class 2 boat does not automatically count for a Class 1 vessel.

## A BC example

A 14 m tour boat runs between Indian Arm and False Creek all year. Both are inside Schedule 1 Item 1, so the voyage is sheltered. In July the same boat runs up Howe Sound to Squamish. That water is sheltered until September 30 under Item 8, but the same run on October 15 is not. If the boat sets out from Tofino and runs 18 nautical miles offshore to a whale sighting and returns, always within 100 nautical miles of a place of refuge, it is a near coastal voyage, Class 2.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the whole Strait of Georgia as sheltered. Only the corner described in Item 7 is.
- Forgetting that some sheltered waters, like Howe Sound, are sheltered only in summer.
- Reading "25 nm from shore" as a maximum average rather than a limit that applies at all times.
- Assuming a lower class certificate covers a higher class. It is the other way round.
- Counting sea service on any boat. The voyage must correspond to the certificate you want.
