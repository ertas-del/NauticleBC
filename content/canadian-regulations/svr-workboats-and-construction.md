# Workboats, construction and compliance

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from the Small Vessel Regulations (SVR), SOR/2010-91, Parts 5 to 8, and the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations. The passenger-vessel lesson covers Part 4; this one covers everything else a small working boat has to meet.

## What you need to know

A ==workboat== is defined by what it is not: a vessel that is ==not a passenger-carrying vessel, not human-powered and not a pleasure craft== (s. 1). The SVR cover workboats of ==15 GT or less== and ==never cover fishing vessels== (s. 2). A ==tug== is a vessel built or converted mainly for towing (log salvage and oil boom vessels excluded), so a small tug is a workboat with extra rules.

Four layers apply, and the exam likes asking which layer catches which vessel.

| Layer | Section | Who it catches |
|---|---|---|
| Part 5 equipment | ss. 500 to 521 | every workboat of 15 GT or less |
| Part 6 critical safety | ss. 600 to 609 | vessels **not** caught by Part 7, for example tugs |
| Part 7 construction | ss. 700 to 742 | engine-driven vessels built, rebuilt or imported for Canadian use; **not tugs** |
| Part 8 compliance notice | ss. 800 to 812 | same vessels as Part 7; **not tugs** |

## Part 5: equipment by length

The tables are close cousins of the passenger-boat ones, with a few differences worth knowing.

- ==Owner's notice (s. 502)==: before first service the owner informs the Minister of the intention to operate, the physical characteristics and the nature of the operation. The Minister can ask for the information later (s. 503).
- ==Lifejackets and throwing gear (s. 506)==: a lifejacket per person (inherently buoyant if the wearer is under 16, s. 505), a reboarding device unless the climb is ==0.5 m or less==, and throwing gear by length: ==15 m buoyant heaving line== to 6 m; line ==or== lifebuoy on a 15 m line over 6 to 9 m; ==both== over 9 to 12 m; over 12 m the line plus a lifebuoy with a ==self-igniting light== or on a 15 m line.
- ==Visual signals (s. 507)==: flashlight plus ==3 / 6 / 12== pyrotechnic signals (to 6 m, over 6 to 9 m, over 9 m; no more than 6 smoke in the 12). A tug of 6 m or less inside a recognized log booming ground need not carry them.
- ==Life rafts (ss. 508, 509)==: a workboat ==over 12 m== carries life rafts for everyone, though a buoyant apparatus will do if the water is ==above 15 degrees C==. A ==tug over 8.5 m with two or more persons== aboard carries life rafts for all. A tug's trigger is lower than the 12 m trigger for other workboats.
- ==Anchor and bilge (s. 510)==: to 9 m a manual propelling device or an anchor with ==15 m== of rode, and a bailer or manual pump; over 9 to 12 m anchor with ==30 m== and a manual pump; over 12 m ==50 m== and a manual pump.
- ==Navigation (s. 511)==: sound-signalling device or appliance, navigation lights, and a magnetic compass meeting the Navigation Safety Regulations; a boat of 8 m or less that stays in sight of seamarks need not carry a compass.
- ==Fire (ss. 512 to 516)==: 1A:5B:C to 6 m; 2A:10B:C over 6 to 12 m with a 10B:C at the engine space entrance; 2A:20B:C over 12 m. A boat of 6 m or less with an enclosed engine space needs direct-discharge provision and heat and fire detectors with an 84 dB alarm (the same numbers as the passenger lesson).
- ==Procedures (s. 520)==: owner and operator establish emergency procedures for the lifesaving and fire equipment and the crew must practise them.

### Towing extras (s. 521)

A towing workboat must also have: ==two lifejackets in the wheelhouse== (two more in the engine space if it is normally occupied); a way to ==release or cut the tow line immediately==; ==two means of escape== from the wheelhouse to the outside, placed so one is usable when heeled; a ==clear deck aft of the towing point== so the line swings freely; and, if decked, watertight construction aft of the towing point or engine space, whichever is further forward, with rapid drainage. An open boat needs positive buoyancy when swamped or a high gunwale. Girding is explained in the towing lesson in seamanship.

## Part 6 and Part 7: stability and construction

Section 601(2) puts a duty on both the ==owner and the operator== of any non-pleasure vessel: it must have ==adequate stability and structural strength== for its intended operations. That is the legal hook for a loading or stability complaint. Part 6 also sets basics such as means to shut off underwater penetrations (s. 602), powered ventilation added to natural ventilation for an enclosed gasoline engine space (s. 603), and no gas or LPG appliance installed on a passenger-carrying vessel (s. 604).

Part 7 sets the construction requirements, which build on the Construction Standards for Small Vessels (TP 1332). It applies to engine-driven vessels built, rebuilt or imported for use in Canada but ==not to tugs== (s. 700(4)). Older non-pleasure vessels are sorted by date: built before April 1, 2005 they conform to the 2004 edition of the standards or an equivalent; between that date and the Regulations coming into force on April 29, 2010 they conform to the 2004 edition; after that they meet the construction requirements of Part 7 (ss. 707 and 708). The ==critical safety elements== (s. 708(8)) are watertight and weathertight integrity, structural strength, underwater penetrations, buoyancy and stability, water-freeing, fall protection, fuel systems, ventilation, fuel-burning installations, ignition-protected electrics, bilge pumping and fire safety.

Two owner duties are testable. ==Stability must be adequate and the owner must demonstrate it if the Minister asks== (s. 716). And a ==major modification== (one that substantially changes capacity, size or a system, or that affects watertight integrity or stability) must meet the construction requirements as they read when the work started, and the owner must tell the Minister (s. 710).

## Part 8: the compliance notice

A compliance notice is a durable plate or label, in English and French, permanently fixed where it is plainly visible from the operating position (s. 801(6)). For a non-pleasure vessel the owner must have one before operating it: ==6 m or less== attesting compliance with the construction requirements; ==over 6 m== attesting compliance with the construction requirements for vessels other than pleasure craft (s. 805). For vessels of 6 m or less the notice also gives the ==maximum load, maximum persons and maximum outboard power== (s. 802(1)(g)). Nobody may remove, deface or alter it except as the Regulations allow, or attach a false one (s. 810). If it becomes illegible, the owner may replace it after informing the Minister and must keep the original or a photo (s. 811). ==Part 8 does not apply to tugs== (s. 800(2)(d)).

## Where the SVR end

Above 15 GT, or at 15 GT or less with more than 12 passengers, the vessel falls under Division 2 of the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations and must hold the applicable ==safety certificate== to engage on a voyage (ss. 11 and 12). The documents lesson picks this up.

## A BC example

You will skipper a 9.5 m, 6 GT aluminum tug with a crew of two, pushing small barges around Howe Sound. List Part 5 for her. She is over 9 m, so: lifejackets for 2 and a reboarding device unless the climb is 0.5 m or less; heaving line **and** lifebuoy on a 15 m line; flashlight plus 12 pyrotechnics (no more than 6 smoke); because she is a tug over 8.5 m with two persons aboard, a life raft for 2; anchor with 30 m and a manual bilge pump; sound device, lights and compass; a 2A:10B:C extinguisher with a 10B:C at the engine space entrance, an axe and a bucket; and the towing extras. Part 7 and Part 8 do not apply to her as a tug, so she needs no compliance notice, but Part 6 requires adequate stability for pushing, and the owner must have informed the Minister before first service.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a tug as a Part 7 vessel with a compliance notice. Tugs are outside Parts 7 and 8.
- Quoting the passenger-vessel life raft rule for a workboat. A workboat's trigger is over 12 m; a tug's is over 8.5 m with two or more aboard.
- Forgetting that the SVR never apply to fishing vessels.
- Forgetting the owner's notice to the Minister before first service.
- Reading "adequate stability" as the owner's duty alone. It is the owner and the operator.
