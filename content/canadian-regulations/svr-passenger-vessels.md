# Small Vessel Regulations: passenger vessels up to 15 GT and 12 passengers

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from Part 4 (ss. 400 to 420) of the Small Vessel Regulations, SOR/2010-91 (SVR), plus ss. 2, 20 and 1001. The tables below are shortened; the live text has footnotes, approval standards and exceptions. Nothing here replaces the Regulations or an inspector's decision.

## What you need to know

Most BC water taxis, small charters and sightseeing boats are covered by one Part of one set of regulations. Part 4 of the SVR applies to a ==passenger-carrying vessel of not more than 15 gross tonnage that carries not more than 12 passengers== and is not human-powered. Above either number the vessel moves into the inspection certificate regime. The SVR do not apply to fishing vessels at all (s. 2(2)). A vessel is a "passenger-carrying vessel" if it usually carries or is carrying one or more passengers, so one paying guest is enough.

Part 4 splits the duties. The **operator** briefs passengers and makes the departure report. The **owner** notifies the Minister before first service. Both must have emergency procedures that the crew practises (s. 420). Everyone must keep the required equipment protected, securely stowed, and, in a locker, clearly marked outside (s. 419). Section 3 prohibits operating a vessel without the required equipment on board and meeting the Regulations.

## Duties before you leave the dock

**Briefing (s. 401).** Before the vessel leaves a place where passengers embark, the operator must ==brief all passengers== in one or both official languages, as their needs require. The briefing covers where the lifejackets are (and ==children's lifejackets==), where the survival craft are, the nearest lifejackets and survival craft for each area of the vessel, the use of lifesaving appliances, signals and safety equipment, the safety measures to take (protecting limbs, avoiding ropes and docking lines, and how the movement and grouping of passengers affects stability), and fire and explosion prevention. During the briefing the operator must ==demonstrate how to don each type of lifejacket==.

**Person-on-board report (s. 402).** Before departure the operator reports the ==number of persons on board== to a person ashore who has been designated to communicate with search and rescue authorities. In a remote area where that is impossible, the operator leaves the voyage information in a place ashore that is known and readily available to search and rescue. A passenger vessel working from a support vessel may designate someone on the support vessel.

**Cold water (s. 403).** If the water temperature is ==below 15 degrees C== and the vessel does not carry a life raft, the operator must carry equipment, or have procedures, that protect everyone from hypothermia and cold shock after swamping, capsizing or falling overboard.

**Other duties.** The vessel must be designed and equipped for its area of operation and must not be operated beyond its design limits (s. 404). Except in an emergency, no passengers may be on board the vessel or the tow while it is towing (s. 404(3)). Before first service the owner informs the Minister of the intention to operate, the physical characteristics and the nature of operation (s. 405). A first aid kit is required (s. 407).

## Equipment by length

Everyone on board needs a lifejacket of appropriate size, inherently buoyant for anyone under 16, and the vessel needs a ==reboarding device== unless the climb to reboard is ==0.5 m or less== (ss. 408 and 409). The rest depends on length.

| Item | Up to 6 m | Over 6 m to 9 m | Over 9 m to 12 m | Over 12 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Throwing gear (s. 409) | Buoyant heaving line, 15 m or more | Heaving line, or a lifebuoy on a 15 m buoyant line | Heaving line and a lifebuoy on a 15 m buoyant line | Heaving line, and a lifebuoy with a self-igniting light or on a 15 m buoyant line |
| Flashlight and pyrotechnics (s. 410) | Watertight flashlight, ==3== pyrotechnics (no smoke) | Flashlight, ==6== pyrotechnics (no smoke) | Flashlight, ==12== pyrotechnics, not more than 6 smoke | Same as over 9 m |
| Portable extinguishers (s. 414) | ==1A:5B:C== (a second one if there is a cooking, heating or refrigerating appliance) | ==2A:10B:C== (a second with an appliance) and a ==10B:C== at the engine space entrance | 2A:10B:C, another at each access to an appliance space, 10B:C at the engine entrance, fire axe, fire bucket | 2A:20B:C, more at appliance and accommodation spaces, 20B:C at the engine entrance, fire pump, hose and nozzle, axe, two buckets |

Anchor and bilge pumping use different length bands (s. 412):

| Length | Anchor and rode | Pumping |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 9 m | Either a manual propelling device, or an anchor with at least ==15 m== of cable, rope or chain | Bailer or manual bilge pump |
| Over 9 m to 12 m | Anchor with at least ==30 m== | Manual bilge pump |
| Over 12 m | Anchor with at least ==50 m== | Manual bilge pump |

Other points:

- ==Pyrotechnic distress signals expire four years after the date of manufacture== (s. 20).
- A fire bucket is metal with a round bottom and a hole in the centre, painted red, with a lanyard long enough to reach the water, and holds at least 10 L (s. 18).
- A ==life raft== for all persons on board is required unless the vessel is ==not more than 8.5 m==, is on a ==sheltered waters voyage==, or is within ==2 nautical miles of the shore of a river or lake== (s. 411).
- Navigation equipment (s. 413): a sound-signalling device or appliance meeting the Collision Regulations (above 12 m it must be an appliance), navigation lights, and a magnetic compass meeting the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020. Up to 9 m the lights are needed only when operating after sunset, before sunrise or in restricted visibility; above 9 m the table lists them without that condition. A compass is not required on a vessel of 8 m or less that stays within sight of seamarks.

## Fire protection details

For a vessel of ==6 m or less== with an enclosed engine space, there must be a provision to discharge an extinguisher straight into the engine space without opening the access (s. 415), and a ==heat detector in each engine space== wired to a ==red visual alarm== and an ==audible alarm of at least 84 dB== at the operating position, with a green power light. Fire detectors in accommodation and service spaces need a built-in audible alarm of ==at least 84 dB== (s. 418). Over 6 m, a vessel with a provision for direct discharge in place of a fixed system carries the engine-space extinguisher of s. 416 (s. 417).

That extinguisher must contain ==1.2 kg of carbon dioxide per cubic metre== of engine space volume, or enough clean agent to protect equivalently, and discharge completely in ==not more than 60 seconds== (CO2) or ==10 seconds== (clean agent). It is in addition to the extinguishers in the s. 414 table.

For gasoline power: no one may start a gasoline-powered vessel until the ==engine space blower has been run for not less than four minutes== immediately before starting (s. 1001).

## Worked example: an 11 m water taxi

An 11 m aluminium water taxi carries up to 10 passengers, is 9 GT, has a diesel engine and runs in False Creek and Vancouver Harbour all year. That water is listed as sheltered (Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations, Schedule 1, Item 1). Assume the January water temperature is about 8 degrees C. Work out the list from the over 9 m to 12 m band.

1. First aid kit.
2. Lifejackets: one of appropriate size for each of the 10 passengers and the crew, inherently buoyant for anyone under 16. Reboarding device unless the climb to reboard is 0.5 m or less.
3. Throwing gear: a buoyant heaving line of 15 m or more, and a lifebuoy on a 15 m buoyant line (over 9 m to 12 m band).
4. Watertight flashlight and 12 pyrotechnic signals, no more than 6 smoke, all within four years of manufacture.
5. Life raft: not required, because the voyage is a sheltered waters voyage (the 8.5 m exemption would not help, since the boat is 11 m). Water below 15 degrees C and no life raft means cold-water measures under s. 403, such as equipment on board or procedures to protect everyone from hypothermia and cold shock.
6. Anchor with at least 30 m of rode and a manual bilge pump.
7. Navigation: sound signal device or appliance, navigation lights, magnetic compass.
8. Fire: 2A:10B:C extinguisher, a second at each access to a galley or heater space if there is one, a 10B:C at the engine space entrance, a fire axe and one fire bucket.

If the same boat made a run outside the listed sheltered waters, for example 12 nautical miles off the coast, the life raft exemption would be gone and a life raft for everyone would be required.

## Common mistakes

- Reading "12 passengers" as the only limit. The 15 GT limit applies too.
- Forgetting the demonstration. The briefing must include showing how to don each lifejacket.
- Treating the sheltered waters life raft exception as covering any water that looks sheltered. It means the legal sheltered waters voyage.
- Mixing up the lengths: 6 m and 9 m are the band edges for flares, 9 m and 12 m for anchor rode.
- Forgetting pyrotechnic expiry: four years after manufacture, not after purchase.
