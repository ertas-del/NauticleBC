# Navigation equipment rules

**Study aid only.** Paraphrased from the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR), SOR/2020-216, Part 1. The radio items (VHF-DSC, listening watch, navigation safety calls, radio certificates) are in the radio lessons; this lesson is the rest of the bridge kit.

## What you need to know

Every number in this lesson is a trigger: a length, a gross tonnage, a type of vessel or a voyage class. The exam asks "what does this vessel need?", and the trick is to read the trigger before the equipment.

First, which division? ==Division 2== (SOLAS Chapter V standard) catches Canadian vessels of ==500 GT or more==, Canadian vessels of ==150 to under 500 GT on international voyages== and foreign SOLAS vessels, but not fishing vessels, cable ferries or pleasure craft (s. 103). Everything else, including every vessel on this ticket, falls under ==Division 3== (ss. 105 to 114), plus the extra equipment in Division 4 and the charts rules in Division 6. Older vessels built before July 1, 2002 that meet the former rules are outside Division 3 (s. 105(b)).

### Keep it working (s. 5)

The master must ensure the vessel does not start a voyage without the required equipment, unless under ==force majeure or to save life or property==. Equipment must be installed, tested and maintained so that it is in ==effective operating condition==; if it fails, the master restores it ==as soon as possible==; and if the port has no repair facilities, the master must plan and execute a safe voyage to one, allowing for the missing equipment.

### Compasses (ss. 106, 132)

- A ==standard magnetic compass==, independent of any power supply, showing the heading at the main steering position. Exceptions: a vessel ==8 m or less== that navigates within sight of navigation marks, and cable ferries.
- A vessel ==under 150 GT== may instead carry a ==steering magnetic compass== (plus a gyro-compass if on an international voyage). The 150 to 500 GT bands have their own options by voyage class.
- Compasses must be ==properly compensated==, with the ==table or curve of residual deviations== near them, and the vessel needs a means of ==correcting headings and bearings to true== (pleasure craft under 150 GT excepted). A means of communication between the compass position and the position from which the vessel is normally navigated is also required.
- At an inspection of a non-pleasure vessel the master shows the ==most recent deviation card for each compass, signed and dated by the master and a deck officer==, or a signed statement that the compasses are satisfactory (s. 132).

### The 150 GT and 300 GT lists (ss. 109, 110)

| Trigger | Required |
|---|---|
| ==150 GT or more== | GNSS receiver; spare magnetic compass interchangeable with the main one; pelorus or compass bearing device on NC1, unlimited or international voyages; daylight signalling lamp on international voyages |
| ==300 GT or more== | echo sounder; 9-GHz radar; speed-and-distance device unless sheltered waters; if under 500 GT, an automatic tracking aid or ARPA and a gyro-compass or transmitting heading device |

A 300 GT or more Canadian vessel also needs a two-way voice communication system between the conning position, working stations, engine room controls, emergency steering position, and the master's and chief engineer's accommodation (s. 120). None of these lists touches a 45 GT BC tug, which is why the towboat rules below matter more for this ticket.

### AIS (s. 118)

==Class A== is mandatory for: a non-pleasure vessel of ==20 m or more==; a vessel carrying ==more than 50 passengers==; a vessel carrying dangerous goods under the IMDG Code; a vessel carrying pollutants in bulk; a dredge or floating plant that is a collision hazard; and a ==towboat of 8 m or more==. Any other vessel on a voyage other than sheltered waters needs ==Class A or Class B== if it is a ==passenger vessel== (more than 12 passengers) or is ==8 m or more and carries a passenger==. A graphic display of AIS targets is required. The AIS must be ==operating at least 30 minutes before departure and for the entire voyage== (subject to exceptions for protection of navigational information).

### Towboats and searchlights (ss. 121, 125)

A Canadian ==towboat of 5 GT or more== needs a radar on an ==inland or NC2== voyage. On ==NC1== it needs an ==echo sounder and two independent radars==; on an ==unlimited== voyage, those plus a ==gyro-compass==. The requirements do not apply to an emergency tow on an exceptional basis. Separately, a vessel ==designed and constructed for towing and of more than 5 GT== carries ==two searchlights== that together sweep ==360 degrees==, each on an ==exclusive circuit==, with ==two spare bulbs== per light unless it is an LED light or a pair of identical lights shares one set (s. 121(5)). Boats that tow only to salvage logs are excepted from the searchlight rule. The lessons on towing in seamanship cover the SVR side.

### Lead line and signal flags (ss. 122, 123)

A vessel of ==20 m or more== on any voyage other than sheltered waters carries a ==hand lead line== at least ==46 m== long, clearly and accurately marked, with a lead of at least ==3.2 kg== that can be armed. Note it is a length trigger, not a tonnage one. A Canadian vessel of more than 150 GT on NC1 or unlimited voyages carries the International Code signal flag set.

### Charts, publications and voyage planning (ss. 142 to 146)

For each area to be navigated the master and authorized representative keep the ==latest charts at the largest scale== from the Canadian Hydrographic Service, the ==annual Notices to Mariners==, ==Sailing Directions==, ==Tide and Current Tables==, the ==List of Lights, Buoys and Fog Signals== and, if radio is required, the ==Radio Aids to Marine Navigation==. An illustrated table of life-saving signals is required on voyages other than sheltered waters (pleasure craft under 150 GT excepted). A ==vessel under 100 GT== is not required to carry them if the person in charge knows the charted routes, lights, buoys, marks and hazards of the area and the tides, currents, ice and weather well enough that safety is not compromised (s. 142(3)); how that exception sits with the chart-plotting duty in s. 144(3) is worth confirming with your instructor. Before a voyage the master must ==plan the voyage==, ==identify a route== allowing for routing systems, sea room, hazards and weather, and environmental protection measures, ==display it on a chart== and ==plot and monitor the position throughout== (s. 144). Charts and publications must be ==corrected from Notices to Mariners or navigational warnings== before use (s. 146). Navigation accessories and binoculars are also required (s. 145). The chartwork lesson on publications and corrections goes deeper.

## A BC example

You are master of a 17.5 m, 46 GT tug on a Near Coastal Class 2 run towing log barges across the Strait of Georgia. Walk the list. Compass: a magnetic compass at the steering position, with the deviation card signed by you and a deck officer. AIS: Class A, because she is a towboat of 8 m or more (not because of her length). Radar: yes, a towboat of 5 GT or more on NC2. Searchlights: two, because she is designed and built for towing and over 5 GT. Hand lead line: no, she is under 20 m. Echo sounder and GNSS: not required by the NSR for her (echo sounder arrives on NC1, GNSS at 150 GT), though sensible. Charts: she is under 100 GT, but you will carry them anyway. Now change the vessel to a 21 m, 70 GT crew boat, no tow: AIS Class A (20 m or more), hand lead line (20 m or more, non-sheltered), but no radar requirement from the NSR, because she is neither a towboat nor 300 GT.

## Common mistakes

- Applying the 150 and 300 GT equipment lists to a small vessel. They are tonnage triggers that small BC vessels mostly never reach.
- Mixing up the AIS triggers: 20 m for ordinary vessels, 8 m for towboats, and the 30 minute rule before departure.
- Calling the compass rule absolute. The 8 m within sight of marks exception and the steering compass option exist.
- Thinking radar is required on every tug. It is required on a towboat of 5 GT or more on inland or NC2 voyages, and two radars on NC1.
- Thinking every tug needs two searchlights. The rule is for vessels designed and constructed for towing and over 5 GT, and it excludes log salvage.
