# Navigation safety calls

**Study aid only.** The call is a legal duty with a precise form. Use the text of NSR 2020 s. 253 and Schedule 4 when you prepare.

## What you need to know

A **navigation safety call** is a short VHF broadcast to tell nearby vessels who you are, where you are, and what you mean to do, so that they can plan around you. It exists to prevent a surprise in a narrow channel, at a bend, in fog or at a berth. It is required, not optional, in the cases listed in the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR 2020), s. 253.

The vessel must be one that has to carry a VHF under s. 204, other than a dredge or floating plant (which has its own rule in s. 254). The call is made on the channel in Schedule 4: Channel 16 outside a traffic zone in BC, and the appropriate traffic channel inside one.

## The eleven circumstances

Section 253(1) lists them as (a) to (k). In plain words, call when:

1. ==a risk of collision exists== under the Collision Regulations;
2. ==another vessel's call== shows a close-quarters situation may develop;
3. you are in a ==narrow channel or fairway== and about to ==overtake or be overtaken==;
4. you are in ==doubt about another vessel's actions or intentions==;
5. you are ==nearing a bend or an obstruction== around which an approaching vessel cannot be seen;
6. you are approaching, in ==restricted visibility==, a ==charted route (including a ferry route)== or a concentration of vessels;
7. you are about to start a ==manoeuvre that could impede== other vessels;
8. you are ==fishing== with nets, lines, trawls or trolling lines, or ==restricted in ability to manoeuvre== in or near a routing system, and another vessel (other than a fishing vessel) is approaching;
9. you are approaching a ==dredge or floating plant== in or near a river, channel or fairway;
10. you are about to ==depart from a berth, anchorage, mooring area, booming ground, dredge or floating plant==;
11. any other time when a call is ==necessary for safe navigation== of your vessel or another.

Memory hook: collision, close quarters, overtaking in a narrow channel, doubt, bend, fog at a route, manoeuvre, fishing or restricted, dredge, departure, and a catch-all.

## The departure call has its own timing

For a departure (item 10) the call must be made ==15 minutes before== and again ==immediately before== leaving the berth, anchorage, mooring area, booming ground, dredge or floating plant (s. 253(2)). It is a **two-call** rule. A passenger vessel leaving a Nanaimo berth calls a quarter hour ahead and again as the lines come off.

## What the call must say, and in what order

Section 253(3): the call must be ==no longer than one minute== and contain **only information that is essential for safe navigation**, including the following **in sequence**:

1. the ==identity== of the vessel;
2. the ==location==;
3. the ==intended course of action==;
4. the ==present course and speed==; and
5. the ==status== if the vessel is one of: engaged in a towing operation; not under command; restricted in her ability to manoeuvre; engaged in fishing other than trolling; severely restricted in ability to deviate because of draft; engaged in pilotage; at anchor; aground.

An invented example: "All stations, all stations, this is the tug Sound Pilot. Southbound in the middle of Active Pass, about to alter to starboard for the south entrance, course one-three-five, eight knots, towing a barge. Sound Pilot, out."

That call is identity, location, intention, course and speed, then status, in under a minute.

## Exceptions

- A ==towboat== need not make the call for approaching a dredge or floating plant, or for departure, when it is manoeuvring in or around a berth, anchorage, mooring area or booming ground **and** it does not impede others **and** it makes a call ==15 minutes before its final departure== (s. 253(4)(a)).
- A vessel ==reporting to a traffic centre== need not make those two calls (s. 253(4)(b)).

## Radio does not replace the rules

Section 251 is short and important: the radio rules do **not** relieve a vessel of its duty to sound the **whistle signals** in the Collision Regulations, and do not permit any manoeuvre that breaks the **steering and sailing rules**. An agreement made by radio is still bound by the Rules. If you say "I'll take you on my port side" and the Rules require something else, the Rules win. Whistle signals are still sounded.

Anyone who makes a call who is **not** the person in charge of the deck watch must tell that person without delay (s. 250(2)).

## A BC example

The ferry in Active Pass, bound for the Gulf Islands, is rounding the bend at Mayne Island. A small vessel coming the other way cannot be seen around the point. Item 5 (nearing a bend or obstruction) tells both vessels to make a call on the appropriate channel in the traffic zone, giving identity, position, intention, course and speed. Each also sounds the signals the Collision Regulations require as the vessels come into view.

## Common mistakes

- Making the call after the situation has developed instead of before.
- Putting the information in the wrong order or running over a minute.
- Making only one departure call. The rule is 15 minutes before **and** immediately before.
- Thinking a radio agreement replaces a whistle signal.
- Giving a status the vessel does not have, or leaving out "towing" when you are.
- Making the call on Channel 16 inside a traffic zone, where the appropriate traffic channel applies.
