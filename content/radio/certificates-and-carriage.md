# Radio certificates and what the vessel must carry

**Study aid only.** Check the current regulations and your vessel's inspection record. The text below is from the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR) and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR 2020).

## What you need to know

Two questions drive this lesson: **does the vessel have to carry a VHF radio, and who is allowed to run it?** The first is in the NSR 2020, Part 2. The second is in the MPR. Both come up on the Master, Limited exam because every small passenger vessel, tug and charter boat on the BC coast is touched by them.

## When a VHF-DSC is compulsory

NSR 2020 s. 204 says a vessel must carry a ==VHF radio installation capable of DSC== if **both** of these are true:

1. Its voyage includes any part of ==sea area A1==, or is within VHF coverage of a Canadian Coast Guard station, or is ==more than five nautical miles from shore== on the sea coasts of Canada.
2. The vessel is ==over 8 m long==, ==carries passengers==, or is a ==towboat==.

Sea area A1 means water within the radiotelephone range of at least one VHF coast station where continuous DSC alerting is available (the next lessons cover the sea areas). The RAMN reports VHF-DSC coverage (A1) reaching about 40 nautical miles off the BC coast, so most BC voyages fall in A1.

There is one **exception**: a vessel that carries ==six passengers or fewer== and is ==not more than 8 m== long does not need the fixed set if it has a ==portable VHF handheld capable of DSC==. Pleasure craft are outside this Division altogether (s. 201(3)).

## Who must hold which certificate

The MPR s. 131 table for Master, Limited (under 60 GT) lists, among the things to provide to the examiner, ==an appropriate radio operator certificate if the vessel is equipped with a VHF radiotelephone installation==. It does not name the certificate there. Section 266 does:

- Section 266(1): on a vessel required to have a VHF, the master ensures a person holding a ROC with Maritime qualification (ROC-M), a General Operator Certificate, or the ROC-MC is in charge of the radio watch.
- Section 266(2): on a voyage that is **not** a sheltered waters voyage, where a VHF **with DSC** is compulsory, the person in charge of the radio watch must hold an ==ROC-MC==. The one exception is a ==Near Coastal Class 2== voyage where that VHF is the vessel's only radiotelephone; there a ==ROC-M issued after 1 January 2005== is enough.

So for most Master, Limited work on the BC coast, the safe answer is the **ROC-MC**. The ROC-MC is for people on compulsorily fitted commercial ships in sea area A1: minimum age ==18==, ==valid for life==, pass mark ==70 percent==. The ROC-M is the minimum certificate for operating any marine radio in Canada and is issued by the Canadian Power and Sail Squadrons under delegated authority; the ROC-MC also satisfies it.

On the deck watch itself, MPR s. 216(2)(a)(ii) says the person in charge holds a radio operator certificate appropriate to the class of vessel and area. NSR s. 250(1) adds that the master, or anyone the master authorises to keep the listening watch or make a navigation safety call, must be a **radio operator**: a certificate holder in charge of the watch.

## Things that must be on board

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Radio operator certificates | On board, readily accessible (s. 212) |
| Radio Aids to Marine Navigation (RAMN) | At the main operating position (s. 212) |
| Radio inspection certificate, if required | At the main operating position (s. 212) |
| Instruction card (such as TP 9878) | Visible at the main operating position (s. 214) |
| Operating and maintenance instructions, parts and tools | Easy to reach (s. 214) |
| A clock that is visible, reliable and accurate | At the main operating position (s. 214) |
| Antenna plan | Vessel 20 m or more (s. 213) |
| Spare antenna with cable | Vessel 20 m or more with one VHF-DSC only (s. 215) |
| Reserve source of energy and radio lighting | Vessel 20 m or more, more than 6 passengers, or a towboat (s. 217) |

A ==radio inspection certificate== is needed to start a voyage for a vessel that is ==20 m or more==, a ==towboat==, or a ==passenger vessel== on a voyage in A1 or more than 5 nautical miles from shore (s. 240).

## A BC example

A 14 m charter boat out of Nanaimo carries eight passengers across the Strait of Georgia. She is over 8 m and carries passengers, so she must have a fixed VHF-DSC. Her master holds a ROC-MC, which is what s. 266(2) asks for on a non-sheltered voyage. She needs a radio inspection certificate because she is a passenger vessel on a voyage in A1. She does not need a spare antenna or antenna plan because she is under 20 m. The RAMN and certificate must be at the radio.

## Common mistakes

- Treating "over 8 m" as a hard test on its own. Passengers or towing also trigger the rule at any length, subject to the handheld exception.
- Saying the rule needs an ROC-M for everyone. The MPR requires the ROC-MC on a DSC voyage other than sheltered waters, with the Class 2 exception.
- Confusing the **operator certificate** (the person) with the **radio inspection certificate** (the vessel).
- Assuming a pleasure craft follows Part 2. It is outside the Division.
- Forgetting that the exam asks for the radio certificate only "if the vessel is equipped with VHF"; no VHF, no certificate needed for that item.
