# Mayday, Pan-Pan and Sécurité

**Study aid only. Never practise on a live channel.** The formats come from the Coast Guard RAMN and the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR 2020). Follow the card at your own radio.

## What you need to know

Radio calls for help have three levels. Each has a fixed spoken form so that anyone can recognise it and act on it. The NSR 2020 (s. 243) says vessels must make distress, urgency and safety calls in line with ==articles 32 to 34 of the ITU Radio Regulations==. The RAMN spells out the voice forms that Canadian operators use. A printed card, such as ==TP 9878== (Distress and Safety Radiotelephone Procedures), must be displayed at the main operating position (s. 214).

| Level | Word | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Distress | ==MAYDAY== | Grave and imminent danger; immediate help wanted |
| Urgency | ==PAN PAN== | Very urgent message about the safety of a vessel or person |
| Safety | ==SECURITE== | Important navigational or weather warning |

All three go out on ==Channel 16== (or 2182 kHz on MF). If a DSC alert has been sent, the voice call follows it on 16.

## The distress call and message

A mayday has two parts, the **call** and the **message**.

==Call==: say in order

1. ==MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY==
2. ==THIS IS==
3. the ==vessel name, three times==
4. the ==call sign== or other identification
5. the ==MMSI== if a DSC alert was sent.

==Message==: say in order

1. MAYDAY
2. vessel name
3. call sign and MMSI (if DSC sent)
4. ==position==
5. ==nature of distress==
6. ==assistance needed==
7. other useful information (number aboard, vessel description, weather)
8. ==OVER==

The call "has absolute priority over all other transmissions". The controlling station ends the traffic with ==SEELONCE FEENEE== and signs off ==OUT==.

An invented example, in the right order: "MAYDAY, MAYDAY, MAYDAY. This is Pacific Dawn, Pacific Dawn, Pacific Dawn, call sign (letters), MMSI (nine digits). MAYDAY. Pacific Dawn. Position, two miles west of Chrome Island. Vessel on fire and abandoning to the life raft. Require immediate assistance. Four persons aboard. Over."

## Mayday relay

A station that hears a mayday may send a ==MAYDAY RELAY== on behalf of the vessel in distress. The RAMN lists these conditions:

- the vessel in distress ==cannot transmit== the message,
- the master of the relaying vessel thinks ==further help is needed==, and
- the relaying station has ==heard a mayday that no one has acknowledged==.

The RAMN text does not make clear whether all three must apply together; treat them as the situations that justify a relay and check the card.

A DSC relay is not sent after receiving a DSC distress alert on VHF or MF. Distress alerts and relays are sent with the master's permission.

## PAN PAN and SECURITE

PAN PAN is used for situations that are urgent but not grave and imminent: for example an engine failure in a dangerous place, or an injured person who needs medical advice (these examples are illustrative). The call is:

==PAN PAN== three times, then ==ALL STATIONS== (or the station called) three times, ==THIS IS==, vessel name three times, call sign, MMSI if sent by DSC, then the message. It is sent on the ==authority of the master== or person responsible. It outranks everything but distress. Anyone hearing it keeps listening for ==at least three minutes==.

SECURITE is for a message that other vessels should know for safe navigation or a weather warning. Say ==SECURITE== three times, ==ALL STATIONS== three times, ==THIS IS==, name three times, call sign, the short description, and the working channel the full message will be sent on, then OUT. Stations shift to that channel and listen until sure it does not concern them. A DSC all-stations safety announcement is not acknowledged.

## The master's legal duty

Under the Canada Shipping Act, 2001 s. 131 a master who receives a signal from any source that a person, vessel or aircraft is in distress must ==proceed with all speed to render assistance==, and ==inform the persons in distress or the sender of the signal== if possible. A master who is unable, or thinks it unreasonable or unnecessary, to go is not required to, but ==must enter the reason in the official log book==. The master of a vessel in distress can **requisition** one or more answering vessels, and a requisitioned master must continue to assist. The obligation ends when the master learns another vessel is complying with a requisition, or is told by the persons in distress or another vessel on scene that help is no longer necessary.

## A BC example

Off Cape Beale at 0230, the lookout on a tug hears on Channel 16 a call that begins "MAYDAY" and ends with a name and a position 8 miles to the south-west. The lookout does not answer. They write down the position and time, and wake the master at once. Under s. 131 the master must proceed with all speed unless it is unreasonable or unnecessary, and then log the reason. If no one is acknowledging, the master may judge that a relay is needed. Every step goes in the log.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the vessel name once instead of three times in the call.
- Leaving out the position or putting it last. Position is the first fact in the message.
- Sending a PAN PAN without the master's authority.
- Using MAYDAY for a problem that is not grave and imminent. Use PAN PAN.
- Relaying a mayday you heard on DSC.
- Forgetting that a master who does not go must log the reason.
- Calling a station "SECURITE" for a routine position report.
