# VHF basics: channels and distress calls

**Study aid only.** It is not a substitute for the radio course, for the Radio Aids to Marine Navigation (RAMN) or for the radio rules that apply to your vessel.

## What you need to know

The VHF radio is the bridge's ordinary voice link on the BC coast. It reaches other vessels, the coast guard centres at Victoria and Prince Rupert (called MCTS), and the continuous weather broadcast. A rating on watch may be the first person to hear it, so you need to know the few channels that matter and the three words that tell you how serious a call is.

## The channels that matter

| Channel | What it is for |
|---|---|
| ==Channel 16 (156.800 MHz)== | ==Distress, urgency, safety and calling only.== Make your first call here, then agree a working channel. |
| ==Channel 70 (156.525 MHz)== | ==Digital selective calling (DSC)==, the radio's automatic alert and call channel. You do not talk on it. |
| ==Channel 13== | Intership navigation (bridge-to-bridge) channel. In the Great Lakes it is the navigation safety channel; in BC the regulations name Channel 16 outside a traffic zone. |
| ==Channel 21B== | The ==continuous marine broadcast== on the BC coast: forecasts, warnings and notices, read on a loop. |

Why call on 16? The coast guard centres listen on Channel 16 and **do not monitor working channels**, so a first call there is the call they can hear. The channel is kept clear by rule: you call, agree a working channel, and shift to it. You do not chat on 16.

On a BC passage the radio is normally set to Channel 16 outside a traffic zone, and to the traffic channel for that zone inside one. That watch, and when it must run, is the subject of the next radio lessons.

## MAYDAY, PAN PAN, SECURITE

Three words in front of a voice message tell every listener how urgent it is.

- ==MAYDAY== means a vessel or person is in ==grave and imminent danger== and needs ==immediate assistance==. It has absolute priority over all other traffic.
- ==PAN PAN== means a ==very urgent message about the safety of a vessel or person==. It is below a mayday and above everything else.
- ==SECURITE== announces an ==important navigational or weather warning==.

Each is said three times to open the call. The full formats come in the distress lesson in the 60 GT radio module.

## The digital side, in one minute

Every modern marine VHF has DSC. A press of the red distress button for five seconds sends an automatic alert on Channel 70 with the vessel's identity and position, and the radio then switches to Channel 16 for the voice call. For this to identify your vessel the set must be programmed with the vessel's own ==nine-digit MMSI== (Maritime Mobile Service Identity). A radio with no MMSI, or the wrong one, sends an alert nobody can trace.

## Who may use the radio

Anyone who operates a marine radio in Canada needs at least a ==ROC-M== (Restricted Operator Certificate, Maritime). The ==ROC-MC== (Maritime Commercial) also meets that requirement and is the certificate usually held by commercial crew. On a vessel with a VHF installation, the ==person in charge of the deck watch must hold an appropriate radio certificate== under the Marine Personnel Regulations. A rating who is not certified listens, reports and fetches the officer. Do not transmit on the vessel's radio unless you are authorised and certified.

A radio must also not be used at more power than is needed to be heard clearly, except in distress. Lower power keeps the channel free for other vessels farther away.

## A BC example

You are on lookout on a coastal passage up Johnstone Strait. The bridge VHF is on Channel 16. You hear: "PAN PAN, PAN PAN, PAN PAN, all stations, this is fishing vessel ..." and a report of an engine failure drifting toward the shore. You do not answer the call. You tell the officer of the watch at once what you heard: the word PAN PAN, the name of the vessel if you caught it, and the position. The officer decides what to do. The note that matters is **how urgent** the first word said it was.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking Channel 70 is for voice. It is for the radio's digital calls only.
- Starting a long conversation on Channel 16 instead of calling and shifting to a working channel.
- Mixing up PAN PAN and MAYDAY. Mayday is grave and imminent danger; PAN PAN is a very urgent message about safety.
- Assuming the Great Lakes channel rule (Channel 13) is the BC rule. In BC outside a traffic zone the regulations name Channel 16.
- Pressing the distress button "to test" it. A test is a deliberate false alert; the cancel steps are in the false-alerts lesson.
- Using full power on a short call between two vessels a mile apart.
