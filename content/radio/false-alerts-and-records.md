# Cancelling false alerts and the radio record

**Study aid only.** The cancel steps depend on your radio, so use the instruction card and the maker's manual. The rules are from the Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020 (NSR 2020), ss. 236 to 247.

## What you need to know

False distress alerts waste rescue effort and can send aircraft and vessels to a place where nobody needs help. Most come from a pressed button, a leaning object on the radio, or a test. The law answers with a firm duty: **cancel at once**, record it, and keep the radio record that proves the radio was checked and used properly.

## Cancel immediately

NSR 2020 s. 244: a person who has ==inadvertently sent a distress alert==, or who, after sending one, finds that ==assistance is no longer needed==, must ==cancel it immediately==, following the Appendix to ==IMO resolution A.814(19)==, Guidelines for the Avoidance of False Distress Alerts.

The RAMN gives the VHF DSC steps in short form:

1. ==Switch off the transmitter== at once.
2. Set the radio to ==Channel 16==.
3. Broadcast to ==all stations==: the ==ship's name, call sign and MMSI== and the words ==cancel my distress alert of== followed by the ==date and time in UTC==.

Do not wait until you are asked. Do not switch off and say nothing. A coast guard centre that received your alert must be told it was false, or it will act on it. The master is told and the event goes in the radio record.

## The radio record

The radio record is the vessel's radio log. NSR 2020 s. 247 says it holds, among other things:

- the vessel's name, registration number, port of registry, gross tonnage, length and its GMDSS identities;
- the period covered, and the ==time of each entry==;
- a summary of all radio communications: date, time, frequency and details of ==distress, urgency, safety communications, unusual radio conditions== and other important incidents;
- the names of the radio operators and their certificates;
- the date, time and results of every ==check, test or inspection== required, and for each day at sea the operating condition (by normal traffic or a test call, with the vessel's position) and the state of the reserve source of energy; if equipment is not working, a note that the master was told;
- the ==time and method of cancelling== any inadvertent distress, urgency or safety transmission;
- maintenance and any corrective action.

==Each entry is initialled== by the radio operator. The record is kept ==at least 12 months after the last entry==, in a place accessible to an inspector, and a paper record is kept in its ==original format== (not recopied).

## Time rules

NSR 2020 s. 246: in voice communications the radio operator states the time as:

- ==UTC== if the vessel is on an ==international voyage==;
- the ==local time of the area== where the vessel is navigating, on any other voyage;

in the ==24-hour system, four figures== from 00:01 to 24:00, then the time zone identifier. For example, 1535 PDT. The RAMN adds that the preferred time for general use is UTC.

## Checks before and during the voyage

| When | What | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Before the voyage | Radio operator ensures the radio is in proper operating condition | 236 |
| Daily at sea | Assess the operating condition of the installation | 237(1)(a) |
| Weekly at sea | VHF, MF or MF/HF sets: by normal communications or a test call to a coast station capable of DSC | 237(1)(b) |
| After more than a week out of range | Make the test call at the first opportunity within range | 237(2) |
| Out of service over 30 days | Verify within the 7 days before the voyage and note it in the radio log | 237(5) |

A test uses the **antenna normally used** for the transmitter (s. 237(3)). A fault in equipment or reserve power must be restored **without delay** (s. 237(4)). Do not test by sending a distress alert. Use a proper DSC test call.

## A BC example

On a Strait of Georgia run the deckhand wipes the console, and the elbow bumps the red button. The alert starts. The mate sees the "distress sent" display, switches off the transmitter, sets the radio to Channel 16 and says, "All stations, all stations, all stations. This is Pacific Dawn, call sign (letters), MMSI (nine digits). Cancel my distress alert of two-three-zero-five UTC." The mate tells the master and enters the time and method of cancellation in the radio record. Later the mate guards the button with the cover.

## Common mistakes

- Letting the alert run, hoping nobody noticed.
- Cancelling only on the DSC panel and not by voice on Channel 16.
- Using local time for the cancel statement. The RAMN steps state UTC.
- Recopying the radio record neatly, which breaks the original-format rule.
- Throwing away the record after one year of the **first** entry; the 12 months run from the **last** entry.
- Testing by sending a real distress alert.
