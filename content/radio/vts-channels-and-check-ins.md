# Traffic channels and check-ins

**Study aid only, not a navigation aid.** Channels and calling-in points change. At sea use the current Radio Aids to Marine Navigation (RAMN) Part 3 and Notices to Mariners. The radio script below is invented.

## What you need to know

"Traffic" is the Vessel Traffic Services (VTS) desk at a Coast Guard MCTS centre. Inside a VTS zone you ==listen on the sector channel, not Channel 16==, and you report at set places. Who must report is in the lesson "Vessel Traffic Services on the BC coast". This lesson is the practical part: ==which channel, where, and what to say==.

## Victoria Traffic: four sectors, four channels

| Sector | Channel | Waters |
|---|---|---|
| ==1== | ==11== | Juan de Fuca Strait (Canadian side, from ==124°40′ W==), Haro Strait, Boundary Pass, Strait of Georgia up to the ==Merry Island, Ballenas Island, Cottam Point== line |
| ==2== | ==74== | ==Fraser River==, south (main) arm, from ==Sandheads== to ==Shoal Point== |
| ==3== | ==12== | Inside the ==Iona breakwater to Gower Point== line: all of ==Howe Sound and Burrard Inlet== (Vancouver Harbour) |
| ==4== | ==71== | North of the Merry Island and Ballenas line, through Discovery Passage and Johnstone Strait, to the ==Cape Sutil and Cape Caution== line |

The call name is ==Victoria Traffic== in all four.

Memory hook, south to north: ==11, 74, 12, 71==. "Strait, River, Harbour, North."

Two extra points:

- Sectors 1, 2 and 3 are watched by ==radar and AIS==. Sector 4 is watched by ==AIS only==. In Sector 4 your report matters more, because nobody sees you on radar.
- In Sector 1, ==Seattle Traffic on Channel 05A== handles calling-in points ==1, 3, 5 and 8== (Zone Limit, Race Rocks, Hein Bank, Patos Island). The two countries run those waters together.

Prince Rupert Traffic covers the north coast and the outer coast of Vancouver Island. Its sector table was not read for this lesson, so take its channels from the RAMN.

## Calling-in points

A ==calling-in point== (CIP) is a named place, with a number, where a participating vessel makes a ==position report==. They are listed in RAMN Part 3.

Some are marked ==Change==. That is where one sector hands you to the next, so you ==change channel== there. Examples from the 2026 table:

| No. | Name | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 12 | Sandheads | Change: Sector 1 (11) and Sector 2 (74) meet |
| 15A, 15B, 15C | Iona, Cape Roger Curtis, Gower Point | Change: Sector 1 (11) and Sector 3 (12) meet |
| 24 | Ballenas Island / Merry Island / Welcome Passage | Change: Sector 1 (11) and Sector 4 (71) meet |
| 11 | Active Pass | Report, Sector 1 |
| 20 | Vanterm | Report, Sector 3 |
| 26 | Cape Mudge | Report, Sector 4 |

Some points are one-way only. For example Dundarave is for eastbound vessels and Berry Point for westbound.

## What to say

The RAMN gives each report as a set of letters. You do not say the letters. You give the items in that order.

| Report | When | Items |
|---|---|---|
| ==Sailing plan, leaving a berth== | at least ==15 minutes before== | ==A== who, ==B== time, ==C or D== position, ==I== destination and ETA, ==L== route, ==N== next report, ==P== cargo, ==Q== defects, ==R== pollution |
| ==Position report== | at each calling-in point, and immediately before leaving a berth | ==A, B, C or D, N== |
| ==Final report== | on arrival at a berth, or immediately before leaving the zone | ==A, B, C or D, K== (arrival or exit) |
| ==Other report== | as soon as feasible (fire, grounding, defect, pollutant and so on) | A, B, C or D, and ==X== (the details) |

The core is always the same four: ==who, when, where, what next==.

## A worked check-in (invented)

The tug *Kelp Runner* is leaving a berth in Vancouver Harbour for Nanaimo.

1. **Before.** Radio on ==Channel 12== (Sector 3) at least 15 minutes before departure. Listen first.
2. **Sailing plan**, 15 minutes before: "Victoria Traffic, Kelp Runner." Wait for the answer. Then: name and call sign, time, berth, bound for Nanaimo with ETA, route by First Narrows and across the Strait, next report at departure, towing one empty barge, no defects, no pollution.
3. **Position report**, immediately before letting go: name, time, berth, departing now.
4. **Calling-in points** in Sector 3 on the way out: name, time, the point, next point.
5. **Change point** at the Iona to Gower Point line: Traffic tells you to go to ==Channel 11==. Change, listen, then call in on 11.
6. **Final report** on arrival at the Nanaimo berth: name, time, berth, secured.
7. **Log it.** Write time, channel, calling-in point and anything Traffic told you.

## Keep it short and correct

- Calls on a sector channel should ==not be longer than one minute==.
- Use the ==24-hour system==. On a voyage that is not international, give ==local time==. On an international voyage, UTC.
- Speak English, plain and in order. Do not chat.
- If you cannot get through, keep trying. The rule for lost communication is in the VTS lesson.
- Tip, not a rule: a vessel that does not have to report can still ==listen on the sector channel==. You hear where the ferries, tugs and ships are before you see them. Keep your required Channel 16 watch as the rules for your vessel say.

## Exam traps

- Mixing up 74 and 71. ==74 is the Fraser River. 71 is the north.==
- Thinking Vancouver Harbour is on 11. Inside the Iona to Gower Point line it is ==12==.
- Saying a position report needs your cargo. It needs only ==A, B, C or D and N==.
- Sending the berth sailing plan as you let go. It is due ==15 minutes before==.
- Forgetting that Sector 4 has ==no radar== coverage from Traffic.
