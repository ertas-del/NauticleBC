# The Master, Limited < 60 GT certificate

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. This lesson explains the certificate in plain words; the regulations and Transport Canada decide what applies to you.*

## What you need to know

The ==Master, Limited== certificate for a vessel of ==less than 60 gross tonnage== is the usual first master's ticket for small commercial vessels in British Columbia: water taxis, small tour and passenger boats, crew boats, workboats, small tugs and survey launches. It is a Transport Canada certificate of competency issued under the Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR).

Three ideas carry most of the exam questions about it:

1. **It is limited.** It only lets you be master of a vessel ==under 60 GT==, and only for the ==type, tonnage, area and period of operation== written on your certificate.
2. **It covers short voyages.** The only voyage types it covers are ==near coastal Class 2== and ==sheltered waters==.
3. **It is domestic.** It is ==not endorsed as meeting STCW==, the international standard for seafarers, so it is not for foreign-going work.

## The legal basis in plain terms

The MPR list the Master, Limited certificate as paragraph ==100(h)==. Section ==131== sets the requirements for the under-60 GT version (the over-60 GT version is in section 130, which asks for more, such as a radar simulator course if radar is fitted). A different section, section 212 (Table 1, item 16), says what the holder may do: act as master on a vessel of less than 60 GT, of a type, tonnage, area and period of operation specified on the certificate, on near coastal Class 2 and sheltered waters voyages.

"Specified on the certificate" matters. The examiner can write limits on your certificate based on the vessel you trained on and how you answered in the oral. A certificate for a 12 m water taxi in Howe Sound does not automatically let you run a 40 m passenger vessel in the Strait of Georgia.

## The two voyage types

**Near coastal voyage, Class 2.** A voyage that is not a sheltered waters voyage and during which the vessel is always:

- ==25 nautical miles or less from shore==, in waters contiguous to Canada, the United States (except Hawaii) or Saint Pierre and Miquelon, **and**
- ==100 nautical miles or less from a place of refuge==.

Both limits must hold for the whole voyage. A boat 20 nm from shore that is 120 nm from the nearest place of refuge is outside Near Coastal Class 2.

**Sheltered waters voyage.** One of these:

- on a river above tidal waters or on a lake, where it is impossible for a vessel to be more than ==2.5 nautical miles== from the closest shore; or
- on waters listed in a schedule to the Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations, during the period given for that item.

British Columbia has a long list. For example, Vancouver Harbour east of a line from Point Atkinson to the "QA" buoy and on to Point Grey, and Juan de Fuca Strait inside the line from the Trial Islands to Albert Head (which takes in Esquimalt Harbour), are listed for the whole year. Howe Sound north of the Gower Point to Point Atkinson line is listed only from 1 June to 30 September. The season matters: waters that are sheltered in summer may not count as sheltered in winter.

## Worked example: which voyage is it?

*Invented routes for practice.*

| Voyage | Distance from shore | Distance from place of refuge | Class |
|---|---|---|---|
| Harbour tour inside a listed sheltered area, all year | under 2 nm | under 5 nm | Sheltered waters |
| Crew run along a coast, never more than 12 nm out | 12 nm | 30 nm | Near coastal Class 2 |
| Delivery trip crossing open ocean, 40 nm from shore at the farthest point | 40 nm | 60 nm | Not Class 2 (over 25 nm from shore) |

The third voyage breaks the 25 nm limit even though the refuge distance is fine. A Master, Limited could not be master for it.

## A BC example

A passenger vessel of 18 GT runs from Vancouver Harbour to Bowen Island and Gibsons. Part of that route is inside the listed Vancouver Harbour sheltered waters, and the rest is open Strait of Georgia water. The harbour part is a sheltered waters voyage; the strait part is a near coastal Class 2 voyage (it is within 25 nm of shore and a place of refuge). A Master, Limited whose certificate names that vessel and area can work both, because the certificate covers both voyage types.

## Renewing the certificate

The Minister sets the period a certificate is valid, and it is renewed, not re-examined from scratch. Under section 106 you must meet **one** of these:

| Option | What it takes |
|---|---|
| Sea service | ==12 months== as master or mate in charge of the deck watch in the last ==5 years==, **or** ==3 months== in the last ==12 months== |
| Refresher plus exam | A ==refresher MED== course in the last 5 years, **plus** a ==practical and oral exam on general seamanship== for the vessel |
| Shore service | ==24 months== in listed marine-related shore jobs (for example harbour master, marine surveyor, examiner) in the last 5 years |

For a Master, Limited the sea service must be ==on the vessel(s) the certificate covers and in the area of operation== on it. Time on a different kind of vessel or in a different area does not count towards renewal.

## Exam traps

- Saying the certificate covers "any voyage" for a vessel under 60 GT. It covers only near coastal Class 2 and sheltered waters.
- Mixing up the two Class 2 limits: **25 nm from shore** and **100 nm from a place of refuge**. Both apply.
- Calling it an STCW certificate. It is not endorsed as meeting STCW.
- Forgetting the 2.5 nm limit that defines lake and river sheltered waters.
- Treating renewal sea service as "any sea service". It has to be on the certificate's vessel and area.
