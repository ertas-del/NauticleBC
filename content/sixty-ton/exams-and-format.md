# The written exams and the oral

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. NauticleBC is not an approved course and its practice exams are not the Transport Canada exams.*

## What you need to know

The Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR) section 131 does not list exam subjects. It says the exams are on ==subject matter appropriate to the area of operation and the type and gross tonnage of the vessel==, ==as set out in TP 2293==. TP 2293 is Transport Canada's publication on the examination and certification of seafarers.

In practice in British Columbia, the usual route for a Master, Limited < 60 GT is three things:

| Exam | What it tests | Reported format | Pass mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NS1: Navigation Safety Level 1** | Collision regulations (COLREGs), watchkeeping, buoyage, rules of the road | ==100 multiple-choice questions in 2 hours==, 1 mark each | ==70 %== |
| **CP1: Chartwork and Pilotage Level 1** | Chart plotting, position fixing, courses, tides and currents in pilotage | ==3 hours==: ==10 multiple-choice questions at 3 marks each== plus ==5 practical chartwork questions at 5 to 15 marks each== | ==70 %== |
| **Oral** | General seamanship and master's knowledge, in person with a Transport Canada examiner | Not a fixed paper; examiner judgement | Examiner judgement |

The NS1 and CP1 formats come from training-provider pages, and the 70 % figure comes from a 2019 draft of TP 2293. TP 2293 itself should be checked before you rely on any number here. The app's NS1 practice exam uses the same shape (100 questions, 120 minutes, 70 %) but is a practice format only.

## How you can show competence

A 2019 draft of TP 2293 says you can show NS1 or CP1 competence in one of two ways:

1. complete an **approved training course**; or
2. score at least **70 %** on the **Transport Canada exam**.

Many BC providers run a course and arrange for the TC exam on the last day. Which route is open to you, and whether a provider course replaces the TC exam for your certificate, is for Transport Canada to say.

## Fees and validity

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Written exam fee | ==$27.50== (MPR s. 121) |
| Oral or practical exam fee for a limited certificate | ==$27.50== (s. 121) |
| Fees are paid | each time an exam is taken |
| A pass is valid for | ==5 years== after the exam, then ==for life== once the certificate is obtained (s. 113) |
| Not following the exam procedure in TP 2293 | ==automatic fail== (s. 112) |
| Application stays valid | 1 year from the day the examiner receives it (s. 110) |

Fees can change, so check the current regulation or the Transport Canada fee page before you book.

## The order matters

The oral is ==taken last==, after all other requirements are in place. It is by appointment with an examiner and can be held on board your vessel with manoeuvres and drills. Practical planning: book NS1 and CP1 first, pass them within the 5 year validity, finish your sea service, MED, radio and first aid, and keep your application inside its 1 year window.

## The CP1 question

Some study sites list CP1 only for larger certificates (150 GT Mate, Master over 60 GT). Several BC training providers list CP1 as part of the Master, Limited < 60 GT route. The study site does not say CP1 is *not* required for the under-60 certificate, and the MPR hand the detail to TP 2293, which says the exams must suit the area of operation. So:

- plan for NS1, CP1 and the oral as the standard BC route;
- ask a Transport Canada Marine Safety office whether your certificate, in your area, needs less (for example, a sheltered-waters-only certificate may need less chartwork);
- do not skip CP1 on the strength of a web page.

This is an open question until a reviewer reads the current TP 2293 chapter for the certificate.

## What a time budget looks like

*Invented example.* Suppose you sit NS1 and plan to move through it at an even pace: 120 minutes for 100 questions is 1.2 minutes (72 seconds) a question. If you spend 90 seconds on each of the first 40, that is 60 minutes, leaving 60 minutes for 60 questions, exactly one minute each. Mark the hard ones, move on, and come back.

## A BC example

A candidate in Victoria finishes a 60-day season on a water taxi in April, passes NS1 in May, and writes CP1 in June. Both passes stay valid for 5 years, so there is no rush to book the oral the next week. But the application filed in March lapses after 1 year, so the oral must be booked inside that window or the application renewed.

## Exam traps

- Quoting 70 % for the oral. The oral has no published percentage; it is the examiner's judgement.
- Mixing up the two formats: NS1 is 100 questions in 2 hours, CP1 is 3 hours with a practical part.
- Forgetting that pass validity (5 years) and application validity (1 year) are different clocks.
- Assuming the oral can be taken before the other exams. It is last.
- Treating provider pages as the regulation. They are reports, not the law.
