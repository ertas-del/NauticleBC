# What you need before the oral

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. Check every requirement with Transport Canada before you spend money on courses.*

## What you need to know

Passing the exams is only one part of getting a Master, Limited < 60 GT certificate. The Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR) set a list of requirements. Section ==131== has the certificate-specific ones and sections ==110== and ==111== have the general ones. The oral comes last, after the rest of the file is complete.

## The checklist

| # | Requirement | Detail | Depends on vessel? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Age** | At least ==18== on the day you receive the certificate; sea service started after you turned ==16== | No |
| 2 | **Sea service** | At least ==2 months== of deck duties on vessels of at least the same GT, on voyages like the certificate's; or ==1 month== from an approved on-board training program | Yes (GT, voyage type) |
| 3 | **MED** (marine emergency duties) | See the table below | Yes (passengers) |
| 4 | **Radio** | An appropriate radio operator certificate ==if the vessel has a VHF radiotelephone== | Yes (VHF fitted) |
| 5 | **First aid** | ==Marine basic first aid== | No |
| 6 | **Medical** | A medical certificate or provisional medical certificate ==only if the vessel is passenger-carrying== | Yes (passengers) |
| 7 | **Qualified person** | A Canadian citizen or permanent resident | No |
| 8 | **Exams** | Written exams and oral set out in TP 2293 (see the exams lesson) | Subject matter fits the vessel and area |

## MED by vessel type

The MPR say which MED training goes with which vessel. In plain words:

| Vessel | MED needed (any one route) |
|---|---|
| **Passenger-carrying** | MED small passenger vessel safety; **or** MED basic safety plus MED small seasonal passenger vessel safety (certificated personnel); **or** the two seasonal passenger courses (certificated and non-certificated personnel) |
| **Not passenger-carrying** | MED ==basic safety==, **or** a ==practical examination on MED using the vessel's emergency equipment== |

Course names change. Training providers in BC now offer a domestic vessel safety course (MED DVS) that replaced the older A1 and A2 courses, and they say it covers these lines. The regulation names the subject matter, not the provider's course name, so ask Transport Canada if the match matters for your file.

## Radio

The MPR say "an appropriate radio operator certificate" if the vessel has a VHF radiotelephone. Many commercial vessels must carry VHF (Navigation Safety Regulations, 2020), so in practice most candidates need a marine radio certificate. Which one is "appropriate" depends on the vessel and area; ask the examiner or a training provider.

## Timing rules

- Documents (signed application, birth certificate, discharge books, sea service testimonials, other certificates) go to the examiner ==at least 2 weeks before your first exam==.
- The application stays valid for ==1 year== from the day the examiner receives it.
- Exam fees are paid each time an exam is taken.
- A passing grade on an exam is valid for 5 years, and for life once the certificate is issued.
- ==MED course currency==: the MED course should be completed within the ==5 years before the application date==. If it is older, the MPR accept a refresher in that period, or ==12 months of deck service== in the last 5 years (with a drills testimonial), or ==3 months== in the last 12 months (with a drills testimonial).

## Worked example: a candidate file

*Invented candidate, for practice only.*

Sam is 17 and works as a deckhand on an 11 GT water taxi that carries passengers. Sam turned 16 before starting the work, has logged 70 credited days, holds an out-of-date MED course from 7 years ago, has a VHF radio certificate and basic first aid, and plans to apply for a Master, Limited certificate for a similar water taxi.

| Item | Status | Problem? |
|---|---|---|
| Age | 17 now; will be 18 before issue | Fine if 18 on the day of issue |
| Sea service started at 16 or older | Yes | Fine |
| 2 months (60 days) on same-size vessel | 70 days | Fine |
| MED | 7 years old | **Needs refresher, or 12 months deck service in the last 5 years, or 3 months in the last 12, with a drills testimonial** |
| Radio, first aid | Held | Fine |
| Medical | Passenger-carrying vessel | **Medical certificate needed; none listed** |

Two gaps: the old MED course and the medical certificate. Sam fixes both before the oral.

## A BC example

A candidate in Nanaimo plans to run a 9 GT workboat that carries no passengers and has a VHF. They need the 2 months of deck service on vessels of at least 9 GT, MED basic safety (or a practical MED exam using the boat's own emergency equipment), the radio certificate, and marine basic first aid. Because the boat does not carry passengers, the MPR do not require a medical certificate for this certificate. If the same candidate later asks for a certificate covering a passenger vessel, the passenger MED route and a medical certificate come into play.

## Exam traps

- Saying a medical certificate is always required. Under the MPR it is needed only when the vessel is passenger-carrying.
- Saying MED is the same for every vessel. The passenger route is different from the non-passenger route.
- Counting sea service on a smaller vessel. The service must be on vessels of **at least** the same GT.
- Forgetting the 2 week lead time for documents.
- Confusing application validity (1 year) with exam pass validity (5 years).

## Check yourself

1. At what age do you need to be on the day the certificate is received, and when must the sea service have started?
2. Which two requirements depend on whether the vessel carries passengers?
3. What radio condition triggers the radio certificate requirement?
4. How long before the first exam must your documents reach the examiner?
5. Your MED course is 6 years old. Name one way to meet the currency rule.
