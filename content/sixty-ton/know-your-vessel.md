# Know your own vessel

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. Equipment lists differ by vessel, so this lesson gives you a way to find your own answers; it does not list your vessel's equipment.*

## What you need to know

Most certificates of competency say "this person can do this job". The Master, Limited < 60 GT says "this person can do this job on **this kind of vessel**, in **this area**". The certificate is valid for a vessel of a ==type, tonnage, area and period of operation specified== on it. That is why the oral is often on board your vessel and why the examiner looks at the vessel you intend to run.

Providers also report that examiners ask about the ==vessel's own systems== (==electrical, plumbing, propulsion==), its ==equipment== and its ==emergency gear==. You cannot learn that from a book: you have to learn your vessel.

## Which rules apply to your vessel

Size and passenger numbers decide which set of safety rules a Canadian vessel falls under. Two regulations matter for a typical Master, Limited candidate. The thresholds are in gross tonnage (GT) and passenger count.

| Regulation | Applies to (summary) |
|---|---|
| **Small Vessel Regulations (SVR)** | Pleasure craft; passenger-carrying vessels of ==not more than 15 GT carrying not more than 12 passengers==; workboats of ==not more than 15 GT==; human-powered vessels other than pleasure craft. Fishing vessels are excluded. |
| **Vessel Safety Certificates Regulations (VSCR), Division 2** | Vessels of ==more than 15 GT==, and vessels of ==15 GT or less carrying more than 12 passengers==. Pleasure craft, special-purpose vessels and some others are excluded. |

Put simply: a small boat with 12 passengers or fewer and 15 GT or less is an SVR vessel. A boat above 15 GT, or one that carries more than 12 passengers, is in the VSCR regime with its inspection and certificate system, and other regulations set its equipment. This lesson does not cover those equipment tables. Look up your own vessel's requirements and be ready to talk about them.

### Worked example: which regime?

*Invented vessels.*

| Vessel | GT | Passengers | Regime |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water taxi | 12 | 10 | SVR (15 GT or less, 12 passengers or fewer) |
| Water taxi, bigger cabin | 12 | 20 | VSCR Division 2 (more than 12 passengers) |
| Crew boat with no passengers | 14 | 0 | SVR as a workboat of not more than 15 GT |
| Tour vessel | 28 | 30 | VSCR Division 2 (more than 15 GT) |

## The deck watch on a small vessel

The Marine Personnel Regulations set the minimum deck watch (section 216) and say how the master counts (section 215). In plain terms:

- The ==person in charge of the deck watch== holds a certificate that authorizes watchkeeping and radio watch duties, and ==the appropriate radio certificate if the vessel has a VHF radiotelephone==.
- On a vessel of ==at least 5 GT==, there must be an ==additional person== on the deck watch. There are exceptions. One is a vessel of up to 500 GT with an unobstructed all-round view from the steering position, on a voyage of not more than five nautical miles inside the limits of a harbour, in good visibility. Check the exact wording for your case.
- The master ==counts as part of the deck watch== on a vessel of ==up to 1 000 GT== (and in the other cases the regulation lists). So on a small vessel the master can be the person in charge of the watch.
- The master can add to the minimum if "the ordinary practice of seamen" calls for it.

## A BC example

A candidate for an 11 GT water taxi in Vancouver is asked: "Show me how you would stop the engines from here, and tell me what happens to your lights if the main battery switch is off." The candidate walks to the helm, points to the shut-off, and says the lights are on the same bank as the main switch, so the batteries must stay on or a separate supply must be used. They also name the fuel valve and the place it is closed. That is the level of detail the oral rewards.

## Exam traps

- Studying a generic vessel and not your own.
- Assuming a certificate for one vessel covers all vessels under 60 GT. It names the type, tonnage, area and period.
- Mixing up the two thresholds: SVR is up to 15 GT and up to 12 passengers; VSCR Division 2 starts above 15 GT or above 12 passengers.
- Forgetting that the person in charge of the deck watch needs the radio certificate when VHF is fitted.
- Saying you "think" the equipment is on board. Know where it is.

## Check yourself

1. Why is the Master, Limited certificate tied to a vessel type, area and period?
2. A 13 GT vessel carries 14 passengers. Is it an SVR or VSCR Division 2 vessel?
3. What extra crew does a vessel of 5 GT or more normally need on its deck watch?
4. Who must hold the radio certificate on the deck watch?
5. Fill in the checklist for your own vessel and say it aloud.
