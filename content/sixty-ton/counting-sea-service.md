# Counting sea service

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. The examiner decides how your own service is credited. The numbers in the examples are invented.*

## What you need to know

The Master, Limited < 60 GT needs at least ==2 months== of sea service doing deck duties. The Marine Personnel Regulations (MPR) section 115 says how that is counted. It is not "calendar months on the payroll". It is **credited days**, and credited days come from **hours**.

Three rules:

1. ==8 hours of service = 1 day== of sea service.
2. For watches of other lengths, service is ==prorated up to a maximum of 12 hours== in that calendar day, and a ==12-hour watch = 1.5 days==.
3. To get months, ==divide the total credited days by 30==.

## Why 60 credited days

Two months is 2 x 30 = ==60 credited days==. That can come from different mixes of days:

| How the service was worked | Calendar days needed for 60 credited days |
|---|---|
| 8-hour days | 60 |
| 12-hour watches (1.5 days each) | 40 |

So 40 calendar days at 12 hours a day equals 480 hours of credit, the same as 60 days of 8 hours. The easiest check is hours: 60 days x 8 hours = 480 hours.

## Reading the proration rule

The MPR give two fixed points: 8 hours = 1 day and 12 hours = 1.5 days. For anything between or below, the Regulations say "prorated". The natural reading is a straight line: **credited days = hours / 8**, with hours capped at 12 for the calendar day. On that reading 10 hours = 1.25 days and 4 hours = 0.5 day. This straight-line reading is our interpretation of "prorated". Ask your examiner how they credit short or odd-length days before you rely on it.

Two limits:

- ==More than 12 hours in a calendar day earns nothing extra.== A 14-hour day counts as 12 hours (1.5 days).
- The cap is per **calendar day**. You cannot pile hours from two days into one.

## Worked examples

All numbers are invented. Credited days = (hours, capped at 12) / 8.

**Example 1: all 12-hour watches.**
30 days x 1.5 = 45 credited days. Months = 45 / 30 = ==1.5 months==. That is not enough for 2 months. It would need 40 days.

**Example 2: all 8-hour days.**
60 days x 1 = 60 credited days. Months = 60 / 30 = ==2.0 months==. Enough.

**Example 3: a mixed log.**

| Block | Days | Hours per day | Credit per day | Credited days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Harbour shifts | 20 | 8 | 1.0 | 20 |
| Charter work | 25 | 12 | 1.5 | 37.5 |
| Short runs | 10 | 10 | 1.25 | 12.5 |
| **Total** | 55 | | | **70** |

Months = 70 / 30 = 2.33 months. The 2 month requirement is met with 10 credited days to spare.

**Example 4: the cap.**
A 14-hour day counts as 12 hours, so it is 12 / 8 = 1.5 days, not 1.75.

**Example 5: how much is still missing?**

| Block | Days | Hours per day | Credit per day | Credited days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Deckhand shifts | 15 | 8 | 1.0 | 15 |
| Long watches | 12 | 12 | 1.5 | 18 |
| Half shifts | 8 | 6 | 0.75 | 6 |
| **Total** | 35 | | | **39** |

Months = 39 / 30 = 1.3 months. The shortfall is 60 - 39 = 21 credited days. At 1.5 days per 12-hour watch, that is 21 / 1.5 = ==14 more 12-hour days==, or 21 more 8-hour days.

## Other things that change the count

- **Junior officer service.** Sea service as a junior officer to a mate in charge of the deck watch is credited at ==two thirds of a day== for each day actually served (MPR s. 117). This rarely matters for a small-vessel master.
- **Deserting a vessel.** Service on a vessel the applicant deserted does not count (s. 118).
- **Other kinds of service.** Service on a vessel doing commercial work other than carrying passengers or handling cargo is assessed under Chapter 3 of TP 2293 (s. 116).
- **Approved training programs.** Service from an approved program of on-board training can be as little as ==1 month== for this certificate. A different counting rule applies to cadets in an approved cadet program (any amount of service in a calendar day counts as one day), so do not assume it applies to you.
- **Right kind of service.** It must be deck duties on a vessel of at least the same GT as the vessel the certificate is for, on voyages like the certificate's. Credited days on a smaller boat do not count towards this certificate.

## Testimonials: proving the service

You do not just claim sea service. You prove it with:

- **Testimonials** with the required signatures and at least the information set out in ==Schedule 4 or 5== of the MPR (which one depends on the kind of certificate); and
- **Discharge books and any certificates of discharge** for the service.

These go to the examiner with the rest of your documents at least 2 weeks before your first exam. In practice, keep a running log of dates, vessel name and GT, hours per day, duties and voyage type, and ask your master to sign off as you go. A gap in the log is much harder to fix two years later.

## A BC example

A deckhand on a 14 GT water taxi out of Granville Island works 8-hour days in winter and 12-hour watches in summer. Over a season they log 25 days at 8 hours and 30 days at 12 hours. Credited days: 25 + 30 x 1.5 = 25 + 45 = 70. Months = 70 / 30 = 2.33. They have the service for a certificate on a vessel of 14 GT or less on matching voyages. They would **not** have it for a 30 GT passenger vessel, because the service was on a smaller vessel.

## Exam traps

- Treating months as calendar months. The MPR divide credited days by **30**.
- Giving extra credit past 12 hours.
- Crediting a 12-hour watch as 1 day. It is **1.5** days.
- Counting service on a smaller vessel than the one on the certificate.
- Forgetting the testimonial: no signed testimonial, no credit.
