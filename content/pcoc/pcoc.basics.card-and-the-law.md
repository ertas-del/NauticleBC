# Your card and the law

To drive a motor boat for fun in Canada, you must prove you know the basic safety rules. This lesson covers who needs proof, how you get it, and the other papers that go with a motor boat.

## Who needs proof of competency

You need ==proof of competency== to operate a ==pleasure craft fitted with a motor== for fun. A pleasure craft is a boat used for recreation, not for work or for hire.

The rule applies to every motor boat, of any length and power. A canoe with a small ==electric trolling motor== counts. So does a sailboat with an engine, even when it is not running. Keep the proof ==on board==.

The rule does not apply in Nunavut or the Northwest Territories.

## What counts as proof

Any one of these is proof of competency:

- A ==Pleasure Craft Operator Card== (PCOC). This is the usual one.
- Proof that you passed a Canadian boating safety course ==before April 1, 1999==.
- A completed ==rental boat safety checklist==. It only counts ==for the rental period==.
- Certain marine certificates on Transport Canada's list.

A photocopy or a phone photo of your card is not accepted. Carry the real card.

A visitor from another country does not need Canadian proof if their own boat is in Canada for ==less than 45 consecutive days==. They should carry proof that they live outside Canada. A visitor who stays longer can use an operator card that meets the rules of their home country or state.

## How the test works

You get the card by passing a test from a course provider ==accredited by Transport Canada==. Most people take a course first, in a class or online.

- The test has ==36 questions==. The questions come from Transport Canada's question bank.
- You need ==75 per cent== to pass.
- The test is supervised from start to finish.
- You may only try the test once in a day.

The card is ==valid for life==. It does not expire. If you lose it, ask the provider who issued it for a new one.

## Young operators

{{figure:pcoc-age-power-limits}}

Young people need proof of competency too. They also have limits on engine power when they are not directly supervised. ==Direct supervision== means a person ==16 or older== is in the boat and watching them.

| Age | Without direct supervision |
|---|---|
| ==Under 12== | Motor of no more than ==10 hp (7.5 kW)== |
| ==12 to 15== | Motor of no more than ==40 hp (30 kW)== |
| ==Under 16== | ==No personal watercraft== at all, supervised or not |

At 16 there are no age limits on power.

## The pleasure craft licence

The ==pleasure craft licence== is for the boat, not the person. You need one if your boat has engines that total ==10 hp (7.5 kW) or more==, unless the boat is registered. This includes a dinghy or tender with a 10 hp outboard.

- It is valid for ==5 years==. Older "lifetime" licences are being replaced.
- Getting, renewing or transferring one costs a fee. It was $24 at the end of 2025 and rises with inflation.
- Report a new name, address or owner within ==30 days==.
- Keep ==a copy of the licence on board==.
- Mark the number on ==both sides of the bow==, above the waterline, in block letters at least ==7.5 cm (3 in)== high, in a colour that stands out.

A licence is not proof that you own the boat. ==Registration== is a different system that gives legal title. A boat is either licensed or registered, not both.

## What to carry

In a motor boat, carry your ==proof of competency==, ==personal ID==, and the ==licence== if the boat needs one.

## The operator's duties

The operator must take ==all reasonable steps== to keep the boat and everyone on board safe. That means good gear, a safety briefing for guests, and a weather check. Make sure one other person can run the boat. Never let someone drive who is not competent.

## Impaired operation

Boating while impaired is a ==Criminal Code== offence. The law treats a boat the same as a car. It is an offence to operate while your ability is ==impaired to any degree== by alcohol, drugs, or both. It is also an offence to have ==80 mg of alcohol in 100 mL of blood== or more within two hours after you stop.

Penalties can include fines, jail, and losing the right to drive a car as well as a boat.

## A BC example

Maya is 14. Her family rents a cabin on the Shuswap. Her uncle has a runabout with a 60 hp outboard. Maya has her PCOC. She can drive the runabout with her uncle beside her in the boat, because he is over 16 and supervising. She cannot take it out alone, because 60 hp is over her 40 hp limit. She cannot drive the neighbour's personal watercraft at all until she turns 16.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a small electric motor needs no card.
- Leaving the real card at home and carrying only a photo.
- Mixing up the licence (for the boat) and the card (for the driver).
- Letting a 15-year-old ride a personal watercraft "just around the bay".
