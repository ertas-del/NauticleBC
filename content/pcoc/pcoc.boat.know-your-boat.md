# Know your boat

Every boat is a little different. Before you drive one, learn its parts, read its labels, and know how much it can safely carry. Most small boat accidents start with too much weight in the wrong place.

## Boat words

{{figure:svop-boat-terms}}

Boaters use special words so that nobody gets confused. Learn these:

- ==Bow==: the front of the boat. ==Stern==: the back.
- ==Port==: the ==left== side when you face forward. ==Starboard==: the ==right== side.
- ==Forward== means toward the bow. ==Aft== means toward the stern.
- ==Hull==: the body of the boat.
- ==Transom==: the flat back end, where an outboard motor usually hangs.
- ==Gunwale== (say "gunnel"): the top edge of the side.
- ==Beam==: the widest part of the boat.
- ==Freeboard==: the height of the hull above the water.
- ==Draught==: how deep the boat sits below the water.
- ==Bilge==: the lowest part inside the hull, where water collects.

Port and starboard do not change when you turn around. Port is always the boat's left side.

## Hull types in brief

A ==displacement hull== sits in the water and pushes it aside. It is steady, but it cannot go very fast. Sailboats, canoes and many cruisers have this kind of hull.

A ==planing hull== rises up and skims along the top of the water at speed. Most runabouts, bowriders and personal watercraft plane. At slow speed a planing boat sits back down and behaves like a displacement boat.

A ==catamaran== has two hulls joined together. It is wide and steady.

## The compliance notice (capacity plate)

Most power boats under 24 m have a ==compliance notice== from the builder. People often call it the ==capacity plate==. You find it ==near the helm==, where the driver can see it.

On a boat ==up to 6 m== long, it shows the ==recommended maximum==:

- number of ==people==,
- total ==weight== it should carry,
- ==outboard motor power==.

The boat is full when you reach the maximum people ==or== the maximum weight, ==whichever comes first==. Five small children may weigh less than three adults with camping gear. Check both numbers.

These limits are for ==good weather== with the load spread evenly. In wind and waves, carry less. Boats over 6 m do not list these limits, so ask the builder.

Never put on a motor bigger than the plate allows. It is an offence to remove or change a compliance notice.

## The hull serial number

Most boats built or brought into Canada after ==April 29, 1981== have a ==hull serial number== (HIN). It has ==12 characters==. It is marked on the outside of the hull, on the ==upper starboard corner of the transom==. Changing or removing it is an offence.

## Loading the boat

How you load matters as much as how much you load.

- Keep weight ==low==. Heavy gear goes on the floor, not on the seats.
- Spread weight ==evenly==, side to side and front to back.
- ==Secure== gear so it cannot slide to one side.
- Do not let everyone sit on the same side, or all in the bow.

When you move around in a small boat, keep your body ==low== and near the ==centre==. Do not stand up in a canoe or small open boat. Hold on with your hands.

## Stability basics

A boat stays upright because its weight pushes down and the water pushes up. When weight is low, the boat comes back up after it rolls. When weight is high, or too much is on board, the boat rolls further and is slower to come back.

Overloading also lowers the boat in the water. That means ==less freeboard==, so small waves can splash over the side.

## Swamping and capsizing

A boat is ==swamped== when it fills with water but stays ==upright==. A boat has ==capsized== when it ==turns over==.

Both often start the same way: too much weight, weight too high, everyone on one side, a sharp turn, or a wave over a low side or the transom. A boat that is low in the water swamps easily.

Prevent it. Load well, slow down in waves, and take big wakes at an angle. Wear your lifejacket, because a capsize puts you in the water in a second.

## A BC example

Four friends take a 4.8 m aluminum boat out on Kalamalka Lake to fish. The plate says 4 people or 340 kg. Together with their coolers they weigh 380 kg. They are under the people limit but over the weight limit, so the boat is over capacity. One friend stays ashore with the big cooler. In the afternoon the wind picks up. They keep everyone seated low, with the heavy tackle box on the floor in the middle, and head in early.

## Common mistakes

- Checking the people number and forgetting the weight.
- Putting a bigger outboard on than the plate allows.
- Standing up to land a fish or to change seats.
- Stacking gear high on the seats.
- Using the plate limits on a rough day.
