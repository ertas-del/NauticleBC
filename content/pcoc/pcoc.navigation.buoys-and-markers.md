# Buoys and markers

Buoys are the road signs of the water. Canada uses one system on the coast, on lakes and on rivers. Each buoy tells you something by its ==colour==, its ==shape==, the ==symbol== on it and its ==light==. Learn the main types and you can read most waterways in BC.

## Lateral buoys: red right returning

{{figure:svop-lateral-buoys}}

Lateral buoys mark the sides of a channel. Canada is in buoyage ==Region B==. The memory aid is ==red right returning==. When you go ==upstream==, or come ==in from the sea== to a harbour, keep the ==red== buoys on your ==right== (starboard) side. Keep the ==green== buoys on your ==left== (port) side.

- ==Starboard-hand buoy==: ==red==, with a ==pointed (conical) top==. It carries an ==even number== and shows a ==red light==.
- ==Port-hand buoy==: ==green==, with a ==flat top== (a "can"). It carries an ==odd number== and shows a ==green light==.

Numbers ==get bigger as you go upstream==. So if the numbers are getting smaller, you are heading downstream or out to sea. Going downstream, everything flips: keep red on your left.

In open coastal water, "upstream" is not always obvious. Check your chart to see which side of a buoy to pass.

**Bifurcation buoys** mark where a channel splits. They have red and green bands. Treat the buoy by its ==top colour==. If the top is green, keep it on your port side going upstream to stay in the main channel.

## Cardinal buoys

{{figure:svop-cardinal-buoys}}

A cardinal buoy shows where the ==safe water== is, using the compass. It is ==yellow and black==, with ==two black cones== on top. It has a letter, not a number.

- ==North cardinal==: cones both point up. ==Pass to the north== of it.
- ==South cardinal==: cones both point down. Pass to the south.
- ==East cardinal==: cones base to base. Pass to the east.
- ==West cardinal==: cones point to point. Pass to the west.

Lights are ==white== and flash quickly. Think of a clock face: east flashes 3 times, south 6 (and one long flash), west 9. North flashes without stopping.

## Isolated danger and safe water

An ==isolated danger buoy== sits on top of a small danger, such as a rock or wreck, with safe water all around. It is ==black with a red band== and has ==two black balls== on top. Do not pass close to it. Check your chart for the size of the danger.

A ==safe water buoy==, also called a ==fairway buoy==, has ==red and white vertical stripes== and a ==red ball== on top. There is ==safe water all around== it. It often marks the entrance or middle of a channel. Keep it on your port side, in either direction.

## Special buoys

==Special buoys== are ==yellow== and show a ==yellow light==. Their ==shape has no meaning==. They are not mainly for navigation. A yellow ==cautionary buoy== warns of something like a firing range, a race course or a pipeline. Check the chart. Other yellow buoys mark anchorage areas and science stations.

## Private and regulatory buoys on lakes and rivers

On BC lakes and rivers you will see ==white buoys with orange symbols==. Many are private buoys, placed by marinas, towns, clubs or resorts. A private buoy must be marked ==PRIV== and show the owner's name and contact details.

| Buoy | What it looks like | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| ==Hazard== | White, ==orange diamond== | A danger, such as rocks, a shoal or rough water. Words inside may say what it is. |
| ==Keep out== | White, ==orange diamond with a cross== | Boats are ==not allowed== in this area. |
| ==Control== | White, ==orange circle== | A rule applies, such as a ==speed limit== or ==no wake==. The ==black number or symbol== in the circle is the rule. |
| ==Information== | White, ==orange square== | Information, such as directions or services, in black words or symbols. |
| ==Swimming== | White | Marks the ==edge of a swimming area==. Stay outside. |

Keep-out and control buoys mark rules made under the Vessel Operation Restriction Regulations. Lighted special and private buoys show a ==yellow== light.

A ==diving buoy== is white with a ==red flag with a white diagonal stripe==. Divers are in the water nearby. Slow down and keep well clear.

## Daybeacons

Daybeacons are fixed signs on posts or on shore. They follow the same lateral rules.

- ==Port-hand daybeacon==: a ==green square==, odd number. Keep it on your port side going upstream.
- ==Starboard-hand daybeacon==: a ==red triangle==, even number. Keep it on your starboard side going upstream.

Buoys can drag off position in storms or strong current, and lights can fail. Always check your position on the chart too.

## A BC example

You are taking a rented boat out of a marina on Okanagan Lake. Near the docks you pass a white buoy with an orange circle and "10" inside: slow to 10 km/h. Further out, a white buoy with an orange diamond and the word "ROCK" marks a reef, so you give it a wide berth. At the beach, a line of white buoys marks the swimming area. You stay well outside them.

## Common mistakes

- Keeping red on the right when going downstream or out to sea.
- Passing a cardinal buoy on the wrong side.
- Treating a white private buoy as less important than a Coast Guard buoy.
- Passing right next to a buoy. It may be off position, or sit on top of the danger.

## Check yourself

1. Going upstream, which side do you keep a red buoy on?
2. What does a white buoy with an orange diamond and a cross mean?
3. Which way do you pass a north cardinal buoy?
