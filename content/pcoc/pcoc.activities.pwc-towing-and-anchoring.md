# PWCs, towing, anchoring and docking

Summer on a BC lake means personal watercraft, wakeboarders, tubers, anchored boats and crowded docks. This lesson covers the rules and skills for each, and how to share the water.

## Personal watercraft

A ==personal watercraft== (PWC) is a small water-jet craft that you ride on, sitting, standing or kneeling, rather than in.

The PWC rules:

- ==No one under 16== may operate a PWC. This applies ==even with an adult on board==.
- The operator needs ==proof of competency== (such as the PCOC) and ==proof of age== on board.
- Everyone on board wears a lifejacket or PFD that is ==inherently buoyant==. ==Inflatable PFDs are not allowed== on a PWC.
- Attach the ==engine cut-off lanyard== to your wrist or lifejacket. If you fall off, the engine stops.
- Do not ride ==after dark== or in poor visibility unless the PWC has proper navigation lights.

Handling:

- A PWC steers by pushing a jet of water. ==Most PWCs need throttle to steer.== If you let go of the throttle, you may ==lose steering== and keep going straight, right into the thing you were trying to avoid.
- A PWC has ==no brakes==. Leave lots of room to stop.
- Avoid high speed near shore. Do not jump other boats' wakes or spray people.
- Ride in water at least about 76 cm (30 inches) deep.

## Towing a skier or tuber

{{figure:pcoc-towing-and-pwc}}

These rules cover water-skiing, wakeboarding, tubing, kneeboarding and parasailing.

- A ==spotter== must be on board, ==other than the operator==. The spotter ==watches the person being towed== at all times and ==tells the operator== what is happening.
- There must be ==an empty seat for each person being towed==, so you can bring them aboard. A PWC used for towing must be built to carry at least three people.
- Each person towed ==wears a lifejacket or PFD==, or the boat carries one for each of them.
- ==No towing in restricted visibility==, or from ==one hour after sunset until sunrise==.
- You may ==not tow yourself== with a remote-controlled boat.
- Some lakes limit towing to certain hours or areas. Check the local rules.

Agree on ==hand signals== before you start, because the skier cannot hear you. Common ones are palm up for faster, palm down for slower, and a pat on the top of the head for "take me back".

## Anchoring

Your anchor line is called the ==rode==. The ratio of rode length to water depth is the ==scope==.

- ==Calm water==: about ==3 times== the depth.
- ==Moderate== wind and waves: about ==5 times==.
- ==Rough== weather: about ==7 times==.

So in 6 m of calm water, let out about 18 m of rode.

How to anchor:

1. Pick a sheltered spot with room to swing, away from channels. Head slowly into the wind or current and stop.
2. ==Lower the anchor from the bow== until it is on the bottom. ==Never throw it.==
3. Back away slowly as you let out the rode.
4. Set it: back down gently in reverse at idle until it holds. Tie the rode to the bow cleat.
5. Pick two fixed marks on shore. ==Check them often== to make sure you are not ==dragging==.

==Never anchor from the stern.== It can let waves swamp the boat.

## Docking

{{figure:svop-mooring-lines}}

- Get your ==fenders== out and your ==lines ready== before you arrive.
- Come in ==slowly==, at the lowest speed that still lets you steer.
- Watch the ==wind==. It often matters more than the current.
- Get a line onto the dock early. A ==bow line== and a ==stern line== hold the ends. ==Spring lines== stop the boat surging ahead or back.
- Use a fender, never your hand or foot, to keep the boat off the dock.

## Passing and sharing the water

- The ==overtaking boat must keep out of the way== of the boat it is passing.
- Use sound signals if you need to: ==one short blast== means "I am turning to starboard", ==two short blasts== means "I am turning to port".
- Slow down and give a ==wide berth== to canoes, kayaks, paddleboards and rowers. Your wake can tip them.
- Keep clear of swimming areas.
- Stay ==at least 100 m== from a ==diver-down flag== (red with a white diagonal stripe) or the ==blue and white Alpha flag==. If you cannot, go dead slow and keep a sharp lookout.

## A BC example

On the Shuswap, Jordan is pulling his niece on a tube behind a 7 m bowrider. His friend sits facing back as the spotter. There are three empty seats, and the niece wears her PFD. At sunset they pull the tube in and anchor in a quiet bay. The water is 4 m deep and calm, so Jordan lowers the anchor from the bow and lets out about 12 m of rode. He lines up a big cedar with a cabin roof to check they are not dragging.

## Common mistakes

- Letting a 14-year-old "just try" the PWC.
- Letting off the PWC throttle to turn away from a hazard.
- Towing a tuber with no spotter, or with every seat full.
- Throwing the anchor and letting out only a little rode.
- Roaring past kayaks without slowing down.
