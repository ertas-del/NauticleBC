# Required safety gear

The law sets a minimum list of safety gear for every pleasure craft. The list grows as the boat gets longer. Know the list for your own boat.

## Lifejackets and PFDs

You need one ==Canadian-approved lifejacket or PFD== of the ==right size== for ==each person on board==. On a pleasure craft, ==either one is allowed==. Look for the approval on the label.

- A ==lifejacket== is better at turning you ==face up== in the water. It is red, orange or yellow.
- A ==PFD== (personal flotation device) is more comfortable to wear all day. It does not turn you over as well.

A device worn by a person ==under 16== must be ==inherently buoyant==. That means it floats by itself, like foam. It cannot be an inflatable.

Cold BC water can make you gasp for about the first minute. A lifejacket you are already wearing keeps your head up while you get your breathing back.

## Inflatable PFDs

Inflatable PFDs have extra rules.

- ==Not allowed for anyone under 16==.
- ==Not allowed on a personal watercraft==, or for water skiing, tubing or whitewater.
- They do not float until they are filled, so they ==must be worn== to count. On an ==open boat==, wear it ==at all times==. On a boat with a cabin, wear it when you are on deck or in the cockpit.

## The minimum list by length

{{figure:pcoc-safety-gear-by-length}}

This table is for power boats and sailboats with a motor. It shows the minimum the Small Vessel Regulations require.

| Item | Up to 6 m | Over 6 m to 9 m | Over 9 m to 12 m | Over 12 m to 24 m |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lifejacket or PFD, right size | 1 each person | 1 each person | 1 each person | 1 each person |
| Reboarding device (if climb is over 0.5 m) | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Buoyant heaving line, 15 m | Heaving line | Heaving line or lifebuoy on 15 m line | Heaving line and lifebuoy on 15 m line | Heaving line and lifebuoy (with light or 15 m line) |
| Watertight flashlight and flares | Flashlight or 3 flares | Flashlight and 6 flares | Flashlight and 12 flares | Flashlight and 12 flares |
| Anchor and line | 15 m, or a paddle or oars | 15 m, or a paddle or oars | 30 m | 50 m |
| Bailer or pump | Bailer or manual pump | Bailer or manual pump | Manual pump or bilge pumping | Bilge pumping |
| Sound-signalling device | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Navigation lights | At night or poor visibility | At night or poor visibility | Yes | Yes |
| Magnetic compass | Yes (see below) | Yes (see below) | Yes | Yes |
| Fire extinguisher | 5B:C if inboard, fixed tank or stove | 5B:C, plus one more if stove | 10B:C, plus one more if stove | 10B:C at stove, cabin and engine space; axe; 2 buckets |

"Stove" here means any fuel-burning cooking, heating or refrigerating appliance.

Some details to remember:

- The ==heaving line== must float and be at least ==15 m== long.
- A ==reboarding device==, such as a ladder, is needed if the climb from the water is ==more than 0.5 m==.
- Up to 9 m, you need ==navigation lights== only if you are out ==between sunset and sunrise== or in ==poor visibility==.
- A ==compass== is not needed on a boat ==8 m or less== that stays ==within sight of navigation marks==.
- Only some flares may be smoke signals: at most 1 of 3, 2 of 6, or 6 of 12.

Transport Canada's guide also lists a radar reflector for many small boats, with some exceptions. It also describes an electronic distress light, carried with a smoke signal, as another option. Check the current Safe Boating Guide before you rely on either.

## When you do not need flares

You do not need ==flares== if your boat is on a ==river, canal or lake== where you can ==never be more than 1 nautical mile from shore==. A boat up to 6 m with no motor needs no flashlight or flares at all.

On a boat over 6 m, you may carry ==half the flares== if you have a ==two-way radio==, a ==406 MHz personal locator beacon== worn by the operator, or a ==406 MHz EPIRB==.

Flares ==expire 4 years== after the date they were made. The date is stamped on each flare.

## Personal watercraft and paddle craft

On a ==personal watercraft==, the simple rule is that everyone wears a lifejacket or PFD. When they do, the PWC needs only a sound-signalling device, a watertight flashlight or three flares, a compass if out of sight of navigation marks, and navigation lights if used at night. Inflatables are not allowed.

Canoes, kayaks and other ==human-powered== craft have a shorter list. It starts with a lifejacket or PFD for each person, a buoyant heaving line and a bailer or pump.

## Checking and caring for gear

The operator must keep all gear ==in good working order== and ==easy to reach==. Show your guests where it is.

- Check each lifejacket and PFD ==at least once a year==. Look for ==rips==, ==fading==, and ==broken or missing straps or buckles==. Replace any that are damaged.
- Dry it in open air, away from direct heat. Never use it as a fender.
- For an inflatable, follow the checks in the owner's manual.
- Replace flares before they expire.

## A BC example

Sam keeps a 7 m cabin boat in Gibsons and crosses Howe Sound to Gambier Island. The boat is over 6 m, so he carries a watertight flashlight and six flares. His VHF radio would let him carry three, but he keeps six. His kids have foam PFDs that fit. His own inflatable counts only because he wears it on deck. In May he finds two flares that expire in June and replaces them.

## Common mistakes

- Carrying adult lifejackets only, with nothing that fits the children.
- Keeping an inflatable PFD in a locker and calling it carried.
- Letting flares go past their expiry date.
- Forgetting navigation lights when a day trip runs late.
