# Navigation lights at night

At night you cannot see the shape of a boat. You see only its lights. Navigation lights tell other boaters ==what you are, which way you are heading and whether you are at anchor==. Learn the colours, and learn where each light shows.

## When to show lights

Show your navigation lights ==from sunset to sunrise==. Also show them in ==restricted visibility==, such as fog, heavy rain or smoke, even in daytime. A pleasure craft that is used at these times must carry lights that meet the Collision Regulations.

Do not show other bright lights that could be mistaken for navigation lights or that hide them. A bright cabin or deck light can make your boat hard to read and can spoil your own night vision.

## The basic lights

{{figure:svop-light-arcs}}

- ==Sidelights==: a ==red== light on the ==port== (left) side and a ==green== light on the ==starboard== (right) side. Each shows from right ahead to ==22.5 degrees abaft the beam==, an arc of ==112.5 degrees==.
- ==Stern light==: a ==white== light that shows ==aft==, over ==135 degrees==.
- ==Masthead light==: a ==white== light over the centreline that shows ==forward==, over ==225 degrees==.
- ==All-round light==: shows in every direction, over 360 degrees.

A simple memory aid: port wine is red, and "port" and "left" both have four letters.

## Lights on your boat

{{figure:pcoc-pleasure-craft-lights}}

**Power-driven boat under 12 m.** Show a ==masthead light, sidelights and a stern light==. Or show ==one all-round white light and sidelights==. The sidelights may be in one combined lantern at the bow.

**Sailboat under sail.** Show ==sidelights and a stern light==, but ==no masthead light==. A sailboat ==under 20 m== may put all three in ==one lantern at the top of the mast==. If you start the engine, you are a ==power-driven boat==, even with the sails up. Then show the power-driven lights.

**Small sailboat under 7 m.** Show sidelights and a stern light if you can. If that is not practical, have a ==white torch or lantern== ready.

**Canoe, kayak, paddleboard or rowboat.** Have a ==white light== ready, such as a ==watertight flashlight==. Shine it ==in time to prevent a collision==. You may instead show the lights of a sailboat of the same length.

**At anchor.** Show ==one all-round white light== where it can best be seen. A boat ==under 7 m== does not need it, unless it is ==in or near a narrow channel, fairway or anchorage==, or where other boats usually travel. A light is still a good idea.

## Reading other boats' lights

{{figure:svop-night-lights}}

Work out which side of the other boat you are seeing.

- ==Red and green together==, with a white light above: a power-driven boat is ==coming straight at you==. Both turn to starboard.
- ==Red only==: you see her port side. If she is on your ==starboard== bow, you are the ==give-way== boat. Think "red means stop and give way."
- ==Green only==: you see her starboard side. If you are both power-driven and she is on your ==port== bow, you are the ==stand-on== boat. Keep your course and speed, and watch her.
- ==White only==: you are looking at her ==stern==, so she is going away or you are overtaking her. A single white light can also be a boat ==at anchor== or a small boat.
- Red or green with ==no white light above==: probably a ==sailboat==, or a barge under tow. A power boat gives way to a sailboat.

## Other vessels you will meet in BC

**Ships and large ferries.** A power-driven vessel ==50 m or more== shows ==two white masthead lights==, the aft one higher. When the two lights line up, one above the other, she is pointing at you.

**Tug with a tow.** A tug towing astern shows ==two white masthead lights in a vertical line==, or ==three if the tow is over 200 m==. She also shows sidelights and a stern light, with a ==yellow towing light== above the stern light. The barge or log boom behind her shows sidelights and a stern light, and may be dim. The towline can be long and hard to see. ==Never pass between a tug and its tow==.

**Other signals.** Green over white: a trawler. Red over red: a vessel not under command. Red, white, red: a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre. Keep clear of all of them.

## A BC example

You are motoring home across Howe Sound after dark in a 7 m boat. Ahead and to your right you see two white lights, one above the other, with a red light below them. Well behind them, low on the water, is a faint red light. This is a tug towing a barge, crossing from right to left. She is on your starboard side, so you give way. You slow down and turn to starboard to pass well behind the barge, not between the barge and the tug.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the red and green sides.
- Forgetting the anchor light at a busy anchorage.
- Paddling at dusk with no flashlight.
- Motor-sailing with only sailing lights.
- Taking a dim barge light for a small boat far away.
