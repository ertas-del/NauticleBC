# Rule 36: Signals to attract attention

Sometimes you need another vessel to notice you, or to notice a danger, and none of the set signals fits. Rule 36
allows it, with limits so that the signal does not cause new confusion.

## What the rule allows

- **Any vessel** may make light or sound signals to attract attention, provided they
  ==cannot be mistaken for any signal authorized elsewhere== in the Rules. A random burst of blasts that happens
  to be five short is not a Rule 36 signal; it is the doubt signal.
- A vessel may ==direct the beam of her searchlight in the direction of the danger==, provided it is done in a way
  that does ==not embarrass any vessel==. "Embarrass" here means dazzle or hamper. Shining a searchlight into another
  wheelhouse can blind the person steering.

## What the rule limits

- Any light used to attract attention must be one that ==cannot be mistaken for an aid to navigation==. A flashing
  green or red light near a channel could be read as a buoy or beacon.
- ==High-intensity intermittent or revolving lights, such as strobe lights==, are ==to be avoided==. Strobes are
  confusing at sea and are used for other purposes (see the Annex IV lesson for the Canadian distress use).

## What it is not

Rule 36 is not a distress rule. If you are in distress and need help, use the signals in ==Rule 37 and Annex IV==.

## A bridge-watch example

At night you are anchored in a small BC cove and a sport-fishing boat is heading straight for a drying rock near
your position. Her crew seem not to have seen it. You may shine your searchlight on the rock to show the danger,
keeping the beam off her wheelhouse. You may also sound a signal on your horn, as long as it is not a pattern that
means something else in the Rules (avoid five short, which is the doubt signal, unless you are in fact in doubt
about a vessel in sight approaching you).

## Common mistakes

- Shining a searchlight directly at another vessel's bridge.
- Using a strobe to "be seen". Rule 36 says to avoid it.
- Using a flashing coloured light that looks like a navigation mark.
- Making up a whistle pattern that matches an existing signal.
- Using Rule 36 signals when you actually need help. That is a distress situation.
