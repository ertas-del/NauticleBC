# Rule 32: Sound signal definitions

Part D of the Collision Regulations covers sound and light signals (Rules 32 to 37). Rule 32 is short, but every
other signal depends on it. If you cannot tell a short blast from a prolonged one, you cannot read a fog signal or a
manoeuvring signal.

## What the rule defines

- **Whistle.** Any ==sound signalling appliance capable of producing the prescribed blasts== that complies with
  Annex III. It does not have to look like a whistle. A horn, an air horn or an electric horn counts if it meets
  the specification.
- **Short blast.** A blast of ==about one second==.
- **Prolonged blast.** A blast of ==four to six seconds==.

That is the whole rule. Everything else in Part D is built from these two blast lengths.

## How signals are written in this app

We write signals in words, in the order they are sounded:

- "one prolonged" means one blast of 4 to 6 seconds.
- "one prolonged, two short" means a 4 to 6 second blast followed by two blasts of about 1 second each.
- "five short" means five blasts of about 1 second each, sounded quickly.

Leave a clear gap between blasts so the listener can count them. A signal that runs together is just noise.

## A bridge-watch example

You are on a 15 metre crew boat leaving a BC harbour in fog, and the skipper asks you to sound the fog signal for a
power-driven vessel making way, which is one prolonged blast. You press the horn for about 1.5 seconds and let go.
To a listener that is a long-ish short blast, not a prolonged blast. Count it out: "one thousand, two thousand,
three thousand, four thousand, five thousand" gives about five seconds, well inside the 4 to 6 second band.

## Common mistakes

- Sounding "prolonged" blasts that are only two or three seconds long.
- Sounding "short" blasts that drag on to two seconds or more, so they blur into prolonged ones.
- Running blasts together with no gap, so the other vessel cannot count them.
- Thinking a horn is not a "whistle". In the Rules, any compliant appliance that makes the blasts is a whistle.
