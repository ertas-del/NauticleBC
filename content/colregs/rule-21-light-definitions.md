# Rule 21: Definitions of the lights

Every later lights Rule is built from a small set of light types. Learn their **colour**, **arc** and **position** and most of Part C becomes a matter of adding them up.

## What the rule says (in plain terms)

| Light | Colour | Arc | Shines from and to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masthead light | ==white== | ==225 degrees== | from ==right ahead to 22.5 degrees abaft the beam== on each side, over the fore and aft centreline |
| Sidelights | ==green starboard, red port== | ==112.5 degrees== each | from right ahead to 22.5 degrees abaft the beam on its own side |
| Sternlight | ==white== | ==135 degrees== | ==67.5 degrees from right aft== on each side, as near the stern as practicable |
| Towing light | ==yellow== | 135 degrees | same characteristics as the sternlight |
| All-round light | any colour the Rule names | ==360 degrees== | unbroken all round the horizon |
| Flashing light | as named | n/a | flashes at regular intervals at ==120 or more flashes per minute== |

On a vessel ==under 20 m== the two sidelights may be ==combined in one lantern== carried on the fore and aft centreline.

## Canadian definitions

The Canadian version of Rule 21 adds two lights:

- **Special flashing light:** a ==yellow== light flashing ==50 to 70 times a minute==, placed as far forward as practicable on the centreline, showing over an arc of 180 to 225 degrees. In Canada it is used at the forward end of vessels being pushed ahead in the Great Lakes Basin, so you are unlikely to meet it on the BC coast.
- **Blue flashing light:** a ==blue all-round== light flashing ==50 to 70 times a minute==. It is the identification signal some government, police and Coast Guard Auxiliary vessels may show (see Rule 20 lesson).

## Plain-language explanation

The arcs fit together like a jigsaw:

- Each sidelight covers 90 degrees (ahead to abeam) plus 22.5 degrees abaft the beam, so 112.5 degrees. Two of them make 225 degrees, the same as the masthead light.
- The sternlight fills the gap behind: 67.5 degrees each side of dead astern makes 135 degrees.
- ==225 plus 135 is 360==. So from any direction you see either the masthead light (and a sidelight) or the sternlight, never both and never neither.

That boundary at **22.5 degrees abaft the beam** is the same line the Rules use to define overtaking (Rule 13). If you can see a vessel's sternlight but not her sidelights, you are coming up on her from more than 22.5 degrees abaft her beam: you are overtaking.

Colours are a quick aspect check. ==Green means you are looking at her starboard side; red means her port side.== If you see red and green together, she is pointing at you, or very nearly.

## A BC bridge example

At night in Haro Strait you see a white light with a green light below it. You are seeing her masthead light and her starboard sidelight, so you are somewhere in her starboard sector, forward of 22.5 degrees abaft her beam. Some minutes later the green light disappears and you see a single white light. You may have moved into her sternlight sector, so she is now showing you her stern. Use radar and compass bearings to confirm the story before you act on it.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the sternlight arc (135) with the masthead arc (225).
- Thinking sidelights shine right round to the stern. They stop at 22.5 degrees abaft the beam.
- Calling the towing light "orange" or "amber all-round". It is a yellow light with the sternlight's 135 degree arc.
- Assuming a flashing light is slow. The Rule 21 flashing light is 120 or more flashes a minute; the Canadian special and blue flashing lights are 50 to 70.
