# Rule 19: Conduct of vessels in restricted visibility

## What it is

Rule 19 is Section III on its own. It applies to vessels ==not in sight of one another== when navigating ==in or
near an area of restricted visibility==: fog, mist, heavy rain, snow, smoke. "Near" matters. A vessel in clear air
just outside a fog bank can have a vessel hidden inside it.

Because the vessels cannot see each other, Rule 19 has ==no stand-on vessel and no give-way vessel==. Each vessel
that detects a risk has to act.

## What each paragraph asks

**19(b) Safe speed and ready engines.** Every vessel proceeds at a ==safe speed== adapted to the conditions of
restricted visibility. A power-driven vessel has her ==engines ready for immediate manoeuvre==.

**19(c) Section I with due regard.** When complying with Rules 4 to 10, have due regard to the restricted
visibility: a sharper lookout (by sight, hearing and radar), radar plotting rather than guesswork, and fog signals
under Rule 35.

**19(d) Radar-only contacts.** A vessel that detects another ==by radar alone== shall determine whether a
==close-quarters situation is developing and/or risk of collision exists==. If so, she takes avoiding action ==in
ample time==. If that action is an alteration of course, then, so far as possible, avoid:

1. an ==alteration of course to port for a vessel forward of the beam==, other than for a vessel being overtaken;
2. an ==alteration of course towards a vessel abeam or abaft the beam==.

**19(e) Fog signal or unavoidable close quarters forward of the beam.** Except where it has been ==determined that a
risk of collision does not exist==, every vessel that ==hears apparently forward of her beam the fog signal== of
another vessel, or cannot avoid a close-quarters situation with a vessel forward of her beam, shall ==reduce her
speed to the minimum at which she can be kept on her course==. If necessary she ==takes all her way off==, and in
any event navigates with ==extreme caution until danger of collision is over==.

## Turning 19(d) into a picture

Think of the contact's relative bearing:

| Contact is... | Avoid | So the usual choice is |
|---|---|---|
| Forward of the beam, either side (not being overtaken by you) | Turning to port | Turn to starboard, or slow down |
| A vessel ahead that you are overtaking | (Exception: no restriction from (i)) | Turn either way, with plenty of room |
| Abeam or abaft the beam on your starboard side | Turning toward it (to starboard) | Turn to port, away from it, or adjust speed |
| Abeam or abaft the beam on your port side | Turning toward it (to port) | Turn to starboard, away from it, or adjust speed |

Notice that a starboard turn is the usual answer for anything ahead, and "turn away" is the answer for anything
abeam or behind. Slowing down is always worth considering: it buys time, and Rule 8 allows it.

## How it differs from Section II

- In clear weather the vessel on your port bow in a crossing is the give-way vessel and you stand on. In fog,
  ==you must both act== if risk exists.
- In clear weather an overtaking vessel keeps clear. In fog there is no overtaking rule (Rule 13 is in Section II).
  The vessel being caught up still has to act if risk develops, and must not turn toward a vessel abaft her beam.

## A BC bridge example

Thick fog in Juan de Fuca Strait. You are on watch on a 20 metre vessel at a reduced safe speed, fog signal
sounding, engines on standby. Radar shows:

- **Contact A** fine on your starboard bow, 5 miles, bearing steady, closing. Forward of the beam: you must not
  turn to port. Make a bold, early alteration to starboard, and plot to check it is working.
- **Contact B** on your port quarter, 3 miles, closing slowly. Abaft the beam on the port side: do not turn to port,
  toward her. Your starboard turn for A takes you away from B. Keep plotting both.
- Then you hear a prolonged blast that seems to be ==forward of your beam==, but you cannot match it to a radar
  contact. Rule 19(e): reduce to the minimum speed at which you can steer, take all way off if necessary, and
  proceed with extreme caution until the danger is over.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a radar contact as "stand-on" or "give-way". Section II roles do not exist in Rule 19.
- Turning to port for a contact on the starboard bow because "it will pass faster".
- Turning toward a vessel on the quarter to "let her pass".
- Making small alterations that the other vessel's radar cannot detect.
- Relying on AIS alone, or assuming every vessel has radar or AIS.
- Keeping speed up after hearing a fog signal forward of the beam because the radar "shows nothing".
