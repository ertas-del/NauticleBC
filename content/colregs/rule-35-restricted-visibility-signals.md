# Rule 35: Sound signals in restricted visibility

When you cannot see, sound tells other vessels what you are. Rule 35 applies
==in or near an area of restricted visibility==, ==by day or by night==. You do not have to be inside the fog bank: if you are near one, sound.

Fog is common around Vancouver harbour and the Fraser River approaches in the late autumn and winter, and the Port
of Vancouver has reminded mariners of these signals. Know them cold.

## Vessels underway

| You are | Signal | Interval |
|---|---|---|
| power-driven, ==making way through the water== | ==one prolonged== | not more than ==2 minutes== |
| power-driven, ==underway but stopped==, no way on | ==two prolonged==, about ==2 seconds== apart | not more than 2 minutes |
| not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught, sailing, fishing, towing or pushing | ==one prolonged, two short== | not more than 2 minutes |
| ==manned== vessel being towed (the last one if several) | ==one prolonged, three short== | not more than 2 minutes |

Notes:

- The one prolonged, two short group replaces the power-driven signal. A tug towing in fog sounds one prolonged,
  two short whether she is making way or not.
- A vessel being towed sounds only if manned. When practicable she sounds her signal
  ==immediately after the towing vessel's signal==, so a listener hears the pair as one unit.
- A pusher and pushed vessel ==rigidly connected== as a composite unit count as one power-driven vessel and give the
  power-driven signals.
- A fishing vessel at anchor, and a vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre working at anchor, also sound
  one prolonged, two short, instead of the anchor bell.

## At anchor and aground

- **At anchor:** ==ring the bell rapidly for about 5 seconds== at intervals of ==not more than 1 minute==.
- **At anchor, 100 m or more:** bell in the ==fore part==, then immediately the ==gong== in the after part, rapidly
  for about 5 seconds.
- **Optional anchor warning:** an anchored vessel may also sound ==one short, one prolonged, one short== to warn an
  approaching vessel of her position and the risk of collision.
- **Aground:** the anchor bell (and gong if required) plus ==three separate and distinct strokes== on the bell
  ==immediately before and after== the rapid ringing. A vessel aground may also sound a suitable whistle signal.

## Pilot vessels

A ==pilot vessel on pilotage duty== may add an identity signal of ==four short== blasts to her normal underway or
anchor signal. You will hear it near pilot boarding stations.

## Small vessels

- A vessel of ==12 m or more but under 20 m== does not have to give the bell signals (anchored or aground). If she
  does not, she must make some other efficient sound signal at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.
- A vessel ==under 12 m== does not have to give the Rule 35 signals at all. If she does not, she must make
  ==some other efficient sound signal== at intervals of ==not more than 2 minutes==.

## Canadian addition: small pushing and pulling vessels

In the ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, a vessel that is under 12 m,
==ordinarily used for pushing or pulling== any floating object, and
==not inside a recognized mooring, storage or booming area==, must sound the signals prescribed for a vessel of 12 m or more. On the Fraser River this catches the
small boom boats that move logs outside the booming grounds.

## A bridge-watch example

You are on a 16 metre power-driven vessel crossing English Bay in thickening fog. You sound one prolonged every
2 minutes or less, keep the radar and a listening lookout, and slow down. You hear a bell rung rapidly for about
5 seconds, then about a minute later the same again: a vessel at anchor, probably in the anchorage ahead. Later you
hear one prolonged, two short, then straight after it one prolonged, three short. That is a tug with a manned tow.
Give the pair a wide berth; there is a towline between them.

## Common mistakes

- Waiting until you are inside the fog to start signalling. The rule says in or near.
- Sounding the power-driven signal on a tug that is towing. A towing vessel sounds one prolonged, two short.
- Mixing up two prolonged (stopped, no way) with one prolonged (making way).
- Thinking the anchor signal is every 2 minutes. It is ==every minute== or less.
- Using manoeuvring signals (one short, two short) in fog to show an alteration.
- Assuming a small boat is silent. Under 12 m still means an efficient sound signal every 2 minutes or less.
