# Annex I: Positioning and technical details of lights and shapes

The Rules in Part C tell you **which** lights and shapes a vessel shows. ==Annex I== tells you **where** they go,
**how big** the shapes are, and **how bright** and **what colour** the lights must be. A builder or electrician
needs all of it. A watchkeeper or small-vessel master needs the parts that help you check your own boat and read
other vessels correctly. This lesson keeps to those parts.

## Vertical position of masthead lights

**Power-driven vessel of 20 m or more**

- The forward masthead light (or the only one) sits at least ==6 m above the hull==. If the vessel is ==wider than
  6 m==, it must be at least ==as high as her breadth==. It never has to be higher than ==12 m==.
  "Above the hull" means above the uppermost continuous deck, measured straight below the light.
- With two masthead lights, the after one is at least ==4.5 m higher== than the forward one.
- The aim of that difference is spelt out too: in normal trim, someone at sea level ==1 000 m== ahead of the stem must
  see the after light above and separate from the forward light. That is why the two white lights open up as a range
  you can read for heading.

**Smaller power-driven vessels**

| Length | Masthead light height |
|---|---|
| 12 m to under 20 m | at least ==2.5 m above the gunwale== |
| under 12 m | may be lower than 2.5 m above the gunwale |

The concession for boats under 12 m has a limit. When a masthead light (or the under-12 m all-round white light of
Rule 23) is carried together with sidelights, it must be at least ==1 m higher than the sidelights==. The Canadian
text states this as its own paragraph and repeats it for power-driven vessels under 12 m in the Canadian waters of a
roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway.

**Canadian screen for small boats.** A power-driven vessel under 12 m built after ==July 31, 1983== must have its
masthead light or all-round white light ==screened== so it does not light up the boat forward of the operator. The
point is to protect the operator's night vision.

## Lights in a vertical line

Many signals use two or three lights one above the other (not under command, towing, restricted in ability to
manoeuvre, constrained by draught, fishing).

| Vessel | Spacing between lights | Lowest light |
|---|---|---|
| 20 m or more | at least ==2 m== | at least ==4 m above the hull== |
| under 20 m | at least ==1 m== | at least ==2 m above the gunwale== |

The lowest-light height does not apply where a towing light is needed. When there are ==three lights==, they are
==equally spaced==.

Two related placements:

- For a vessel engaged in fishing, the lower of the two all-round fishing lights must be above the sidelights by at
  least twice the gap between the two fishing lights.
- With two anchor lights, the forward one is at least 4.5 m higher than the after one. On a vessel of 50 m or more the
  forward anchor light is at least 6 m above the hull.

## Horizontal position

- **Two masthead lights:** the gap between them is at least ==half the vessel's length==, but need not be more than
  ==100 m==. The forward light is no more than ==a quarter of the length from the stem==.
- **One masthead light:** forward of amidships. A vessel ==under 20 m== need not have it forward of amidships but
  carries it as far forward as practicable.
- **Sidelights on a vessel of 20 m or more:** not in front of the forward masthead light, and at or near the side of
  the vessel.

## Sidelights

- On a power-driven vessel, sidelights are ==no higher than three-quarters== of the height of the forward masthead
  light, and not so low that deck lights interfere with them.
- On a power-driven vessel under 20 m with a ==combined lantern==, the lantern is at least ==1 m below the masthead
  light==.
- **Screens.** On a vessel of 20 m or more, sidelights have ==inboard screens painted matt black==. Under 20 m, screens
  are needed only if the lantern cannot meet the sector rules without them. A combined lantern with one vertical
  filament and a very narrow red/green division needs no external screen.

## Shapes

Shapes are ==black==.

| Shape | Size |
|---|---|
| Ball | diameter at least ==0.6 m== |
| Cone | base diameter at least 0.6 m, ==height equal to its diameter== |
| Cylinder | diameter at least 0.6 m, ==height twice its diameter== |
| Diamond | ==two cones== (as above) ==with a common base== |

The vertical distance between shapes is at least ==1.5 m==.

A vessel ==under 20 m== may use ==smaller shapes in proportion to her size==, and the distance between them may be
reduced to match. The Regulations give no fixed small-vessel sizes.

## Colours

Annex I defines each light colour as an area on the international (CIE) colour chart. You do not need the
co-ordinates. Know that the international colours are ==white, green, red and yellow==, and that the
**Canadian modification** adds a ==restricted blue== (used for the Canadian blue flashing light).

## Intensity and range

The minimum intensity of a light comes from a formula that ties intensity to range, worked out for an atmosphere
with a meteorological visibility of about ==13 miles==. A few values from the table:

| Range (miles) | Minimum intensity (candelas) |
|---|---|
| 1 | 0.9 |
| 2 | ==4.3== |
| 3 | ==12== |
| 4 | 27 |
| 5 | 52 |
| 6 | ==94== |

The lesson in the table: ==intensity rises much faster than range==. Going from a 3 mile light to a 6 mile light takes
almost eight times the candelas, not twice. A dim or dirty lantern loses range quickly.

Lights should not be so bright that they cause glare, but this must not be done with a dimmer (variable control).
The **Canadian modification** adds that every light except a flashing light must look ==constant and steady==.

## Sectors

**Horizontal sectors.** Sidelights must give full intensity right ahead and fade to a practical cut-off within
==1 to 3 degrees== outside their sector, so a vessel dead ahead sees both colours only over a very small arc.
Masthead lights, sternlights and sidelights at their after limit (22.5 degrees abaft the beam) hold full intensity to
within 5 degrees of the edge of the sector, may fade by up to half from there to the edge, and must cut off within
5 degrees outside it. All-round lights must not be
hidden by masts or structure over any sector of more than ==6 degrees== (anchor lights excepted where the height
needed would be impracticable). If one light cannot manage that, two lights are used so that they look like one at
a mile.

**Vertical sectors.** Electric lights must keep full intensity from ==5 degrees above to 5 degrees below== the
horizontal and at least 60 per cent of it out to 7.5 degrees. Sailing vessels underway keep at least 50 per cent out
to 25 degrees, because they heel. This is why a light still shows properly when a vessel rolls or pitches.

## High-speed craft

A high-speed craft (in Canada, one certified under the IMO High-Speed Craft Code) may carry her masthead light lower
than the normal height for her breadth, as long as the triangle formed by her sidelights and masthead light, seen from
ahead, has base angles of at least ==27 degrees==. A large high-speed craft may also reduce the 4.5 m separation of
her two masthead lights by a formula. Expect her lights to look lower and flatter than a conventional ship of the
same size.

## Approval

Lights and shapes, and the way lights are installed, must satisfy the authority of the flag State. For Canadian
vessels that is ==the Minister== (Transport Canada). A further Canadian section requires navigation light power,
including emergency power, to meet Transport Canada's ship electrical standard (TP 127).

## A BC bridge example

You are relief skipper on a 17 m crew boat. The owner has fitted a new LED masthead light on a short post, 1.8 m above
the gunwale, and a combined red/green lantern 1.2 m below it. The lantern spacing meets the 1 m minimum, but the
masthead light is too low: a power-driven vessel of 12 to 20 m needs it at least 2.5 m above the gunwale. Raise the
masthead light, keep the lantern at least 1 m below it, and have the installation checked against Transport Canada's
requirements before night work.

## Common mistakes

- Using the 20 m-and-over figures (6 m masthead height, 2 m spacing) for a small vessel. Under 20 m the vertical line
  spacing is 1 m and heights are measured above the gunwale.
- Making a cylinder as tall as it is wide. A cylinder is twice as high as its diameter; a cone is as high as its base.
- Thinking a 6 mile light is twice as bright as a 3 mile light. It is closer to eight times.
- Putting the after masthead light lower than, or level with, the forward one.
- Using a dimmer to cut glare from navigation lights.
