# Rule 3: General definitions

## What the rule asks of you

Rule 3 does not tell you what to do. It tells you what the **words mean**, and every later Rule uses these words. If you misjudge a vessel's category, you will apply the wrong Rule. In your own words, the main ones are:

- **Power-driven vessel**: any vessel ==propelled by machinery==.
- **Sailing vessel**: a vessel under sail, ==as long as any propelling machinery she has is not being used==. A sailboat motoring is a power-driven vessel while the engine is running.
- **Vessel engaged in fishing**: a vessel fishing with nets, lines, trawls or other gear that ==restrict manoeuvrability==. A vessel fishing with ==trolling lines== or other gear that does not restrict manoeuvrability is **not** included.
- **Vessel not under command**: a vessel that, because of some ==exceptional circumstance==, cannot manoeuvre as the Rules require, and so cannot keep out of the way of another vessel.
- **Vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre**: a vessel that, because of the ==nature of her work==, cannot manoeuvre as the Rules require, and so cannot keep out of the way of another vessel. The list includes work such as servicing navigation marks, cables or pipelines, dredging, surveying or underwater work, transferring people or cargo while underway, launching or recovering aircraft, mine clearance, and towing that severely limits the tug and tow's ability to alter course.
- **Vessel constrained by her draught**: a ==power-driven== vessel whose draught, compared with the depth and width of navigable water available, severely limits her ability to deviate from her course.
- **Underway**: ==not at anchor, not made fast to the shore, and not aground==.
- **Length and breadth**: ==length overall== and ==greatest breadth==.
- **In sight of one another**: only when one can be ==seen by eye== from the other.
- **Restricted visibility**: any condition in which ==fog, mist, falling snow, heavy rain, sandstorms or similar causes== limit visibility.

The international wording of the definition of "vessel" (paragraph (a)) is broad, covering craft used or capable of being used for transport on water. The Canadian version of Rule 3 adds its own note about that definition, so read the current text of Rule 3 for it. The Rules also define seaplane and wing-in-ground craft; you will meet them again in the lights and right-of-way Rules.

## Plain-language explanation

Notice the pattern in the three "restricted" categories. **Not under command** is about an *exceptional circumstance*, such as an engine failure or steering failure. **Restricted in ability to manoeuvre** is about the *nature of the work*, such as a cable ship laying cable. **Constrained by draught** is about *water depth*, and applies only to power-driven vessels. All three share the same effect: the vessel cannot do what the Rules usually expect, so others have to keep clear.

Two small phrases have big consequences. **Underway** does not mean moving. A vessel drifting with her engine off is underway, because she is not anchored, tied up or aground. **In sight** means seen by eye. Seeing a target ==on radar does not put you in sight== of it, and that matters because the steering and sailing Rules for vessels in sight (Rules 11 to 18) work differently from the rules for restricted visibility (Rule 19).

## On the bridge

In Johnstone Strait you meet two vessels working fishing gear. One is a trawler towing a net. The other is a small boat trolling with hand lines. By the definition, the trawler is a vessel engaged in fishing because her gear restricts her manoeuvrability. The troller's gear does not restrict manoeuvrability, so she is not a vessel engaged in fishing for Rule purposes, and you treat her as an ordinary power-driven vessel (or sailing vessel, as the case may be). Treating both the same way would mean applying the wrong rules in a crossing or overtaking situation.

## Common mistakes

- Treating every vessel with a fishing rod or trolling lines out as "engaged in fishing".
- Calling a sailing vessel with her engine running a sailing vessel.
- Thinking "underway" means "making way". It only means not anchored, not fast to shore and not aground.
- Using "not under command" for a vessel that is simply doing difficult work. That is **restricted in her ability to manoeuvre**.
- Believing radar contact means vessels are "in sight" of each other.
