# Rule 17: Action by stand-on vessel

## What it is

Rule 17 tells the vessel that is **not** directed to keep out of the way what to do. It works in three stages as
the range closes.

| Stage | When | What the stand-on vessel does | Word in the rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Risk of collision exists; give-way vessel should act | ==Keep her course and speed== | shall |
| 2 | It becomes ==apparent== the give-way vessel is ==not taking appropriate action== | ==May== act to avoid collision ==by her manoeuvre alone== | may |
| 3 | So close that ==collision cannot be avoided by the action of the give-way vessel alone== | ==Shall== take such action as will ==best aid to avoid collision== | shall |

## Plain explanation

**Stage 1: be predictable.** The give-way vessel plans her action around where you will be. If you change course or
speed, you spoil her plan. The point of "keep course and speed" is to be predictable: no surprises.

**Stage 2: you may act.** If the give-way vessel is clearly not doing her job, you do not have to wait for stage 3.
As soon as you are in doubt whether she is taking sufficient action, Rule 34(d) requires you to ==immediately==
sound the ==doubt signal== of ==at least five short and rapid blasts== on the whistle (a light signal may be added).
Then, if you act, make it bold.

**Stage 3: you must act.** At some point only your own action can prevent collision, whatever the other vessel
now does. From that moment, doing nothing is a breach of the Rules. Take the action that best helps, which may be a
large alteration, a crash stop, or both.

## The port-turn trap (Rule 17(c))

A ==power-driven vessel== that acts under stage 2 ==in a crossing situation== with another power-driven vessel
shall, if the circumstances admit, ==not alter course to port for a vessel on her own port side==.

Why? The give-way vessel is on your port side. Her correct action is a turn to starboard to pass astern of you.
If she wakes up late and turns to starboard just as you turn to port, you are turning toward each other and the
two turns can cancel out or bring you together. A turn to starboard, or slowing down, avoids that trap.

## Rule 17(d)

Rule 17 ==does not relieve the give-way vessel== of her obligation to keep out of the way. The stand-on vessel's
freedom to act at stage 2 does not shift the duty.

## A BC bridge example

You are stand-on in a 16 metre power vessel. A power-driven vessel on your port bow is crossing, bearing steady,
range 2 miles. She shows no sign of altering.

- At 2 miles: keep course and speed (stage 1). Watch her bearing and aspect closely.
- At about 1 mile, with no change: it is now apparent she is not taking appropriate action. Sound at least five
  short and rapid blasts. Try VHF if you can identify her, but do not let the call delay action.
- Stage 2: you may act. Make a bold turn to starboard, possibly turning right round, or slow down sharply. Do not
  turn to port toward a vessel on your port side.
- If she is still coming and is so close that her action alone cannot avoid collision: stage 3. You must take
  whatever action best helps, including stopping and going astern.

## Common mistakes

- Altering early "to be safe" in stage 1. It confuses the give-way vessel.
- Holding on into stage 3 because "I have the right of way". At stage 3 the stand-on vessel must act.
- Turning to port for a crossing vessel on your port side.
- Treating a VHF call as a substitute for action.
- Thinking the give-way vessel is off the hook once the stand-on vessel acts.
