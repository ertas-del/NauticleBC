# Rule 26: Fishing vessels

BC waters are busy with fishing vessels: gillnetters, seiners, draggers, trollers and prawn and crab boats. Rule 26 tells you how to recognise the ones whose gear really restricts them, and therefore which ones you need to keep clear of.

Remember Rule 3: a "vessel engaged in fishing" uses gear that ==restricts manoeuvrability==. A vessel ==trolling== is ==not== engaged in fishing under the Rules, so she shows the ordinary lights for her type and length.

## What the rule asks of you

A vessel engaged in fishing, ==whether underway or at anchor==, shows ==only the lights and shapes of Rule 26==. (So a fishing vessel at anchor does not show anchor lights.)

**Trawling** (dragging a dredge net or other fishing apparatus through the water):

- two ==all-round lights in a vertical line, green over white==;
- a ==masthead light abaft of and higher than the green light== (a vessel ==under 50 m need not== show it but may);
- when ==making way through the water==, ==sidelights and a sternlight== as well.

**Fishing other than trawling** (gillnets, seines, longlines and similar):

- two ==all-round lights in a vertical line, red over white==;
- if ==outlying gear extends more than 150 m horizontally==, an ==all-round white light== (by day a ==cone, apex upwards==) ==in the direction of the gear==;
- when making way through the water, sidelights and a sternlight as well.

Annex I places the outlying gear light on the fishing vessel itself, 2 to 6 m horizontally away from the red and white lights, no higher than the white one and no lower than the sidelights. It is not a light out on the end of the gear.

**By day, both kinds** show ==two cones with their apexes together==, one above the other.

A vessel ==not engaged in fishing== must ==not== show these lights or shapes. She shows the lights for a vessel of her length.

## Memory aids

- ==Green over white, trawling at night.==
- ==Red over white, fishing at night.== (A common extra: "red over white, fishing all night.")
- The fishing shape looks like an hourglass: two cones, points together.

## Fishing close to other fishing vessels (Annex II)

These extra lights sit below the Rule 26 lights and are visible at least 1 mile, but less far than the main fishing lights.

- **Trawlers of 20 m or more** (smaller trawlers may also use them):
  - ==two white== in a vertical line: ==shooting nets==;
  - ==white over red==: ==hauling nets==;
  - ==two red==: ==net fast on an obstruction==.
- **Pair trawlers** also shine a searchlight forward and towards the other vessel of the pair at night.
- **Purse seiners** may show ==two yellow lights in a vertical line, flashing alternately every second==, only when hampered by their gear.

**Canadian modifications.** In Canadian waters and fishing zones, a vessel ==pair trawling== shows the International Code ==flag "T"== at the foremast by day. In Canadian waters and fishing zones, vessels trawling or purse seining as described in Annex II also sound whistle signals in any condition of visibility: two prolonged and two short when shooting, two prolonged and one short when hauling, and one short, two prolonged, one short when the gear is fast.

## A BC bridge example

At night in the lower Strait of Georgia during a gillnet opening you see red over white all-round lights with no sidelights. A little to one side of them, on the same vessel and a bit lower, is a third white all-round light. The vessel is fishing, not trawling, and is not making way. The offset white light is her outlying gear light: her net extends more than 150 m horizontally in that direction. Keep well clear and do not pass on that side of her.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a troller as a vessel engaged in fishing.
- Mixing up the colours: green over white is trawling; red over white is other fishing.
- Expecting a fishing vessel at anchor to show anchor lights.
- Reading the Annex II white over red (hauling) as a pilot vessel (pilot is white over red at the masthead, with no fishing lights above).
- Confusing the outlying gear cone (apex up) with the motor-sailing cone (apex down).
