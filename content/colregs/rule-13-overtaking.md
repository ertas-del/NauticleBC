# Rule 13: Overtaking

## What it is

Rule 13 says that ==any vessel overtaking== any other vessel ==keeps out of the way== of the vessel being
overtaken. It starts with "notwithstanding anything contained in" Sections I and II. That wording puts Rule 13
**above** Rule 12 (sailing), Rule 15 (crossing) and Rule 18 (responsibilities between vessels).

So a sailing vessel overtaking a power-driven vessel keeps clear of her. So does a vessel engaged in fishing.
The Rule 18 list of who keeps out of whose way starts with "except where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require".

## When are you overtaking?

You are overtaking when you come up with another vessel from a direction ==more than 22.5 degrees abaft her
beam==. Picture it from the vessel ahead: anything approaching from behind a line 22.5 degrees aft of her beam,
on either side, is in her overtaking sector.

The rule ties this to her lights. From that sector, at night, you would see ==only her sternlight== and neither of
her sidelights. The arcs line up:

| Light | Arc | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Each sidelight | ==112.5 degrees== | Right ahead to 22.5 degrees abaft the beam, on its own side |
| Sternlight | ==135 degrees== | 67.5 degrees each side of dead astern |

112.5 + 112.5 + 135 = 360, so every direction sees either a sidelight or the sternlight. The boundary between
"crossing" and "overtaking" is the same line where her sidelight disappears and her sternlight appears.

## Doubt, and a changing bearing

- **Doubt.** If you are in ==any doubt== whether you are overtaking, ==assume that you are== and act accordingly.
  Near the 22.5 degree line, at night with one light flickering between sidelight and sternlight, you are in
  doubt. Treat yourself as overtaking.
- **Bearing drift.** As you draw up on the other vessel her bearing from you changes, and you may end up forward of
  her beam. That does ==not make you a crossing vessel==. You keep the duty to keep clear until you are
  ==finally past and clear==.

## Canadian modification

The Canadian Regulations add **Rule 13(e)** for the ==waters of the Great Lakes Basin== only. There, at night, a
vessel is also treated as overtaking if she could see the all-round white light (or lights) that some
power-driven vessels on the Great Lakes may show instead of a separate sternlight, but not the sidelights. It does
not change Rule 13 on the BC coast.

## A BC bridge example

You are on watch in a 15 metre crew boat making 20 knots up Haro Strait at night. Ahead, slightly to starboard, is a
single white light that is drawing slowly closer. No red or green.

- A single white light ahead that you are gaining on is most likely a sternlight. (It could also be a vessel at
  anchor or a small boat showing an all-round white light, so use radar to check her movement.) If it is a
  sternlight, you are coming up from her stern sector: you are overtaking and you keep out of the way.
- Pass with plenty of room, on whichever side gives the most room and the least conflict with other traffic.
  There is no rule that you must overtake on her port side in open water.
- As you pull ahead and her bearing moves forward of your beam, you are still the overtaking vessel. Do not turn
  across her bow until you are finally past and clear.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a sailing vessel or a fishing vessel has priority over a power-driven vessel she is overtaking.
- Claiming the crossing rule because the bearing has drifted forward of the other vessel's beam.
- Guessing "crossing" when you are near the 22.5 degree line instead of assuming you are overtaking.
- Cutting in ahead of the overtaken vessel before you are finally past and clear.
- Confusing Rule 13 with the Rule 9 overtaking signals. In a narrow channel the signals are added; the duty to keep
  clear is the same.

3. You are a sailing vessel overtaking a tug. Who keeps clear?
4. What must you do if you are unsure whether you are overtaking or crossing?
5. When does an overtaking vessel's duty end?
