# Rule 25: Sailing vessels underway and vessels under oars

Sailing vessels are lit to look **different from power-driven vessels**: they show ==no masthead light==. If you see sidelights and a sternlight but no white masthead light, think "sail".

## What the rule asks of you

- A ==sailing vessel underway== shows ==sidelights and a sternlight==.
- On a sailing vessel ==under 20 m==, these may be ==combined in one lantern== (a tricolour) ==at or near the top of the mast==, where it can best be seen.
- A sailing vessel may also show, at or near the top of the mast, ==two all-round lights in a vertical line, red over green==. These ==may not be used together with the tricolour lantern==.
- A sailing vessel ==under 7 m== shows the sailing lights if practicable. If she does not, she must have ==ready at hand an electric torch or lighted lantern showing a white light==, shown in time to prevent collision.
- A ==vessel under oars== may show the sailing lights. If she does not, she needs the same ==white torch or lantern== ready at hand.
- A vessel ==under sail and also propelled by machinery== shows by day, forward where it can best be seen, a ==cone, apex downwards==.

## Canadian modification

In ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, a vessel ==under 12 m== that is motor-sailing ==is not required to show the cone==, but may do so.

## Plain-language explanation

The key definition is in Rule 3: a sailing vessel is under sail ==with any propelling machinery not being used==. The moment the engine is in gear she is a power-driven vessel, whatever her rig. At night that means she must show a masthead light (or, under 12 m, an all-round white light) as well as sidelights and sternlight. By day she shows the cone, apex down, so others know not to treat her as a sailing vessel.

Memory aid for the optional masthead lights: ==red over green, sailing machine==. They make a sailing vessel easier to recognise from a distance. Remember that the Rule does not allow them together with the tricolour lantern.

## A BC bridge example

Off English Bay at dusk you see a red light and a green light close together, and no white light above them. That is very likely a sailing vessel heading towards you. Ten minutes later she shows a white light above her sidelights. Either you are now looking at a different vessel, or she has started her engine and is now a power-driven vessel. Treat her as power-driven: the responsibilities in Rule 18 change with the light.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking a sailboat with her sails up and engine running is still a sailing vessel.
- Showing the tricolour and the red-over-green lights at the same time.
- Assuming the cone is optional everywhere for small boats. The Canadian exemption is for vessels under 12 m in Canadian inland waters.
- Thinking a rowboat must carry full navigation lights. It needs a white torch or lantern ready at hand if it does not.
