# Rule 23: Power-driven vessels underway

This is the light set you will see most often at night: ferries, tugs running light, water taxis, workboats and pleasure boats under power.

## What the rule asks of you

A ==power-driven vessel underway== shows:

1. a ==masthead light forward==;
2. a ==second masthead light abaft of and higher than the forward one==. A vessel ==under 50 m is not obliged== to show it, but may;
3. ==sidelights==;
4. a ==sternlight==.

Extra lights for special craft:

- An ==air-cushion vessel== (hovercraft) operating in the ==non-displacement mode== adds an ==all-round flashing yellow light==.
- A ==WIG craft== (wing-in-ground), only when ==taking off, landing and in flight near the surface==, adds a ==high-intensity all-round flashing red light==.

## Small power-driven vessels

- **Under 12 m:** instead of the full set, she may show an ==all-round white light and sidelights==. If centreline fitting is impracticable, the white light may be off the centreline, provided the sidelights are in one combined lantern on the centreline or as nearly as practicable in the same fore and aft line as the white light.
- **Under 7 m and maximum speed not more than 7 knots (international text):** she may show ==only an all-round white light==, with sidelights if practicable.
- **Canadian modification:** that under-7 m option ==does not apply to a Canadian power-driven vessel in any waters==, or to a non-Canadian power-driven vessel in Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway. In practice a small, slow Canadian powerboat still needs sidelights, using either the under-12 m option or the full set.

## Positioning (Annex I highlights)

- On a power-driven vessel of 20 m or more, the forward masthead light is at least 6 m above the hull (higher on broad vessels, but never required above 12 m). The ==after masthead light is at least 4.5 m higher== than the forward one.
- With two masthead lights, they are ==at least half the vessel's length apart== horizontally (never required to exceed 100 m), and the forward one is no more than a quarter of the length from the stem.
- Sidelights on a power-driven vessel are no higher than three-quarters of the height of the forward masthead light.
- A masthead light or the under-12 m all-round white light carried with sidelights must be ==at least 1 m higher than the sidelights==. A Canadian modification repeats this for vessels under 12 m in Canadian inland waters.
- **Canadian modification:** on power-driven vessels under 12 m built after 31 July 1983, the masthead or all-round light must be screened so it does not light up the vessel forward of the operator.

## Plain-language explanation

Two masthead lights act like a range. The forward one is lower and the after one higher, so as the vessel turns the two lights open and close. Two white masthead lights ==in line, one above the other, with both sidelights below== means a power-driven vessel heading straight for you. If she alters course, the lights open out: the lower (forward) light swings towards the side her bow is pointing, and one of the sidelights disappears. The forward light is on the side her bow points to, and the sidelight you can still see confirms which side of her you are looking at.

A single masthead light does not prove the vessel is small. Any vessel under 50 m may legally show just one.

## A BC bridge example

Crossing the Strait of Georgia at night you see two white lights, the left one lower than the right, with a red light below them. The lower light is her forward masthead light, so her bow points to your left: she is a power-driven vessel, often 50 m or more (only vessels of that size must carry two masthead lights), showing you her port side and crossing from right to left. Later you see a white masthead light, sidelights and a quick-flashing yellow light: an air-cushion vessel in non-displacement mode, which may be fast and can move sideways in a way displacement vessels cannot.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking every power-driven vessel must show two masthead lights. Only 50 m and over must.
- Assuming the under-7 m "white light only" option applies to Canadian boats. It does not.
- Forgetting that a motor-sailing yacht is a power-driven vessel and shows these lights, not sailing lights.
- Mixing up the hovercraft light (flashing yellow) with the WIG light (high-intensity flashing red).
