# Rule 11: Application of Section II

## What it is

Rule 11 is one sentence: the rules in this Section apply to ==vessels in sight of one another==. "This Section" is
**Section II**, ==Rules 11 to 18==. It holds the rules most people mean when they say "right of way": sailing
vessels (Rule 12), overtaking (13), head-on (14), crossing (15), the give-way vessel (16), the stand-on vessel (17)
and the responsibilities between different kinds of vessel (18).

## Plain explanation

The key words are "in sight". Rule 3 defines them: vessels are in sight of one another only when one can be
==observed visually== from the other. That means with your eyes (binoculars count), not with electronics.

- A contact you see **only on radar** is not in sight.
- A target you see **only on AIS** is not in sight.
- A vessel you can see through a gap in the fog **is** in sight, even if visibility is poor.

Why does this matter? The give-way and stand-on roles work because each bridge can see the other vessel's aspect,
lights and shapes, and can read what she is doing. Without that, neither vessel can be sure who she is or which
way she is heading. So when you are not in sight of each other, there is **no stand-on vessel and no give-way
vessel**. In or near restricted visibility, ==Rule 19== (Section III) tells both vessels what to do instead.

Section I (Rules 4 to 10) does not switch off. Lookout, safe speed, risk of collision and the Rule 8 qualities of
an avoiding action apply alongside Section II all the time.

## A BC bridge example

You are northbound in the Strait of Georgia on a hazy morning. Radar shows a target on your starboard bow at 4
miles, bearing steady. You cannot see her.

- Right now Section II does not apply between you. You are not the give-way vessel, and she is not the stand-on
  vessel. If you are in or near an area of restricted visibility, Rule 19 governs.
- At 2 miles the haze thins and you make her out by eye: a tug and tow crossing from starboard. From this moment
  you are in sight of each other and Section II applies. Rules 15 and 18 now decide who keeps out of the way.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a radar or AIS contact as if the crossing or overtaking rules apply to it.
- Thinking "in sight" needs good visibility. It only needs one vessel to be able to see the other.
- Forgetting that Section I still applies once Section II applies.
- Assuming the Section II roles carry on after the other vessel disappears back into fog.
