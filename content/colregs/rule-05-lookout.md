# Rule 5: Keeping a proper lookout

**Pipeline sample.** Written to exercise the content tools. A certified master must review it before release.

## What the rule asks of you

Every vessel must keep a proper lookout, **at all times**. That means:

- by **sight**,
- by **hearing**,
- and by **all other means that suit the conditions** at the time.

The rule says "at all times" because the sea does not give notice. Clear daylight in open water is exactly when
people stop looking.

## What it is for

The lookout exists to let you make a **full appraisal of the situation** and of the **risk of collision**. It is
the input to every later decision. If you have not seen a vessel, you cannot judge risk or decide how to avoid it.

## "All available means"

Think of everything on the vessel that adds to the picture:

- radar, AIS and electronic charts, used as aids and not as a replacement for looking out of the window,
- VHF radio, listening for other traffic and for broadcasts,
- sound signals from other vessels, especially in poor visibility,
- other crew who can look or listen from a different position.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a plotter or radar screen as the lookout.
- Letting the person at the helm be the only lookout while also navigating, plotting and talking on the radio.
- Relaxing the watch because "nobody is around".

## Check yourself

1. Does Rule 5 only apply in restricted visibility?
2. Name three ways, other than sight, you can keep a lookout.
3. What decision depends on the lookout being done properly?
