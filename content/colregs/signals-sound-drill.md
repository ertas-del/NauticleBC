# Sound signal drill: what did you hear?

This lesson is a drill. Read the method, look at the decoder table, then work through the questions. Each one gives
you a sound and asks what it is and what you should do.

## The method

1. **Can you see the other vessel?** If yes, think ==Rule 34== (manoeuvring, overtaking, doubt). If you cannot see
   it because of fog, rain or snow, think ==Rule 35== (fog signals). The ==bend signal== is the exception: it is used
   whenever an obstruction hides other vessels.
2. **Whistle or bell?** A ==bell== (and maybe a gong) means a vessel ==at anchor or aground==. A whistle means a
   vessel underway, or a special anchored case.
3. **How does it start?** Underway fog signals ==start with a prolonged blast==. Manoeuvring signals and the doubt signal
   ==start with short blasts==. Overtaking signals ==start with two prolonged==.
4. **How often?** Underway fog signals repeat at ==not more than 2 minutes==. The anchor bell repeats at
   ==not more than 1 minute==.
5. **Is it distress?** A ==continuous== horn, or ==SOS== (three short, three prolonged, three short), means distress.

## Decoder

| You hear | Where | It is |
|---|---|---|
| one prolonged | fog | power-driven, making way |
| two prolonged | fog | power-driven, stopped, no way |
| one prolonged, two short | fog | not under command, restricted, constrained by draught, sailing, fishing, or towing/pushing |
| one prolonged, three short | fog | manned vessel being towed |
| bell rapidly about 5 s | fog | at anchor |
| bell then gong | fog | at anchor, 100 m or more |
| three strokes, bell, three strokes | fog | aground |
| one short, one prolonged, one short | fog | anchored vessel warning you |
| four short after the normal signal | fog | pilot vessel on duty |
| one short / two short / three short | in sight | altering to starboard / altering to port / astern propulsion |
| two prolonged, one short / two prolonged, two short | narrow channel | intends to overtake on your starboard / port side |
| prolonged, short, prolonged, short | narrow channel | agreement to be overtaken |
| five or more short and rapid | in sight | doubt or danger |
| one prolonged | at a bend | vessel approaching the bend (answer with one prolonged) |
| one prolonged | leaving a berth, Canadian harbour | power-driven vessel leaving the dock |
| continuous sounding | any | distress |

Note that ==one prolonged== appears three times: fog (making way), at a bend, and leaving a berth. Context tells you
which.

## What you do after hearing a fog signal

Rule 19 (restricted visibility) says that a vessel that hears the fog signal of another vessel
==apparently forward of her beam==, unless it has been determined that no risk of collision exists, must
==reduce speed to the minimum at which she can be kept on course==, ==take all her way off if necessary==, and
==navigate with extreme caution== until the danger of collision is over. You cannot tell a vessel's bearing
accurately by ear in fog, so do not assume "it sounds well clear".

## A bridge-watch example

You are crossing the Fraser River approaches in fog in a 14 metre power-driven vessel, sounding one prolonged every
2 minutes or less. Fine on the port bow you hear one prolonged, two short, then straight after it one prolonged,
three short. A tug and a manned tow, ahead of your beam. You slow to steerage way, tell the skipper, watch the
radar, and do not try to cross between the two signals.

## Common mistakes

- Answering a fog signal with a manoeuvring signal.
- Reading two prolonged as "two vessels" instead of one vessel stopped.
- Reading "one prolonged, two short" as a sailing vessel only. It covers several kinds of vessel.
- Ignoring a bell because "it's only an anchored boat". Anchored vessels are hazards you can hit.
- Treating the direction of a fog signal as reliable.

## Check yourself

Work through the drill questions for this lesson. Aim to answer each one in under ten seconds.
