# Recognising vessels by their lights and shapes

On the exam and on the bridge you will be given a set of lights or shapes, often in words, and asked "what is she, what is she doing, which way is she heading, and what do I do?" This lesson gives you a routine so you never have to guess.

## The four-step routine at night

1. **Special all-round lights first.** Look for coloured all-round lights in a vertical line, usually high up. They tell you **what she is doing** (fishing, NUC, RAM, pilot, aground).
2. **Masthead lights.** Count the white masthead lights. One or two **fore and aft** (lower forward, higher aft) means a power-driven vessel underway. Two or three **in a vertical line** means towing or pushing.
3. **Sidelights.** If you see a sidelight, she is underway and, for vessels whose special signal adds them only when moving, ==making way==. The colour gives her ==aspect==: ==green, you see her starboard side; red, her port side; both, she is heading towards you==.
4. **Stern lights.** A ==white sternlight== alone means you are more than 22.5 degrees abaft her beam (overtaking if you are closing). A ==yellow towing light above the sternlight== means she is ==towing astern==, so look for the tow.

Then confirm with radar and compass bearings before you act.

## The all-round light combinations

| What you see (top to bottom) | She is... |
|---|---|
| ==Red over white== | ==fishing (not trawling)== |
| ==Green over white== | ==trawling== |
| ==White over red== at the masthead | ==pilot vessel on duty== |
| ==Red over red== | ==not under command== |
| ==Red, white, red== | ==restricted in her ability to manoeuvre== |
| ==Red, red, red== | ==constrained by her draught== (not shown in Canadian inland waters) |
| Red over green at the masthead, with sidelights | sailing vessel (optional lights) |
| Anchor light(s) plus ==two reds== in a vertical line | ==aground== |
| ==Three greens==, one high and one each side lower | ==mine clearance== |
| All-round flashing yellow, with power-driven lights | air-cushion vessel |
| High-intensity all-round flashing red, with power-driven lights | WIG craft taking off, landing or flying low |
| Blue flashing light (Canada) | government or police vessel identifying herself |

## Memory aids

- ==Red over white, fishing at night.==
- ==Green over white, trawling at night.==
- ==White over red, pilot ahead.==
- ==Red over red, the captain is dead.== (not under command)
- ==Red over green, sailing machine.==
- Red, white, red: "restricted" (think of the white in the middle as the work going on).
- Aground: "anchored plus not under command".

The pairs red/white, white/red and green/white are easy to mix up in the dark. Always say the **upper** colour first.

## Reading masthead lights and aspect

- **Two masthead lights fore and aft, in line one above the other, with red and green below:** a power-driven vessel (often 50 m or more) heading straight at you. Head-on situation: think Rule 14.
- **Two masthead lights fore and aft, the lower one to your left, red sidelight:** her bow points to your left, you see her port side, she is crossing from right to left.
- **One masthead light, one sidelight:** a power-driven vessel, possibly under 50 m. Size is not certain from one light.
- **Sidelights and sternlight but no masthead light:** a sailing vessel under sail, or a vessel being towed. Look for a tug ahead with vertical masthead lights before you decide.
- **A single all-round white light low down:** could be a small power-driven vessel under 12 m seen from astern, a small vessel at anchor, or one of the white lights of a log tow or barge. Watch for more lights and use radar.

## Day shapes

| Shape (top to bottom) | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ==One ball== | ==at anchor== |
| ==Two balls== | ==not under command== |
| ==Three balls in a vertical line== | ==aground== |
| ==Ball, diamond, ball== | ==restricted in ability to manoeuvre== |
| ==Cylinder== | ==constrained by her draught== |
| ==Diamond== (on tug and tow) | ==tow longer than 200 m== |
| ==Two cones, apexes together== | ==engaged in fishing== |
| ==One cone, apex up== (beside the fishing shape) | ==outlying gear over 150 m in that direction== |
| ==One cone, apex down== (forward) | ==sailing vessel also under power== |
| Two balls on one side, two diamonds on the other (with ball-diamond-ball) | dredging or underwater work: pass on the diamond side |
| Rigid flag "A", at least 1 m high | small vessel engaged in diving |
| Three balls, one at the foremast head and one at each end of the fore yard | mine clearance |

## A BC bridge example

Night, Haro Strait, your vessel heading north. Fine on the starboard bow you see three white lights in a vertical line, a green light below them, and some distance astern of them, low on the water, two white lights side by side. Step 1: no special coloured all-round lights. Step 2: three masthead lights in a vertical line, so towing, with a tow over 200 m. Step 3: green sidelight, so you see her starboard side. Step 4: the low white lights astern are probably her tow. Conclusion: a tug with a long tow crossing ahead of you. Do not try to pass between the tug and the low lights; go astern of the whole tow and check with radar.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the lower colour first. Read from the top down.
- Forgetting that fishing, NUC, RAM and pilot vessels add sidelights only when moving (pilot vessels: when underway), so no sidelights usually means stopped.
- Assuming one masthead light means a small vessel.
- Missing the towed object because you only looked at the tug.
- Confusing three balls in a vertical line (aground) with three balls on the mast and yardarms (mine clearance).

## Check yourself

1. Red over white with a green sidelight: what is she and which side are you seeing?
2. Two whites in a vertical line, sidelights, sternlight, no yellow light: what is she doing?
3. Two all-round reds plus a white light forward and a lower white light aft: what is she?
4. By day, a black cylinder: what is she?
5. By day, two cones point to point and a single cone point up to one side: what is she, and where is her gear?
