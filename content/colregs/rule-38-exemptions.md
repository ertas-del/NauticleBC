# Rule 38: Exemptions

Rule 38 is the only rule in ==Part E, Exemptions==. It was written to ease the change-over from the 1960 collision
rules to the 1972 rules for vessels already built. You need to know what it is, not every detail.

## What the rule does

A vessel may be exempted from some requirements if:

- her ==keel was laid, or she was at a corresponding stage of construction==, before the Regulations came
  into force, and
- she complies with the ==1960 collision rules==.

The exemptions are about equipment that was expensive to change on an existing vessel: light ranges and colours, the
position of lights, and sound signal appliances.

- Most exemptions were ==time-limited==, either 4 or 9 years from entry into force.
- A few, all about the ==position of lights==, are permanent. For example, small shifts in light positions that
  came only from converting imperial measurements to metric.
- The exemption from the ==Annex III sound signal appliance== requirements lasted ==9 years==.

## Canadian paragraphs

The Canadian text adds paragraphs that fix when the clock started. The 9-year periods are counted from
==July 15, 1977==, the date the 1972 rules came into force internationally, or from ==March 1, 1983== for certain
vessels. That means the 9-year exemptions ended in 1986 or 1992. There is also a Canadian paragraph about the lights
of certain vessels not required to be inspected; check its exact terms in the current text if it ever matters to you.

## A bridge-watch example

You join a wooden seiner built in the 1960s. The owner says "she's grandfathered" when you ask why the whistle is a
weak hand horn. For sound signal appliances the 9-year exemption has long expired, so the boat must meet Rule 33 and
Annex III like any other vessel her size. Rule 38 is not a general "old boat" exemption.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Rule 38 as a blanket exemption for any older vessel.
- Forgetting that the vessel also had to comply with the 1960 rules.
- Thinking the sound signal equipment exemption still applies. It was 9 years.

## Check yourself

1. Which vessels can Rule 38 exempt?
2. How long did the exemption for Annex III sound signal appliances last?
3. From what date does the Canadian text count the 9-year periods?
4. Which exemptions are permanent, in general terms?
