# Annex II: Fishing in close proximity

Rule 26 gives every vessel engaged in fishing her basic lights: ==green over white== for trawling and ==red over
white== for other fishing. When a fleet is working close together, those lights alone do not tell the next boat what
a trawler is doing with her net. ==Annex II== adds signals for that. Rule 26(d) brings it in: it applies to a vessel
engaged in fishing ==in close proximity to other vessels engaged in fishing==.

This lesson builds on the Rule 26 lesson; read that first.

## Where the lights go and how far they show

- They are placed where they can best be seen.
- They are at least ==0.9 m apart==.
- They sit ==lower than the Rule 26 all-round lights== (the green over white, or red over white).
- They are visible ==all round the horizon== at ==at least 1 mile==, but at ==less range than the Rule 26 fishing
  lights==.

So the picture is always the same: the main fishing lights on top, the Annex II pair underneath and a little dimmer.

## Trawlers

A trawler of ==20 m or more==, using bottom (demersal) or midwater (pelagic) gear, shows:

| Activity | Lights in a vertical line |
|---|---|
| ==Shooting== nets | ==two white== |
| ==Hauling== nets | ==white over red== |
| Net ==fast on an obstruction== | ==two red== |

A trawler ==under 20 m== ==may== show the same lights but does not have to.

**Pair trawling** (two vessels towing one net between them): each vessel of 20 m or more shows, ==at night==, a
==searchlight directed forward and towards the other vessel== of the pair, and shows the trawler lights above when
shooting, hauling or fast.

## Purse seiners

A vessel fishing with purse seine gear ==may== show ==two yellow lights in a vertical line==. They ==flash
alternately every second==, with equal on and off periods, and are shown ==only when she is hampered by her gear==.

At night, alternating yellow lights under red over white tell you the seine is out and the vessel is hampered by it.
Give her and her net a wide berth.

## Canadian modifications

**Flag T.** In Canadian waters and fishing zones, a vessel ==pair trawling== flies International Code ==flag "T"==
at the foremast during daylight. Annex II has no day shapes for shooting, hauling or fast gear; by day you rely on the
Rule 26 shapes, the flag, the whistle signals below and what you can see of the gear.

**Whistle signals.** In Canadian waters and fishing zones, a vessel trawling or purse seining as described above
sounds these signals on her whistle ==in any condition of visibility==:

| Activity | Whistle signal |
|---|---|
| Shooting a net or gear | ==two prolonged, two short== |
| Hauling a net or gear | ==two prolonged, one short== |
| Net or gear fast on an obstruction | ==one short, two prolonged, one short== |

**In fog.** In or near restricted visibility she sounds the signal ==4 to 6 seconds after== her Rule 35(c) fog signal
(==one prolonged, two short==), so the two signals are heard as a pair.

A prolonged blast is 4 to 6 seconds; a short blast is about 1 second (Rule 32).

## A BC bridge example

Fishing grounds off the west coast of Vancouver Island, thick fog. Your radar shows two targets close together, about
a mile ahead. You hear one prolonged and two short blasts, then a few seconds later two prolonged and two short. The
first signal says a vessel engaged in fishing (or another vessel of the Rule 35(c) group). The second, in Canadian
waters, says a trawler or seiner is ==shooting== her net. Expect gear in the water astern and between the targets if
they are pair trawling, slow down and keep well clear.

## Watch out: similar whistle signals

Two prolonged and one short is also the narrow channel signal "I intend to overtake you on your starboard side", and
two prolonged and two short is "I intend to overtake you on your port side" (Rule 34(c)). Context tells you which: the
overtaking signals are only for vessels in sight of each other in a narrow channel or fairway; the fishing signals come
from vessels working gear on the fishing grounds, and in fog they follow the Rule 35(c) signal.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking Annex II replaces the Rule 26 lights. The Rule 26 lights stay on; Annex II lights are extra and lower.
- Reading the hauling signal (white over red, below green over white) as a pilot vessel. A pilot vessel shows white over
  red at or near the masthead, with no fishing lights above.
- Thinking the yellow purse seine lights are shown all the time. Only when she is hampered by her gear.
- Assuming trawlers under 20 m must show the lights. They may.

## Check yourself

2. How far must the Annex II lights be visible, and how do they compare with the main fishing lights?
3. What does a pair trawler show at night that a single trawler does not?
4. In Canadian waters you hear two prolonged and one short from a trawler on the fishing grounds. What is she doing?
5. In fog, when does she sound that signal?
