# Rule 18: Responsibilities between vessels

## What it is

Rules 12 to 15 deal with two vessels of the same kind (two sailboats, two power-driven vessels). Rule 18 deals with
vessels of **different** kinds that are in sight of one another. It is often taught as a pecking order: the less
able a vessel is to manoeuvre, the higher she sits.

It opens with an important qualifier: ==except where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require==. So in a narrow
channel or a traffic separation scheme, the duty "not to impede" can apply to a vessel that would otherwise sit
higher, and an ==overtaking vessel always keeps clear== whatever she is.

## The order

From most privileged to least:

1. ==Vessel not under command== (NUC)
2. ==Vessel restricted in her ability to manoeuvre== (RAM)
3. Vessel ==constrained by her draught== (CBD), a special case, see below
4. ==Vessel engaged in fishing== (underway)
5. ==Sailing vessel== (underway)
6. ==Power-driven vessel== (underway)
7. Seaplane on the water and WIG craft taking off, landing or flying near the surface

What each one must keep out of the way of:

| Vessel | Keeps out of the way of |
|---|---|
| Power-driven, underway | NUC, RAM, fishing, sailing |
| Sailing, underway | NUC, RAM, fishing |
| Engaged in fishing, underway | NUC, RAM, ==so far as possible== |

**Constrained by her draught.** Any vessel ==other than NUC or RAM== shall, ==if the circumstances of the case
admit==, ==avoid impeding== the safe passage of a vessel constrained by her draught that is showing the signals in
Rule 28. The CBD vessel herself navigates with particular caution, having full regard to her special condition.
"Avoid impeding" is a duty to act early so that a give-way situation never arises; it is not the same as
"keep out of the way".

**Seaplanes.** A seaplane on the water shall, in general, ==keep well clear of all vessels== and avoid impeding
them. Once risk of collision exists, she follows the Rules of Part B like any vessel.

**WIG craft** (wing-in-ground craft). When taking off, landing and in flight near the surface, she keeps well clear of
all other vessels and avoids impeding them. On the water surface, she complies with the Rules ==as a power-driven
vessel==.

## Definitions that catch people out

- **Engaged in fishing** means fishing with nets, lines, trawls or other gear that ==restricts manoeuvrability==.
  A vessel ==trolling==, or using gear that does not restrict manoeuvrability, is not "engaged in fishing" for
  the Rules. A sport boat trolling for salmon is just a power-driven vessel.
- **Sailing vessel** means under sail with no machinery being used. Motorsailing makes her power-driven.
- **NUC and RAM** depend on what the vessel can do, and are shown by her lights and shapes, not by her size.

## Canadian provisions that change who keeps clear

No Canadian paragraph was found in Rule 18 itself. But other Canadian paragraphs change the outcome in some
Canadian waters, and they matter on the BC coast:

- **Rule 9, current or tidal stream.** In the Canadian waters of a narrow channel or fairway with a current or tidal
  stream, between two power-driven vessels meeting from opposite directions, the vessel ==going with the stream is
  stand-on==.
- **Rule 9, barges.** In the Canadian waters of a narrow channel or fairway, a barge or an inconspicuous, partly
  submerged vessel or object must not be navigated, moored or anchored so as to impede other vessels.
- **Rule 15(b), rivers.** In Canadian waters, a vessel ==crossing a river== keeps out of the way of a
  power-driven vessel going up or down it (except on the St. Lawrence seaward of Ile Rouge).
- **Rule 13(e).** A Great Lakes Basin overtaking paragraph. Not relevant on the BC coast.

## A BC bridge example

You are in a power-driven vessel heading out of a harbour on a clear day. Ahead:

- A seine boat with her net out, showing the fishing signal: she is engaged in fishing. You keep out of her way.
- A sailboat under sail, close-hauled across your bow: you keep out of her way.
- The same sailboat is now coming up from your quarter and gaining in a fresh breeze: she is overtaking, so she keeps
  clear of you (Rule 13 comes first).
- A dredge showing the RAM signal: both you and the seine boat (so far as possible) keep out of her way.
- A floatplane taxiing toward the dock: in general she keeps well clear of you, but if risk of collision develops
  the ordinary Rules apply to both of you.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting the opening words. Rule 13 overtaking beats the pecking order every time.
- Treating a trolling sport boat as "engaged in fishing".
- Treating a motorsailing yacht as a sailing vessel.
- Thinking a large vessel is automatically RAM or CBD. It depends on her signals and situation.
- Confusing "avoid impeding" (act early so no risk of collision develops) with "keep out of the way" (a give-way
  duty once risk exists).
- Assuming a seaplane can never be stand-on. Once risk of collision exists she follows the Rules.
