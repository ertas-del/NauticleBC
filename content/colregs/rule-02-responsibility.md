# Rule 2: Responsibility

## What the rule asks of you

Rule 2 has two parts.

**Part (a), responsibility.** Nothing in the Rules frees ==a vessel, her owner, her master or her crew== from the consequences of:

- neglecting to comply with the Rules, or
- neglecting any **precaution** that the ==ordinary practice of seamen== or the ==special circumstances of the case== call for.

**Part (b), departure.** When you read and apply the Rules, you must have ==due regard== for all the dangers of navigation and collision and for any special circumstances, ==including the limitations of the vessels involved==. Those circumstances can make it ==necessary to depart from the Rules to avoid immediate danger==.

## Plain-language explanation

The Rules are not a complete checklist that you can tick off and then say you did everything. Part (a) says that responsibility sits with the owner, master and crew, and it also covers precautions that are not written anywhere in the Rules but that a prudent seaman would take, such as slowing down early, calling the other vessel on the VHF, or posting an extra lookout.

Part (b) is the safety valve. In a real emergency, sticking rigidly to the usual action might make things worse. For example, a vessel that is normally required to hold her course may need to act when the other vessel plainly is not giving way. But the wording is tight. The departure is justified only to avoid **immediate danger**, and you must first take into account the dangers around you and the **limitations of the vessels** involved (how fast each can turn or stop, for instance). Departing from the Rules is a last resort and not a way of saving time.

## On the bridge

You are the give-way vessel in a crossing situation in a narrow BC passage, and you have already decided to turn to starboard. Just as you begin, you see a third boat, a small unlit skiff, appear on that side. A turn to starboard would put you into it. Rule 2(b) allows you to do what the circumstances demand, for example a different turn or stopping, to avoid the immediate danger. Afterwards you should be able to explain why you departed from the usual action, what dangers you weighed, and why the departure was needed. If instead you had simply ignored the other vessel because "she should have given way," Rule 2(a) would still leave you answerable for neglecting a precaution.

## Common mistakes

- Reading "it was the other vessel's duty" as ==a complete defence==. Neglect of ordinary seamanship precautions can still count against you.
- Treating Rule 2(b) as permission to ignore a Rule whenever it is inconvenient. It is for avoiding **immediate danger**.
- Forgetting that responsibility is shared by the owner, master and crew, and not only by whoever was at the helm.
- Departing from the Rules without thinking about the other vessel's limitations, such as a vessel that cannot stop or turn quickly.
