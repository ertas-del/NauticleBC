# Part F: Additional Canadian provisions

The international Rules end at Rule 38. The Canadian Collision Regulations add a ==Part F, "Additional Canadian
Provisions"==, with ==Rules 39 to 46==. They apply to Canadian waters and Canadian vessels in the ways each Rule sets
out. The Rule 20 lesson already introduced three of them (Rules 39, 45 and 46); this lesson covers the whole Part.

## Rule 39: Dangerous goods

In the Canadian waters of a ==roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, a vessel taking in, discharging or
carrying ==dangerous goods== shows, where it can best be seen:

| Situation | Signal |
|---|---|
| ==Not underway== (alongside, at anchor) | an ==all-round red light== or International Code ==flag "B"== |
| ==Underway== | ==flag "B"==, ==but not== the red light |

## Rule 40: Radar reflectors

A vessel ==under 20 m==, or one ==built mainly of non-metallic material== (wood, fibreglass), ==shall, if
practicable==, carry a ==radar reflector== or other means that lets vessels using ==3 GHz or 9 GHz== radar (S-band and
X-band) detect her.

It does not apply when:

- the vessel operates in ==limited traffic==, ==daylight== and ==favourable conditions==, and a reflector is not
  essential for her safety; or
- her ==small size== or her operation ==away from radar navigation== makes it impracticable.

The reflector should meet the IMO performance standards named in the Rule (the newer MSC.164(78) standard for
reflectors fitted on or after July 1, 2005), as far as practicable.

## Rule 41: Radar transponders

==No vessel, except one in distress==, may use a transponder that sends radar responder or radar beacon signals in the
==3 cm or 10 cm marine radar bands== (the same X-band and S-band). The exception is an offshore exploration or
exploitation vessel that the Minister has authorized, where the transponder will not interfere with radar navigation.
(Survival craft radar transponders are distress signals under Annex IV.)

## Rules 42 and 43: Offshore exploration vessels and safety zones

An "exploration or exploitation vessel" is one able to drill for, produce or process oil or gas.

- A stationary drilling or production vessel shows ==white light(s) flashing Morse "U"== (dot dot dash) at intervals of
  not more than 15 seconds, visible all round at a nominal 15 miles, and in visibility of 2 miles or less sounds
  ==Morse "U" every 30 seconds==. She also has yellow identification panels with black letters at least 1 m high.
- Her ==safety zone== runs from her outer edges to ==500 m in all directions== or ==50 m beyond her anchor pattern==,
  ==whichever is greater== (the Minister may set a larger one).
- ==No vessel may navigate in the safety zone== unless she is in distress, saving life or helping a vessel in distress,
  operating for the government with jurisdiction, or has the ==permission of the person in charge== of the
  exploration vessel.

## Rule 44: Ocean Data Acquisition Systems (ODAS)

An ODAS is any object on or in the water designed to collect, store or send data about the sea or the
atmosphere, such as a data buoy. A Canadian ODAS shows its identification number. One
that ==floats with part of it above the water== must:

- be coloured ==yellow==, with a shape that cannot be mistaken for a navigation mark; and, where technically
  practicable,
- carry a ==yellow "X" topmark==,
- show a ==yellow light giving five flashes every 20 seconds==, visible at least ==5 miles==, and
- have a sound signal at intervals of not more than two minutes that cannot be confused with other signals.

It must also have a radar reflector meeting Rule 40, unless the Minister has decided in writing that it is not a danger
to navigation. An underwater ODAS is either escorted by a surface vessel or tethered to a marked, lit float.

## Rule 45: Blue flashing light

A ==government vessel== or a vessel of a harbour, river, county or municipal ==police force== may show a ==blue
flashing light== as an identification signal when helping anyone in grave and imminent danger, or on law enforcement
in Canadian waters. A ==Canadian Coast Guard Auxiliary== vessel may show it in search and rescue ==at the Coast Guard's
request==. Showing it ==does not relieve the vessel from the steering and sailing rules==.

## Rule 46: Alternate system of navigation lights

A Canadian vessel that Part C requires to show lights must have an ==alternate system of navigation lights==: a backup
set of masthead lights, sidelights, sternlight and anchor lights.

- It does ==not apply to vessels under 15 m==, ==cable ferries== or ==pleasure craft==.
- Vessels built before 1991 of under 500 gross tonnage may use oil or electronic lanterns as the backup.
- A towed or pushed vessel or object that cannot be fitted must have spare lights readily available.
- Lights using a flame must not be the backup on a vessel carrying, towing or pushing volatile or explosive cargo.

## A BC bridge example

You skipper an 18 m fibreglass water taxi in Vancouver Harbour. Under Rule 40 she should carry a radar reflector,
because she is under 20 m and non-metallic and the harbour has heavy radar-navigating traffic. Under Rule 46, as a
commercial vessel of 15 m or more required to show lights, she needs a backup set of navigation lights. On the way in
you pass a tanker barge alongside a terminal showing an all-round red light: Rule 39, dangerous goods, not underway.

## Common mistakes

- Showing the red dangerous goods light underway. Underway it is flag "B" only.
- Thinking Rule 40 is optional for every small boat. It says "shall, if practicable", with two specific exceptions.
- Thinking a blue flashing light gives a police or Coast Guard vessel right of way.
- Applying Rule 46 to pleasure craft or vessels under 15 m.
