# Rule 28: Vessels constrained by their draught

A deep-draught vessel in a narrow, shallow stretch may be unable to turn off her track. Rule 28 gives her a way to say so.

## What the rule says

- A ==vessel constrained by her draught== ==may==, in addition to the ==power-driven vessel lights of Rule 23==, show ==three all-round red lights in a vertical line==.
- By day she ==may== show a ==cylinder==.
- The signal is ==optional== in the international rule.

Under Rule 3 only a ==power-driven vessel== can be constrained by her draught, and the test is her draught compared with the ==available depth and width== of navigable water.

## Canadian modification

In ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, ==no vessel may show three all-round red lights in a vertical line or a cylinder==. So inside Canadian harbours and inland waters you should not see this signal at all; it is something you may meet in waters outside that phrase, or in foreign waters.

The Regulations do not define "inland waterway" for this purpose, and how the phrase applies to particular stretches of the BC coast is a question for your instructor or examiner. When in doubt, recognise the signal wherever you see it.

## What the signal asks of others

Rule 18(d): any vessel other than one not under command or restricted in her ability to manoeuvre shall, if the circumstances of the case admit, ==avoid impeding the safe passage== of a vessel constrained by her draught that is showing the Rule 28 signals. The deep-draught vessel herself must navigate with particular caution.

## Memory aid

Three reds stacked up: ==three reds, deep draught ahead==. Two reds is not under command; three reds is constrained by draught.

## A BC bridge example

Your vessel, normally working out of Vancouver, is delivering a barge to a port outside Canada. In the dredged approach channel at night you see three all-round red lights in a vertical line with the masthead lights and sidelights of a large ship. She is a power-driven vessel constrained by her draught. Plan early to avoid impeding her, so she does not have to leave the deep water she depends on. Back in a Canadian harbour, the same ship would not be allowed to show those three reds.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing three reds (constrained by draught) with two reds (not under command).
- Thinking a sailing vessel can show the signal.
- Thinking the signal is required. It is optional internationally, and banned in Canadian inland waters.
- Thinking the signal gives her the full stand-on status of a NUC or RAM vessel. Others must avoid impeding her if circumstances admit.
