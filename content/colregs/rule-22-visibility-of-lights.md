# Rule 22: Visibility of lights

Rule 22 sets how far away each light must be visible. The ranges are **minimums**, in nautical miles, and they depend on the length of the vessel.

## What the rule says (in plain terms)

| Light | 50 m or more | 12 m to under 50 m | Under 12 m |
|---|---|---|---|
| Masthead | ==6 miles== | ==5 miles== (==3 miles if under 20 m==) | ==2 miles== |
| Sidelight | ==3 miles== | ==2 miles== | ==1 mile== |
| Sternlight | 3 miles | 2 miles | 2 miles |
| Towing light | 3 miles | 2 miles | 2 miles |
| All-round (white, red, green or yellow) | 3 miles | 2 miles | 2 miles |

Two more lines:

- An ==inconspicuous, partly submerged vessel or object being towed==: its white all-round lights ==3 miles==.
- **Canadian modification:** a ==special flashing light== or ==blue flashing light==: ==2 miles==.

The lights must have the intensity set out in ==Annex I, section 8== to achieve these ranges. A "mile" in the Regulations is the international nautical mile of 1 852 m.

## Plain-language explanation

Three patterns make the table easy to remember:

1. **The masthead light always has the longest range** for its size class, because it is the first light you want to see.
2. **Under 12 m, only the sidelight drops to 1 mile.** Every other light on a small vessel is 2 miles.
3. **The 20 m split only affects the masthead light.** A 15 m vessel and a 45 m vessel both have 2 mile sidelights, but the 15 m vessel's masthead light needs only 3 miles while the 45 m vessel's needs 5.

## What the range does not tell you

These are the minimum ranges in clear weather that the equipment must meet, worked out with an atmospheric transmissivity matching about 13 miles meteorological visibility (Annex I section 8). They are not a promise that you will see a light at that range. Rain, sea spray, dirty lenses, background shore lights and a low height of eye all cut the real range. A light seen for the first time at close range may be on a small vessel, not a distant big one.

## A BC bridge example

Approaching Active Pass at night, you pick up a red sidelight but no masthead light at what radar says is about 1.5 miles. A sidelight on a vessel of 12 m or more must be visible at 2 miles or more, so a large ferry's red light would normally have shown up earlier, together with her masthead lights. It may be a small vessel whose masthead or all-round light is hidden behind the shore lights. Keep watching and do not assume size from one light.

## Common mistakes

- Giving the under-12 m sternlight 1 mile. Only the sidelight is 1 mile.
- Forgetting the 3 mile masthead light for vessels of 12 m to under 20 m.
- Treating the ranges as "you will see it at this distance". They are minimum design ranges.
- Mixing up statute miles and nautical miles.
