# NS1 review: the classic COLREGs traps

*Study aid, not a navigation aid. This lesson does not add new rules. It pulls together the exam-focus facts from the COLREGs lessons already in the app and puts the common mistakes side by side. Go back to the rule lessons for the full detail.*

## What you need to know

Most lost marks on a COLREGs paper are not from rules the candidate never learned. They come from a rule they know being applied in the wrong situation, or from a tempting shortcut ("bigger vessel wins", "red means stop"). This review lists eleven of those traps. Each has the same shape: **what people assume**, **what the rule says**, and **why the assumption fails**.

## A routine that avoids most traps

For any scenario, ask three questions in this order:

1. **Can I see her?** Visually, not on radar. In sight: Section II (Rules 11 to 18). Not in sight in or near restricted visibility: Rule 19.
2. **Which rule?** Overtaking (13) is checked before head-on (14) and crossing (15). Rule 18 only sorts vessels *after* Rules 9, 10 and 13.
3. **Who acts?** Give-way acts early and substantially (Rule 16). Stand-on keeps her course and speed until the stages of Rule 17 say otherwise.

Section I (Rules 4 to 10: lookout, safe speed, risk of collision, action to avoid collision, narrow channels, traffic separation) is always in force in the background.

## The traps

### Trap 1: "She is on my port side, so I am stand-on" (in fog)

- **Assumption:** the crossing rules still decide who gives way once the other vessel is on radar.
- **Rule:** ==Section I applies in any visibility==. ==Section II applies only to vessels in sight of one another==, and ==a contact seen only on radar or AIS is not in sight==. In or near restricted visibility, when you are not in sight of each other, Rule 19 applies.
- **Under Rule 19:** there is ==no stand-on and no give-way vessel==. Every vessel that detects risk acts. If you turn, avoid a turn to port for a vessel forward of the beam (unless you are overtaking her) and avoid turning towards a vessel abeam or abaft.

### Trap 2: "My boat has sails up, so I am a sailing vessel"

- **Assumption:** sails up means sailing-vessel privileges.
- **Rule:** a sailing vessel is under sail provided any propelling machinery is not being used. ==A sailboat with her engine in use is a power-driven vessel==. At night she shows power-driven lights; by day she shows a cone, apex down (a vessel under 12 m in Canadian inland waters need not, but may).
- **Result:** Rule 12 does not apply to her, and she does not get the right-of-way over power-driven vessels that Rule 18 gives to true sailing vessels.

### Trap 3: "A sailing vessel always has right of way"

- **Assumption:** Rule 18 puts sailing vessels ahead of power-driven vessels in every case.
- **Rule:** Rule 18 applies **except** where Rules 9, 10 and 13 otherwise require. ==Rule 13 overrides Rules 12 and 18==: ==any vessel overtaking any other vessel keeps out of the way==, so a sailing vessel that comes up on a power-driven vessel from more than ==22.5 degrees abaft her beam== must keep clear. If in any doubt whether you are overtaking, ==assume you are==. A later change of bearing does not turn an overtaking vessel into a crossing vessel; her duty lasts until she is finally past and clear.

### Trap 4: "Red means she has to give way to me" (and the head-on mix-up)

- **Crossing:** the vessel which has the other on her own ==starboard== side keeps out of the way, and if circumstances admit ==avoids crossing ahead== of her. At night, a **red** sidelight on your starboard side normally makes you the give-way vessel. Red to your starboard bow means *you* act.
- **Head-on:** both power-driven vessels ==alter to starboard== and pass port to port. ==Neither is stand-on.== By night, masthead lights in a line and/or both sidelights ahead. If in doubt, assume it is head-on. Head-on does not apply to sailing vessels, or to a power-driven vessel meeting a sailing vessel.
- **Canadian twist:** in a Canadian narrow channel with a current, the power-driven vessel going with the stream is the stand-on vessel, overriding Rule 14(a).

### Trap 5: "The stand-on vessel must hold on until the crash"

- **Rule 17 has three stages.** Stage 1: keep course and speed. Stage 2: she ==may== act by her own manoeuvre as soon as it is apparent the give-way vessel is not acting appropriately. Stage 3: when collision cannot be avoided by the give-way vessel alone she ==shall== act to best aid avoidance.
- **Port-turn limit:** a power-driven vessel acting at stage 2 in a crossing situation avoids, if circumstances admit, a ==turn to port for a vessel on her own port side==.
- **Doubt signal:** a vessel in doubt whether the other is taking sufficient action sounds at least ==five short and rapid blasts==.
- **And:** Rule 17 does not relieve the give-way vessel of her duty.

### Trap 6: "Fishing boats always have priority"

- **Rule 18:** power-driven vessels keep out of the way of vessels not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, ==engaged in fishing== and sailing vessels.
- **But:** ==a vessel trolling, or fishing with gear that does not restrict her manoeuvrability, is not engaged in fishing==. She is treated as the kind of vessel she is (usually power-driven), so the ordinary crossing and head-on rules apply.
- Also: only a **power-driven** vessel can be constrained by her draught, and others should avoid impeding her if she shows the Rule 28 signals.

### Trap 7: Mixing up the vertical pairs of lights

| Lights (all-round, vertical) | Means |
|---|---|
| ==White over red== at the masthead | ==Pilot vessel on duty== |
| ==Red over white== | ==Fishing== (not trawling) |
| ==Green over white== | ==Trawling== |
| ==Two reds== | ==Not under command== |
| ==Red, white, red== | ==Restricted in ability to manoeuvre== |
| ==Anchor lights plus two reds== | ==Aground== |

Memory aid: the pilot vessel is "white over red" and fishing is the reverse. Sidelights and a sternlight added to any of these mean the vessel is making way.

### Trap 8: Fog signals and manoeuvring signals

- **Manoeuvring signals** (==one short = altering to starboard, two short = port, three short = astern propulsion==) are for vessels **in sight** of each other. They are ==not used in restricted visibility== when vessels cannot see each other.
- **In fog:** ==one prolonged and two short every 2 minutes== is a shared signal. It may be a vessel not under command, restricted in ability to manoeuvre, constrained by draught, sailing, fishing, or towing or pushing. The signal does not tell you which. Do not read "two short" as "altering to port".
- A power-driven vessel making way sounds one prolonged at intervals of not more than 2 minutes; underway but stopped, two prolonged.

### Trap 9: "The radar is clear, so the water is clear" and "bearing changing, so no risk"

- ==A clear radar or AIS screen does not prove the water is clear==: radar may miss small vessels and floating objects, and AIS shows only vessels that transmit.
- A **steady** compass bearing means risk of collision exists, but ==risk may exist even when the bearing changes==, especially with a very large vessel, a tow, or any vessel at close range. ==If there is any doubt, risk is deemed to exist.==

### Trap 10: "It was her duty to give way"

- ==Rule 2==: nothing excuses a vessel, owner, master or crew from the consequences of neglect, including neglecting any precaution required by the ordinary practice of seamen or by the special circumstances. "The other vessel was give-way" is ==not a complete defence==.
- A departure from the Rules is allowed ==only to avoid immediate danger==, and with due regard to all dangers of navigation and collision.

### Trap 11 (small ones that still cost marks)

- **Narrow channels:** a vessel under 20 m, or a sailing vessel, must not impede a vessel that can safely navigate only inside the channel.
- **Traffic lanes:** cross a lane on a heading as nearly as practicable at right angles; Rule 10 does not relieve you of other rules.
- **Masthead light range:** 12 m to under 50 m is 5 miles, but ==3 miles if under 20 m==. Under 12 m the sidelight is the only light at 1 mile.
- **Sound gear:** whistle at ==12 m or more==, bell at ==20 m or more==, gong at ==100 m or more==.
- **Whistle lengths:** short ==about 1 second==; prolonged ==4 to 6 seconds==.
- **Distress vs attention:** Rule 37 (distress, Annex IV) is not Rule 36 (attract attention). Do not use a distress signal for anything but distress.

## A BC example

Approaching Active Pass in fog, your radar shows a vessel two miles off your port bow, and you hear one prolonged and two short blasts every two minutes. Traps 1, 8 and 9 all apply. You cannot see her, so Rule 19 applies, not Rule 15: you are not stand-on. The signal could come from a tug with a tow, a fishing vessel or a sailing vessel, so it does not tell you what to do, only that someone restricted is near. A clear radar picture to starboard does not mean nothing is there. You slow to a safe speed with engines ready, avoid a turn to port for a contact forward of the beam, and if a close-quarters situation forward of the beam cannot be avoided you reduce to the minimum speed at which you can keep your course.

## Check yourself

Cover the traps and answer from memory:

1. Does a radar contact in fog make you stand-on or give-way?
2. What kind of vessel is a sailboat with her engine running?
3. A sailing yacht comes up on a tug from astern. Who keeps clear, and which rule decides?
4. You see a red sidelight on your starboard bow, bearing steady. What are you?
5. May the stand-on vessel act before collision is certain? Which stage is "may" and which is "shall"?
6. Is a boat trolling with a single line "engaged in fishing" for Rule 18?
7. White over red or red over white: which is the pilot vessel?
8. Does "two short" in fog mean "altering to port"?
9. The bearing of a close tow is changing. Is there risk of collision?
10. Does "she was give-way" excuse you under Rule 2?
