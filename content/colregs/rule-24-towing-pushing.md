# Rule 24: Towing and pushing

Towing is everyday work on the BC coast: tugs with log tows and barges, chip scows, fuel barges and pushed equipment. A tow is long, slow and hard to stop, so the lights have to tell you **there is something on the end of that line**.

## Towing astern

A power-driven vessel towing astern shows:

- instead of her forward or after masthead light, ==two masthead lights in a vertical line==. If the ==tow is longer than 200 m==, measured ==from the stern of the towing vessel to the after end of the tow==, ==three masthead lights in a vertical line==;
- sidelights and a sternlight;
- a ==towing light (yellow)== in a vertical line ==above the sternlight==;
- by day, when the tow exceeds 200 m, a ==diamond shape== where it can best be seen.

If she is 50 m or more she must still also carry her after masthead light (Rule 24(d) points back to Rule 23(a)(ii)), so a big tug may show the vertical masthead lights plus one more masthead light abaft them.

## Pushing ahead and towing alongside

- A pushing vessel and the vessel she pushes, ==rigidly connected in a composite unit==, are ==lit as one power-driven vessel== under Rule 23.
- Otherwise, a vessel ==pushing ahead or towing alongside== shows ==two masthead lights in a vertical line==, sidelights and a sternlight. There is ==no towing light==, because there is nothing astern of her.

## The vessels being towed or pushed

- A vessel or object being towed astern shows ==sidelights and a sternlight== (no masthead light), and a ==diamond== where best seen if the tow exceeds 200 m.
- A vessel ==pushed ahead== (not a composite unit) shows ==sidelights at her forward end==.
- A vessel ==towed alongside== shows a ==sternlight and sidelights at her forward end==.
- Any number of vessels towed alongside or pushed in a group are lit as one vessel.

## Inconspicuous, partly submerged vessels or objects

Low objects that are hard to see, such as a raft or a boom (the Regulations say "raft" includes a boom), have their own pattern:

- ==under 25 m wide==: one ==all-round white light at or near each end== (a dracone need not show the forward one);
- ==25 m or wider==: two more all-round white lights ==at or near the extremities of its breadth==;
- ==longer than 100 m==: extra all-round white lights so that ==no gap is more than 100 m==;
- by day, a ==diamond at or near the after end== of the last object towed, and, if the tow exceeds 200 m, another diamond as far forward as practicable.

## Canadian modifications that matter in BC

These apply in ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, where it is impracticable to meet the international rule:

- **Log tows.** Instead of the inconspicuous-object pattern, a log tow shows all-round white lights:
  - under 25 m wide: ==one at or near each end==, plus ==one at mid-length if over 100 m long==;
  - 25 m or wider: ==four, one at or near each corner==, plus ==one at the mid-point of each side if over 100 m long==.
- **Barges.** A towed barge that cannot show sidelights and a sternlight shows ==an all-round white light at each end==. A group of barges may instead light ==each end of the group==, and if the group is ==over 100 m== long, add one light as near as practicable to its ==mid-point==.
- Annex I adds that these white lights on barges and inconspicuous objects are all at the same height, at least 2 m above the water.

Other Canadian towing rules (two towing lights for pushing, special flashing light on pushed barges) apply only in the Great Lakes Basin.

## Other points

- A towing operation that severely restricts the tug and tow's ability to deviate from their course adds the restricted-in-ability-to-manoeuvre signals (Rule 27(c)).
- A vessel not normally used for towing that tows a vessel in distress need not show the towing lights if it is impracticable, but must do all she can to show the relationship, in particular by ==lighting up the towline==.

## A BC bridge example

In Johnstone Strait at night you see three white lights in a vertical line with a green light below them. That is a power-driven vessel towing astern, showing you her starboard side, with a tow longer than 200 m. If you later see her from astern, you will see a yellow towing light above her white sternlight. Somewhere behind her is the tow, perhaps a log tow lit only by low white lights, with a long towline between. **Do not pass between a tug and her tow.** By day, the same tug shows a black diamond, and so does the tow.

## Common mistakes

- Counting masthead lights in a vertical line and forgetting what the third light means (tow over 200 m).
- Expecting a towing light on a vessel pushing ahead.
- Looking for masthead lights on the tow. Towed vessels show sidelights and a sternlight only.
- Missing the log tow's low white lights against shore lights.
- Measuring tow length from the tug's bow instead of her stern.
