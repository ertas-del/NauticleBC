# Rule 30: Anchored vessels and vessels aground

BC anchorages, booming grounds and log storage areas are full of vessels and objects that are not underway. Rule 30 tells you how they are lit, and the aground signal warns you that the water there is too shallow.

## At anchor

A vessel at anchor shows, where it can best be seen:

- in the ==fore part==, an ==all-round white light== (by day ==one ball==);
- ==at or near the stern==, and ==lower than the forward light==, a second ==all-round white light==.

Options and extras:

- A vessel ==under 50 m== may show ==one all-round white light== where it can best be seen instead.
- A vessel at anchor ==may==, and a vessel of ==100 m or more shall==, also use her ==working or equivalent lights to illuminate her decks==.
- Annex I: when two anchor lights are carried, the forward one is ==at least 4.5 m above the after one==, and on a vessel of 50 m or more the forward anchor light is at least 6 m above the hull.

## Aground

A vessel aground shows the ==anchor lights== (two, or one if under 50 m) and, where they can best be seen:

- ==two all-round red lights in a vertical line==;
- by day, ==three balls in a vertical line==.

Memory aid: ==aground looks like "anchored plus not under command"==: the anchor lights plus two reds. By day it is three balls, one more than not under command. Either way, the water there is too shallow for her, and maybe for you.

## Small vessel exemptions

- A vessel ==under 7 m== at anchor, ==not in or near a narrow channel, fairway or anchorage, or where other vessels normally navigate==, need not show the anchor lights or ball.
- A vessel ==under 12 m== aground need not show the ==two red lights or three balls== (she still shows her anchor signals).

## Canadian modifications

In ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==:

- A ==barge== at anchor may show the Canadian barge lights of Rule 24(j) (an all-round white light at each end, or at each end of a group) instead of the normal anchor lights.
- An ==inconspicuous, partly submerged vessel or object== (for example a boom) at anchor may show the Rule 24(g) lights instead.
- A barge or inconspicuous object at anchor ==need not show any light== while it is ==inside a recognized mooring, storage or booming area== that is ==not in or near a narrow channel or fairway or where other vessels normally navigate==.

## A BC bridge example

Coming into a sound at night, you pass a cluster of unlit dark shapes along the shore: a log storage area. If it is a recognized booming area away from the fairway, the booms are not required to be lit, so treat the shoreline edge as unlit obstructions and use radar and the chart. Further in, a 30 m vessel shows a single all-round white light, legal for a vessel under 50 m at anchor. Near the entrance you see a white light forward, a lower white light aft, and two all-round red lights in a vertical line: a vessel aground. Whatever put her there could put you there too.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking the stern anchor light is higher than the forward one. It is lower.
- Confusing aground (anchor lights plus two reds) with not under command (two reds, no anchor lights).
- Mixing up the day shapes: one ball at anchor, three balls aground, two balls NUC.
- Expecting every boom or barge in a Canadian booming area to be lit.
