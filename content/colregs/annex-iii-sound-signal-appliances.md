# Annex III: Technical details of sound signal appliances

Rule 33 says **which** sound gear a vessel carries (whistle from 12 m, bell from 20 m, gong from 100 m). ==Annex III==
says **what that gear must be like**. For a watchkeeper the useful parts are the ones that let you judge a signal you
hear in fog and check that your own whistle and bell are fit for purpose.

## Whistle pitch: bigger vessel, lower note

Every whistle's ==fundamental frequency== must lie between ==70 and 700 Hz==. Within that, the band depends on length,
so that a deep note means a big ship and a higher note a smaller one:

| Vessel length | Fundamental frequency |
|---|---|
| ==200 m or more== | ==70-200 Hz== |
| 75 m to under 200 m | ==130-350 Hz== |
| ==under 75 m== | ==250-700 Hz== |

The range at which a whistle carries comes from the frequencies (fundamental or higher harmonics) in the band
==180-700 Hz for vessels of 20 m or more== and ==180-2 100 Hz for vessels under 20 m==. So a small vessel's whistle
may rely on higher-pitched tones to reach its range.

**Canadian modifications.**

- In the ==Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway==, the upper limit of that band may be
  extended to 2 100 Hz.
- In the same waters, a whistle on a vessel of ==12 m to under 20 m== may meet slightly different sound level figures
  over 250-2 100 Hz (the same 120, 115 and 111 dB steps as the international under-20 m figures).
- A power-driven vessel that normally ==pushes ahead or tows alongside== in those waters may use a whistle suited to the
  ==combined length of the vessel and her tow==. A big tug-and-barge unit can therefore sound like the big unit it is.

## Loudness and audibility range

Annex III sets a minimum sound level at 1 m from the whistle for each length class, and gives a typical audibility
range for each:

| Vessel length | Level at 1 m (dB) | Typical audibility range |
|---|---|---|
| 200 m or more | 143 | ==2 miles== |
| 75 m to under 200 m | 138 | ==1.5 miles== |
| 20 m to under 75 m | 130 | ==1 mile== |
| under 20 m | 120, 115 or 111 (higher frequency, lower figure) | ==0.5 mile== |

The audibility range is roughly the distance at which the whistle is heard ahead with 90 per cent probability, in
still air, on a vessel with average background noise at her listening posts. Annex III itself warns that real range
varies a great deal and can be much less in strong wind or high background noise.

**Canadian modification:** the ==audibility range column and its notes are not part of the Canadian Regulations==.
Learn the figures as typical values, never as a promise.

## Directional whistles

Some whistles throw most of their sound forward. A directional whistle is allowed if:

- within ==45 degrees== of its axis, it is no more than ==4 dB== below the required level, and
- in every other horizontal direction, it is no more than ==10 dB== below,

so that its range in any direction is ==at least half its range ahead==.

## Where the whistle goes

- As ==high as practicable==, to clear obstructions and to protect the crew's hearing.
- A directional whistle that is the vessel's ==only whistle== points ==straight ahead==.
- The vessel's own whistle must not exceed ==110 dB(A)== at her listening posts, and so far as practicable should not
  exceed ==100 dB(A)==. The Canadian text defines ==listening posts== as the ==bridge wings or the bow==: the places a
  lookout listens for other vessels' signals.

## More than one whistle

- Whistles fitted ==more than 100 m apart== must ==not be sounded at the same time==. (Rule 34 adds that only one of them
  is used for manoeuvring and warning signals.)
- If one whistle would leave a "dead zone" behind an obstruction, a ==combined whistle system== is recommended. Its
  whistles are ==no more than 100 m apart==, sounded together, and differ in frequency by ==at least 10 Hz==. For the
  Rules it ==counts as a single whistle==.

## Bell and gong

- A bell or gong (or a device with similar sound) must give at least ==110 dB at 1 m==.
- Bells and gongs are made of ==corrosion-resistant== material and give a clear tone.
- On a vessel of ==20 m or more==, the mouth of the bell is at least ==300 mm== in diameter.
- The striker's mass is at least ==3 per cent== of the bell's mass.
- A ==power-driven striker== is recommended where practicable, for an even strike, but ==manual operation must be
  possible==.

## Small vessels

**Under 20 m.** Rule 33 does not require a bell, and the 300 mm bell size applies only from 20 m. The whistle may use
frequencies up to ==2 100 Hz== to reach its range, and its typical audibility range is only ==0.5 mile==. In fog, a
small boat's horn may not be heard until she is very close.

**Under 12 m.** Rule 33 does not require a whistle or bell, but the boat must have ==some other means of making an
efficient sound signal==. In the Canadian waters of a roadstead, harbour, river, lake or inland waterway, a vessel
under 12 m ordinarily used to push or pull floating objects (and not only yarding or warping) must carry the full
12 m-plus gear (see the Rule 33 lesson).

## Approval

Construction, performance and installation of sound signal appliances must satisfy the flag State's authority. For
Canadian vessels that is ==the Minister== (Transport Canada).

## A BC bridge example

Fog in Haro Strait, one mile visibility dropping fast. You hear a very deep, long note fine on the starboard bow, and
a little later a thin, high-pitched blast close to port. The deep note suggests a large ship, whose whistle may carry
1.5 to 2 miles in good conditions. The high note suggests a small vessel whose whistle may
carry only about half a mile, so she may be much closer than her faint sound suggests. Use radar, slow down, and do
not judge distance by loudness alone.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking big ships have high-pitched whistles. The bigger the vessel, the lower the pitch.
- Treating the audibility ranges as legal minimums. In Canada they are information only, and real ranges vary.
- Forgetting that a combined whistle system counts as one whistle, while whistles more than 100 m apart must not be
  sounded together.
- Assuming a bell must be 300 mm on every vessel. That size applies from 20 m.
