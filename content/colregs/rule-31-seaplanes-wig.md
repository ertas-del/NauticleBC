# Rule 31: Seaplanes and WIG craft

Floatplanes are part of everyday traffic in several BC harbours and coastal communities. Rule 31 is short, but you need to know where seaplanes fit.

## What the rule says

Where it is ==impracticable== for a ==seaplane or a WIG craft== to show lights and shapes of the prescribed characteristics or in the prescribed positions, she shows lights and shapes ==as closely similar in characteristics and position as is possible==.

In practice, expect a seaplane on the water at night to show lights that resemble a power-driven vessel's as closely as her design allows, even if they cannot meet the exact arcs and spacing of Annex I.

## How the Canadian Regulations bring seaplanes in

- The Regulations apply to ==every seaplane on or over Canadian waters==.
- Throughout the Regulations, "vessel" is read to ==include a seaplane when it is on or over the water== (with limited exceptions in the application sections).
- The owner, operator and person in charge of a seaplane must ensure the applicable Rules are met.

## Related rules to remember

- **Rule 18(e):** a seaplane on the water shall, in general, ==keep well clear of all vessels and avoid impeding their navigation==. Where ==risk of collision exists==, she must ==comply with the steering and sailing rules==.
- **Rule 18(f):** a WIG craft taking off, landing or in flight near the surface keeps well clear of all other vessels; on the water surface she follows Part B as a power-driven vessel.
- **Rule 23(c):** a WIG craft taking off, landing or in flight near the surface shows a ==high-intensity all-round flashing red light== in addition to her power-driven lights.

## A BC bridge example

In Vancouver Harbour at dusk a floatplane taxis out from the terminal and turns into the wind for take-off. Her lights do not look exactly like a boat's, but Rule 31 expects them to be as close as possible. In general the seaplane should keep clear of you. If a risk of collision does develop, she is treated under the steering and sailing rules, so the normal crossing, head-on and overtaking rules apply. Keep a good lookout: a seaplane accelerating for take-off closes fast.

## Common mistakes

- Thinking seaplanes are outside the Collision Regulations because they are aircraft.
- Assuming a seaplane always has "right of way". The general duty in Rule 18(e) is for her to keep clear.
- Expecting a seaplane's lights to match a boat's exactly. Rule 31 only asks for the closest possible match.
