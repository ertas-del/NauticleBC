# Rule 27: Not under command and restricted in ability to manoeuvre

These are the vessels that cannot get out of your way. Under Rule 18 almost everyone must keep clear of them, so recognising them quickly matters.

Recall the difference from Rule 3: ==not under command== is caused by an ==exceptional circumstance== (engine or steering failure). ==Restricted in ability to manoeuvre== is caused by the ==nature of her work==.

## Not under command (NUC)

- ==two all-round red lights in a vertical line==, where they can best be seen;
- by day, ==two balls== (or similar shapes) in a vertical line;
- when ==making way through the water==, ==sidelights and a sternlight== as well. ==No masthead light==: she is not able to manoeuvre as a normal power-driven vessel.

Memory aid: ==red over red, the captain is dead==. (Grim, but it sticks.)

## Restricted in ability to manoeuvre (RAM)

Except for mine clearance:

- ==three all-round lights in a vertical line: red, white, red==;
- by day, ==ball, diamond, ball==;
- when ==making way==, ==masthead light or lights, sidelights and a sternlight== as well;
- ==at anchor==, the ==anchor lights or ball== as well.

**Towing that restricts.** A power-driven vessel in a towing operation that ==severely restricts== her and her tow from deviating from their course shows the ==towing lights of Rule 24(a) plus red, white, red== (and by day ball, diamond, ball).

## Dredging and underwater operations

A vessel dredging or doing underwater work, when restricted, shows the RAM signals and, if there is an obstruction:

- ==two all-round red lights or two balls== in a vertical line on the ==side where the obstruction is==;
- ==two all-round green lights or two diamonds== in a vertical line on the ==side where another vessel may pass==;
- at anchor, these signals ==instead of== the anchor lights or ball.

Memory aid: red is "danger, keep off"; green is "go, pass this side".

## Small diving vessels

If a vessel engaged in ==diving operations== is too small to show all the dredging signals, she shows:

- ==red, white, red== all-round lights in a vertical line;
- a ==rigid replica of International Code flag "A"==, ==at least 1 m high==, visible all round.

## Mine clearance

A vessel engaged in mine clearance shows, in addition to her power-driven or anchor signals, ==three all-round green lights or three balls==: one near the foremast head and one at each end of the fore yard. They mean it is ==dangerous to approach within 1000 m==.

## Exemptions and warning

- Vessels ==under 12 m==, ==except those engaged in diving operations==, are ==not required== to show the Rule 27 lights and shapes.
- These are ==not distress signals==. Distress signals are in Annex IV.

## A BC bridge example

Off the mouth of the Fraser River you see by day a vessel with ball, diamond, ball on her mast, two black balls on her port side and two black diamonds on her starboard side. She is dredging, restricted in her ability to manoeuvre, with an obstruction (her pipeline or gear) on her port side. Pass on her starboard side, the diamond side, at a slow speed and a good distance.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up NUC (red, red) and RAM (red, white, red).
- Expecting masthead lights on a NUC vessel that is making way. She shows sidelights and sternlight only.
- Reading the dredger's two red lights as "pass this side". Red marks the obstruction.
- Thinking a small dive boat under 12 m is exempt. Diving operations are the exception.
- Treating NUC lights as a distress signal.

## Check yourself

1. What lights does a NUC vessel show when stopped? When making way?
2. What are the RAM lights and day shapes?
3. Which side should you pass a dredger showing two diamonds on one side?
4. What does a small dive boat show by day?
5. How close is it dangerous to approach a vessel showing three green lights?
