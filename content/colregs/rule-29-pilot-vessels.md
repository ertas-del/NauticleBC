# Rule 29: Pilot vessels

Pilot boats meet ships to put pilots aboard and take them off. They often manoeuvre close to large ships at speed, sometimes in the dark, so you need to recognise them.

## What the rule says

A vessel ==engaged on pilotage duty== shows:

- ==at or near the masthead==, ==two all-round lights in a vertical line, white over red==;
- when ==underway==, ==sidelights and a sternlight== as well;
- when ==at anchor==, the ==anchor lights or ball of Rule 30== as well.

A pilot vessel ==not engaged on pilotage duty== shows the ==lights or shapes of a similar vessel of her length==, for example the normal lights of a power-driven vessel.

Rule 29 gives ==no day shape== for a pilot vessel.

## Memory aid

==White over red, pilot ahead.== Compare it with fishing: red over white is fishing. The order of the two colours is the whole difference, so say it to yourself every time.

## Plain-language explanation

Notice what is missing underway: Rule 29 lists ==no masthead light== for a pilot vessel on duty. The white over red all-round lights take that place. Sidelights and sternlight tell you her aspect and that she is underway.

## A BC bridge example

At night off Victoria you see an all-round white light above an all-round red light, high up, with a green sidelight below. A pilot boat on duty is showing you her starboard side. She may be heading for an inbound ship to put a pilot aboard. Expect her to alter course and speed to come alongside the ship, and keep clear of the boarding area.

## Common mistakes

- Reversing the colours and calling it a fishing vessel.
- Expecting a pilot vessel on duty to show a masthead light.
- Thinking a pilot vessel keeps her pilot lights on when returning home off duty.
- Looking for a pilot day shape that Rule 29 does not prescribe.

## Check yourself

1. What lights does a pilot vessel on duty show at her masthead?
2. What does she add when underway? When at anchor?
3. What does she show when not on duty?
4. How do you tell her lights from those of a vessel fishing (not trawling)?
