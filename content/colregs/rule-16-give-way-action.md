# Rule 16: Action by give-way vessel

## What it is

Every vessel which is directed to keep out of the way of another vessel shall, ==so far as possible==, take
==early and substantial action== to ==keep well clear==.

"Directed to keep out of the way" means the give-way vessel under any Section II rule: the port-tack or windward
sailing vessel (Rule 12), the ==overtaking vessel== (Rule 13), the vessel with the other on her starboard side
(Rule 15), and the vessel lower in the Rule 18 order.

Because it is in Section II, Rule 16 applies to vessels ==in sight of one another==. For radar-only contacts in
restricted visibility, Rule 19 applies instead.

## The three key words

**Early.** Act while you still have time and room, well before the stand-on vessel has to wonder whether you
have seen her. Rule 17 lets the stand-on vessel act on her own once it becomes apparent you are not taking
appropriate action. If you wait, you invite her to do something you did not expect.

**Substantial.** The change of course, speed or both should be large enough to be ==readily apparent== to the other
vessel, by eye or on radar (Rule 8). A few degrees at a time does not tell the other bridge anything.

**Well clear.** The target is not "no contact". It is a passing distance that is safe for the vessels, the speed
and the conditions, with room to spare. Keep checking until the other vessel is finally past and clear.

## A BC bridge example

You are the give-way vessel in a 12 metre workboat crossing Trincomali Channel. A power-driven vessel is on your
starboard bow at 2.5 miles, bearing steady.

- Early: act now, at 2.5 miles, not at half a mile.
- Substantial: alter well to starboard, enough that her bridge sees your aspect change from red-and-masthead to a
  clear stern-on view, or slow right down so the bearing draws ahead quickly.
- Well clear: aim to pass a comfortable distance astern of her, then check the bearing and range until she is
  past and clear before you resume course.

## Common mistakes

- "Nibbling": a 5 degree turn, then another, then another.
- Leaving the action so late that the stand-on vessel has already started to manoeuvre.
- Aiming for the minimum distance that avoids contact rather than a clear, safe passing distance.
- Thinking Rule 16 does not apply to you because you are overtaking. An overtaking vessel is a give-way vessel.
- Applying Rule 16 to a radar-only target in fog. That is Rule 19.
