# Your SVMO plan

## 1. Paperwork first (do this early, it takes longest)
1. Start logging sea service now. You need at least 2 months, with 1 month in the engine room on motor vessels. Keep your discharge book and get Form 82-0666 signed.
2. Confirm with a Transport Canada marine centre which MED course counts (DVS or MED A2) and whether the online fatigue-management course applies.
3. Book Marine Basic First Aid (about 2 days).
4. Ask TC whether a seafarer medical is needed and what the oral format is.
5. Check whether your vessel path qualifies for SVMO-R (under 750 kW, one named vessel, 10 days on board) or whether you need the full SVMO.

## 2. Study (4 to 6 weeks, about 45 minutes a day)
Work through the 19 topics in this order, easiest and most testable first:
- Week 1: 3.18 engines, 3.12 fuels, 3.13 lubricants, 3.14 cooling
- Week 2: 3.15 electricity, 3.16 hydraulics, 3.17 pneumatics
- Week 3: 3.6 pumps, 3.7 piping, 3.8 power transmission, 3.9 steering, 3.10 underwater fittings, 3.11 deck machinery
- Week 4: 3.2 to 3.5 fire, safety, safe working, pollution
- Week 5: 3.1 materials, 3.19 watchkeeping, then mixed practice
- Week 6: two 60-question timed mocks, review every miss

For each topic: read TP 14814E's discussion questions for that section, answer each in your own words (write it down), then do that topic's practice questions.

## 3. Exam technique
- 60 questions, four options. Pick the MOST appropriate answer. Safety usually beats convenience.
- If two answers look right, ask which one a cautious chief engineer would choose first.
- Do the 82 draft questions untimed, then timed. Anything under 80% on a topic goes back on the list.

## 4. Oral prep (unrestricted ticket)
Practise answering aloud: "What would you do if..." for low oil pressure, high jacket water temperature, low coolant, high bilge alarm, engine-room fire, unexpected engine stop, and a suspected grounding. Those come straight from the study guide.

## 5. Get real
Reading isn't enough for an engine-room ticket. Spend time on actual engines: check oil, coolant and belts, bleed a fuel system, trace a bilge pump line, and read an electrical schematic.
