# SVOP syllabus brief


## Ticket
Small Vessel Operator Proficiency (SVOP). A training certificate, not an exam-only certificate. There is no challenge exam: the candidate must complete a Transport Canada approved course.

## What it allows (Transport Canada page, read directly)
On a near coastal Class 2 or sheltered waters voyage:
- Operator of a commercial vessel of up to 5 gross tonnage, except tugs.
- Master of a fishing vessel of up to 15 gross tonnage or not more than 12 m overall length.

Normal conditions, including darkness and restricted visibility.
The 15 GT figure applies to fishing vessels only. It is not the general SVOP limit.

## Who and what else is needed
- Age: must be at least 18 to use the certificate as operator. Some providers let students take the course at 16 (e.g. NIC) or require a CDN number first (e.g. Excel Career College).
- No marine medical certificate is needed for the SVOP.
- Often also needed for the vessel's safe manning (TC page): MED/Domestic Vessel Safety (SDV-BS, formerly MED A3, per providers), ROC-M radio certificate, and one person with Marine Basic First Aid (or a recognized two-day provincial first aid course).
- Naming of the safety course varies by source (MED A3, SDV-BS, MED-DVS). Confirm with TC.

## Course and exam (TP 14692, 2007, read directly)
- Minimum 26 hours including 2 hours of evaluation; usually 4 days.
- Written exam: 50 multiple-choice questions, pass mark 70%. Oral administration may be allowed case by case.
- Class limits: 12 per instructor for practical work, 24 for lectures.
- Instructor must hold at least Fishing Master 4th Class or Master 150 GT Domestic.
- Practical items in class: knots, chart plotting, buoy-channel exercises with models. The app can drill the knowledge but not replace the course.

## Syllabus detail by topic (paraphrased from TP 14692)
1. **Legal:** scope and limits of the SVOP; definitions of vessel, passenger, pleasure craft; when a vessel is not a pleasure craft; who needs the SVOP; TC Small Vessel Inspections and Monitoring Program; master's responsibility for life, environment and property.
2. **Construction terms:** direction, locations and structural parts of a small vessel.
3. **Hull types:** displacement and planing hulls; open, enclosed, inflatable rescue craft, catamaran and others.
4. **Propulsion:** outboards, stern drives, inboards, jet drives; basic start-up and shut-down; monitoring and action on faults or alarms.
5. **Mooring and seamanship:** ropes (synthetic and natural), lines, knots and splices; mooring lines and securing to floating and fixed docks; fenders; basic knots, bends and hitches.
6. **Manoeuvring:** berthing, departing, anchoring; turning circle; effect of propellers, rudders, jets and outboards ahead and astern; wind and current; a good anchorage; setting, riding at, weighing and stowing the anchor; types of small-vessel anchors.
7. **Navigation and collision prevention:** basic COLREGs and Canadian modifications; terms (making way, underway, risk of collision, stand-on, give-way, safe speed); lookout; recognising lights, signs and shapes; distress signals; radar reflector; actions in and out of sight in good and reduced visibility; navigation lights, all-round and anchor lights; sound signals; shapes.
8. **Stability:** free surface, loose water or fish on deck, loading and unloading, load distribution and trim, icing, freeboard and Fresh Water Allowance, watertight integrity, stowage, reserve buoyancy. Qualitative level, not calculations.
9. **Safe working:** shipshape vessel, workplace hazards, working aloft, confined spaces, signals for winch and crane, safe refuelling, care of lifejackets, PFDs and anti-exposure suits, fire equipment care, flares (types, use, storage, disposal), towing safety.
10. **Weather:** origin and reliability of forecasts; wind categories; small craft, gale and storm warnings; wind shifts and barometer; bad-weather procedures; sea states, land effects and deflection; thunderstorms and squalls; icing; loading and weather.
11. **Radar:** principle, start-up, main controls, interpreting the image; positioning by radar; bearings and distances; collision avoidance use.
12. **GPS:** starting and functioning; using the data; recognising errors and the need to double check.
13. **Charts and publications:** plotting a position; carrying charts; chart reading, courses and positions; List of Lights, Canadian Tide and Current Tables, Notices to Mariners.
14. **Compass:** using a magnetic compass with the chart; deviation on small vessels; checking and making a simple deviation card.
15. **Buoyage:** Canadian system; lateral buoys and day beacons; fairway, bifurcation, cautionary, isolated and special purpose buoys; cardinal buoys; scuba diving buoys and the diver's flag.
16. **Emergencies:** collision, grounding, flooding, fire, man overboard, pollution release; protection of crew and passengers; the master's reporting obligations.
17. **SAR:** SAR resources and coordination in the operating area; what happens after a distress call; VHF Distress, Urgency and Routine prefixes; duties on hearing a distress call.
18. **Pollution:** precautions when fuelling; reporting duties; the sewage and garbage divisions of the pollution regulations.
19. **Law:** basic Canada Shipping Act, 2001; small vessel or fishing vessel safety regulations as applicable; parks and marine mammal protection if applicable; pollution regulations.
20. **Departure:** bilge blower, fuel planning, departure checklist, filing a sail or trip plan, local hazards.
21. **Checklists:** daily and weekly maintenance, safety gear, engine start-up, departure, pre-voyage, post-voyage, fuelling, heavy weather.
