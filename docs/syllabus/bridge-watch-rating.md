## 1. Certificate requirements

### 1.1 What the law says (Marine Personnel Regulations, SOR/2007-115)

Read in full from the Justice Canada consolidated XML (current to 2025-07-24). Source: `bw-mpr-sor-2007-115`.

| Requirement | What the MPR says | Section | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certificate | Bridge Watch Rating is a certificate of competency (a "rating" certificate, next to Able Seafarer and Engine-room Rating). | 100(uu); heading "Ratings" before 170 | Confirmed |
| Minimum age | At least **18 on the day the certificate is received**. Sea service must have started **at 16 or older**. | 110(1) | Confirmed |
| Sea service | Sea service **performing bridge watchkeeping duties**: **at least 6 months**, or **at least 2 months** if the applicant completed an **approved training program in bridge watchkeeping duties**. | 171, table item 1 | Confirmed |
| Documents for the examiner | (a) **MED, STCW Basic Safety**; (b) **MED, Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats other than fast rescue boats**; (c) **steering testimonial** (Schedule 3 to Part 1); (d) **Marine Basic First Aid**. | 171, table item 2 | Confirmed |
| Examination | **Written examination on general seamanship**, taken after meeting the other requirements. No oral and no practical exam in s. 171 (compare Able Seafarer, s. 170, which has written **and** practical). | 171, table item 3 | Confirmed |
| Steering testimonial | Signed by the master: vessel details, sign-on and sign-off dates, rank, and a declaration that the seafarer "stood regular watches for a total of __ hours at the wheel" and is "a competent wheelsman". **No minimum number of hours is in the regulation.** | Schedule 3 to Part 1 | Confirmed |
| Medical | A medical certificate (or provisional one) is needed before the certificate is issued; Bridge Watch Rating is not in the exempt list. | 111(b) | Confirmed |
| Colour vision | The colour vision standard applies: Bridge Watch Rating is **not** in the list of certificates exempted from it (Engine-room Rating, Ship's Cook and others are). | 270(3) | Confirmed (by reading the exemption list) |
| MED currency | A certificate that needs an MED course is issued only if the course was completed **within 5 years** before the application (or refresher / recent sea service equivalency). | 103(1)-(2) | Confirmed |
| Application | Application and documents to the examiner **at least two weeks** before the first exam; an application stays valid **1 year**. Examination fees apply each time. | 110(2), (3), (5) | Confirmed |
| Exam procedures | Exams follow TP 2293; not following them is an automatic fail. | 112 | Confirmed |
| Exam result validity | A pass in a Bridge Watch Rating exam stays valid **5 years**, and for life once the certificate is obtained. | 113(1)(a), (3) | Confirmed |
| Training validity | Course completions are valid for life once the certificate is obtained, except MED, first aid, tanker and passenger courses. | 103(3) | Confirmed |
| What it lets you do | On a vessel of **500 GT or more**, the "additional person" in the deck watch must hold **at least an Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating** certificate; on vessels **over 1,000 GT** not anchored or moored, a **second additional person** with the same minimum. (Exceptions in 216(3)-(6).) Similar rules for MOUs (s. 238) and foreign vessels (s. 247). | 216(2)(b)-(c) | Confirmed |
| Certificate expiry | No renewal rule for ratings was found (s. 106 renewal applies to officer certificates). Medical certificates expire separately. | 104-106 | Inferred, verify |

### 1.2 What the course standard says (TP 10936, Edition 2, in force 2024-09-01)

Source: `bw-tp10936-ed2-html` (HTML version; seen through a summarising tool with verbatim quotes requested).

- The program "incorporates the training requirements of the Rating Forming Part of a Navigational Watch and also
  includes components of the Able Seafarer Deck" (STCW A-II/4 plus parts of A-II/5). **Confirmed.**
- **Duration 300 hours.** **Confirmed.** Breakdown (teaching hours, **Confirmed**):

| # | Competence (paraphrased title) | Hours |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contribute to a safe navigational watch, steer, understand helm orders in English | 30 |
| 2 | Keep a proper look-out by sight and hearing | **40** |
| 3 | Contribute to monitoring and controlling a safe watch | 20 |
| 4 | Operate emergency equipment and apply emergency procedures | 5 |
| 5 | Operate survival craft and rescue boats | 5 |
| 6 | Contribute to berthing, anchoring and other mooring operations | 25 |
| 7 | Contribute to the handling of cargo and stores | 35 |
| 8 | Operate deck equipment and machinery | 65 |
| 9 | Occupational health and safety, and pollution prevention | 40 |
| 10 | Shipboard maintenance and repair | 35 |
| | **Total** | **300** |

- Entry: **16 to enrol**, **18 on the day the certificate of competency is issued**. MED may be done before or during the
  course. **Confirmed.**
- Class size: at most 12 students per instructor for practical demonstrations and 24 for lectures. Main instructor holds at
  least Master 150 GT Domestic or Watchkeeping Mate (or Near Coastal). **Confirmed.**
- **Assessment: a written examination approved by Transport Canada Marine Safety, 50 multiple-choice questions, pass mark
  60 %; and a practical examination by the training provider, approved by TC, pass mark 70 %.** **Confirmed** (tool quote).
- Assessment evidence: practical tests, approved in-service experience or approved training-ship experience; criteria
  include a steady course within limits, smooth controlled course alterations, closed-loop communications, prompt
  detection and reporting of sound signals, lights and objects in degrees or points. **Confirmed.**

### 1.4 Other prerequisites seen

- **Fatigue Management at Sea e-course** (free, CCOHS) is mandatory for anyone taking MED courses; the certificate must be
  produced before the MED certificate is issued (SSB 01/2026). **Confirmed** `bw-tc-ssb-01-2026-fatigue`.
- A **Candidate Document Number (CDN)** from TC is needed to enrol (BCIT, Camosun). **Confirmed** (provider pages).
- ROC-MC is bundled by BC providers but is **not** a s. 171 requirement. **Confirmed** by comparison.

### 1.5 Conflicts and open questions (flag these to the reviewer)

1. **Pass mark.** TP 10936 Ed. 2: written 60 %, practical 70 %. Holland College and BCIT: 70 % final/aggregate course mark.
   Our plan doc said "one written exam, 70 % pass mark". TP 2293 (2007) says most TC written exams pass at 70 %, some at 60 %.
   Resolution likely: 60 % is the TP 10936 course written exam; 70 % is the overall course mark providers use; the TC
   exam pass mark is unverified. **Do not state a single pass mark in the app until a reviewer confirms.**
2. **Is there a separate TC exam after the course?** MPR s. 171 requires a "written examination on general seamanship".
   TP 10936 Ed. 2 describes a TC-approved written exam inside the course. The Marine Institute (2018) and Maritime Study
   Guides describe a TC exam after the course and sea time. Unclear whether course graduates also write at a TC office, or
   whether the course exam is that exam. **Unverified.**
3. **Steering hours.** Schedule 3 leaves the hours blank. Holland College and Arctic Marine say 10 hours. Treat "10 hours"
   as provider practice, not law.
4. **60 days vs 6 months.** MPR: 2 months after an approved program or 6 months without. Holland College says 60 days gives
   a certificate for Canadian waters and 6 months gives an unrestricted international one. That split is not in the MPR
   text read. **Unverified.**
5. **Medical validity.** BCIT says 2 years. The MPR text read does not state the period (likely in the medical standards
   document). **Verify before stating.**
6. **Topic scope.** TP 10936 Ed. 2 does not list chartwork or stability; BCIT (MSSM 1030) and Arctic Marine teach basic
   chartwork, and BCIT teaches stability and construction. These lessons are marked "provider topic" below.

#### bridge-watch.certificate-pathway (order 1)
What the certificate is, who needs it, and the steps to get it. Keep numbers that conflict out of `key_facts` until
reviewed (see 1.5).
- Bridge Watch Rating is a TC certificate of competency for ratings who take part in a navigational watch. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 100(uu)]
- Minimum age **18** on the day the certificate is received; sea service counts only from age **16**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 110(1)]
- Sea service on bridge watchkeeping duties: **6 months**, or **2 months** after an approved course (TP 10936). [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 171]
- Documents: **MED STCW Basic Safety**, **MED Proficiency in Survival Craft and Rescue Boats (other than fast rescue boats)**, **steering testimonial**, **Marine Basic First Aid**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 171]
- The exam named in the regulation is a **written exam on general seamanship**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 171]
- The approved course is **TP 10936**, **300 hours**, of which **40 hours** are look-out. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- The steering testimonial is signed by the **master** and states hours at the wheel and that you are a **competent wheelsman**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` Sch. 3]
- A seafarer's medical certificate is required, including colour vision. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 111, 270]
- A pass in the exam stays valid **5 years**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 113]
- On vessels of **500 GT or more**, the second person on the deck watch must hold at least Bridge Watch Rating or Able Seafarer. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 216]

#### bridge-watch.deck-watch-and-roles (order 2)
Who is on watch, who is in charge, and what the rating does.
- The **officer of the watch (OOW)** / person in charge of the deck watch holds a certificate authorising watchkeeping. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 216(2)(a)]
- On vessels of **5 GT or more** an **additional person** is on the deck watch; on **500 GT or more** that person holds at least Able Seafarer or Bridge Watch Rating. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 216(2)(b)]
- Over **1,000 GT** and not anchored or moored, a **second additional person** is needed (with exceptions). [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 216(2)(c), (5)]
- Deck watches are kept in line with **STCW Code A-VIII/2**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 213]
- The OOW may be the **sole look-out only in daylight**, after careful assessment, with help immediately available. [`bw-tc-ssb-07-2017-deck-watch`]
- The rating's duties on watch: look-out, helmsman, reports to the OOW, rounds, security; the rating does not take over the OOW's decisions. [needs source; TP C1-C3 imply]
- The master is not counted in the deck watch except in listed cases (e.g. vessels of not more than 1,000 GT). [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` 215(2)]

#### bridge-watch.lookout-duties (order 3)
What a look-out does, how, and what gets in the way. Link `colregs.rule-05.lookout`; do not repeat the Rule.
- Look-out is kept **by sight and hearing** and by all available means, **at all times**. [`colregs-canada` Rule 5; `bw-stcw-a-viii-2-review-stw40`]
- The look-out's purposes: continuous vigilance, full appraisal of the situation and the risk of collision, stranding and other dangers, and detecting ships or aircraft in distress, shipwrecked persons, wrecks and debris. [`bw-stcw-a-viii-2-review-stw40`]
- The look-out reports **every** light, shape, sound signal and object, with its **approximate bearing in degrees or points**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`; `bw-stcw-table-a-ii-4`]
- Look-out duties must not be combined with tasks that interfere with keeping a look-out (the helmsman is not normally the look-out). [`bw-stcw-a-viii-2-review-stw40` partial; read STCW A-VIII/2 para on helmsman] [needs source for exact wording]
- Night vision needs time to adapt; keep bridge lights dim and avoid looking at bright screens. [needs source]
- Scan the horizon in sectors rather than sweeping quickly; check astern too. [needs source]
- Additional look-outs are posted in darkness and restricted visibility when circumstances call for it. [`bw-mca-mgn-137` (UK guidance)]
- Radar and AIS support the look-out; they do not replace eyes and ears. [`colregs-canada` via existing lesson]

#### bridge-watch.relative-bearings-and-points (order 4)
How to say where something is.
- A **relative bearing** is measured **clockwise from the ship's head**: dead ahead **000°**, starboard beam **090°**, dead astern **180°**, port beam **270°**. [`bw-navedtra-14067-seaman`]
- The compass has **32 points**; **1 point = 11¼°**. [`bw-wiki-points-of-the-compass`; `bw-navedtra-14067-seaman`]
- Points are counted from ahead, the beam and astern: e.g. "**two points on the starboard bow**" = 22.5° relative. [`bw-wiki-points-of-the-compass`]
- **Broad on the bow** = 4 points (45°) from ahead; **abeam** = 8 points (90°); **broad on the quarter** = 4 points abaft the beam (135° / 225°). [`bw-wiki-points-of-the-compass`]
- "Fine on the bow" means a little off the bow (commonly about one point). [`bw-wiki-points-of-the-compass`]
- A **true bearing** is from true north, a **compass bearing** from the compass; the look-out normally reports **relative** bearings unless asked. [needs source]
- Always say **port or starboard** side and give the side before the number of points. [needs source]
Drill idea: picture of a ship with an object; candidate taps the correct name.

#### bridge-watch.lookout-reports (order 5)
What to report, how to word it, and why a steady bearing matters.
- A report says **what** (e.g. "white light", "small boat"), **where** (relative bearing or points), and if possible **range** and **movement** (drawing left/right, closing). [needs source; TP criteria require prompt detection and bearing]
- Reports are **prompt**: report first, then keep watching. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` criteria]
- Use **closed-loop** communication: the OOW acknowledges; if not acknowledged, report again. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` criteria]
- A vessel whose **compass bearing does not appreciably change** while the range closes is a **risk of collision**. [`colregs-canada` Rule 7; existing lesson]
- Watch the bearing against a fixed part of the ship or a compass repeater; even an apparent change may not mean safety with large vessels or at close range. [`colregs-canada` Rule 7(d)]
- Report aids to navigation, floating objects, persons in the water and changes in visibility, not only ships. [`bw-stcw-a-viii-2-review-stw40`]
- Report the lights seen and what they tell you about the other vessel (e.g. "red and two white masthead lights, fine on the starboard bow"). [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`; link `colregs.lights.night-recognition`]

#### bridge-watch.distress-recognition (order 6)
Seeing, hearing and hearing-by-radio distress, and reporting at once. Link Annex IV lessons; add what is new.
- Distress signals are recognised **by sight, sound, radiotelephone and from aircraft**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- Report any distress signal **immediately** to the master or OOW. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- Radiotelephone: **MAYDAY** (distress), **PAN PAN** (urgency), **SÉCURITÉ** (safety). [`bw-ccg-ramn-2026-general`]
- Visual and sound distress signals are listed in Annex IV of the Collision Regulations (reuse `colregs.annex-iv.distress-signals`). [`colregs-canada`]
- Aircraft signals directing a vessel to a casualty (circling, crossing ahead low, rocking wings, heading in the direction) and "assistance no longer required" (crossing the wake). [needs source: IAMSAR Vol. III or a CCG publication]
- Note where and when the signal was seen (bearing and time) so the OOW can log it. [needs source]

#### bridge-watch.watch-handover (order 7)
Taking over and handing over, including at the wheel.
- The watch is not handed over to a relief who is **not capable** of carrying out the duties (e.g. unfit, fatigued). [`bw-stcw-a-viii-2-review-stw40`]
- Handover happens **at sea, at anchor and in port**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- At the wheel, the relieved helmsman tells the relief the **course to steer**, the **compass in use** and the **helm being carried**; the relief repeats the course and the OOW is told of the change. [needs source]
- The relief allows time for **night vision** before taking over a look-out at night. [needs source]
- Information passed: traffic, aids, weather and visibility, special orders, equipment status. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` "information required to maintain a safe watch"] [detail needs source]
- Do not hand over during a manoeuvre or alteration; wait until it is finished. [needs source]

#### bridge-watch.anchor-and-port-watch (order 8)
- Look-out is kept at anchor and in port as well as at sea. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- At anchor: watch the **anchor bearings / position** and signs of **dragging** (bearings changing, cable jerking or leading differently); report at once. [needs source]
- The anchored vessel shows **anchor lights or a ball** (reuse `colregs.rule-30.anchored-aground`). [`colregs-canada`]
- Alongside: tend moorings with the tide and loading, check the gangway and **rat guards**, control access. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` lists rat guards; detail needs source]
- **Parted moorings**: raise the alarm, inform the OOW, keep clear of the bight, stand by to put out new lines. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names topic; detail needs source]
- **Fuel or water transfer**: continuous two-way communication able to stop the transfer, adequate lighting at night, scuppers plugged, watch for overflow, stop if a hose leaks. [`bw-vpdcr-sor-2012-69` ss. 33-35; scuppers needs source]
- Burst oil line or tank overflow: **stop the transfer**, raise the alarm, contain the spill with the spill kit. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail needs source]

#### bridge-watch.bridge-communications (order 9)
- Communications are **closed-loop**: order, repeat-back, confirmation. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- If an order is unclear, **ask** the OOW for clarification. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- Use correct **shipboard terms**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`; `bw-stcw-table-a-ii-4`]
- The master records a **working language** for navigational safety; crew must understand orders in it. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 137]
- Internal systems: telephones, talk-back, public address, alarm panels; know where each is and how to use it. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`] [examples need source]
- A two-way voice link exists between the bridge and the **emergency steering position**. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 108]

#### bridge-watch.fatigue-and-hours-of-rest (order 10)
- Sheltered and near coastal (domestic) voyages: at least **6 consecutive hours** of rest in every 24 hours and **16 hours in every 48**; no more than **18 hours** between rest periods. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` s. 320]
- Unlimited voyages or foreign waters: either no more than **14 hours' work in 24** and **72 in 7 days**, or at least **10 hours' rest in 24** and **77 in 7 days**. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` s. 321(1)]
- Rest may be split into **no more than two periods**, one at least **6 hours**; no more than **14 hours** between rest periods. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` s. 321(2)]
- The master must consider **fatigue** when scheduling; records of hours are kept. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` ss. 322-323]
- Drills and emergencies may interrupt rest; compensatory rest follows. [`bw-mpr-sor-2007-115` s. 322(3), (5)]
- The **Fatigue Management at Sea** e-course is required before an MED certificate is issued. [`bw-tc-ssb-01-2026-fatigue`]
- Fatigue signs and countermeasures. [needs source; CCOHS course]

#### bridge-watch.helm-orders (order 11)
The single most testable steering topic. **Read IMO SMCP A2/1 directly before writing** (`bw-imo-smcp-a918`).
- Helm orders are given and understood **in English**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`; `bw-stcw-table-a-ii-4`]
- The helmsman **repeats every order** word for word, carries it out, and **reports** when it is done; the OOW checks. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`; `bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- **Midships**: rudder fore-and-aft (zero). [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Port / Starboard five, ten, fifteen, twenty, twenty-five**: hold that rudder angle. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Hard-a-port / Hard-a-starboard**: full rudder to that side. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Ease to (ten)**: reduce rudder to the stated angle. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Steady / Steady as she goes**: stop the swing and steer the heading at the time of the order; report that heading. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Nothing to port / starboard**: do not let the head go to that side of the course. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- **Report if she does not answer the wheel** and **Finished with wheel**. [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`]
- Courses are given **digit by digit** (e.g. "steer one-two-five") and the helmsman reports when steady on the course. [`bw-mma-rfpnw-helm-assessment` (U.S.); verify SMCP wording]
Note: U.S. sources say "right/left rudder"; Canadian and IMO practice uses **port/starboard**. Do not mix.

#### bridge-watch.steering-technique (order 12)
- A steady course is steered **within acceptable limits** for the area and sea state; alterations are **smooth and controlled**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` criteria]
- Use small rudder angles and anticipate the swing; apply **counter rudder** to check the swing ("meet her"). [`bw-smcp-wheel-orders-copy`; technique needs source]
- Rudder effect is weak at low speed and stronger at higher speed through the water / with propeller wash over the rudder. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names topic; detail needs source]
- Watch the **rate-of-turn indicator** and **rudder angle indicator**, not only the compass. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- **Steering by ranges**: keep the two range marks in line; if they open, steer back toward the line. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`; `bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`; which way to steer needs source]
- A loaded ship answers the helm more slowly and carries her way longer than one in ballast; in ballast wind has more effect. [needs source]
- Relieving at the wheel follows a set procedure (see watch handover). [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]

#### bridge-watch.steering-in-confined-waters (order 13)
- **Squat**: a vessel moving in shallow water sinks lower and changes trim; it increases with speed. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names topic] [needs source]
- **Bank effect**: near a bank the stern is drawn toward it and the bow pushed away. [needs source]
- **Interaction** between vessels passing or overtaking close can cause sudden sheering. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names topic] [needs source]
- In rivers, **current** and **tide** set the vessel; steer to the marks, not only the compass. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`] [detail needs source]
- Link `colregs.rule-09.narrow-channels` for the rules side.

#### bridge-watch.steering-systems-and-checks (order 14)
- **Follow-up** steering: the rudder goes to the angle set on the wheel/control and stays there; **non-follow-up**: the rudder moves while the control is held and stops when released. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; definitions need source]
- Steering gear must be **checked and tested within 12 hours before departure** (main and auxiliary gear, remote controls, bridge controls, emergency power, **rudder angle indicator vs actual rudder**, alarms, full rudder movement, visual inspection, bridge-to-steering-gear communication). [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 136(1)]
- Vessels on regular voyages under one week may test **weekly**. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 136(2)]
- **Emergency steering drills at least every three months**, including direct control from the steering gear compartment. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 136(3)]
- Changeover instructions and a **block diagram** are displayed on the bridge and in the steering gear compartment. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 135]
- Where two power units can run together, **both run** in areas needing special caution. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 134]
- If steering fails: **tell the OOW at once**; follow the emergency steering procedure. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail needs source]
- Propulsion and steering types: single/twin screw, controllable pitch, azimuth/podded drives, rudder types. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail needs source]

#### bridge-watch.autopilot-changeover (order 15)
- In **high traffic density**, **restricted visibility** or other hazardous situations, it must be possible to change **immediately** to manual steering. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 133(1)]
- In those situations a **qualified helmsman** must be available at all times. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 133(2)]
- Every changeover is made **by or under the supervision of the person in charge of the deck watch**. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 133(3)]
- **Manual steering is tested at least once a day** and before areas needing special caution. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 133(4)]
- Check that the autopilot is **holding the course**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- Changing over at close quarters is dangerous: confirm the mode, the heading and the rudder before and after. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; procedure needs source]

#### bridge-watch.magnetic-and-gyro-compass (order 16)
- Every vessel (with exceptions) has a **standard magnetic compass that needs no power**. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 106]
- A **gyro-compass** is also required on near coastal Class 1, unlimited and international voyages (per the section's rules). [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 106]
- Magnetic compasses are **compensated** and a **deviation card/curve** is kept near the compass. [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 106]
- The gyro feeds **repeaters** (steering, bearing, emergency steering position). [`bw-nsr-2020-sor-2020-216` s. 111 (SOLAS-type vessels)]
- Parts of a magnetic compass (bowl, card, lubber line, gimbals, binnacle, correctors). [needs source]
- The gyro seeks true north, needs power and a settling time; report any gyro failure alarm. [needs source]
- In **high latitudes** (Arctic) magnetic compasses become sluggish and unreliable and gyro errors grow. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail needs source]

#### bridge-watch.compass-error (order 17) (inferred topic; also 60GT chartwork)
- **Variation**: angle between true and magnetic north; changes with place. [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
- **Deviation**: angle between magnetic and compass north, caused by the ship; changes with **heading**. [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
- **Compass error** = variation + deviation (algebraic sum). [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
- Order: **True, Variation, Magnetic, Deviation, Compass**. [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
- Easterly error makes the compass read **lower** than true; westerly **higher**. [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
- **Gyro error** is named east or west and applied the same way. [`bw-bowditch-compass-conversions`]
Keep the arithmetic light for ratings; deep conversion belongs to the 60 GT chartwork lessons.

#### bridge-watch.pyrotechnics-epirb-sart (order 20)

#### seamanship.buoyage-lateral (order 1)

- **Upstream direction**: from seaward toward the headwaters of a river or into a harbour. Proceeding upstream, keep **starboard hand aids to starboard** and **port hand aids to port**. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]

#### seamanship.buoyage-cardinal-and-other-marks (order 2)
- Cardinal buoys show where the **safe water** is: pass **north of a north** cardinal, and so on. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- Topmarks: **N** two cones **up**; **S** two cones **down**; **E** cones **base to base**; **W** cones **point to point**. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- Colours: **N** black over yellow; **S** yellow over black; **E** black with a yellow band; **W** yellow with a black band. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- White lights: **N** continuous Q or VQ; **E** Q(3)/VQ(3); **S** Q(6)+LFl / VQ(6)+LFl; **W** Q(9)/VQ(9). [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **Isolated danger**: black with red band, **two black spheres**, white **Fl(2)**. Danger with navigable water all round. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **Safe water / fairway**: **red and white vertical stripes**, **red sphere**, white **Mo(A)** or **LFl 10s**. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
Memory aid for flashes (3 o'clock, 6, 9 like a clock face): inferred teaching device; fine to use, flag it as a memory aid.

#### seamanship.special-buoys-daybeacons-ranges (order 3)
- **Special buoys** are **yellow** with yellow **Fl 4s** lights (ODAS **Fl(5) 20s**); types include cautionary, anchorage, mooring, swimming, keep out, control. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **Daybeacons**: fixed unlit aids; port hand square (green/black), starboard hand triangle (red). [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **Ranges**: two marks in line show a safe track; may be lit. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **RACONs** and radar reflectors. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]

#### seamanship.light-characteristics (order 4)
- **F** fixed; **Iso** equal light and dark; **Oc** light longer than dark; **Fl** light shorter than dark; **Fl(2)** group flashing. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- **Q** 50 to under 80 flashes a minute; **VQ** 80 to under 160. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`] (tool reported 159; check the PDF)
- **LFl**: a flash of at least **2 seconds**. **Mo(A)**: Morse "A" (short-long). [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- The **period** is the time for one full cycle (e.g. "Fl 4s"). [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023`]
- Aids can be off station, damaged or unlit; do not rely on one aid alone, use charts and other aids. [`bw-ccg-aids-to-navigation-2023` ("in conjunction with ... nautical charts"); detail needs source]

#### seamanship.ship-terms-and-structure (order 5)
- All the terms in the TP 10936 C8 list (winch, windlass, capstan, derrick, crane, block, freeing port, scupper, hatch, rudder, propeller, draft marks, load line, bitts, bulwark, gangway, accommodation ladder, tween deck, fairlead, watertight vs weathertight door, porthole, landing boom, valves, pumps). [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`] [definitions need source]
- Directions: forward, aft, port, starboard, athwartships, amidships, abaft, inboard, outboard. [needs source]
- **Watertight** keeps water out under pressure from either side; **weathertight** keeps out weather in normal sea conditions. [needs source]

#### seamanship.vessel-types-and-organisation (order 6)

#### seamanship.rope-types-and-strength (order 7)
- Main rope families: **natural fibre** (e.g. manila), **synthetic** (nylon, polyester, polypropylene), **high-modulus** (e.g. HMPE), **wire**. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; properties need source]
- **SWL** is well below **breaking strength**; never load equipment beyond its SWL, which is marked on it. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 240; `bw-tackle-crc-1494`]
- Stage ropes and supports: **safety factor of at least six**. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 14]
- Nylon stretches most and stores energy (snap-back danger); polypropylene floats. [needs source]
- Care: keep dry, out of sun and chemicals, inspect for chafe. [needs source]

- Whipping stops a rope end unlaying. [needs source]

#### seamanship.anchoring-operations (order 12)

- Pilot ladder rigging details. [needs source: SOLAS V/23 / IMO A.1045(27) or Canadian equivalent]

#### seamanship.flags-and-single-letter-signals (order 15)
- **A** diver down, keep well clear at slow speed. [`bw-nga-pub-102-ics`]
- **B** taking in, discharging or carrying dangerous goods. [`bw-nga-pub-102-ics`]
- **G** I require a pilot (fishing vessels close together: hauling nets). [`bw-nga-pub-102-ics`]
- **H** I have a pilot on board. [`bw-nga-pub-102-ics`]

#### seamanship.draft-marks-and-soundings (order 16)
- Draft marks are on both sides **forward, aft** and often **amidships**. [`bw-wiki-draft-hull`]

- Imperial figures are **6 inches** high, 12 inches apart. [`bw-wiki-draft-hull`]
- Load line marks show the deepest permitted draft. [needs source: Load Line Regulations]
- Sounding holds, tanks and bilges with a tape or rod through sounding pipes; record results. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail needs source]
- Bilge and ballast suctions and wells, pipeline systems. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]

#### seamanship.cargo-and-stores (order 17)
- Safe handling, stowage and securing of cargo and stores, including dangerous goods. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`]
- Nine UN hazard classes: **1** explosives, **2** gases, **3** flammable liquids, **4** flammable solids, **5** oxidizers and organic peroxides, **6** toxic and infectious, **7** radioactive, **8** corrosives, **9** miscellaneous. [`bw-tdg-sor-2001-286`]

- Defective tools are taken out of service. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` (Tools and Machinery part)]
- Surface preparation steps and paint systems. [needs source]

#### canadian-regulations.safe-working-practices (order 1)
- Shipboard OHS law: **Part II of the Canada Labour Code** and the **Maritime Occupational Health and Safety Regulations**; also the **Safe Working Practices Regulations**. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120`; `bw-swpr-crc-1467`]
- **Fall protection** for unguarded work more than **2.4 m** above a permanent safe level, above an open hold or moving machinery; structures over **3 m**. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 144]
- **Drowning hazard**: life jacket (or net / fall protection) and rescue equipment ready. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 147]
- **PPE**: headwear, footwear, eye and face, respiratory, skin protection. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` Part 10]
- Lifting and back safety, electrical and mechanical safety, working over the side. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html`] [detail needs source]
- WHMIS / safety data sheets for hazardous products. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` (Hazardous Products part)]

#### canadian-regulations.enclosed-spaces-and-permits (order 2)
- A **confined space**: not designed for occupancy, restricted entry and exit, may become hazardous. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 2]
- **No entry without a work permit.** [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` ss. 166, 169]
- A marine chemist or qualified person assesses the space; oxygen must be **19.5 % to 23 %** by volume. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` s. 171]
- Work permits also for live electrical work, **hot work**, and other work that could kill or seriously injure. [`bw-mohs-sor-2010-120` ss. 165-166]
- Never enter to rescue without breathing apparatus; raise the alarm. [needs source]

#### canadian-regulations.pollution-prevention (order 3)
- Oily mixture discharge only through equipment giving **15 ppm** or less, under conditions. [`bw-vpdcr-sor-2012-69` s. 30]
- **Plastics may never be thrown overboard**; other garbage limits outside Canadian waters: **25 nm** floating dunnage, **12 nm** other, **3 nm** if ground to 25 mm. [`bw-vpdcr-sor-2012-69` s. 101]
- **Garbage placards** on vessels **12 m** and over; **garbage management plan** at **400 GT** or **15 persons**. [`bw-vpdcr-sor-2012-69` ss. 103-104]
- Oil transfer: continuous communication, lighting, hose tests, stop if leaking. [`bw-vpdcr-sor-2012-69` ss. 33-35]
- Anti-pollution equipment (spill kit, scupper plugs, drip trays); report spills. [`bw-tp10936-ed2-html` names; detail and reporting number need source]
